# Status and strongest comparable results

Snapshot: 2026-10-14 (America/Los_Angeles). This is a bounded primary-source review, not a proof that no relevant result exists.

## 1. The actual question and current verdict

The book asks two different things:

- **C1:** whether the observed Fibonacci parastichy patterns are least-*elastic*-energy configurations of identical units on a cone; it also discusses an equal-disk-packing version.
- **C2:** whether varying disk size in packing toward the apex alternates which counter-rotating family has the larger consecutive-Fibonacci count.

**Overall verdict here: PARTIAL.** We found no verified general theorem resolving these statements under the book's unspecified elastic law and growth conditions. This is **not** a certified literature-wide “still unsolved” claim. The book itself calls both questions conjectural; the retrieved modern sources concern different precise models and do not announce a general solution of Cao's conjecture.

The central modern distinction is explicit in Lee and Levitov [3], Section II.1: “the structure does not track the global energy minimum.” The same passage says the Fibonacci structures characterize a progression of deformation, “rather than the result of global energy minimization.” Consequently, citing that theory as a global-minimum proof would reverse its stated qualification.

**What this folder settles:** a conventional fixed-golden-angle, equal-area spiral loses to a triangular-lattice quotient in a certified 183-center annular comparison, and in an asymptotic density comparison after any finite apex deletion. This does not classify the much larger family of deformed Fibonacci-contact patterns. Reflection degeneracy is proved for achiral models; alternating relative chirality is proved only for an explicitly defined local Fibonacci-family model.

**Difficulty assessment:** the finite witness and reduced-model algebra are accessible because the cone has a 60-degree commensurate development, distances reduce to an integer quadratic form, and a single short pair suffices to disprove an ansatz. Those are the actual bounded obligations. The full experimental elastic minimization is not shown to be “not too hard”: it requires a constitutive functional, stress and boundary data, a global competitor class, and a proof excluding all lower-energy competitors. No ease claim follows from having a numerical simulator.

## 2. What is established, by model

| Model and primary baseline | Established result or observation | What does not follow |
|---|---|---|
| Stressed Ag/silica microstructures [1,2] | Experiments produce Fibonacci patterns under conical support; the 2007 abstract reports 3x5 through 13x21 with definite chirality and interprets them as least elastic energy. | Experimental self-assembly is not a proof of a global minimum over all admissible shapes, nor a constitutive-energy specification. |
| Deformable cylindrical lattices [3,4] | Mechanical theories organize local rearrangements with modular symmetry and Fibonacci transitions. The 1998 chapter posted in 2021 explicitly separates these from global minimization. | The arXiv date does not make the chapter a new 2021 cone-global-optimality theorem. |
| Discrete dynamical plant-pattern model [5] | Rigorous fixed-point stability and bifurcation-diagram analysis explains Fibonacci occurrence in that system. | Stability of a growth-map fixed point does not certify a global disk-packing or elastic minimum. |
| Cylindrical disk packing and slow variation [6,7] | Cylindrical dense packings and local packing evolution can be organized by parastichy integers; local homogeneity and continuity can generate Fibonacci and related states. | A cylinder has different global closure conditions from a cone; local packing rules need not find global optima. |
| Disk accretion, including irregular states [8,9] | The same simple framework permits Fibonacci and quasi-symmetric states. The 2024 preprint compares stacking models with a large sunflower dataset and includes Lucas, double-Fibonacci, Fibonacci-plus/minus-one, and near-equal non-Fibonacci counts. | “All plants have consecutive Fibonacci counts” is false as a descriptive premise; model-data agreement does not prove global optimality. |
| Constant-divergence Fermat spirals [10,11] | Bacher provides a rigorous local-lattice/geodesic description and parastichy transitions. Akiyama proves a Fermat constant-angle set is Delone iff its angle is badly approximable. | Uniform discreteness, good uniformity, and golden-angle Diophantine extremality are not maximum disk density among all point sets. |
| Repulsive vortices on conical frusta [12] | Molecular dynamics and Monte Carlo find transitions between phyllotactic states, including non-Fibonacci states such as `(3,3,0)`, `(4,2,2)`, `(5,4,1)`, and seam/scar structures. | Agreement of two numerical methods is not a replayable global-minimum certificate. Their Bessel interaction and reservoirs are not the Ag/silica elastic experiment. |
| Hard-disk growth on cones [13] | Greedy, irreversible growth yields seams and near-tip disorder away from special commensurate cone angles. Perfect triangular crystals at sector angles `60 degrees * P` are a known baseline. | The authors explicitly distinguish growth from the earlier basin-hopping energy studies; the greedy algorithm does not solve equilibrium packing. |
| Colloidal crystals on cones [14] | 2025 experiments observe geometry-set tilt grain boundaries and, at small cone angles, commensurate bands limited by dislocations. | Real cone crystals need not be globally Fibonacci. These are physical observations, not universal hard-disk bounds. |
| Riesz energy on rectifiable surfaces [15] | For the stated classes and `s>=d`, minimal configurations asymptotically equidistribute with respect to `d`-dimensional Hausdorff measure; the critical leading term is explicit. | Equidistribution does not identify contact topology. This is ambient Euclidean Riesz distance, not automatically intrinsic geodesic distance, and surface Coulomb `s=1<2` is outside that quoted regime. |
| Planar equal-circle packing [16] | Triangular packing has density `pi/(2*sqrt(3))`. The `pi/3` cone is a sixfold rotational quotient, so lifting and quotienting transfer this asymptotic optimum. | The finite 183-point optimum need not be 1; our witness only supplies a strict comparator. |

### Strongest same-model baseline we must beat

For intrinsic equal disks on the `pi/3` quotient cone, the known triangular lattice already attains the planar density bound. There is no headroom to improve that asymptotic benchmark. The correct question is whether the proposed golden-spiral class reaches it. RESULTS.md proves a strictly smaller upper bound `pi/(2*sqrt(5))` for that fixed ansatz. This is a certification/corollary, not a claim to beat the classical packing optimum or discover commensurate cone crystals.

For the general elastic question, there is no meaningful numerical “best score” until the energy, boundaries, particle shape and relaxation constraints are fixed. A Coulomb or Riesz search would be a different model, not a substitute baseline.

## 3. Chirality status and the exact remaining gap

C2 requires distinguishing **relative parastichy chirality** (which of the two spiral families has more members) from the **ontogenetic/generative spiral** (the temporal insertion sequence). These signs can differ and can change for different reasons.

- The 2007 experiment [2] demonstrates definite chirality under its fabrication conditions, but its abstract does not establish a theorem of disk-size-driven alternation.
- Classical and modern stacking/local-lattice models [3,8–11] provide mechanisms for consecutive parastichy transitions. RESULTS.md derives explicit thresholds and alternation in a frozen golden-angle linearization, rather than assuming the qualitative mechanism solves C2.
- A 2025-online/2026-issue fossil-cone study [17] reports associations between ontogenetic chirality and seed number. The primary abstract describes an observational morphometric study, not a controlled disk-size experiment; it cannot establish the proposed causal alternation law.
- The 2026 Keller–Segel paper [18] reproduces Fibonacci and alternating patterns through growth and signaling instabilities. Its “alternating patterns” are not, by that phrase alone, proof of the book's chirality alternation.
- In an achiral domain with a reflection-invariant energy, opposite chiral minimizers have equal energy at every disk size. That symmetry rules out energetic selection of a unique absolute sign without a symmetry-breaking ingredient, but does not rule out path-dependent branch alternation.

The unresolved obligation is therefore concrete: fix an apex-directed accretion/relaxation rule, a boundary or seed that fixes the external length scale and any chirality bias, and a robust contact-network count; then prove or certify the parameter intervals and transition sequence. No universal implication “changing size means changing chirality” has been proved here.

## 5. Research direction after these certificates

The attainable result is not “Fibonacci is always optimal,” but a precise separation between geometric constraints, kinetic branch selection, and equilibrium. The full target needs a physically grounded energy and a broader contact-topology class. Fixing those assumptions is the single highest-value prerequisite; more random starts in an arbitrary energy landscape would not repair their absence.

## 6. Approved scoped target: sharp tail density — prior-art screen

The approved continuation asked for the exact tail separation/density of `r_n=c*sqrt(n), theta_n=2*pi*n*alpha` for every divergence. Its number-theoretic mechanism is **ALREADY-SOLVED**, not a justified new open selection:

- **S. E. Graiff Zurita, B. Kane, R. Oishi-Tomiyasu**, *Packing theory derived from phyllotaxis and products of linear forms*, [arXiv:2106.12333v8](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.12333v8), 26 September 2024. **Theorem 1(2), equation (12)** explicitly states `liminf_(epsilon->0) Delta(L_(phi1,phi2,epsilon)) = pi/(2*LagrangeNumber(phi1))`; its proof identifies the reciprocal Lagrange number with `liminf q||q*phi1||`. Theorem 1(1) and Example 1 give `pi/(2*sqrt(5))`. This is a lattice-deformation theorem, not literally our cone-tail statement; Bacher's local-lattice analysis supplies the established context. The elementary transfer in RESULTS.md states and proves the exact geometric quantifiers rather than claiming the formula is new.
- The related journal publication is **S. E. Graiff Zurita and R. Oishi-Tomiyasu**, *Constructive Approximation* **60**, 515–545 (2024), DOI [10.1007/s00365-024-09691-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00365-024-09691-3). The publisher lists two authors whereas the inspected arXiv versions list three. The theorem citation above is deliberately to the inspected arXiv version; full journal text was not accessible.
- **Jean-François Sadoc, Nicolas Rivier, Jean Charvolin**, *Phyllotaxis: a non conventional crystalline solution to packing efficiency in situations with radial symmetry*, [arXiv:1201.1432v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1201.1432v2) (2012), §IV.3, equations (3)–(4), already derives golden neighboring-distance minima tending to `sqrt(2*pi/sqrt(5))`. Its local approximation is not an endpoint-attainment proof.
- **Yohay Ailon Tevet**, *Chabauty Limits of Fermat Spirals*, [arXiv:2506.22863v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22863v2) (2025), Theorems 1–2, characterizes asymptotic local lattices for badly approximable divergences. It is additional prior art, not an open-status certificate.
- **A. Hurwitz**, *Ueber die angenäherte Darstellung der Irrationalzahlen durch rationale Brüche*, *Mathematische Annalen* **39**, 279–284 (1891), [original record](https://eudml.org/doc/157573), is the classical sharp `mu(alpha)<=1/sqrt(5)` baseline. The original bibliographic record was inspected; the sharp constant is also instantiated in the inspected 2024 theorem.

No nearby model is invented to preserve a novelty claim; the active slot was reassigned to the separately approved parentage-deficit-five problem.
