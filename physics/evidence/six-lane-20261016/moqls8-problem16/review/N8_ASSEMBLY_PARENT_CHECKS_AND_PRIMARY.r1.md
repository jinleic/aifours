## B. Precisely what Problem16 asks, with page and n8 scope

**Primary source:** Simeon Ball and Robin Simoens, arXiv:2607.12933v2,
[versioned primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12933v2), §5 “Conclusion and future directions”,
**printed page7 (PDF page7)**, Problem16. Exact wording, with mathematical glyphs typeset normally:

> **Problem 16.** Is a set of n − 3 MOQLS(n), n ≠ 4, necessarily classical?

Here MOQLS means mutually orthogonal quantum Latin squares. “Classical” is the paper's isotopy notion
(§§2.2–2.3): entry phases, common row/column permutations among a set, and separate per-square global unitaries;
a classical set can thereby be represented using standard orthonormal basis entries.

**Substitution n=8 gives n−3=5 and satisfies n≠4.** If all pending assembly links are discharged, the conclusion
“every set of five MOQLS(8) is classical” answers Problem16 **affirmatively at n=8**, for every genuine five-set,
not only a fixed Gram template, reducedK5 class or subset of non-classical-square counts. At this checkpoint
that answer remains CONDITIONAL: accepted direct12 plus a partially reviewed normal form and planned six
hashes alone do not establish it.

It would NOT answer Problem16 for all other n, address its explicitly excluded n=4, assert that an individual
QLS(8) is always classical, classify/count all classical five-sets, or solve the separate Problem17 on existence of
MOQLS without MOLS. No result about other t,n, whole-base BooleanF_one UNSAT, kernel verification,
novelty/priority or publishability is claimed by this statement.
