# Unrestricted q2 candidate for one explicit two-MOLS net

**Status: WRITTEN necessary-condition reduction + independently reconstructed finite formula and local DRAT observation; strict parent replay and disjoint mathematical/encoder review remain required.** Suppose an actual ordinary four-MOQLS(8) has exactly two classical members and two nonclassical members Q,R, with its two classical mates' four-net paratopic to the first pair in the pinned Wanless maximal-two-MOLS snapshot. The candidate exclusion says this q2 stratum is empty on that **one explicit net**, for arbitrary complex Q,R amplitudes and phases. It makes no flatness, fixed-support-size or component assumption.

No classification-completeness or maximality premise is needed to state this one-net candidate: its input is the concrete pair of matrices. The result does not exclude q2 for other two-MOLS species, q3/q4, unrestricted four-family nonclassicality, M(8), A10/A11, or imply novelty or independent/human/formal acceptance.

## 1. Physical-to-Boolean reduction

A four-net has the row class, column class and the two classical-mate symbol classes. Each of Q and R is an ONB on every one of these four types of line: on a fixed symbol line a classical mate's rays coincide, so actual pair-product orthogonality forces Q or R orthogonal. Permuting the four classes and reindexing the cells therefore preserves these actual ONBs, the other Q/R pair-product ONB and both members' nonclassicality.

Choose the representative's row 0 and independently map the first rows of Q and R to their own bases `e_0,...,e_7`; normalize the two classical symbols by first-row column. At off-row cell x=(i,j), both supports avoid the three distinct labels `j,L_1(x),L_2(x)`, so each is a nonempty subset of the same five-element set A_x. Their supports are **disjoint at that cell**: comparing their product vector at x with that at row-0 cell (0,t) gives `⟨Q_x,Q_(0,t)⟩⟨R_x,R_(0,t)⟩=0` for every t. Both Q and R being nonclassical means each has at least one off-row support of size at least two.

The formula uses only necessary consequences of the actual family:

1. nonempty off-row supports inside A_x for both members; same-cell disjointness;
2. on each of the four actual net lines, every first-row coordinate is covered by some ray; when that coordinate's first-row ray is not already on the line, exactly five off-row cells allow it;
3. for either member, two cells on a common net line cannot have support intersection of size one;
4. three pairwise line-adjacent rays of either member cannot have all three pairwise **actual support** intersections equal to the same pair of axes: their projections would be three nonzero mutually orthogonal vectors in a two-dimensional space;
5. each member has a support of size at least two.

Condition 4 is encoded conditionally, not by assuming that five-element allowed sets themselves intersect in exactly two axes. An auxiliary `B_(member,x,y,t)` is equivalent to both member-support bits on axis t. For each candidate triangle and axis pair `{t,u}`, a clause excludes the conjunction that for all three edges `B_t=B_u=1` and every other shared-axis B is zero. All actual families satisfy these clauses for arbitrary nonzero amplitudes. The formula deliberately does **not** encode any other cross-cell Q/R pair-product restrictions, so it is a necessary relaxation; UNSAT of the exact bound CNF is sufficient for this q2 exclusion.
