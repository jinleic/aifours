# Four-family q2/q3/q4: two nonclassical four-ray members are impossible

**WRITTEN author candidate, no catalogue or computation premise; disjoint mathematical review pending.** A QLS has the *four-ray property* when every pair of its cell rays has squared overlap in `{0,1/4,1}`. In any actual ordinary four-MOQLS(8), **at most one nonclassical member has this property**, regardless of whether the other members are classical, real-flat or have the property.

Consequences are exact partial exclusions for the original four-family mixtures: in q2, its two nonclassical members cannot both be four-ray; in q3, at least two of the three nonclassical members must fail the property; in q4, at least three must fail it. In particular a q3/q4 family cannot have two nonclassical members that are real-flat in their own separate output frames, since every real-flat QLS is four-ray. These do **not** rule out q2, q3 or q4 in general or provide an M(8) bound.

## Written argument from the original ONBs

Independently standardize the actual first row of each member to basis rays `e_j` with common column labels j. If a member is four-ray, each of its cells has coordinate squared magnitudes in `{0,1/4,1}` relative to this row; normalization forces either a **light** singleton ray or a **heavy** ray supported on four coordinates with squared magnitudes 1/4. A member is nonclassical exactly when some cell is heavy.

At an off-first-row cell in column j, every member excludes coordinate j. The product vector at that cell is orthogonal to the product vector at first-row cell (0,k) for each pair of members, so their supports are pairwise disjoint subsets of the seven remaining coordinate labels. In particular, two four-ray members cannot both be heavy at the same cell. On any row or column containing a heavy ray, no light ray can use one of its four axes; the resolution of identity on that axis then needs at least four heavy contributions of 1/4. Thus each heavy column contains at least four heavy cells. Heavy cells of two members are disjoint, so they cannot occupy the same column: 4+4 exceeds the seven off-first-row cells.

Every nonclassical four-ray member needs at least **five** heavy columns. Otherwise some heavy row gives at least four heavy columns, so suppose there are exactly four, call them C. Every heavy row has exactly four heavy cells, all in C. On an ONB line with exactly four heavy rays, their supports coincide: each axis used by one requires all four 1/4 contributions. The common row support avoids every label in C, so it equals `S=C^c` for every heavy row. A heavy column contains mutually orthogonal vectors in the four-dimensional space S, hence at most four heavy rows; the line lower bound gives at least four. The heavy cells therefore form a 4×4 board R×C with common support S.

At each R×C cell, Q occupies the four labels S; each of its three mates must avoid S and the column label j∈C. Their nonzero pairwise-disjoint supports exhaust the remaining three labels in C as singletons. For each mate, the four singleton vectors in a heavy row on C form a basis of its C-space, so the other four entries of that row lie in S. The same is true columnwise; completeness on the other rows/columns then places the mate in its C-space on all cells `(R×C) ∪ (R^c×C^c)`. Any two mates consequently have 32 pair-product vectors in the 16-dimensional product of their C-spaces, contradicting their actual product ONB. Thus the four-column board is impossible, independently of the mates' own support restrictions.

Each nonclassical four-ray member therefore has at least five heavy columns, and distinct such members have disjoint heavy-column sets. Two would require at least ten of the eight columns, contradiction.

A real-flat QLS is four-ray because all first-row real sign vectors have the same parity and every other real sign vector is orthogonal to one first-row vector in its column, hence has that parity; pairwise Hamming distances are even and squared overlaps are 0,1/4 or1. The four-ray property also admits standalone complex non-real-flat QLS; an exact example and control are pinned in `frontier-20261028-four-ray-classicality-v1.md` SHA256 f39acce9b5bd88e25fe2ea5bffa809b568e6ccad54516254206cffe80c003606. That example has no asserted mates.
