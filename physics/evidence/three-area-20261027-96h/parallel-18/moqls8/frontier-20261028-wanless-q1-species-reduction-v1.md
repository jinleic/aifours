## Claim and remaining case

Form the ordinary five-net of the row, column and three classical-symbol classes. For each of the 38 explicitly identified catalogue representatives listed in the orientation receipt below, if this five-net is paratopic to that representative then Q is classical.

Under the additional external import that the pinned Wanless catalogue contains all 39 species of triples of mutually orthogonal Latin squares of order 8, any such nonclassical Q can occur only with **catalogue species index 22**, zero-based, i.e. the 23rd maximal triple, its three squares being the 67th–69th in the retained maximal file. This is a remaining necessary net type, **not an example** and not a proof that this net admits a real-flat nonclassical Q. All other 28 have empty core. The index/representative convention is that of the pinned complete screen receipt; it is not a canonical mathematical numbering.

## All-five-line reorientation, not just a relabeling assumption

After separate output unitaries the three classical mates are arrays of basis vectors encoding three classical MOLS. On any row or column, Q is an orthonormal basis by its QLS property. On a line on which one mate has a fixed symbol, every two distinct cells have identical mate vector; product orthogonality forces the corresponding Q vectors to be orthogonal, hence they are an eight-vector orthonormal basis. Therefore **all five classes of net lines are Q-bases**. Any two classes may be selected as new row/column classes; the remaining three become classical mates. Orthogonality to those mates and the real-flat property of Q survive the common cell reindexing. Consequently finding even *one* zero-core ordered orientation for a net species excludes a nonclassical Q on that entire species. A zero core for merely one arbitrarily fixed first-row labeling would not suffice; all 30 structures below are checked.

Independent symbol permutations and permutations of the five net classes are exactly the paratopy/species operations being imported. Even if an actual paratopy maps its old first row elsewhere, the representative's row 0 is a Q-basis, so it may be used anew as the first row. Its induced Walsh labeling is included among all 30 structures. Normalize each of the other three class symbols so that its value on first-row column j is j; this is an independent symbol renaming and does not alter the Q vectors.

## The necessary XOR and five-line core

The real-flat first-row ONB is an order-8 real Hadamard basis. The self-contained order-8 Hadamard/Walsh analysis and ray alphabet in the named flat-real packet give: in that first-row basis each Q ray is either **light**, supported on exactly one first-row axis, or **heavy**, supported on an affine two-plane of four axes, with all four squared coordinate magnitudes 1/4. This classification is a named written import; it concerns arbitrary real-flat Q, not merely character-form Q.

For a cell (i,j) off the first row, its Q ray is orthogonal to Q(0,j) by its column and also to Q(0,L_a(i,j)) for each normalized classical mate a. The four forbidden first-row labels are pairwise distinct by the five-net's pairwise orthogonality. If the ray is heavy, its support is exactly their four-label complement; since the complement of a four-point affine plane in F_2^3 is itself an affine plane, the forbidden four labels must be an affine plane. The screen calls such a cell eligible. Eligibility is necessary, not sufficient for a Q ray or a full family.

On any of the five net lines, Q gives an ONB. If one heavy ray has nonzero coefficient on a first-row axis, no light ray on that same axis can occur on that line. The axis's resolution of identity thus forces at least four heavy rays, each contributing 1/4. Hence every line containing a heavy cell contains at least four heavy cells. Starting from all eligible off-first-row cells, iteratively remove any cell lying on any net line with fewer than four currently eligible cells. Inductively no actually heavy cell is ever removed. The unique greatest fixed point is the **five-line 4-core**. If it is empty, Q has no heavy cells and all 64 rays belong to the same first-row ONB; Q is classical. The iteration uses all five original net classes and is a necessary-condition argument, not an abstract replacement for the actual rows.

## Affine-label coverage and finite evidence

A Walsh structure on eight labeled first-row rays is a bijection to F_2^3 modulo affine relabeling. Translate so label 0 maps to 0; there are 7! bijections, and GL(3,2) has 7·6·4 = 168 elements. Thus there are 7!/168 = 30 affine-plane systems. The producer explicitly enumerates these 30 systems from the 5040 bijections; an independent checker instead enumerates all Steiner triple systems on the seven nonzero labels, pairs each of their seven planes through 0 with its complement, and matches the resulting 30 plane systems to the producer's list. Each checker validates all three Latin squares and their three pairwise orthogonality relations in every retained triple.

The complete screen treats 39 recorded triples × 20 ordered distinct row/column class choices × 30 plane structures = 23,400 contexts. Its core-size histogram is 0: 23,168; 32: 140; 40: 80; 56: 12. The independent checker reconstructs every context and computes the core by simultaneous fixed-point sweeps rather than the producer's deletion queue, matching all eligible and core counts. It also reports negative controls for a non-Latin row, an omitted species record, and an altered core count. Those controls are limited sensitivity checks, not a formal proof of either program. The orientation selector reports a zero-core orientation for 38 species, and none for index 22; in that species and orientation (0,1), only structures 2 and 5 survive. The full per-orientation vectors are retained, not reconstructed from this summary.
