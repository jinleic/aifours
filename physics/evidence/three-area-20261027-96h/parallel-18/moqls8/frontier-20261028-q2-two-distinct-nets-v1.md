## 1. Shared all-variable support reduction

The full derivation and Net A formula are in the cited candidate packet. In brief, use any actual net line as row 0 and identify Q's and R's row-0 bases independently as `e_0,...,e_7`. For each off-row cell x, the column and two normalized classical-mate symbols rule out three distinct axes, leaving five allowed axes A_x for both Q and R. Their actual supports are nonempty subsets of A_x and are disjoint at that cell, by comparing the Q⊗R product vector at x to the one at each first-row cell. Each of Q,R is an ONB on all four net line classes; thus every axis is covered on each line, and two same-line supports cannot meet in exactly one axis. Both members being nonclassical requires a support of size at least two for each.

For a single Q or R, three pairwise line-adjacent cells cannot have all three actual pairwise support intersections equal the same two axes: their nonzero projections would be three pairwise orthogonal vectors in C². An exact intersection auxiliary B is equivalent to both support bits for the axis. The CNF excludes the conjunction of the three exact-two-axis edge events; this uses arbitrary amplitudes and does not replace the actual line ONBs. Other cross-cell Q/R pair-product restrictions are intentionally **omitted**, so every physical q2 family induces a model of the weaker formula. Consequently a valid UNSAT certificate excludes q2 on the selected net.

## 3. Exact zero-versus-positive transversal invariant

A common transversal of two classical order-8 MOLS is a set of eight cells with exactly one in each row, column and each of the two symbol classes. Any classical third QLS orthogonal to both is an ordinary third MOLS; each of its eight symbol classes is such a common transversal. This condition is invariant under permuting the four net classes, so a zero-versus-positive count separates net species under paratopy.
