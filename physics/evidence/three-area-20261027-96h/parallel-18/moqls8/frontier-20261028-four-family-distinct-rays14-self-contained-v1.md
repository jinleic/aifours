# A nonclassical four-MOQLS(8) member needs at least 14 distinct rays

**SELF-CONTAINED WRITTEN AUTHOR CANDIDATE; complete independent review pending.** In every ordinary complex four-MOQLS(8), each nonclassical member Q has at least **14 distinct entry rays**. This is a global ray-count restriction, distinct from the 13-mixed-cell candidate: it neither proves a four-family witness nor rules out all nonclassical four-families, q1/q2/q3/q4, numerical M(8), or A10/A11. No catalogue, selected witness, numerical solver, equal-modulus or reality assumption is used. The proof for at most four novel rays is included in full as Appendix A, not assumed from an external file. All claims here remain author-written, not parent, independent-family, formal, human, executable or novelty acceptance.

## Definitions and common tools

An ordinary order-8 QLS is an 8×8 array of unit vectors in C^8, each row and column an ONB. Four arrays Q,R,S,T are mutually orthogonal when every pair's 64 same-cell tensor products is an ONB of C^8⊗C^8. Independently standardize an arbitrary actual board row of each array to its own coordinate basis with common column labels. For distinct cells x,y and distinct members M,N, actual orthogonality gives `<M_x,M_y><N_x,N_y>=0`; equal numerical indices across the locally standardized factors do not identify their physical Hilbert spaces.

The selected Q row already has eight different rays. Call every other Q ray *novel*. A novel ray uses at least two Q-coordinate axes. Each mixed line has at least two novel entries and they form an ONB of the complementary coordinate subspace, whose dimension is the number of entries. A degree-2 line uses one common 2-support. A degree-3 line has at most one support-2 ray and hence at least two full-support-3 rays. Distinct orthogonal support-2 rays on one line have disjoint supports.

The appendix proves two reusable facts directly for arbitrary complex vectors and actual products:

1. **Isolated support-pair obstruction.** If two orthogonal novel rays p,q use one common 2-support U, no other novel Q ray uses one axis a∈U, and every line meeting p or q contains both, a four-family is impossible. The proof uses the n−k light-e_a M rays to put the p/q mate bases in one k-space, actual row/column transition zeros to force k≥5, and the other U-axis to force k≤4.
2. **Four-line facts.** A 4-mixed line cannot contain one support-2 ray plus two support-3 rays, nor two support-2 rays plus one support-3 ray. If all supports are 2 or 3 and one is 2, all four are support 2 in disjoint pairs.

We also repeatedly use the following saturation fact. If a full-support-3 Q ray p on T appears in five cells, its five M rays are an ONB of T^⊥ for every mate M. At any off-standard cell y with Q_y=e_z, z∈T, product orthogonality implies M_y∈T and M_y⊥e_z. Three mate vectors then cannot have pairwise disjoint nonempty supports in T\{z}, as required by their pair-products against the first-row products. Thus such a y rules out the four-family.

For the five-novel-ray analysis below, a *type-A* row/column contains the indicated full triple or quadruple and a *type-B* line the indicated complementary rays. Counts k_A,k_B are determined by repeated actual Q ray classes, not by assuming that the cells connecting two line types stay within the same type. When all selected Q rays use an axis a, the 8−k unmixed rows have light Q=e_a cells; their mate rays are orthogonal to the selected mate rays, putting them in a common space of dimension k, where k is the number of mixed rows. Two k'-ray mate classes that span one k'-space and have two actual row/column zeros in each transition row force k'≥5: three mate transition matrices have pairwise-disjoint supports and each row is nonzero.

Appendix A excludes at most four novel rays. Assume exactly five distinct novel Q rays and classify their maximal mixed-line degree.

## 1. A degree-5 mixed line

The five novel rays are an ONB of a 5-axis coordinate set K. A degree-4 mixed line is impossible: its four rays span a coordinate 4-subspace and the fifth global novel ray is the remaining singleton in K. If any degree-2 line uses a support-2 pair, the other three global novel rays avoid those two axes; every line meeting that pair contains both by completeness, so the isolated-pair obstruction applies. Similarly, if a degree-3 line uses three rays on a 3-axis set, the other two global novel rays form an orthonormal pair on the complementary 2-axis set and the isolated-pair obstruction applies. Thus all mixed lines have degree 5.

Let k be the number of mixed rows. Every such row and column contains all five novel rays exactly once. A coordinate of K is used by at least two novel rays: a lone nonzero coefficient in that coordinate would have modulus 1 by basis completeness and make its ray a singleton. For two classes using the same coordinate a, the 8−k unmixed rows provide light-e_a mate rays and the two k-ray mate classes are ONBs of one k-space. The two row/column transition zeros and three mates give k≥5. A novel ray with support s contributes k mutually orthogonal mate rays in the (8−s)-coordinate complement of its support, so k≤8−s and no support-4 or support-5 ray exists. All five rays cannot have support 2: their supports would form disjoint 2-axis planes, each filled by exactly two rays. At least one ray has support 3, so k=5.

Any two support-3 rays use 3-subsets of K and hence share an axis a. Their two five-ray mate classes lie in the same 5-space, the complement of the three light-e_a mate rays, and each also fills the 5-dimensional coordinate complement of its own Q support. Therefore their support-3 sets agree; call it T. There cannot be four such Q rays in T. With one support-3 ray, the other four support-2 rays fill two disjoint 2-planes and force that ray to be a singleton, impossible. With two support-3 rays, at most one remaining support-2 ray can lie in T, since the complement of their span in T is one-dimensional; any other support-2 ray must be in K\T because it cannot meet T in exactly one nonzero coordinate and be orthogonal to a full-T ray. Thus the other two fill K\T. With three support-3 rays, they fill T and the remaining two fill K\T. In either possible case K\T supplies an isolated support-2 pair, excluded by the lemma.

## 2. A degree-4 but no degree-5 mixed line

Let its four novel rays be an ONB of a 4-axis set K, and let v be the fifth novel ray. There is only one possible four-ray subset on a degree-4 line. Two different four-subsets of five rays share three rays. If their coordinate 4-spaces coincide, the fourth ray is uniquely forced; if they differ, their intersection has at most three axes and the shared three orthogonal rays span that intersection, forcing the fourth ray of the first basis to be a singleton. Both contradict distinct novelty. A degree-3 line using only the original four rays would likewise force their fourth ray to be a singleton. Any degree-2 line using only those four would split all four into two support-2 pairs on complementary coordinate planes.

### 2a. The fifth ray occurs on a degree-3 line

Write that line's rays v,q_1,q_2 on a 3-axis set T. At least one of q_1,q_2 has full support T, so T⊂K. Let z be the fourth axis in K, and let s,t be the two remaining rays of the degree-4 basis. They are orthogonal to q_1,q_2 and hence lie in span{v,e_z}. Both coefficients are nonzero: a zero e_z coefficient would make an A ray collinear with v; a zero v coefficient would make it the singleton e_z. Therefore s,t overlap v and have support `supp(v)∪{z}`.

There is a coordinate a used by all five novel rays. If v has support 2, q_1,q_2 have full support T and any a in supp(v) works. If v has support 3, s,t have full support K, and q_1,q_2 have at most one support-2 ray, so choose a in that ray's support if it exists, or any a∈T otherwise. Since s,t have support at least 3, no degree-2 line can use only rays of the original quadruple; no degree-3 line can use only that quadruple. Consequently every mixed line is either the degree-4 quadruple (A) or contains v (B). Let their row counts be k_A,k_B; repeated ray classes give the same column counts and k=k_A+k_B≤7.

For each mate M, all selected mixed M rays are orthogonal to the 8−k light-e_a M rays and lie in a k-space. The k_B M rays at v are orthogonal and orthogonal to the k_A rays of each of s,t because Q_v overlaps both. Thus the s and t mate classes are ONBs of their common k_A-dimensional complement. Their actual row/column transition zeros and three mates force k_A≥5. If v has support 3, s,t have support 4 and give k_A≤4, impossible. Therefore v has support 2 U, s,t have full support U∪{z}, and k_A=5. In every B line, v can meet only q_1,q_2: it is not orthogonal to s,t, and q_1,q_2 have full support 3, so v has no degree-2 partner. Hence an actual B row has three mixed rays on T and avoids z. It contains a light Q=e_z cell; the s-class five-ray saturation on U∪{z} gives the contradiction in the common saturation fact.

### 2b. The fifth ray occurs on a degree-2 line

The preceding case is impossible, so there is no degree-3 line containing v. Its degree-2 partner p is one of the original four, and p,v are an orthonormal pair on support U. There is no degree-2 line containing only rays of the original quadruple: that would split its basis into two support-2 pairs, forcing its U-partner of p to be v, contrary to distinctness. No degree-3 line using only the quadruple exists, and no degree-5 line exists in this case. Thus every mixed line is the original quadruple (A) or the pair p,v (B). Each line contains p, so k=k_A+k_B≤7, with the same A/B counts on rows and columns.

Among the other three quadruple rays, at least two, s,t, overlap v. Indeed v⊥p and expansion in the quadruple basis cannot have only one nonzero coefficient without making v one of those rays. Their nonzero projections onto U are collinear with v and use both axes of U; since s,t differ from v, each has support at least 3. Choose a∈U. The 8−k unmixed rows have light Q=e_a cells, and the mate classes of v,s,t lie in their common k-dimensional complement. The k_B mate rays of v are orthogonal and orthogonal to the k_A mate rays of each s,t. Thus s,t form ONBs of one k_A-space; three transition matrices force k_A≥5. Their supports have at least three axes, so k_A≤5 and s has full support U∪{z} for some z∈K\U. Any B row has only p,v mixed on U and therefore contains a light Q=e_z cell. The five s-class mate rays fill `(U∪{z})^⊥`, so the common saturation fact gives a contradiction.

## 3. No degree-4 or degree-5 mixed line

If all mixed lines have degree 2, every novel ray has a unique support-2 orthogonal partner; the novel-ray set partitions into pairs, so it cannot have five elements. Assume at least one degree-3 line. Two different three-ray subsets of the five novel rays cannot share two rays: at least one shared ray has full support 3 and identifies their common coordinate 3-space, where the shared orthogonal pair uniquely determines the third ray. Thus distinct triples intersect in at most one ray. There can be at most two triples: two triples meeting in one ray already use all five rays, and no third 3-subset of those five can meet each in exactly one ray.

### 3a. Two distinct triples

Write the triples `{u,p,q}` and `{u,r,s}`. If u has full support 3, both triples lie in its 3-space and p,q and r,s are distinct ONBs of u^⊥, so all cross overlaps are nonzero. If u has support 2 U, the other four rays have full support 3. Their 3-spaces either agree, giving the same 2-plane argument, or meet precisely in U; their nonzero U-projections are all collinear with u^⊥, so all cross overlaps are again nonzero.

No degree-2 line is possible. Each triple has at most one support-2 ray; if u has support 2 it is the only such ray, while if u has support 3 there can be at most one in each of the two other pairs, and those cross-pair rays have nonzero inner product. Every mixed line therefore contains one of the triples and contains u. Let k_A,k_B be the numbers of lines of the two types and k=k_A+k_B≤7; the same counts hold for rows and columns by the p and r ray classes.

All five novel Q rays use a common coordinate a: if u has support 2 choose a in that support; if u has support 3, both bases lie in its 3-space and among p,q,r,s at most two support-2 rays omit at most two axes. The 8−k light-e_a rows put all mixed mate rays in a k-space. The two groups' mate spans are orthogonal by the cross Q overlaps, have dimensions at least k_A,k_B, and hence have exactly those dimensions. Within each group its two novel-ray classes are ONBs of that group space. Each p/q and r/s transition row has two actual row/column zeros, so the three mates require k_A,k_B≥5. This contradicts k≤7.

### 3b. One triple and one extra pair

Let the unique triple be `{u,p,q}` on a 3-axis set T, and let the two remaining novel rays be r,s. They must occur on degree-2 lines. Each support-2 ray has a unique orthogonal partner on its support. If an extra ray paired the triple's sole possible support-2 ray, the other extra ray would have no possible partner; hence r,s pair with each other on a common 2-support V. The triple and the pair are disjoint Q-ray classes. Let their line counts be k_A,k_B, so k_A+k_B≤7 and k_A≥3.

If V∩T is empty, the isolated-pair obstruction applies. If V and T meet in exactly one axis a, at least two of the three triple rays use a: otherwise basis completeness would make the unique user a singleton. Those two Q rays overlap both r,s on exactly a. The 8−k_A−k_B unmixed rows supply light-e_a mate rays. The mate span of the selected triple classes and the span of the r/s classes are orthogonal, and each has dimension at least its line count; dimension 8 forces dimensions exactly k_A,k_B. Thus each of the two selected triple classes is an ONB of the first space and r/s are ONBs of the second. Three mate transition supports force k_A,k_B≥5, contradiction.

Finally suppose V⊂T. Some triple ray w overlaps both r and s. Otherwise each triple ray has nonzero V-projection proportional to r or to s; two projections choose the same direction, and the two orthogonal rays span that direction plus the third axis T\V. Their orthogonal triple mate would be the other ray r or s, contrary to the five Q-ray classes being distinct. Choose any coordinate a∈V used by w. The w-class k_A mate rays and both pair classes' k_B mate rays are orthogonal across groups, as are the 8−k_A−k_B light-e_a mate rays. Dimension 8 forces the r/s group span to have dimension k_B, so r/s are ONBs there. Three transition supports force k_B≥5, but k_A≥3, again contradicting k_A+k_B≤7.

All maximal-degree cases with exactly five novel rays are excluded. Appendix A excludes fewer. The standardized row has eight coordinate rays, so every nonclassical Q in an ordinary four-MOQLS(8) has at least **14 distinct entry rays**, subject to independent verification of this entire written proof. This does not decide four-family existence/full classicality, numerical M(8), A10/A11, priority or novelty.

### Physical setup and line facts

An ordinary QLS of order 8 is an array of unit vectors in C^8 whose rows and columns are ONBs. Four QLS Q,R,S,T are mutually orthogonal when, for every pair of arrays, their 64 same-cell tensor products form an ONB of C^8⊗C^8. Independently standardize an arbitrary actual board row of each array to its own coordinate basis, with common column labels. In particular, for different cells x,y and any distinct members M,N, `<M_x,M_y><N_x,N_y>=0`. The local coordinate bases belong to different Hilbert spaces; using the same index does not identify the spaces.

A Q ray is *light* if it is one of the chosen row's eight coordinate rays and *novel* otherwise. A novel ray has support size at least two. A mixed line has at least two novel entries; with k such entries, they form an ONB of the complementary k-axis space. Two novel entries on a degree-2 line are orthogonal and have one common 2-support. A degree-3 line has at most one support-2 ray, hence at least two full-support-3 rays. Distinct orthogonal support-2 rays in a line are disjoint; equal support-2 rays fill that 2-plane.

On a degree-4 line, one support-2 ray and two support-3 rays are impossible. Each support-3 ray must contain both axes of the support-2 ray; its nonzero projection there lies on the same one-dimensional perpendicular direction. If their third axes differ their inner product is the nonzero inner product of those projections. If their third axes agree, the three rays form an ONB of those three coordinates and force the fourth ray to be a singleton. Likewise two support-2 rays and one support-3 ray are impossible: equal support-2 rays fill their plane, while disjoint support-2 sets cover all four axes and every 3-support meets one in exactly one nonzero coordinate. Therefore a degree-4 line using only support sizes 2 or 3 and containing a support-2 ray consists entirely of support-2 rays, in two disjoint pairs.

### Isolated support-pair lemma

Suppose the only novel Q rays using some coordinate a are two orthogonal rays p,q on a common support U={a,b}, and every line meeting p or q contains one of each. Let k be their number of incident rows; p and q each occur k times, once per such row and column. The standardized row contains no novel ray, so k≤7. All rows without p/q have a light Q=e_a cell, since their novel rays avoid a. For any mate M, these 8−k M rays are pairwise orthogonal and orthogonal to every M ray at p/q cells; the k M rays at p cells and the k at q cells are each orthogonal. Both classes are ONBs of the same k-dimensional space W_M.

For any p cell, the q cells in its actual row and column are distinct, so the transition matrix between the p and q M bases has two zeros in every row. Distinct mates have disjoint transition supports by M⊗N orthogonality; since three unitary matrices need a nonzero entry in each row, k≥5. On the other hand the first-row M=e_b ray is orthogonal to W_M, because every p/q Q ray uses b. The 8−k light Q=e_a M rays form an ONB of W_M^⊥ and include first-row M=e_a. Thus at least one of the other `7−k` light-e_a cells has nonzero M overlap with e_b. Different mates cannot use the same such cell, by their pair-product orthogonality against the first-row product at b. Three mates require `7−k≥3`, so k≤4, contradiction. This holds even if the p/q incidence graph has several cycles and other novel rays occur on disjoint supports.

### If every mixed line has degree 2

Every novel ray is paired on each incident line with the unique orthogonal ray on the same 2-support. With at most four novel rays there are at most two such distinct orthonormal ray pairs. If there is one pair, the isolated-pair lemma applies. If there are two pairs with different supports, each has an axis absent from the other and the lemma applies to either pair. If they have the same support U but different ray bases, every cross-basis Q overlap is nonzero. The two groups have disjoint mixed row sets, since each mixed row has just one pair. If their row counts are k_1,k_2, each group's M rays span at least k_i dimensions, the group spans are mutually orthogonal by Q⊗M, and the 8−k_1−k_2 light Q=e_a rays are orthogonal to both. Dimension 8 forces each group span to have dimension exactly k_i. Its two k_i-ray parts are ONBs there. Three disjoint nonzero transition supports and two row/column zero positions then force k_i≥5 for both groups, contradicting k_1+k_2≤7. Thus the all-degree-2 case is impossible in a four-family with at most four novel Q rays.

### Degree 3 but no degree 4

Assume there is a degree-3 mixed line. If there are at most three novel rays, they are exactly its three orthonormal rays, on a 3-axis set T. If there are four novel rays, any degree-3 line also uses three of them. Two different three-ray subsets would share two rays; at least one shared ray has full support 3, identifying the same coordinate 3-space for both lines. Their shared orthonormal pair then forces the third ray to coincide, a contradiction. Hence only one three-ray subset can occur.

Call that triple `{u,p,q}`, with p,q of full support T; u may also be full support. Any fourth novel ray u' cannot occur in a degree-3 or degree-4 line, so it must lie in a degree-2 line. Its partner must be the unique possible support-2 ray u of the triple; u,u' are perpendicular on a common support U⊂T. If u has full support or no fourth ray occurs, there are no such degree-2 lines. Thus every mixed line is either type A, containing u,p,q, or type B, containing u,u'.

Every mixed line contains u exactly once, so the number k of mixed rows equals the number of mixed columns. Let k_A and k_B be the numbers of A and B rows, with k=k_A+k_B≤7. They also equal the corresponding column counts because p (respectively u' when present) occurs exactly once on every A (respectively B) line. Choose a coordinate a in the support of u. Every novel ray uses a: p,q use all T, and u' uses U when present. The 8−k unmixed rows contain light Q=e_a cells. For any mate M their M rays are orthogonal, and all mixed M rays lie in their k-dimensional orthogonal complement W_M.

The k_B M rays at u' cells are pairwise orthogonal, and every one is orthogonal to all M rays at p or q cells: p,q have nonzero projection on U perpendicular to u and hence overlap u'. Each of the p and q classes contributes k_A orthogonal M rays in the complement of those k_B rays inside W_M, whose dimension is k_A. They are two ONBs of the same k_A-space. At each p cell its row and column contain two distinct q cells, so three pairwise-disjoint unitary transition supports force k_A≥5. But p has full support T, and the k_A p-class M rays lie in the 5-dimensional coordinate complement of T by comparison with the three first-row products. Hence k_A=5 and these rays form an ONB of T^⊥ for each mate.

If a type-B row exists, its two mixed rays u,u' avoid the third axis z∈T\U, so completeness provides a light Q=e_z cell y in that row. If no B row exists, exactly five rows are mixed; choose a light Q=e_z cell y in any off-standard unmixed row, with any z∈T. In either case Q_y=e_z overlaps the full-support-T ray p, so Q⊗M forces M_y orthogonal to the p-class ONB of T^⊥, i.e. M_y∈T. Comparison with the first-row Q⊗M product at z also gives M_y⊥e_z. Therefore all three mate rays R_y,S_y,T_y are nonzero and supported on the other two axes of T. Pair-product orthogonality against each corresponding first-row product makes their supports pairwise disjoint, impossible for three nonzero vectors in two coordinates.

### A degree-4 mixed line

A degree-4 line uses all four novel rays as an ONB of a 4-axis subspace K. No degree-3 line can exist: any three of those rays on a degree-3 line would span its 3-axis subspace, and the fourth global novel ray, orthogonal to all three in K, would be the remaining coordinate singleton. A degree-2 mixed line, if present, uses two novel rays on one support U; the other two global novel rays are orthogonal to that 2-plane in K and, being novel, are an orthonormal pair on the complementary 2-support V. The isolated-pair lemma applies. Hence every mixed line has degree 4.

Let k be the number of mixed rows, equal to the number of mixed columns since each contains all four novel rays once. Each coordinate of K is used by at least two of the four rays: if only one used it, basis completeness would make that vector a coordinate singleton. Select any coordinate a and two novel rays using a. The 8−k unmixed rows supply light Q=e_a cells. For any mate, each of the two k-ray classes is an ONB of their common k-dimensional orthogonal complement. Two actual row/column neighbor positions are zero in each transition row, and three mates have disjoint nonzero transition supports, so k≥5.

A novel ray with support s appears k times, and its k mate rays are orthogonal and avoid its s first-row coordinate axes. Thus k≤8−s. In particular no support-4 ray exists. If any ray has support 2, the degree-4 line facts above and the absence of support 4 force all four rays to have support 2, in two disjoint orthonormal 2-support pairs; the isolated-pair lemma applies. Thus all four novel rays have support 3, so k≤5 and k=5.

Any two of their support-3 sets meet in at least two coordinates. Choose a shared coordinate a. The 8−k=3 light-e_a M rays force both associated five-ray M classes into the same 5-space. Each class is an ONB of the five-dimensional complement of its own 3-axis Q support, so these two coordinate complements agree and the two Q supports are equal. All four supports are therefore the same 3-set, which cannot contain four orthogonal Q rays. Contradiction.

Every possible mixed-line degree with at most four novel rays has been excluded. A chosen row already supplies eight distinct Q rays, so a nonclassical Q needs at least five novel rays: **at least 13 distinct entry rays**.
