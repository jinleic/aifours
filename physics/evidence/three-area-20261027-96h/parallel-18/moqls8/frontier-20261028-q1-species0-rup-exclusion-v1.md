## 1. The actual physical-to-support reduction

After separate output unitaries, the three classical companions are ordinary MOLS; rows, columns and their three symbol classes make a five-net. Q is an ONB on every line of all five classes: on a mate-symbol line the classical ray is identical, so product-ONB orthogonality forces Q rays pairwise orthogonal. Under any paratopy to the pinned representative, Q can be reindexed using representative classes (0,1) as rows and columns, preserving its ray set, ONBs on all five classes, and nonclassicality. Normalize the remaining three class labels by their values on representative row 0.

Let the eight actual Q rays in row 0 be the basis `e_0,...,e_7`. For each cell x=(i,j), i>0, Q(x) is orthogonal to four distinct first-row rays: the same-column ray e_j and the three rays at the first-row intersections of its mate-symbol lines. Its actual support S_x is a nonempty subset of the four remaining basis labels A_x. The first row has fixed singleton supports `{j}`. Q is nonclassical if and only if at least one actual S_x has size at least two: if all supports are singletons all rays belong to the first-row ONB, and any classical Q must use that ONB because its first row already contains eight orthogonal rays.

The CNF uses the following **necessary**, not asserted sufficient, conditions:

1. Each S_x is nonempty and contained in A_x. Each of the 224 allowed coordinate incidences has one Boolean variable.
2. On any of the five actual ONB lines and for each coordinate t, at least one ray has a nonzero t-coordinate. If the first-row e_t lies on that line it already supplies this coordinate and all off-row cells on the line exclude t; otherwise precisely four off-row cells allow t, and their Boolean disjunction is required.
3. Two distinct cells on a common net line cannot have support intersection of size one: their complex inner product would have exactly one nonzero summand. Standard conjunction auxiliaries encode the two-coordinate-or-more alternative.
4. Whenever three same-line-adjacent cells pairwise have **allowed** intersections equal to the same two-element set T, they cannot all have both T coordinates nonzero. Their projections to the two-dimensional span of T would otherwise be three nonzero pairwise orthogonal vectors. This is valid for arbitrary complex magnitudes and phases. The formula records all 160 such triangles in this net.
5. A nonclassicality clause requires at least one cell to have two supported axes. Auxiliary variables are only forced to imply their two axes; one auxiliary must be true. This is satisfiability-equivalent to the required positive two-axis occurrence, without assuming any converse from CNF model to physical realization.

Any actual nonclassical Q supplies an assignment satisfying all these clauses. Consequently UNSAT of the exact bound CNF proves the claimed net exclusion, subject to the implementation/proof assurance below. There is no hidden equal-weight, generic-position or sign-vector premise.
