# Four heavy mixed cells force a four-axis confinement

**SELF-CONTAINED WRITTEN AUTHOR CANDIDATE; full independent review pending.** In an ordinary complex four-MOQLS(8), fix any nonclassical member Q and any actual Q row. Call a mixed Q cell *heavy* if its support in that row basis has size at least 3. Then Q has either at least six heavy cells, or exactly four heavy cells whose common support is a 4-axis set D containing the support of **every** mixed Q cell. In the latter case every chosen-row coordinate outside D is a complete Q ray occurring once in every row and column. In particular, exactly four heavy cells with a common 3-axis support are impossible in a four-family.

This is a universal component/span restriction, not a proof of full four-family classicality, a physical four-family witness, a numerical M(8) upgrade, an A10/A11 result, formal verification or novelty. The axis-cover proof used below is included in full as Appendix A, not imported from an external file. All claims remain author-written and unreviewed.

## Physical normalization and heavy-pairing facts

An ordinary QLS is an 8×8 array of unit vectors in C^8 whose rows and columns are ONBs. Four QLS form a four-MOQLS(8) if each pair's 64 same-cell tensor products form an ONB of C^8⊗C^8. Independently standardize any actual board row of all four arrays to their own coordinate bases at common column indices. For distinct board cells x,y and distinct members M,N, the original product ONB gives `<M_x,M_y><N_x,N_y>=0`. Support means nonzero coordinates in Q's fixed chosen-row basis; locally standardized mate spaces are separate.

In any mixed line the mixed Q rays form an ONB of the complementary coordinate space. A mixed line cannot contain exactly one heavy ray. Indeed its support-2 rays have equal or disjoint 2-axis supports. In each such support plane they occupy either one or two orthogonal directions. If all but one line ray are support-2, the one-dimensional complement of their span is either the missing direction in a half-filled 2-plane or one unused coordinate axis, so the remaining ray has support at most 2. Thus every heavy cell has another heavy cell in its row and in its column. Its heavy-incidence graph has minimum degree 2; exactly five edges are impossible, and exactly four form a 2×2 rectangle.

In a mixed line with exactly two heavy rays, the support-2 rays' orthogonal complement has dimension two and is a direct sum of missing directions in half-filled 2-planes and unused coordinate axes. Both heavy rays have nonzero components in both one-dimensional summands: otherwise one is a singleton or a support-2 ray. Two unused axes cannot occur. Therefore the two heavy rays have the same support, either a 2-axis plane plus one coordinate (size 3) or two disjoint 2-axis planes (size 4). Applying this on both pairs of rows and columns of the heavy rectangle gives one common support D for all four heavy rays. In a four-family, |D|≤4: at each off-standard cell the four mate supports are pairwise disjoint by pair-product comparison with the standardized row, and their common board-column axis is forbidden by the four column ONBs, so one support uses at most four of the seven available coordinates.

Appendix A proves the axis-cover condition: any axis used by a mixed support-2 ray must also occur in some heavy mixed ray. Since the four heavy rays all have support D, **all mixed Q rays have supports contained in D**. It remains to exclude |D|=3.

## Common 3-axis heavy rectangle

Assume D has three axes. Any line meeting the heavy rectangle has mixed degree exactly 3: it contains two full-D heavy rays, cannot have mixed degree 2, and has mixed subspace contained in D. Thus it has exactly one additional support-2 ray. Every other mixed line has degree 2: with no heavy ray, three support-2 rays cannot form an ONB in a 3-axis space. There are exactly two heavy rows and two heavy columns.

In the 2×2 heavy board, at least one pair of opposite Q rays is collinear. If one opposite pair were independent, the other pair, perpendicular to both in a 3-space, would be collinear. Relabel so the diagonal rays are p; call the off-diagonal rays q and r. Let x be the support-2 boundary ray on q's high row and y the support-2 boundary ray on r's high row. The boundary ray on q's high column is collinear with x, because both are perpendicular to p and q; similarly the remaining boundary ray is collinear with y. Let their 2-supports be U,V⊂D.

Every other mixed Q ray is support 2. Join support-2 cells when they share a degree-2-only row or column. The four boundary cells on heavy lines are the graph's degree-one vertices; every other support-2 cell has degree two. Thus the components are two paths plus cycles. Every path or cycle has one common support pair and its Q rays alternate between two perpendicular rays. Since every boundary ray is orthogonal to the full-D ray p, two endpoints with the same support pair are collinear: p has a nonzero projection on that pair. Each path consequently has an odd number of cells and connects one heavy row to one heavy column. A path with 2j+1 cells occupies j+1 rows and has j+1 cells carrying its endpoint ray; a cycle has equally many row vertices and occurrences of either ray.

## The exact axis-group dimension argument

First suppose U≠V. They share exactly one axis a. The U path has the x/x^⊥ basis and the V path the y/y^⊥ basis. Group every support-2 cell using a by support pair and projective orthonormal basis on that pair. There are principal groups containing the U and V paths, possibly with cycles of the same respective basis, and any further group consists only of cycles. The group row sets are disjoint: on a low-only line two support-2 rays sharing a must use the same pair and basis; on a heavy line there is only one support-2 cell. Their total row count K is at most 7. Each principal group has at least two rows.

For any mate M, the M span of a group has dimension at least its row count: the endpoint-ray class supplies one orthogonal M ray per row on a path, and either class does so on each cycle. Distinct group Q rays have nonzero inner product, because different 2-supports containing a meet in one coordinate, or because distinct orthonormal ray bases of the same 2-plane have no zero cross-overlaps. Hence distinct group M spans are orthogonal. Every row outside all groups contains a light Q=e_a cell: both heavy rows already contain principal boundary cells, and all remaining mixed rays using a are grouped. The 8−K light M rays are mutually orthogonal and orthogonal to all group spans. Dimension 8 forces every group span to have exactly its row count.

An additional group has only cycles. Its two Q-ray classes therefore give mate ONBs of its whole group span. For each transition row the actual shared row and column supply two zero positions; the three mate transition supports are pairwise disjoint by their pair-product ONBs. Its row count would be at least 5, impossible in addition to the two principal groups' at least two rows each, since K≤7. Thus only the two principal groups remain.

Let W_U,W_V be their mate spans for one fixed M. On the U path, the x-class gives an ONB of W_U and the x^⊥-class is an orthogonal family of codimension one; cycles with the same basis add equally many of each class. The analogous assertion holds for y and y^⊥ in W_V. The heavy ray q is orthogonal to x, but overlaps x^⊥ because q is full on D; it overlaps y because x,y have distinct 2-supports and, in p^⊥, a nonzero ray cannot be perpendicular to two independent rays x,y. Consequently M_q is orthogonal to the x^⊥ family and to the y-class ONB. It is also orthogonal to the light-e_a mate rays. Hence M_q lies in the one-dimensional residual of W_U. Similarly M_r lies in the one-dimensional residual of W_V. The full-D ray p is orthogonal to x and y, so it overlaps both x^⊥ and y^⊥; both diagonal p-cell mate rays lie in the two-dimensional sum of these residuals. They are orthogonal to each other because their Q rays are collinear and therefore form an ONB of that sum. But the q-cell shares a row or column with **both** diagonal cells, so its nonzero M_q must be orthogonal to that entire two-dimensional sum, contradiction.

## Equal boundary supports

Suppose U=V. All four boundary Q rays are orthogonal to the full-D ray p and lie on the same 2-support U, so they are collinear with one ray x. The two off-diagonal heavy rays q,r are also collinear, since both are perpendicular to p and x in the 3-space.

Choose a∈U and group support-2 cells using a as above. The two paths both belong to the principal support/basis group x,x^⊥. Its x-class has one occurrence per group row, while its x^⊥-class has two fewer occurrences: each path contributes one more x than x^⊥, and any included same-basis cycles contribute equal numbers. Every other group is made only of cycles. As above, group mate spans are mutually orthogonal, the rows outside the groups supply light-e_a rays, and exact dimension saturation makes each group span equal to its row count. Each principal path occupies at least two rows, so the principal group uses at least four. A different cycle group would need at least five rows by the three-mate transition argument, contradicting K≤7. There are therefore no other groups using a.

For each mate M, the principal x-class is an ONB of a k-dimensional space W, while its x^⊥ class is an orthogonal family of size k−2. The 8−k light-e_a M rays form an ONB of W^⊥. Every heavy Q ray is full on D and perpendicular to x, hence overlaps x^⊥; its mate ray lies in the 2-dimensional complement of the x^⊥ family within W. Yet the four heavy mate rays are pairwise orthogonal: adjacent cells share a row or column and opposite Q rays are collinear (the diagonal p and off-diagonal q classes). Four orthogonal rays cannot lie in dimension two. Contradiction.

The |D|=3 case is impossible. If Q is nonclassical, Appendix A ensures at least one heavy cell. Heavy pairing gives at least four and excludes five. Therefore either there are at least six heavy cells or there are exactly four, all with the same 4-axis support D and all mixed Q supports contained in D. Coordinates outside D occur as light Q rays once in every row and column. Under the separately unreviewed 13-mixed-cell candidate, summed support surplus is at least 17 per nonclassical member; this arithmetic is not needed for the written theorem and supplies no global upper-bound contradiction.

### Exact physical setup

An ordinary QLS of order 8 is an 8×8 array of unit vectors in C^8 whose rows and columns are ONBs. Q,R,S,T form a four-MOQLS(8) when, for each pair, their 64 same-cell tensor products form an ONB of C^8⊗C^8. Independently standardize the chosen actual board row of each array to its own coordinate basis at the shared column labels. For distinct cells x,y and distinct members M,N, actual product orthogonality is `<M_x,M_y><N_x,N_y>=0`. A Q cell is mixed if its ray is not one of Q's standardized row rays; its support is its set of nonzero Q coordinates.

Assume, toward contradiction, that at least one mixed Q ray uses axis a and **every** mixed Q ray using a has support size 2. Other mixed rays avoiding a may have any support and may share rows or columns with the rays considered below.

### Axis groups and exact span saturation

A mixed ray on support `{a,b}` has nonzero coefficients in both coordinates. In any row containing it, another mixed ray must use a: a light e_a is not orthogonal to it, and row completeness requires total squared coefficient 1 on axis a. That other ray also has support 2 by the assumption. Its other support coordinate must be b, since a different 2-support `{a,c}` would give a single nonzero shared-coordinate inner product. The two rays are orthogonal and form an ONB of span{e_a,e_b}. A third same-line mixed ray using a cannot occur, since these two fill that plane. The identical statement holds in every column.

Group all mixed Q cells using a by (i) their support pair `{a,b}`, and (ii) the projective orthonormal two-ray basis on that pair, up to phases and exchange. Every row or column that meets a group contains one ray from each of that group's two ray classes. A row cannot meet two groups: different support pairs containing a give a single nonzero-coordinate overlap, and two distinct bases on the same 2-plane give nonzero cross-overlaps. The chosen standardized row meets no group. If group g uses k_g rows, it also uses k_g columns, and `K=Σ_g k_g≤7`.

For any actual mate M, the k_g M rays over either fixed Q-ray class are pairwise orthogonal by Q⊗M. Rays from distinct groups have nonzero Q inner products: distinct support pairs share exactly a, while distinct orthonormal bases in one 2-space have no zero cross-overlaps. Therefore the mate spans of distinct groups are mutually orthogonal. Each of the 8−K other rows has a light Q=e_a cell, even if it contains other mixed Q rays avoiding a. The corresponding M rays are pairwise orthogonal and orthogonal to every group span. Consequently

`(8−K) + Σ_g dim(span M_g) ≤ 8`,

while each group span has dimension at least k_g. Equality holds throughout. Thus in every group the two k_g-ray mate classes are ONBs of the same k_g-dimensional space.

For any p cell of a group, its actual row and column contain two distinct q cells. These give two zeros in the corresponding transition-matrix row between its p and q mate ONBs. For distinct mates M,N, their transition supports are disjoint by M⊗N orthogonality. Three unitary matrices require three distinct nonzero positions in each transition row, so `k_g−2≥3`, i.e. `k_g≥5`. Since K≤7, there can be only one group.

### The one-group light-cell pigeonhole

Let its support be `{a,b}` and row count k. For each mate M, its group span W_M has dimension k, and the 8−k light-Q=e_a M rays form an ONB of W_M^⊥. All group Q rays use b, so comparison of their Q⊗M products with the standardized first-row product at b shows that the first-row M=e_b ray lies in W_M^⊥. The first row is one of the light-e_a rows and contributes M=e_a, which is perpendicular to e_b. Hence each mate must have nonzero e_b overlap at at least one of the **other** 7−k light-e_a cells.

Two distinct mates cannot choose the same such cell: their pair-product at that cell would have nonzero inner product with their first-row product at b. Three mates therefore require `7−k≥3`, or k≤4. This contradicts k≥5.

Thus an occupied mixed coordinate a cannot be used exclusively by support-2 mixed rays. The axis-cover conclusion follows for every chosen Q row.
