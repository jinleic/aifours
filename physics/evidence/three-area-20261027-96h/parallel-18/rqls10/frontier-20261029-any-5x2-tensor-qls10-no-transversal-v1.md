# No 5×2 tensor-product QLS10 has a transversal

**WRITTEN AUTHOR CANDIDATE, UNREVIEWED.** Native `/bin/date`: **Thu Oct 29 00:04:01 PDT 2026**. This is an unrestricted-factor structural exclusion at the original order10, not an impossibility theorem for general QLS10.

Let Q be ANY QLS of order5 and R ANY QLS of order2. Then the tensor-product QLS `Q tensor R` has **no orthogonal transversal**, including repeated rays. Up to a common unitary and cell phases, every R is the classical cyclic Latin square of order2: its first row is an orthonormal basis `(u,v)` and its column constraints force the second row `(v,u)`. Apply the n=5,m=2 case of `frontier-20261029-odd-quantum-even-cyclic-tensor-no-transversal-v1.md` SHA **12a38a89c17da80b0b534324058957519138fdcbd75e441a77a27699efdaa397**. The tensor factors can be interchanged, and arbitrary global unitary, cell phases and row/column relabelings preserve absence of a transversal.

Thus any 1-resolvable QLS10 must avoid this ARRAY-LEVEL 5×2 tensor form. This says nothing about an arbitrary QLS10 merely having individually separable vectors in some C5 tensor C2 identification: those vectors need not factor into two QLS arrays with product indexing.

Independently, Q has at most25 distinct rays and R has exactly2, so any such tensor array has at most50 rays; a 100-ray maximal QLS10 cannot have this tensor form even without the no-transversal result. The no-transversal conclusion is stronger for all lower-cardinality examples. This written corollary needs independent review together with the tensor parity theorem.
