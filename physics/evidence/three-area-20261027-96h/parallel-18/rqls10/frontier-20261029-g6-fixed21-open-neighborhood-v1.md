# Open neighborhoods of the fixed 21-ray partial lifts also cannot extend

**WRITTEN AUTHOR CANDIDATE, UNREVIEWED.** Native `/bin/date`: Thu Oct 29 01:53:47 PDT 2026. This is a direct conditional consequence of the exact fixed-anchor exclusion in `frontier-20261029-g6-fixed21-asymmetric-no-go-v1.md`, SHA-256 `6bac0485ffcb6746892ea803fd55e1c4904315abde640790267c258b0a562c69`; the latter and its exact certificate remain subject to review.

For each of the two labeled 21-ray partial configurations in that packet, there exists an `epsilon>0` such that no G6 edge-ray assignment forming ONBs at all 15 vertices can have its 21 corresponding rank-one projectors all within epsilon of those anchor projectors in Frobenius norm. This includes arbitrary perturbed anchor rays and puts **no** symmetry or distinctness restriction on the remaining 24 rays. No numerical value or computable lower bound for epsilon is asserted.

**Proof.** Consider all 45-tuples of rank-one projectors in C6. This is a compact space, and the subset satisfying pairwise orthogonality of all incident edges is closed. Since every vertex has exactly six incident rays in C6, that condition already makes each vertex an ONB. Projection to the 21 labeled anchor coordinates therefore has compact, hence closed, image. The preceding packet proves that neither of the two anchor tuples lies in that image, even when repetitions among nonincident rays are permitted. The distance from each excluded tuple to this compact image is consequently positive. Equivalently, if no epsilon existed, a sequence of extensions approaching the fixed anchors would have a convergent subsequence of all 45 rays, yielding a forbidden exact extension.

The result extends exclusion from two exact points to open sets in the 21-ray projective configuration space. It still concerns only the specific G6 incidence and neighborhoods of the specified anchors; it does not exclude other C6 assignments, other 4D cores, or a maximal 1RQLS10. It is not a formal-kernel proof or external review.
