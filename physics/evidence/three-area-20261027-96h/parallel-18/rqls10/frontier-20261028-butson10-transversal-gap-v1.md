## 2. From an original transversal to a half-frame matching

The explicit K is complex Hadamard: in each pair of distinct rows or columns, each difference residue mod5 appears exactly twice, so their fifth-root sum is zero. For K' the same remains true under all listed transformations. Thus every row and column of the physical array is actually an ONB.

Write E for the FIVE source rows occupying the even Fourier coordinates after the arbitrary row permutation. Fix two distinct physical columns c,d and set

`S_E(c,d)=sum_(t even) conjugate(K'_(t,c)) K'_(t,d)`.

The coordinate-row phases cancel and column phases only multiply the sum by a unit scalar. Full K-column orthogonality gives `S_even+S_odd=0`, so for physical rows r and r+5,

`<psi_(r,c),psi_(r+5,d)> = (S_even-S_odd)/10 = S_even/5`.          (3)

Therefore the opposite-row cells are orthogonal iff the corresponding FIVE-entry half-column sum vanishes.

For fifth roots, a sum of five terms vanishes iff all five residues occur once: the degree-at-most4 integer count polynomial must be a multiple of `Phi5=1+X+X²+X³+X⁴`, and its coefficient sum is5. Define a graph Gamma_E on the ten K columns, with edge {c,d} exactly when their differences on E contain each residue0,...,4 once. A transversal permutation uses all ten columns once, and its five opposite-row pairs would form a PERFECT MATCHING of Gamma_E if it were orthogonal. Row permutation makes E an arbitrary five-element subset; column permutation merely relabels the graph; row/column phases preserve its edge status and overlap magnitudes. Galois powers preserve zero/nonzero status but do NOT in general preserve magnitudes; the numerical-gap claim for every Galois power is separately checked in §4.
