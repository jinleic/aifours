**1. Irreducibility and projectivity.** The 15 contexts and 45 distinct rays form an odd parity system: every ray is in exactly two contexts. Any orthogonal projection commuting with every ray projector has even rank, because tracing the 15 basis identities on its range gives `15 rank(Q)=2 sum_e tr(QP_e)`. A nontrivial rank-2 sector is impossible: a ray in it appears in two contexts, and its unique orthogonal partner inside the sector would have to occur as two distinct edge rays, contradicting distinctness. Rank 4 is excluded by its rank-2 complement. Thus the projector algebra is irreducible. If unitaries implement D, two unitaries implementing the same group element differ by a scalar, so they form a projective representation.

**2. The projective multiplier is trivial.** Choose implementing unitaries R,S and rephase so `R^6=S²=I`; then `S R S^(-1)=alpha R^(-1)` with `alpha^6=1`. Rephasing R by a sixth root replaces alpha by a square times alpha, so alpha can be chosen as +1 or −1. If alpha=−1, S anticommutes with `R³` and therefore `tr(R³)=0`. But R cyclically permutes the three pairwise orthogonal triangle rays, and `R³` acts on all three by the same sign chi. In the ONB at any triangle vertex, `R³` fixes those two incident triangle rays and swaps the remaining four rays, so `tr(R³)=2 chi`, a contradiction. Therefore alpha=+1 and R,S form an ordinary representation.

**3. Character restriction.** At a vertex fixed by a reflection, the reflection permutes its ONB with no fixed ray; thus every `tr(S R^k)=0`. Decompose the ordinary D6 representation into multiplicities `n_(a,b)` of the four characters and u,v of E1,E2. Traces of the two reflection classes give `n_(+,+)=n_(+,-)=p` and `n_(-,+)=n_(-,-)=q`. The +1 central eigenspace contains these only if `p>=1` and `v>=1`, hence p=v=1. Up to twisting, only two representations remain:

- **A:** all four one-dimensional characters plus E2 (`q=1,u=0`);
