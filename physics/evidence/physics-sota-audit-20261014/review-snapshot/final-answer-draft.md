**Short answer: no major contribution.** By "major" I mean a new method, a long-standing or prominent problem resolved, a better bound than the current state of the art, or a physical result. None of the five clears that bar. What we have are short answers to recent, explicitly posed questions in a small specialist literature, built mostly from known tools. If they survive human review and priority checks, two are genuine specialist contributions (forest, universality), one is a companion construction, one is mostly a corollary of 2014 work, and one is a narrow no-go.

None of the four phyllotaxis results says anything about the book's physics (least-energy Fibonacci patterns on cones). Physical work there is numerical ground-state and defect modeling, e.g. repulsive particles on cones and horns (Ansell–Tomlinson–Wilkin 2020); ours are geometric existence theorems, a different kind of claim.

Best honest framing: two coherent items plus a note. (A) Norm-ranking spiral constructions (forest + density): one shared mechanism, so one shared priority risk. (B) Chabauty limits of Fermat spirals (universality, with classification as a proposition crediting Bacher first). (C) A scoped supersoliton no-go.

What would count as major here: a spiral forest with an explicit near-optimal visibility rate, or a Danzer spiral (Tevet's follow-up questions); a Delone spiral forest with an explicit rate; a dynamically natural spiral with density for n ≥ 3 (Akiyama's question); a classification of supersymmetric mKdV-type bilinear equations (left open by Delisle–Jaouadi); or a theorem about actual energy minimizers on cones.
