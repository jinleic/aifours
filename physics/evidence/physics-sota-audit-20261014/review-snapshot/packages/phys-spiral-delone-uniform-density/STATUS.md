# Eligibility, baseline, and later-source screen

Snapshot: **2026-10-14 (PT)**. This folder is the user-approved reselection target **spiral Delone sets admitting uniform asymptotic density**, connected to the book's identical-unit packing/phyllotaxis discussion. It is not a claim about the elastic-energy optimum on a cone.

**Mathematical outcome:** the exact existence statement is proved in `RESULTS.md` for every fixed \(n\ge2\) and has a completed independent other-vendor review. **SOLVED / DELIVERY-READY applies to correctness of that stated theorem; evidence is Certification.** The explicit source question establishes eligibility, while the bounded source screen below does not certify worldwide novelty or priority.


## Explicit primary open statement and exact normalization

**Faustin Adiceam and Ioannis Tsokanos**, *Higher Dimensional Spiral Delone Sets*, [arXiv:2010.06428v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.06428v3), **30 March 2021**, §6, pp. **22–23**. At the top of p.23 the authors write (original grammar retained; fewer than 40 words):

> “It is not clear to the authors whether a spiral Delone set with asymptotic density \(r=V_n^{-1}\) can exists in any dimension \(n\ge3\).”

The paragraph on p.22 defines the density limit for **every bounded measurable set with Lebesgue-null boundary**, and relates it to uniform distribution of the spherical directions. The exact-radius definition is Eq. (1.3), p.3: \(s_k=k^{1/n}u_k\), \(u_k\in\mathbb S^{n-1}\), \(k\ge1\). Uniform discreteness and relative density are the usual all-pair and ambient-covering properties, defined on pp.1–2. The definition does not require a continuous curve, a transitive orbit, or distinct spherical directions; p.2, footnote 1 expressly cautions that general spiral sets need not visually resemble spirals.

**OCR correction verified against the actual page images:** the formula is \(V_n^{-1}\), not \(V_{n-1}\). The rendered primary pages are preserved as `source/open-question-page-22.png` and `source/open-question-page-23.png`. Independently, exact radii imply \(\#(S\cap B_R)=\lfloor R^n\rfloor\), so the source's density definition forces \(1=rV_n\). A different normalization would not be the author's question.

The public primary PDF, image hashes, short quote, definition and book-source pins are recorded in `source/primary-evidence.json`. The supplied cone excerpt was copied byte-for-byte to `source/problem.zh.txt`; the old cone and optics folders were not changed.

## Book bridge and tractability

The book chapter is **Cao Zexian**, *Are the Fibonacci Parastichous Spirals a Least-Energy Configuration on Conical Surface?*, *10000 Selected Problems in Sciences: Physics* (Science Press, 2009), pp.541–543 (PDF pp.574–576). Its introductory discussion concerns configurations of identical units, packing, and the geometry behind phyllotactic structures. The selected question studies exact-radius spiral configurations whose point spacing, covering and spatial density are simultaneously regular in higher dimensions. This is a legitimate **mathematical subproblem within that theme**, not an equivalent formulation of the cone-energy conjecture.

The bounded proof obligation is tractable because sorting a known lattice by radius leaves only a radial-rank correction to control. A classical ball discrepancy **strictly better than \(R^{n-1}\)** supplies a vanishing displacement. What needs care is not a large numerical search: it is uniform control across tied lattice spheres, finite-prefix/cross-prefix separation, and density for arbitrary Jordan regions. `RESULTS.md` provides all those bridges. The existence of a simulator is not used as an argument for tractability.

## Classical ingredients and novelty discipline

1. **Lattice-ball discrepancy.** The proof uses
   \[
   \#(\mathbb Z^n\cap B_R)=V_nR^n+O_n(R^{n-2+2/(n+1)}).
   \]
   This is a classical estimate, not an improvement claimed here. **Jingwei Guo**, *On lattice points in large convex bodies*, [arXiv:1007.4284v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1007.4284), 24 July 2010, introduction p.1, explicitly recalls the Hlawka exponent \(2/(n+1)\) for fixed compact convex bodies with smooth, everywhere nonzero Gaussian curvature (in dimensions at least three). The Euclidean ball satisfies these hypotheses. Guo develops stronger bounds; those improvements are not needed here. Attribution of the historical estimate is through that primary research paper's introduction, not a claim that the original Hlawka text was inspected.
   A cleaner **published, all-dimensional** reference was also checked directly: **Jingwei Guo**, “Lattice points in rotated convex domains,” *Revista Matemática Iberoamericana* **31**(2),411–438 (2015), [DOI 10.4171/rmi/839](https://doi.org/10.4171/rmi/839), [publisher PDF](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/38537), §1, p.411. Its introduction states precisely the above standard bound for every fixed \(d\ge2\), compact convex \(B\) containing the origin, smooth boundary and everywhere positive curvature. We use that **standard introductory estimate**, not its stronger main theorem involving almost-every rotation. Balls meet all the stated hypotheses; there is no averaging or exceptional-radius qualification.
2. **Ball-specific proof instead of an unread black box.** `RESULTS.md` §3 proves the needed bound for every fixed \(n\ge2\) using Poisson summation and smooth majorants/minorants. The continuous shell-volume error is kept separate from the lattice sum; no unsupported thin-shell lattice count is substituted for volume.
3. **Bessel formulas.** [NIST DLMF 10.9.4](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.9.E4) supplies the integral representation with \(\Re\nu>-1/2\); [DLMF 10.17.3](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.17.E3) supplies the fixed-order positive-real large-argument decay. These authoritative standard identities were read directly. For \(n=3\), an elementary sine/cosine transform is additionally derived, so that case does not rely on a general Bessel asymptotic.
4. **No priority claim from a local proof.** Sorting a lattice and using its discrepancy are classical ingredients. The construction's historical status is a separate source question; an indexed-citation miss is not proof of worldwide novelty. Even if the construction answers the exact stated question, the evidence category may properly be **Certification**, not a new-method Discovery.
5. **Historical attribution and smoothing source.** The classical audit read the relevant portions of **Ivić–Krätzel–Kühleitner–Nowak**, “Lattice points in large regions and related arithmetic functions: Recent developments in a very classic topic,” [arXiv:math/0410522v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0410522), §3.2, pp.16–17, and **W. M. Schmidt**, “Edmund Hlawka (1916–2009),” *Acta Arithmetica* **139**(4) (2009), [10.4064/aa139-4-1](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa139-4-1). These attribute the ellipsoid case to Landau (1915/1924), predating Hlawka's general convex-body result. Those original Landau papers and Hlawka's subscription text were not read. The integrating agent also checked **Colzani–Gariboldi–Gigante**, “\(L^p\) norms of the lattice point discrepancy,” [arXiv:1805.06520v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.06520), published 2019, [10.1007/s00041-019-09665-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00041-019-09665-1), §2, Remark 2.2 and Lemma 2.3: the smoothing sandwich and ball/Bessel Fourier representation are established machinery.
