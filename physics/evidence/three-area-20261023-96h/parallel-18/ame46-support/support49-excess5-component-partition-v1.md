## 2. Generic normal-pair and attachment lemmas, with no EX4 transfer

Two distinct rows of a unitary cannot support-intersect in exactly ONE column: their inner product would be one nonzero term. The same applies to columns.

Call a degree-two row normal; call a row of higher degree high. In a connected positive-excess component:

1. Distinct normal rows have DISJOINT two-column supports. A one-column intersection is forbidden. If two normal rows share the same two columns, they form an orthonormal basis of that C^2 coordinate space. Every other row must vanish there, so they isolate a closed 2x2 component, contrary to connected positive excess.
2. Every other row meets a normal pair either in both columns or in neither. A one-column intersection is forbidden.
3. Every normal pair is contained in a high row support. Otherwise its component could contain only the normal row itself: other normal pairs are disjoint. In particular the union of high supports is the COMPLETE column set. (Every column occurs, and any column not occurring in a high row would occur only in an unattached normal pair.)
4. The overlap graph of high supports is connected: a path between high rows through a normal row uses that normal row's same pair, hence can be replaced by a direct high-support overlap. High rows plus their attached normal pairs cannot form disconnected groups in a connected support component.
5. **Normal-pair parallelism.** If P is a normal pair with row vector n, the restriction of ANY other row that contains P lies in the one-dimensional orthogonal complement of n in C^P. Hence two such high rows whose supports outside P are disjoint have a nonzero inner product on P, with no other overlap to cancel it. They cannot be orthogonal. This is a complex inner-product argument; no reality, common phase or equal-modulus assumption is used.

If r is the number of high rows, there are q-r disjoint normal pairs. Therefore `2(q-r)<=q`, or **q<=2r**. Exactly one high row is impossible: its degree exceeds two, but q<=2. In particular no connected excess-1 component exists.

For excess zero all rows have degree two. A row's neighbors must be shared together by any row meeting them, so the connected component is exactly a full 2x2. Conversely a full nonzero-entry 2x2 unitary is a valid zero-excess component. These facts apply to zero-excess pieces INSIDE a matrix of any larger total excess; they do not assert that a positive-excess slice or retained sector is closed.
