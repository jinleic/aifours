## 4. Two superficially close PPT tools do not supply the missing bound

### 4.1 Idempotent maps versus Choi projections

Read the actual nine-page primary [Kennedy–Manor–Paulsen, *Composition of PPT maps*, QIC18(2018)472–480](https://www.rintonpress.com/xxqic18/qic-18-56/0472-0480.pdf). Its Proposition3.2 concerns an **idempotent unital PPT linear map**, which is entanglement breaking. Our established fact is instead that the **ordinary Choi matrix** `Pi_S` is an orthoprojection. `Pi_S^2=Pi_S` does not say `Phi composition Phi=Phi`, nor establish unitality. Thus that proposition cannot be imported by equating two different meanings of projection.

The same paper's Proposition4.1 concerns a pure Schur-product map. The nonzero peeling map here annihilates P=cc^T. In the positive c interior all entries of P are nonzero; a Schur-product map annihilating it would have zero symbol and be the zero map. Hence this peeling map is not that literal Schur-product class. No composition theorem was used to assert the inverse completion bound.

### 4.2 Diagonal symmetric states versus this p state

Read the actual publisher abstract and explanatory symmetry definition of [Tura–Aloy–Quesada–Lewenstein–Sanpera, *Separability of diagonal symmetric states: a quadratic conic optimization problem*, Quantum2,45(2018)](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2018-01-12-45/), DOI [10.22331/q-2018-01-12-45](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2018-01-12-45).
