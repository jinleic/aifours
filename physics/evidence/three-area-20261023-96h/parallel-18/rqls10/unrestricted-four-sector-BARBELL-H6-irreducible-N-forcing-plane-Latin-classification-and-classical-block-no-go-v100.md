## 4. Theorem PLS3 (classification)

Let P_{v,k} (v, k = 1..3) be rank-two projections in C6 with every row and every column summing to I. Then exactly one of the following holds.
(Classical) The array is a Latin square on ONE orthogonal decomposition {A, B, C}.
(K33) There is an ONB of lines x1, x2, x3, y1, y2, y3 with every P_{v,k} = x_i ⊕ y_j, where the index pairs come from two orthogonal Latin squares of order three.

Proof. Name row 1 (A, B, C), column 1 (A, D, E), row 2 (D, F, G), column 2 (B, F, H), column 3 (C, G, J) and row 3 (E, H, J). D ⊥ A gives D ⊂ B ⊕ C. Split by (dim D∩B, dim D∩C).
- (2,0): D = B forces E = C, F ⊂ A ⊕ C and H = G. G ⊥ C then gives G = A, so the array is classical.
- (0,2): symmetric to (2,0).
- (0,0): then B ⊕ D = B ⊕ C, so F = A and H = C. E = (B ⊕ C) ⊖ D ⊥ C forces E = B and D = C, a contradiction.
- (1,0): write D = b1 ⊕ l with l = beta b2 + gamma c and beta·gamma ≠ 0. Then F ⊂ A ⊕ c', H = c ⊕ f0 and E = c' ⊕ m with m ∝ conj(gamma) b2 - conj(beta) c. Since <c|m> ≠ 0, E is not orthogonal to H, contradicting row 3.
- (0,1): symmetric to (1,0).
- (1,1): D = b1 ⊕ c1 gives E = b2 ⊕ c2 and F = (A ⊕ c2) ⊖ f0. Then G = b2 ⊕ f0 and H = c1 ⊕ f0, and row 3 forces f0 ∈ A. Write A = a0 ⊕ f0; then J = b1 ⊕ a0. With X = {a0, b2, c1} and Y = {f0, b1, c2} this is K33.

## 5. Lemma ZALG (the algebra at Z)

Hypothesis H2': the G_X planes form orthogonal triples at Z, T1 and T2, and the G_Y planes do so at R1, R2 and Z. Then U6 applies at Z with T = G_X; name z.1 the row whose G_X cells lie in U_Z. Let A be the C*-algebra generated by the six Z-planes, so that C6 = ⊕ C^{n_k} ⊗ C^{m_k} with A = ⊕ M_{n_k} ⊗ I. The projection S onto U_Z lies in the commutant ⊕ I ⊗ M_{m_k}, and every plane Q satisfies rank Q = Σ rank(q_k) m_k = 2 and tr(SQ) = Σ rank(q_k) rank(s_k) = 1.

Hence each plane is either (A) a rank-one-times-I_2 piece of an m=2 block, or (B) two lines in two distinct m=1 blocks. Several kinds of block are impossible:
- m ≥ 3 blocks cannot be covered by planes.
- An m=2 block with n=1 is a plane shared by G_X and G_Y at Z. Two columns at Z would then share one plane, and their four cells force a repeat.
- A one-dimensional m=1 block lies in U_Z or U_Z-perp. Either way it equals both the G_X cell of one z-row and the G_Y cell of the other, again a repeat.
- (2,2,2) forces a triangle of plane-touches whose 0/1 block indicator must sum to one on every edge, which is impossible by parity.
- Mixed m=2 and m=1 structures leave a single m=1 block for type-(B) planes, which is impossible.

The survivors are therefore:
- (a) A = M_3 ⊗ I_2 in some frame C3 ⊗ C2, with Z-planes q_i ⊗ C2 and q'_j ⊗ C2 and U_Z = C3 ⊗ zeta;
- (b1) two m=1 blocks B1 = U_Z and B2 = U_Z-perp of dimension 3, with every Z-plane equal to a line of B1 plus a line of B2.
