## 1. Exact named residuals and claim

Assume a full physical P1-B2 five-MOQLS(8) configuration with square1 projector span13. Write B=span(e4,e5). Earlier optional candidates reduce its B/complement cross image to a real2-dimensional plane of rank-one matrices with either:

- **SHARED-B:** a fixed B factor ray b and a real2-plane E of complement factors;
- **SHARED-C:** a fixed complement ray c and a real2-plane E of B factors.

The generators of E are complex-linearly independent. If they were complex-dependent too, both physical factor rays would be fixed and the one-channel case would apply instead.

On E define the real alternating bilinear form

    omega(z,w)=Im<z,w>.

**Claim I.** In either residual, omega must vanish identically. If omega is nonzero, the full configuration is impossible by actual row-class contradictions below.

**Residual left open:** an **ISOTROPIC-SHARED-B** or **ISOTROPIC-SHARED-C** plane with omega=0. It can be given a real orthonormal basis and hence supports a LOCAL real three-space cone of mixed cell rays. This is a derived necessary local structure, not an assumption that the full QLS or the other four squares are real. The isotropic cones are NOT excluded or realized here.

## 2. A nonzero alternating form on a real2-plane

A nonzero alternating bilinear form on a two-dimensional real vector space is nondegenerate. In a basis e,f its matrix is [[0,d],[-d,0]] with d!=0, so

    omega(z,w)=0 iff z,w are real-linearly dependent

for nonzero z,w.

Both shared-factor cases allow an independent phase on EACH mixed square1 vector so that its coefficient along the fixed factor ray is positive real. In SHARED-B it becomes

    v=alpha b+z,      alpha>0 real, z in E, z!=0.

Indeed its cross image is alpha b z*, so z belongs to the real factor plane after division by alpha. In SHARED-C it becomes

    v=z+gamma c,      gamma>0 real, z in E, z!=0.

For two such mixed vectors, orthogonality has imaginary part omega(z,w)=0. Under omega!=0 their variable factors must therefore lie on the SAME real line. They consequently lie in the same two-dimensional physical space F generated by the fixed ray and that variable-factor ray.

In an actual row or column containing mixed cells, the cross-block ONB sum must cancel, so there are at least two mixed cells. Pairwise orthogonality makes ALL their variable factors lie on one real line, and then all vectors lie in one two-space. There are at most two of them. Thus every mixed line has exactly two mixed vectors forming an ONB of the corresponding F.

The same two-matchings capacity fact is used throughout: if each of the four other squares has ONBs on two disjoint matchings of m actual rows/columns in the SAME m-space, then4disjoint nonempty transition supports avoid TWO distinct row/column-forced zeros, yielding m>=6. These square-specific spaces are proved by saturation below, not assumed for projected parts.

## 3. SHARED-B with omega nonzero

All mixed vectors have the same B direction b. In each mixed row the two F-vectors resolve the projector on b inside B, leaving exactly one B-pure vector on b-perp. The same holds in every mixed column. Let k be the number of mixed rows; there are k mixed columns and2k mixed cells.

Group B-pure cells of nonmixed rows by their unordered B-ray-pair. Put beta={b,b-perp}, let m_beta count its nonmixed rows and m_alpha the counts of other pairs. Then

    8=k+m_beta+sum_alpha m_alpha.                          (1)

The two actual B-axis bases are different, so some B-pure ray lies outside beta; it belongs to an alpha class. Such a class's rays occur as two matchings on the same m_alpha rows and columns. Beta contains m_beta copies of b and m_beta+k copies of b-perp.

For each other square separately, distinct pure-B pair classes give orthogonal spans by tensor-product orthogonality. The lower dimensions m_beta+k and m_alpha sum to8, forcing equality. Every alpha matching is therefore an ONB of its m_alpha-space, and the capacity fact gives

    m_alpha>=6.                                           (2)

Mixed-cell entries in each other square are orthogonal to every alpha span and to the m_beta pure-b entries. They all lie in a k-dimensional subspace W_s of that square.

Now group mixed square1 cells by their orthonormal ray-pair. Different variable-factor real lines give ALL nonzero cross overlaps: the imaginary part omega(z,w) cannot vanish for independent real lines. On the same variable-factor line, different orthonormal pairs in the resulting complex two-space also have all cross overlaps nonzero. Thus distinct mixed pair classes give orthogonal spans in each W_s.

The class sizes ell_a sum to k. Repeated rays force dimensions at least ell_a, so saturation inside W_s makes both matchings of each class ONBs of its ell_a-space. The capacity fact gives ell_a>=6 and therefore k>=6. With any alpha from(2), whose rows are nonmixed,

    8>=k+m_alpha>=12,

contradiction. This excludes the whole non-isotropic SHARED-B case.

## 4. SHARED-C with omega nonzero

The mixed vectors now share c in B-perp, while their B factors vary over E. Each mixed row/column contains exactly two vectors with their B factors on one real line. Call that line channel j, with unit direction b_j in B and two-space F_j=span(b_j,c). There are k_j rows and k_j columns in channel j, with2k_j cells.

Different variable-factor real lines have nonzero imaginary inner product. Their B rays are neither parallel nor orthogonal, so their unordered B-ray-pairs beta_j={b_j,b_j-perp} are different. No actual row or column contains two channels: orthogonal mixed vectors must have real-dependent B factors. The channel row sets are therefore disjoint.

A channel-j row contains exactly one B-pure b_j-perp cell. A nonmixed row has an ONB pair in B; let m_alpha count those pure pair classes, including any beta_j that occurs there. Then

    8=sum_j k_j+sum_alpha m_alpha.                        (3)

In the pure-B class beta_j, b_j occurs m_beta_j times and b_j-perp occurs m_beta_j+k_j times. Every other class has m_alpha occurrences of each ray. In each other square their class spans are orthogonal, because distinct B-ray-pairs have all cross overlaps nonzero. Their dimension lower bounds sum to8 by(3), so every bound is tight.

For channel j, mixed-cell entries of each other square are perpendicular to all pure-B classes except beta_j and to its m_beta_j pure-b_j entries. They lie in a k_j-space W_{s,j}. Grouping their F_j board by orthonormal ray-pairs, repeated-ray dimensions and cross-class orthogonality saturate W_{s,j}. The capacity fact gives every class size at least6, hence

    k_j>=6 for every actual channel j.                    (4)

There must be at least two channels: otherwise all actual B factors would lie on one real line, making the real cross-image span one-dimensional rather than the prescribed two-dimensional E. Even allowing a single fixed complex B ray would be the separately excluded fixed-two-ray channel; the generators here are complex-independent.

Two nonempty disjoint channel row sets now give8>=sum_j k_j>=12, contradiction. This excludes the non-isotropic SHARED-C case.

## 5. Exact residual and the derived local real form

In either case omega must be zero. The ordinary positive Hermitian inner product restricted to E is then real-valued. Real Gram–Schmidt produces two real-linear combinations e1,e2 in E that are orthonormal over C and span E over R. The fixed factor ray is orthogonal to E because it is on the other side of B/B-perp.

After the already allowed individual entry phase, every mixed vector therefore has REAL coordinates in the local orthonormal triple consisting of that fixed ray and e1,e2. This is a NECESSARY consequence, not a phase restriction imposed on the primary problem. It does not constrain B-pure/B-perp entries outside the mixed cone to be real, identify subspaces across different squares, fix a global real basis, or turn the entire five-family into real QLS.

What remains is exactly the local real3space rank-one cone of ISOTROPIC-SHARED-B/C, including arbitrary continuous real angles and arbitrary complex behavior elsewhere. The present two-regular argument fails there: three mutually orthogonal mixed vectors can have independent factor directions when omega=0, so rows may carry a genuine three-dimensional mixed frame. No whole residual exclusion or construction follows.
