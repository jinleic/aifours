## 3. Catalogue-free regridding and a two-sided degree bound

At a point x outside L, each square's support avoids the column coordinate, since x is orthogonal to the L-point in its column. For distinct squares, their supports at x are disjoint: product orthogonality against the same L-point indexed by a gives the product of their a-coefficients zero. Five nonempty supports fit in seven coordinates. Hence each support has size at most3; a size3 support leaves the other four supports of size1. This is the elementary W8 argument with its same-point exception explicitly removed.

`U_(x,y)=<Q(x),Q(y)>`, x∈L, y∈M,

## 4. Complete sparse-unitary support classification over C

**Lemma S3.** A unitary matrix having row/column support degrees at most3 decomposes, under independent row/column permutations, into connected support blocks of exactly these types:

- a singleton;
- a full2-by2block;
- a full3-by3block;
- a3-by3block with exactly one zero;
- a4-by4block with zeros exactly a perfect matching (all other entries nonzero).

This is only a necessary pattern classification of actual unitary matrices; no pattern is declared realizable solely because it is in the list.

*Proof.* A degree-one row/column isolates a singleton: its sole nonzero has modulus1, forcing the corresponding column/row to be zero elsewhere.

Suppose a connected nonsingleton block has a degree-two row with support{a,b}. Any other row meeting one of a,b must meet both, or its inner product with that row would have a single nonzero summand. Thus columns a,b have the same neighbor set, containing two or three rows by the degree bound.

If there are two rows, the two columns are an orthonormal basis on those rows. Their row norms already equal1, leaving no third entry; this is a full2-block.

If there are three rows, call the other two r,r'. Neither can have support just{a,b}: two such rows would already span those two coordinates and leave no nonzero a/b components for the third row. Both therefore have a third coordinate, c,c'. If c!=c', an outside row using c cannot use a or b, whose three row slots are full; it would meet r in exactly c, contradicting orthogonality. Column c would then have degree one, impossible because r also has nonzero a/b components. Hence c=c'. The block consists of the original row{a,b} and two rows{a,b,c}: a3-block with exactly one zero. The transposed argument treats any degree-two column.

It remains that EVERY row and column in a connected block has degree3. Take one row with support{a,b,c}. The other rows contribute exactly six incidences to those three columns, because each column has two remaining neighbors. Each such row meets{a,b,c} in two or three positions, never one. If a three-position row occurs, the remaining three incidences must be another three-position row, since three cannot be partitioned into parts of size at least two other than3. This closes a full3-block.

Otherwise exactly three other rows meet the original triple in two positions each. Each coordinate a,b,c is used twice, so these pairs are exactly{a,b},{a,c},{b,c}. Each row has one third coordinate outside the original triple. Two of these rows already share exactly one of a,b,c; their third coordinates must coincide to avoid singleton overlap. Thus all three third coordinates are one coordinate d. The four rows have supports abc,abd,acd,bcd and all four columns are full at degree3. This closes the4-block with a zero matching. There is no room for any additional row or column. QED.

The number of **weight3 rows** contributed by these block types is respectively0,0,3,2,4. In particular:

- no transition can have exactly one weight3 row;
- if it has exactly two, those two belong to ONE3-block with one zero, have the SAME three-coordinate support B, and their orthogonal complement inside B is the third row, of weight2;
- if it has exactly three, they belong to ONE full3-block and have the SAME three-coordinate support.

These statements apply to an entire N-line M in a fixed L-frame, since its transition matrix is the actual unitary just classified. Other blocks contribute no extra heavy row when the total is2or3.

## 7. Fixed rays: a coupled, full-physical inequality

A ray of exact multiplicity8 occurs once in every N-line: its occurrences cannot share a line, and every class has eight lines partitioning the points. In particular it occurs in every reference frame. Distinct such rays are orthogonal, because they occur together in any line ONB. Every other vector is orthogonal to them, by the same-line occurrence. Thus they are genuine global fixed one-dimensional sectors, not merely coordinates with many nonzero overlaps.

Let f_s be their number in Q_s. In a chosen N-line frame there are eight axis rays. The multiplicity gap leaves every axis-ray multiplicity either1/2/3or8. Therefore the number n_(s,1) of weight1 cells satisfies

`n_(s,1) <= 8f_s+3(8−f_s)=24+5f_s`.

Writing n_(s,2), n_(s,3) for the weight2/3 counts gives

`n_(s,2)+n_(s,3) >=40−5f_s`.                                   (4)

These are full64-cell counts. They do not count merely the heavy or positive-overlap subboard.

Regrid with L as row0. The two constructed classical squares have weight1 at EVERY cell. At each of the56cells off L the five-square support budget leaves at most2 excess support coordinates over weight1 among the three Q_s:

`Σ_(s=1)^3 (weight Q_s(x)−1) <=2`.

The eight L-cells contribute zero excess. Summing gives

`Σ_s [n_(s,2)+2n_(s,3)] <=112`.                                (5)

Sum(4), put F=Σf_s, and subtract from(5):

`Σ_s n_(s,3) <=112−(120−5F)=5F−8`.                             (6)

This is a simultaneous physical constraint on allthree assignments, not an independent count model. It remains true when some assignments are classical (then f_s=8 and n_(s,3)=0).

If allthree are nonclassical, (3) makes the left side at least24. Thus

`24<=5F−8`, hence `F>=7`.                                      (7)

At least one f_s is therefore at least3. All its other vectors lie in the orthogonal complement of those f_s fixed rays, of dimension at most5. This is the claimed reduction of the allthree-nonclassical branch. No argument here excludes that complement.
