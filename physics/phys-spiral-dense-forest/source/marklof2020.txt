                                                   A BSTRACT. Delone sets are locally finite point sets, such that (a) any two points are separated by a given
                                                   minimum distance, and (b) there is a given radius so that every ball of that radius contains at least one
                                                   point. Important examples include the vertex set of Penrose tilings and other regular model sets, which
                                                   serve as a mathematical
                                                                 p
                                                                              model for quasicrystals. In this note we show that the point set given by the val-
                                                       p
                                                   ues n e2πiα n with n = 1, 2, 3, . . . is a Delone set in the complex plane, for any α > 0. This complements
                                                                                                                        p
                                                   Akiyama’s recent observation (see arxiv.org/abs/1904.10815) that n e2πiαn with n = 1, 2, 3, . . . forms a De-
                                                   lone set, if and only if α is badly approximated by rationals. A key difference is that our setting does not
                                                   require Diophantine conditions on α.

   Given M ordered real numbers ζ1 ≤ · · · ≤ ζ M in the unit interval [0, 1], we define the corresponding
gaps by δ j = ζ j+1 − ζ j for j = 1, . . . , M − 1. We furthermore set δ M = ζ1 + 1 − ζ M , which represents the
gap between ζ1 and ζ M , after gluing the end points of the interval [0, 1]. The numbers δ1 , . . . , δ M thus
describe the gaps of ζ1 , . . . , ζ M mod 1.
   Recall that the fractional part of a real number x is defined by x −b xc, where b xc is the largest integer
less than or equal to x. Given (ξn )n , h > 0 and R > 0, we denote by g hR the minimal gap mod 1 (as defined
above) between the fractional parts of the elements ξn with n ranging over R 2 ≤ n < (R + h)2 . Note that
for large R (and h fixed) there are approximately 2hR such elements, and hence the average gap size
between the fractional parts is about 1/2hR. We have the following two facts.

  It is interesting to note that, unlike the case of nα mod 1 considered in [1] (with α badly approxi-
                                                                p
mated by rationals), the overall fine-scale distribution of α n mod 1 does not display a bounded gap
phenomenon. For α2 ∈ Q, the gap distribution is well understood and indeed arbitrarily large and small
gaps (on the scale of the average gap) will occur [3]. In the case of typical α2 ∉ Q, the fine-scale statistics
are conjectured to follow a Poisson law, with an exponential gap distribution. This conjecture is based
on numerical and heuristic evidence [3, 5], but its proof is still an open problem. Remarkably, the two-
                        p
point statistics of ξn = n mod 1 is Poisson [2] (if one removes the density-zero subset of indices n that
are perfect squares), even though the gap distribution and other local statistics are not Poisson [3].
