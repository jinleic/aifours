     2	
     3	Date: 2026-10-14 (America/Los_Angeles).
     4	
     5	**Claim class: Certification. Novelty status: NOVELTY-UNCLEAR.** The argument below is a corollary of classical spherical-harmonic theory and Rogers's Gegenbauer linearization. Neither the formula nor those ingredients are claimed as new. The historically explicit conjecture is Caprio–Rowe–Welsh (CRW), *Computer Physics Communications* **180**, 1150–1163 (2009), arXiv:0902.0020, §2.5, Eq. (29). This is a proof of that precise statement in its own convention, not a solution of every CFP recurrence in Bao's book problem.
     6	
     7	## 1. Statement and conventions
     8	
     9	Let \(\mathcal H_v\) be the space of degree-\(v\) spherical harmonics on the unit sphere \(S^4\subset\mathbb R^5\). Its dimension is
    10	\[
    11	d_v={v+4\choose4}-{v+2\choose4}
    12	=\frac{(v+1)(v+2)(2v+3)}6,
    13	\]
    14	where a binomial coefficient with upper argument smaller than 4 is zero. These are the symmetric irreducible SO(5) representations \((v,0)\) of CRW. The SO(3) branching multiplicity label is retained in an orthonormal basis; no multiplicity-free SO(3) hypothesis is imposed.
    15	
    16	**Use CRW's measure**, not unit probability measure and not ordinary surface area:
    17	\[
    18	d\mu=\sin3\gamma\,d\gamma\,d\Omega,
    19	\quad0\le\gamma\le\pi/3,
    20	\quad\int_{\mathrm{SO}(3)}d\Omega=8\pi^2,
    21	\quad V:=\int d\mu=\frac{16\pi^2}{3}.
    22	\tag{1}
    23	\]
    24	On functions of the quadrupole direction, this is the invariant sphere measure scaled to mass \(V\). Ordinary unit-sphere area is \(8\pi^2/3\); substituting it would give a wrong factor of two in the squared reduced element. This measure and the constant harmonic \(\sqrt3/(4\pi)\) are given in CRW §2.2 and Table I, and Welsh–Rowe (2016), Eqs. (43)–(44).
    25	
    26	Write
    27	\[
    28	R(v_3;v_2,v_1)
    29	=\langle\Psi_{v_3}\|\widehat\Psi_{v_2}\|\Psi_{v_1}\rangle.
    30	\]
    31	Here \(\widehat\Psi\) is **multiplication by the normalized harmonic**, not an arbitrary SO(5) tensor operator. The reduced-element normalization is CRW Eqs. (26)–(28). Its phase is chosen positive as stated immediately after Eq. (28). Different CG phase choices need not give the same signed number.
    32	
    33	### Theorem 1 (the full quantified CRW Eq. (29))
    34	
    35	For every \(v_1,v_2,v_3\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), the multiplication reduced element is zero unless the sum is even and all triangle inequalities hold. For every admissible triple, set
    36	\[
    37	s=(v_1+v_2+v_3)/2,\qquad j_i=s-v_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
    38	\]
    39	Then, in the specified positive phase,
    40	\[
    41	\boxed{
    42	R(v_3;v_2,v_1)=\frac1{4\pi}
    43	\frac{(s+1)!}{j_1!j_2!j_3!}
    44	\sqrt{\frac{(2v_1+3)(2v_2+3)}{(v_3+1)(v_3+2)}}
    45	\sqrt{\frac{(2s+4)(2j_1+1)!(2j_2+1)!(2j_3+1)!}{(2s+3)!}}.
    46	}
    47	\tag{2}
    48	\]
    49	The forbidden-case rule precedes factorial evaluation. There are no negative factorials or limiting prescriptions.
    50	
    51	## 2. Reduced-element normalization as a basis-independent trace
    52	
    53	Choose any orthonormal bases \(Y_{v,a}\) in \(L^2(S^4,d\mu)\), and put
    54	\[
    55	T_{abc}=\int_{S^4}\overline{Y_{v_3,c}(x)}Y_{v_2,b}(x)Y_{v_1,a}(x)\,d\mu(x).
    56	\]
    57	Combining the ordinary SO(3) Wigner–Eckart convention in CRW Eq. (20) with its Eq. (26) cancels the factor \(\sqrt{2L_3+1}\). Thus
    58	\[
    59	T_{abc}=R(v_3;v_2,v_1)\,C_{ab}^{c},
    60	\]
    61	where \(C\) is the full SO(5) Clebsch–Gordan coefficient, including the ordinary SO(3) CG factor. For each fixed output index \(c\), unitarity in Eq. (25), equivalently Eq. (27) followed by ordinary SO(3) CG orthogonality, gives \(\sum_{a,b}|C_{ab}^c|^2=1\). Consequently
    62	\[
    63	\boxed{d_{v_3}|R|^2=\sum_{a,b,c}|T_{abc}|^2.}
    64	\tag{3}
    65	\]
    66	This is precisely the squared quantity in Eq. (28), not an independently redefined reduced element.
    67	
    68	There is no suppressed SO(5) outer multiplicity in this coupling of three symmetric traceless representations. One way to see this is to realize each representation as traceless symmetric tensors. An invariant contraction of three such tensors must contract indices between different tensors. The numbers of pairwise contractions are uniquely \(j_1,j_2,j_3\); contractions within one tensor vanish by tracelessness. An SO(5) epsilon invariant would require five independent antisymmetrized slots, but with only three symmetric tensor groups at least two slots come from the same group and it vanishes. Thus the invariant space has dimension at most one. Positivity of the integral below proves it is nonzero for every admissible triple. SO(3) internal multiplicities have all been summed in (3), rather than discarded.

    70	## 3. Addition theorem and one-dimensional reduction
    71	
    72	Define the reproducing kernel
    73	\[
    74	K_v(x,y)=\sum_aY_{v,a}(x)\overline{Y_{v,a}(y)}.
    75	\]
    76	Rotational invariance makes it a polynomial of \(t=x\cdot y\). A zonal degree-\(v\) harmonic satisfies
    77	\[
    78	(1-t^2)u''-4tu'+v(v+3)u=0.
    79	\]
    80	Its polynomial solution is a multiple of the Gegenbauer polynomial \(C_v^{3/2}(t)\). Since \(K_v(x,x)\) is constant and its integral is \(d_v\),
    81	\[
    82	K_v(x,y)=\frac{d_v}{V}P_v(x\cdot y),
    83	\quad P_v(t)=\frac{C_v^{3/2}(t)}{k_v},
    84	\quad k_v=C_v^{3/2}(1)=\frac{(v+1)(v+2)}2.
    85	\tag{4}
    86	\]
    87	This also proves the needed addition theorem normalization without importing an area convention.
    88	
    89	For fixed \(y\), the first-coordinate distribution of a uniform point on \(S^4\) has density \(\tfrac34(1-t^2)\) on \([-1,1]\). The exponent follows from the three-dimensional latitude sphere; the factor follows from \(\int_{-1}^1(1-t^2)dt=4/3\). Thus
    90	\[
    91	\frac1V\int_{S^4}f(x\cdot y)d\mu(x)=\frac34\int_{-1}^1f(t)(1-t^2)dt.
    92	\tag{5}
    93	\]
    94	Expanding the finite sum of \(|T|^2\) and using the three kernels gives a double integral of \(K_{v_1}K_{v_2}K_{v_3}\). There are no convergence or exchange-of-infinite-sum issues. Equations (3)–(5) give

    96	|R|^2=\frac{3d_{v_1}d_{v_2}}{4V}
    97	\int_{-1}^1P_{v_1}(t)P_{v_2}(t)P_{v_3}(t)(1-t^2)dt.
    98	\]
    99	Since \(d_v/k_v=(2v+3)/3\), this is
   100	\[
   101	\boxed{|R|^2=\frac1{32\pi^2}
   102	\frac{(2v_1+3)(2v_2+3)}{(v_3+1)(v_3+2)}I(v_1,v_2,v_3),}
   103	\tag{6}
   104	\]

   107	## 4. Evaluate the classical integral
   108	
   109	The following is a **classical theorem**, not a conjecture or a new result here. Rogers's linearization formula (DLMF [18.18.22](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.18.E22); Andrews–Askey–Roy, *Special Functions* (1999), Theorem 6.8.2) states, for \(\lambda>0\),
   110	\[
   111	C_m^\lambda C_n^\lambda=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\min(m,n)}A_{m,n,\ell}^{\lambda} C_{m+n-2\ell}^\lambda,
   112	\]
   113	\[
   114	A_{m,n,\ell}^{\lambda}=
   115	\frac{(m+n+\lambda-2\ell)(m+n-2\ell)!}
   116	{(m+n+\lambda-\ell)\ell!(m-\ell)!(n-\ell)!}
   117	\frac{(\lambda)_\ell(\lambda)_{m-\ell}(\lambda)_{n-\ell}(2\lambda)_{m+n-\ell}}
   118	{(\lambda)_{m+n-\ell}(2\lambda)_{m+n-2\ell}}.
   119	\tag{7}
   120	\]
   121	Here \((z)_q=z(z+1)\cdots(z+q-1)\), with \((z)_0=1\). The polynomial identity and all its coefficients are well-defined for \(\lambda=3/2\).
   122	
   123	Gegenbauer orthogonality and its squared norm, specialized to this parameter, are
   124	\[
   125	\int_{-1}^1C_p^{3/2}(t)C_q^{3/2}(t)(1-t^2)dt
   126	=\delta_{pq}\frac{2(p+1)(p+2)}{2p+3}.
   127	\tag{8}
   128	\]
   129	For completeness, the norm and orthogonality follow without gamma conventions from \(C_n^{3/2}=P'_{n+1}\), the Legendre differential equation, integration by parts with vanishing \((1-t^2)\) boundary term, and \(\int_{-1}^1P_kP_l=2\delta_{kl}/(2k+1)\). Endpoint normalization in (4) follows either from the Gegenbauer generating function at \(t=1\), or \(P'_{v+1}(1)=(v+1)(v+2)/2\).
   130	
   131	Set \(a=j_1,b=j_2,c=j_3\). Then
   132	\[
   133	v_1=b+c,\quad v_2=a+c,\quad v_3=a+b,\quad s=a+b+c.
   134	\]
   135	In (7), set \(m=b+c,n=a+c,\ell=c,\lambda=3/2\); the output degree is \(p=a+b\). All other terms vanish on integrating against \(C_p^{3/2}(1-t^2)\). Multiplying its coefficient by (8) and cancelling the factor involving \(p\) gives
   136	\[
   137	I=\frac{2}{(s+3/2)a!b!c!}
   138	\frac{(3/2)_a(3/2)_b(3/2)_c(3)_s}{(3/2)_s}.
   139	\tag{9}
   140	\]
   141	For example, the cancelled product is exactly
   142	\[(p+3/2)\,p!\,\frac{2}{(p+2)!}\,
   143	\frac{2(p+1)(p+2)}{2p+3}=2.\]
   144	Using
   145	\[
   146	(3/2)_q=\frac{(2q+1)!}{4^q q!},\qquad (3)_s=\frac{(s+2)!}{2},
   147	\]
   148	with the powers of 4 cancelling because \(a+b+c=s\), (9) becomes
   149	\[
   150	\boxed{I=\frac{4(s+1)!(s+2)!}{(2s+3)!}
   151	\prod_{i=1}^3\frac{(2j_i+1)!}{(j_i!)^2}.}
   152	\tag{10}
   153	\]
   154	This integral is also the \(l'=0,n=5\) case of Ališauskas's published general triple-Gegenbauer integral: arXiv:math-ph/0509035v1, **PDF Eq.
