## 1. Statement and conventions

Let \(\mathcal H_v\) be the space of degree-\(v\) spherical harmonics on the unit sphere \(S^4\subset\mathbb R^5\). Its dimension is
\[
d_v={v+4\choose4}-{v+2\choose4}
=\frac{(v+1)(v+2)(2v+3)}6,
\]
where a binomial coefficient with upper argument smaller than 4 is zero. These are the symmetric irreducible SO(5) representations \((v,0)\) of CRW. The SO(3) branching multiplicity label is retained in an orthonormal basis; no multiplicity-free SO(3) hypothesis is imposed.

**Use CRW's measure**, not unit probability measure and not ordinary surface area:
\[
d\mu=\sin3\gamma\,d\gamma\,d\Omega,
\quad0\le\gamma\le\pi/3,
\quad\int_{\mathrm{SO}(3)}d\Omega=8\pi^2,
\quad V:=\int d\mu=\frac{16\pi^2}{3}.
\tag{1}
\]
On functions of the quadrupole direction, this is the invariant sphere measure scaled to mass \(V\). The standard Bohr-coordinate Jacobian is \(d^5q=\text{constant}\cdot\beta^4|\sin3\gamma|\,d\beta\,d\gamma\,d\Omega\), with \(q=\beta x\). Invariance of Euclidean volume therefore makes the angular pushforward a scalar multiple of surface area; its total mass fixes that multiple to two. Ordinary unit-sphere area is \(8\pi^2/3\); substituting it would give a wrong factor of two in the squared reduced element. This measure and the constant harmonic \(\sqrt3/(4\pi)\) are given in CRW §2.2 and Table I, and Welsh–Rowe (2016), Eqs. (43)–(44).

Write
\[
R(v_3;v_2,v_1)
=\langle\Psi_{v_3}\|\widehat\Psi_{v_2}\|\Psi_{v_1}\rangle.
\]
Here \(\widehat\Psi\) is **multiplication by the normalized harmonic**, not an arbitrary SO(5) tensor operator. The reduced-element normalization is CRW Eqs. (26)–(28). Its phase is chosen positive as stated immediately after Eq. (28). Different CG phase choices need not give the same signed number.

### Theorem 1 (the full quantified CRW Eq. (29))

For every \(v_1,v_2,v_3\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), the multiplication reduced element is zero unless the sum is even and all triangle inequalities hold. For every admissible triple, set
\[
s=(v_1+v_2+v_3)/2,\qquad j_i=s-v_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\]
Then, in the specified positive phase,
\[
\boxed{
R(v_3;v_2,v_1)=\frac1{4\pi}
\frac{(s+1)!}{j_1!j_2!j_3!}
\sqrt{\frac{(2v_1+3)(2v_2+3)}{(v_3+1)(v_3+2)}}
\sqrt{\frac{(2s+4)(2j_1+1)!(2j_2+1)!(2j_3+1)!}{(2s+3)!}}.
}
\tag{2}
\]
The forbidden-case rule precedes factorial evaluation. There are no negative factorials or limiting prescriptions.

## 2. Reduced-element normalization as a basis-independent trace

Begin with CRW's orthonormal SO(5)⊃SO(3)-adapted bases \(Y_{v,a}\) in \(L^2(S^4,d\mu)\), where \(a\) includes the internal multiplicity label, and put
\[
T_{abc}=\int_{S^4}\overline{Y_{v_3,c}(x)}Y_{v_2,b}(x)Y_{v_1,a}(x)\,d\mu(x).
\]
Multiplication followed by orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal H_{v_3}\) is an SO(5) intertwiner. Combining the ordinary SO(3) Wigner–Eckart convention in CRW Eq. (20) with its Eq. (26) cancels the factor \(\sqrt{2L_3+1}\). Thus
\[
T_{abc}=R(v_3;v_2,v_1)\,C_{ab}^{c},
\]
where \(C\) is the full SO(5) Clebsch–Gordan coefficient, including the ordinary SO(3) CG factor. For each fixed output index \(c\), unitarity in Eq. (25), equivalently Eq. (27) followed by ordinary SO(3) CG orthogonality, gives \(\sum_{a,b}|C_{ab}^c|^2=1\). Consequently
\[
\boxed{d_{v_3}|R|^2=\sum_{a,b,c}|T_{abc}|^2.}
\tag{3}
\]
This is precisely the squared quantity in Eq. (28), not an independently redefined reduced element. CRW's SO(3)-reduced elements are real in its chosen convention, so its plain squares equal modulus squares. The right side of (3) is a squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm and is invariant under independent unitary changes of basis in all three slots. Equation (3) therefore extends from CRW's adapted bases to **any** orthonormal bases.

There is no suppressed SO(5) outer multiplicity in this coupling of three symmetric traceless representations. These representations are real, so the intertwiner multiplicity equals the dimension of the invariant subspace in their triple tensor product. By the **first fundamental theorem of invariant theory for the special orthogonal group**, invariant tensors are generated by metric contractions and epsilon tensors (pairs of epsilon tensors reduce to metric contractions). Realize the three representations as traceless symmetric tensors. Internal metric contractions vanish by tracelessness; the numbers of contractions between the three distinct groups are uniquely \(n_{12}=j_3,n_{13}=j_2,n_{23}=j_1\). A single SO(5) epsilon tensor has five antisymmetrized slots, so at least two slots belong to the same symmetric group and it vanishes. Thus the invariant space has dimension at most one. Positivity of the integral below proves it is nonzero for every admissible triple. SO(3) internal multiplicities have all been summed in (3), rather than discarded.

## 3. Addition theorem and one-dimensional reduction

Define the reproducing kernel
\[
K_v(x,y)=\sum_aY_{v,a}(x)\overline{Y_{v,a}(y)}.
\]
Rotational invariance makes the kernel a function of \(t=x\cdot y\); its spherical-harmonic polynomial realization makes this a polynomial in \(t\). A zonal degree-\(v\) harmonic satisfies
\[
(1-t^2)u''-4tu'+v(v+3)u=0.
\]
Its polynomial solution is a multiple of the Gegenbauer polynomial \(C_v^{3/2}(t)\). Since \(K_v(x,x)\) is constant and its integral is \(d_v\),
\[
K_v(x,y)=\frac{d_v}{V}P_v(x\cdot y),
\quad P_v(t)=\frac{C_v^{3/2}(t)}{k_v},
\quad k_v=C_v^{3/2}(1)=\frac{(v+1)(v+2)}2.
\tag{4}
\]
This also proves the needed addition theorem normalization without importing an area convention.

For fixed \(y\), the distribution of \(t=x\cdot y\) for a uniform point \(x\) on \(S^4\) has density \(\tfrac34(1-t^2)\) on \([-1,1]\). The exponent follows from the three-dimensional latitude sphere and its latitude Jacobian; the factor follows from \(\int_{-1}^1(1-t^2)dt=4/3\). Thus
\[
\frac1V\int_{S^4}f(x\cdot y)d\mu(x)=\frac34\int_{-1}^1f(t)(1-t^2)dt.
\tag{5}
\]
Expanding the finite sum of \(|T|^2\) and using the three kernels gives a double integral of \(K_{v_1}K_{v_2}K_{v_3}\). There are no convergence or exchange-of-infinite-sum issues. Equations (3)–(5) give
\[
|R|^2=\frac{3d_{v_1}d_{v_2}}{4V}
\int_{-1}^1P_{v_1}(t)P_{v_2}(t)P_{v_3}(t)(1-t^2)dt.
\]
Since \(d_v/k_v=(2v+3)/3\), this is
\[
\boxed{|R|^2=\frac1{32\pi^2}
\frac{(2v_1+3)(2v_2+3)}{(v_3+1)(v_3+2)}I(v_1,v_2,v_3),}
\tag{6}
\]
where \(I=\int_{-1}^1C_{v_1}^{3/2}C_{v_2}^{3/2}C_{v_3}^{3/2}(1-t^2)dt\).

## 4. Evaluate the classical integral

The following is a **classical theorem**, not a conjecture or a new result here. Rogers's linearization formula (DLMF [18.18.22](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.18.E22); Andrews–Askey–Roy, *Special Functions* (1999), Theorem 6.8.2) states, for \(\lambda>0\),
\[
C_m^\lambda C_n^\lambda=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\min(m,n)}A_{m,n,\ell}^{\lambda} C_{m+n-2\ell}^\lambda,
\]
\[
A_{m,n,\ell}^{\lambda}=
\frac{(m+n+\lambda-2\ell)(m+n-2\ell)!}
{(m+n+\lambda-\ell)\ell!(m-\ell)!(n-\ell)!}
\frac{(\lambda)_\ell(\lambda)_{m-\ell}(\lambda)_{n-\ell}(2\lambda)_{m+n-\ell}}
{(\lambda)_{m+n-\ell}(2\lambda)_{m+n-2\ell}}.
\tag{7}
\]
Here \((z)_q=z(z+1)\cdots(z+q-1)\), with \((z)_0=1\). The polynomial identity and all its coefficients are well-defined for \(\lambda=3/2\).

Gegenbauer orthogonality and its squared norm, specialized to this parameter, are
\[
\int_{-1}^1C_p^{3/2}(t)C_q^{3/2}(t)(1-t^2)dt
=\delta_{pq}\frac{2(p+1)(p+2)}{2p+3}.
\tag{8}
\]
For completeness, the norm and orthogonality follow without gamma conventions from \(C_n^{3/2}=P'_{n+1}\), the Legendre differential equation, integration by parts with vanishing \((1-t^2)\) boundary term, and \(\int_{-1}^1P_kP_l=2\delta_{kl}/(2k+1)\). Endpoint normalization in (4) follows either from the Gegenbauer generating function at \(t=1\), or \(P'_{v+1}(1)=(v+1)(v+2)/2\).

Set \(a=j_1,b=j_2,c=j_3\). Then
\[
v_1=b+c,\quad v_2=a+c,\quad v_3=a+b,\quad s=a+b+c.
\]
In (7), set \(m=b+c,n=a+c,\ell=c,\lambda=3/2\); the output degree is \(p=a+b\). All other terms vanish on integrating against \(C_p^{3/2}(1-t^2)\). Multiplying its coefficient by (8) and cancelling the factor involving \(p\) gives
\[
I=\frac{2}{(s+3/2)a!b!c!}
\frac{(3/2)_a(3/2)_b(3/2)_c(3)_s}{(3/2)_s}.
\tag{9}
\]
For example, the cancelled product is exactly
\[(p+3/2)\,p!\,\frac{2}{(p+2)!}\,
\frac{2(p+1)(p+2)}{2p+3}=2.\]
Using
\[
(3/2)_q=\frac{(2q+1)!}{4^q q!},\qquad (3)_s=\frac{(s+2)!}{2},
\]
with the powers of 4 cancelling because \(a+b+c=s\), (9) becomes
\[
\boxed{I=\frac{4(s+1)!(s+2)!}{(2s+3)!}
\prod_{i=1}^3\frac{(2j_i+1)!}{(j_i!)^2}.}
\tag{10}
\]
This integral is also the \(l'=0,n=5\) case of Ališauskas's published general triple-Gegenbauer integral: arXiv:math-ph/0509035v1, **PDF Eq. (13b)**. That paper explicitly attributes the \(l'=0\) case to Vilenkin. It is prior art, not an original identity of this project.

## 4A. Stronger prior art: the same invariant was already in closed form

The independent mathematical referee pointed to Ališauskas **PDF Eq. (26a), p. 11**, not merely the integral (13b). The original page was subsequently checked visually and is preserved as `source/alisauskas-page-11.png`. With \(s=\tfrac12\sum_i v_i\), \(j_i=s-v_i\), it gives the class-one symbol
\[
G_n^2:=\begin{pmatrix}v_1&v_2&v_3\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}_n^2
=\frac1{\Gamma(n/2)^2}
\frac{(s+n-3)!}{(n-3)!\Gamma(s+n/2)}
\prod_{i=1}^3
\frac{(v_i+n/2-1)\Gamma(j_i+n/2-1)}{d^{(n)}_{v_i}j_i!},
\tag{A1}
\]
where \(d^{(n)}_v=(2v+n-2)(v+n-3)!/[v!(n-2)!]\). This is stated for admissible triples and vanishes for half-integer \(s\). The source says it was derived in its Ref. [5], G. Junker, *J. Phys. A* **26**, 1649 (1993). **The 1993 attribution is source-reported; that original paper was not independently inspected. The equivalent closed expression is directly verified in the 2005 arXiv primary text.**

To fix the normalization rather than compare shapes of formulas, set all canonical indices \(M_i=0\) in Ališauskas Eq. (23a). Its unit-mass Haar integral then gives
\[
G_5^2=\int_{\mathrm{SO}(5)}D^{v_1}_{00}(g)D^{v_2}_{00}(g)D^{v_3}_{00}(g)\,dg
=\mathbb E_{S^4}[P_{v_1}(t)P_{v_2}(t)P_{v_3}(t)].
\tag{A2}
\]
Here the last equality follows from its Eq. (21) at \(l'=0\), which gives \(D^v_{00}=C_v^{3/2}/C_v^{3/2}(1)=P_v\), and the sphere pushforward of normalized Haar measure. The formula in §3 therefore gives the exact dictionary
\[
\boxed{R(v_3;v_2,v_1)^2=\frac{d_{v_1}d_{v_2}}{V}\,G_5^2,\qquad
\pi^2R^2=\frac3{16}d_{v_1}d_{v_2}G_5^2.}
\tag{A3}
\]
This scalar does not depend on whether one uses a canonical subgroup chain or CRW's physical SO(3) chain.

Specialize (A1) to \(n=5\), substitute \(d_v\), and use
\(\Gamma(q+\tfrac12)=\sqrt\pi(2q)!/(4^q q!)\). Direct cancellation gives
\[
G_5^2=
\frac{24(s+1)!(s+2)!}{(2s+3)!}
\frac{\prod_i (2j_i+1)!/(j_i!)^2}
{\prod_i(v_i+1)(v_i+2)}.
\tag{A4}
\]
Multiplying (A4) by \(3d_{v_1}d_{v_2}/16\) gives exactly \(\pi^2\) times (2) squared. For example, \(G_5(2,2,2)^2=1/42\) and \((3/16)\cdot14^2/42=7/8\).

Thus the mathematical content is **already solved in equivalent class-one coupling literature before CRW's 2009 empirical presentation**. The normalization dictionary is an explicit reproduction/certification, not evidence of a new invariant or newly open problem. Under the reselection contract this candidate is rejected for new-open-problem counting. `code/prior_art_identity.py` checks (A1)–(A4) symbolically, compares its \(n=3\) specialization with independent Wigner 3j values, and checks the \(n=5\) dictionary in exact arithmetic.


## 5. Finish the proof, including every boundary

Substitution of (10) in (6) gives
\[
|R|^2=\frac1{8\pi^2}
\frac{(2v_1+3)(2v_2+3)}{(v_3+1)(v_3+2)}
\frac{(s+1)!(s+2)!}{(2s+3)!}
\prod_i\frac{(2j_i+1)!}{(j_i!)^2}.
\tag{11}
\]
Since \((s+2)!=(s+2)(s+1)!\) and \(2s+4=2(s+2)\), (11) is exactly the square of (2). Every factor is strictly positive for an admissible triple. Taking the **positive** square root, as required by CRW's phase convention, proves (2).

If the sum of the three degrees is odd, the integrand defining \(I\) is odd and the integral vanishes. If the sum is even but a triangle inequality fails, \(v_3\) is not among the degrees \(v_1+v_2-2\ell\), \(0\le\ell\le\min(v_1,v_2)\), in (7), so (8) makes the integral zero. Equation (3) then makes every corresponding multiplication matrix element zero. (When no coupled irrep exists, the reduced-element notation is extended by zero rather than positing a CG coefficient.)

The proof permits \(a,b,c=0\), including triangle saturation and the all-zero triple; \(s+3/2>0\) always. It does not use a large-\(v\) asymptotic approximation, finite verification cutoff, or missing-label convention. **This completes the universal proof.**
