## Exact open statement and book connection

**Primary text inspected:** Yohay Ailon Tevet, *Chabauty Limits of Fermat Spirals*, [arXiv:2506.22863v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.22863v2), submitted 16 September 2025, **Further Questions, p. 12**. This is the latest publicly listed arXiv version. The paper also appeared in the refereed *Combinatorics and Number Theory* **14**(3–4), 239–249, published 24 October 2025, DOI [10.2140/cnt.2025.14.239](https://doi.org/10.2140/cnt.2025.14.239). The publisher's full PDF was subscription-blocked, so the quote below is verified in the **preprint**, not asserted to be checked against the journal body. The 21-word extract is:

> whether every lattice with co-volume π is achieved as a Chabauty limit of some Fermat spiral with badly-approximable angle difference remains.

The source definition on p. 2 uses limits of translations `T_j X` with `T_j(0)` escaping to infinity. The Fermat spiral is `X_alpha={sqrt(n)e^(2*pi*i*n*alpha)}`. The question allows the badly approximable angle to depend on the target lattice; it does **not** allow a different angle at every index, an unbounded continued-fraction alphabet, or post-hoc dilation. The source's Theorem 2 describes the limit family for a **fixed** badly approximable angle; that classification is not already the quoted universal-existence answer.

The physical connection is Cao Zexian's “Are the Fibonacci Parastichous Spirals a Least-Energy Configuration on Conical Surface?”, *10000 Selected Problems in Sciences: Physics* (Science Press, 2009), pp. 541–543. The chapter concerns phyllotactic arrangements, disk packing/energy and chirality. The present target asks which crystalline local arrangements can occur asymptotically in a standard phyllotaxis model. It is an explicitly open, precisely quantified geometric continuation within that topic, **not** a full resolution of the cone's elastic-energy or chirality conjectures. The original excerpt is copied unchanged under `source/problem.zh.txt`.

The earlier cone-folder density law was screened as prior art and remains preserved at `../phys-fibonacci-cone-spirals/`; it is excluded from this reselection's scientific-open-question count. ## Bounded proof obligation and decision value

The exact obligation was to match **any** covolume-pi target, not to show that a few attractive lattices appear numerically. The written route is:

1. Choose a negatively oriented target basis and a radial direction. Its two relevant projection ratios satisfy a one-dimensional Möbius relation `u=h^2/(beta-x)-x` on a nonempty interval.
2. Use the precisely stated McMullen theorems to choose **both** ratios badly approximable. The extension and interval/density arguments are written out, including orientation reversal.
3. Concatenate increasingly long reversed and forward CF blocks, with parity padding, to build **one** angle with a **fixed finite** digit bound. This explicitly closes the realization step without a geodesic-specification assumption.
4. Use exact consecutive-convergent bases, the fixed area `pi`, local Taylor comparison and sublinear integer-index windows to recover the target with translations only.

This is a bounded, checkable proof obligation because the only selection is a pair of real ratios and the realization is an explicit digit construction; no unbounded optimization or unknown elastic functional is hidden inside it. A successful proof settles the complete quoted geometric question. It does not determine which configuration minimizes any physical energy.
