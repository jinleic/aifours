## Selection correction: the chapter is not a verified easy open theorem

The original book goal remains **OPEN-BLOCKED in this project**: we have not supplied a necessary-and-sufficient classification for all source models and physical propagation environments. This is **not** a claim that no such classification exists anywhere in the literature. The 2009 chapter itself explicitly calls its questions unresolved; we did **not** locate a current primary source explicitly designating that unrestricted chapter-level question as a still-open theorem in 2026. Search misses do not establish unsolved status.

The broad question “are there more general invariance conditions?” has received affirmative answers, including publications before the book and much broader structured-source examples afterward. Accordingly, the initial selection as a problem both “still unsolved” and “not too hard” is **not validated**. The tractable obligation here is the finite-mode **all-scalar-probes** classification, reducible to affine Bloch-sphere geometry; that does not make the unrestricted Fresnel/Maxwell classification easy. The simulator is not an argument about difficulty. This folder is a partial research contribution and a correction to the selection, not a substitute solved problem.

## Observables and quantifiers must be distinguished

1. **Spectrum:** the local normalized spectral density, determined by `tr J`; it may be invariant over a far-zone angular plane without being equal to every source-plane local spectrum.
2. **Degree of polarization:** the eigenvalue contrast of the local ensemble coherency matrix `J`, at a specified frequency. Constant degree does not require a constant ellipse/orientation.
3. **Pattern invariance:** `P(r,z)=P(r,0)` can allow a nonuniform transverse pattern. It is weaker/different from one common degree at every observation point.
4. **Source to far zone**, **all Fresnel distances**, **all scalar linear probes**, **every random mixture**, and **full Maxwell propagation** are different quantified statements.
5. Averaging instantaneous degrees is not the degree of an ensemble-averaged coherency matrix. A full-plane integrated Stokes vector is also a different observable.

### A verified non-Schell, all-distance patterned class (2022)

**Rosario Martínez-Herrero, Gemma Piquero, Massimo Santarsiero, Franco Gori and Juan Carlos González de Sande**, “A class of vectorial pseudo-Schell model sources with structured coherence and polarization,” *Optics & Laser Technology* **152**, 108079 (2022), DOI [10.1016/j.optlastec.2022.108079](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.optlastec.2022.108079). [Institutional record](https://oa.upm.es/87042/); [full primary article read](https://oa.upm.es/87042/1/9906481.pdf).

In polar coordinates, Eqs. (19)–(21) use a realizable radial kernel `W_PS(r1,r2)` times an angular polarization matrix. In this folder's conjugation convention its entries are proportional to

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\theta_1\cos\theta_2 & b\cos\theta_1\cos(\theta_2-\alpha)\\
b^*\cos(\theta_1-\alpha)\cos\theta_2 & \cos(\theta_1-\alpha)\cos(\theta_2-\alpha)
\end{pmatrix},\quad |b|\leq1.
\]

The displayed Gaussian example has radial envelope proportional to `r exp(-r²/w0²)` and radial coherence `exp[-(r2-r1)²/delta²]`. Both angular functions involve the same absolute azimuthal order. Under the **paraxial free-space Fresnel integral**, Eq. (33) retains precisely the angular matrix and changes only the radial scalar kernel. Thus the normalized coherency matrix, polarization pattern, and degree are independent of `z` wherever the intensity is nonzero, although the degree is generally **not uniform in angle**. The on-axis dark point is excluded. This is an explicit, physically relevant answer to the book's request for more general conditions, not just a GSM example. It also warns against replacing invariance along propagation by invariance under every conceivable probe.

### Deterministic ABCD and scalar versus vector environments

The elementary factorization result `W=g C -> J=h C` holds at **all distances** for any common scalar kernel, including polarization-independent paraxial ABCD/Collins systems, apertures, Fresnel propagation and scalar angular-spectrum propagation. It is a direct linearity consequence, independently proved in C3; it is not advertised as new.

For a polarization-dependent kernel `K_ij`, the common scalar factor cannot be pulled outside in the same way. Likewise, a full three-component Maxwell field has longitudinal components constrained by transversality, and the two-component DOP formula is no longer an unqualified full-field observable. This folder does not pretend that scalar angular spectrum is a full nonparaxial electromagnetic solution.

## Baseline to beat and remaining obligation

A proposed research advance must exceed: (i) established factorized GSM invariance, (ii) the known nonuniform all-distance structured classes such as the 2022 pseudo-Schell sources, and (iii) the already established quantum-channel classification machinery. Reproducing a simulator, deriving `P` from `det J`, or showing another factorized source is not enough.

The present package gives a precise all-finite-mode/all-probe classification plus an all-mixtures distinction, with self-contained proofs and positive-source examples. It is retained only as a **stronger-invariance-requirement partial lemma**. To settle the original target, one must characterize kernels on the **actual restricted Fresnel probe family**, and separately specify nonparaxial/vector and random-environment models. `RESULTS.md` C6 now supplies an explicit finite-energy rank-four source with constant degree under every Fresnel distance but degree changed by a scalar mode projector. Thus the naive all-probe-to-Fresnel necessity transfer is **refuted**, not merely awaiting a proof. A genuinely different restricted-propagator characterization is needed. Novelty positioning and that characterization are mathematical/literature obligations, not computational throughput limitations.
