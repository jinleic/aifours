2. **Gauss (1839), Allgemeine Theorie des Erdmagnetismus, §40.** Read through the revised English translation by K.-H. Glassmeier and B.T. Tsurutani (2014), *History of Geo- and Space Sciences* 5,11–62. DOI [10.5194/hgss-5-11-2014](https://doi.org/10.5194/hgss-5-11-2014); [publisher PDF](https://hgss.copernicus.org/articles/5/11/2014/hgss-5-11-2014.pdf). Read §39–40, printed pp.40–41 (PDF pp.30–31), and introductory historical comments. The primary text says combining horizontal and vertical forces separates internal and external potentials, with only the external constant undetermined. Its final p.40 display has missing factors in two internal rows; the image confirms the typo. The immediately preceding equations independently give the correct general formula. We certify that algebra in proof.md §2. This is a reading of Gauss in translation, not inspection of the 1839 German original. Also read the opening portion of the [1841 Sabine translation transcription](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Scientific_Memoirs/2/General_Theory_of_Terrestrial_Magnetism); it was not used to adjudicate §40's typographical provenance.

3. **Sabaka, T.J., Hulot, G., Olsen, N. (2010), Mathematical Properties Relevant to Geomagnetic Field Modeling.** *Handbook of Geomathematics*, chapter 17, pp.503/504–538; DOI [10.1007/978-3-642-01546-5_17](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-01546-5_17). [Author PDF](https://www.spacecenter.dk/~nio/papers/GEM_TJS.pdf), discovered via [Olsen's publications](https://www.spacecenter.dk/~nio/papers/). Read §6.1 pp.523–526, Eqs.(119)–(127), including the last paragraph of p.525: simultaneous radial and colatitudinal field components on one sphere determine all internal/external coefficients. Read §6.3 pp.527–530 for finite/internal intensity uniqueness and the Backus series. Read the §6.5 theorem attribution at pp.531–532: Backus (1986) addresses a more general current-carrying setting. This is the **2010 edition**, predating the 2011 Chinese book; its equation/page numbering is not attributed to a later edition. Scope caution: §6.3's informal claim of an orthogonal partner for arbitrary finite fields is stronger than the convergence caveat in Alberto et al.; we use only the convergent dipole series.

4. **Olsen, N., Glassmeier, K.-H., Jia, X. (2010), Separation of the Magnetic Field into External and Internal Parts.** *Space Science Reviews* 152,135–157. DOI [10.1007/s11214-009-9563-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11214-009-9563-0); [author PDF](https://www.spacecenter.dk/~nio/papers/Olsen-2010_SSR.pdf). Read §2.1 pp.136–139, Eqs.(1)–(4), and §2.2 pp.139–141 for limits when currents cross the observation region. Primary review independently identifies Gauss's radial/horizontal separation and explicitly distinguishes source altitude from planetary surface. It does not identify arbitrary physical currents uniquely.

The paper's own proof/rederivation is primary evidence, even though the original Backus articles could not be read here. proof.md gives a full alternative finite-degree argument and independently derives the same infinite-series recurrence with a convergence proof. No generic-nonzero-coefficient assumption is carried into our theorem.

6. **Hörmander, L. (1993), Remarks on Holmgren's uniqueness theorem.** *Annales de l'Institut Fourier* 43(5),1223–1251. DOI [10.5802/aif.1371](https://doi.org/10.5802/aif.1371); [journal archive PDF](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/aif.1371.pdf).
