## 1. An admissible output-value P modification

Fix `T>0`, `0<h<=T`, `ell>0`, and define

\[
\phi(t)=\min(t/h,1),\qquad \theta(t)=1-\phi(t).
\]

For the integrator `dot y_i=u_i`, uncontrolled starts `y_i(0)=a_i`, `|a_i|<=epsilon0`, zero initial input `u_0=0`, feasible reference `r=Jv`, `v` continuous, use

\[
u_{i+1}(t)=u_i(t)+\ell[e_i(t)-\theta(t)e_i(0)],\qquad e_i=r-y_i.
\]

This uses only the current-time and initial values of the previous measured error. It does not differentiate error, invert the plant, observe a full state, impose resets, or restrict the target to a finite-dimensional span. It is physically causal but not pointwise-local, so Theorem D in `continuation-theorems.md` does not apply. The positive theorem in this section assumes **no process or measurement noise**. Section 4 proves that this exclusion cannot be silently removed for this controller.

Put `A=I-ell J` and

\[
S_{h,T,\ell}=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\|A^k J\phi\|_\infty.
\]

**Theorem F1.** This constant is finite. For every bounded, possibly adversarial and nonconvergent start sequence,

\[
\|e_i\|_\infty\le\|A^i r\|_\infty+
(1+\ell S_{h,T,\ell})\epsilon_0,
\qquad \|A^i r\|_\infty\longrightarrow0.
\]

Consequently the law has a full-interval bound linear in the arbitrary initial-error radius and nominal zero-error convergence. If the start is fixed, `a_i=a`, then `e_i -> -theta a` uniformly, giving exact tracking on `[h,T]` but not at the uncontrolled initial point. No zero-mean, bias-zero, or random-start assumption occurs. The initial-only statement also applies to the genuinely nonlinear degree-one coordinate plant `dot x=u/(1+alpha cos x)`, `y=x+alpha sin x`, `0<alpha<1`, because its output equation is exactly the integrator and no cancellation is used by the learner.

## 2. Uniform Laguerre bounds without an endpoint asymptotic gap

The source identities actually inspected are the NIST DLMF [Laguerre–Bessel integral 18.10.9](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.10.E9.tex) and [fixed-order Bessel asymptotic 10.17.3](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.17.E3.tex). For `alpha=0,2`, continuity near zero and that asymptotic at infinity give a finite constant `B_alpha` with

\[
|J_\alpha(s)|\le B_\alpha s^{-1/2}\quad(s>0).
\]

Here `J_alpha` is the Bessel function, not the integration operator J. The integral identity reads, for `x>0` and the nonnegative integer parameters used here,

\[
L_n^{(\alpha)}(x)=\frac{e^x x^{-\alpha/2}}{n!}
\int_0^\infty e^{-s}s^{n+\alpha/2}J_\alpha(2\sqrt{xs})\,ds.
\]

Substitution yields the explicit bound

\[
|L_n^{(\alpha)}(x)|\le
\frac{B_\alpha}{\sqrt2}\,e^x x^{-\alpha/2-1/4}
\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha/2+3/4)}{n!}.
\]

The integral identity can also be checked directly: expand the Bessel series, integrate each term (absolute summability follows from the factorial denominators), and apply Kummer's transformation to the resulting confluent hypergeometric series. Thus no Laguerre asymptotic uniform down to zero is being assumed. The standard gamma-ratio asymptotic, or its elementary finite-product bounds for this fixed shift, gives `Gamma(n+7/4)/n!=O((n+1)^(3/4))` and `Gamma(n+3/4)/n!=O((n+1)^(-1/4))`.

A finite binomial expansion of `(I-ell J)^n` gives the exact identities

\[
A^n(t^2/2)=\ell^{-2}L_{n+2}^{(-2)}(\ell t)
=\frac{t^2 L_n^{(2)}(\ell t)}{(n+1)(n+2)},
\qquad A^n1=L_n(\ell t).
\]

The alpha=2 bound above proves

\[
\|A^n(t^2/2)\|_\infty\le C_{T,\ell}(n+1)^{-5/4}.
\]

Although the intermediate bound has `x^(-5/4)`, multiplication by `t^2` leaves `t^(3/4)` and the estimate extends to zero continuously. The alpha=0 bound, integrated over `[0,T]`, proves

\[
\|A^n J\|_{C\to C}\le\int_0^T|L_n(\ell s)|ds
\le C'_{T,\ell}(n+1)^{-1/4}.
\]

The kernel identity for `A^nJ` follows by expanding J powers; this is not the sup norm of `A^n` itself. Therefore `A^n r=A^nJv ->0` for every feasible continuous input v.

## 3. Proof of the initial-only robustness statement

Causal delay by h commutes with J. With `f(t)=t^2/2` and `D_h f(t)=0` before h and `f(t-h)` thereafter,

\[
J\phi=(f-D_hf)/h,\qquad
\|A^nJ\phi\|_\infty\le(2/h)\|A^nf\|_\infty.
\]

The preceding summable `n^(-5/4)` bound proves `S<infinity`.

Since `e_i(0)=-a_i`, the learner obeys `u_{i+1}=Au_i+ell(r-phi a_i)`. Iterating and using `ell J=I-A` gives the exact physical-error formula

\[
e_i=A^i r-a_i1+\ell\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}A^{i-1-j}J\phi\,a_j.
\]

Its norm proves F1 for every start sequence. If `a_i=a`, telescoping gives

\[
e_i=A^i(r-\phi a)-\theta a.
\]

Both r and phi lie in the range of J on bounded functions; the same integrable-kernel bound applies to the essentially bounded derivative `phi'=h^{-1}1_{[0,h]}`. Thus the first term tends uniformly to zero, proving the fixed-start tail claim. QED.

**Regularity correction.** The parent's more general Lipschitz-theta suggestion is not needed. Mere `phi=O(t)` or Lipschitz continuity does not by itself justify a `n^(-5/4)` operator estimate: writing `Jphi=J^2 phi'` leaves an arbitrary bounded derivative, whereas the kernel of `A^nJ^2` has the slower `n^(-3/4)` scale. The explicit piecewise-linear theta above is proved. More generally the same proof works when phi(0)=0 and phi' has bounded variation: represent Jphi as a bounded-variation superposition of delayed quadratics. No claim of necessity of that regularity is made.

## 4. Theorem F2: every initial-output rectifier fails arbitrary measurement noise

Fix **any** continuous rectifying function theta, any nonzero real ell, any integer `q>=1`, any `t_* in (0,T]`, and a zero reference. On the q-fold integrator with zero initial states and `u_0=0`, use the same law `u_{i+1}=u_i+ell[e_i-theta e_i(0)]`. There exists one bounded infinite sequence of continuous measurement-noise traces, each identically zero on a common nontrivial initial interval, such that both the measured and physical outputs at t_* are unbounded across trials. In particular, the promising F1 rectifier does not solve the original arbitrary-noise problem, even at relative degree one.

**Proof.** Choose `0<tau<t_*`. Let X be the closed Banach subspace of `C[0,T]` whose functions vanish on `[0,tau/2]`. For any noise sequence in `ell_infinity(X)`, zero starts imply `e_i(0)=0`. Hence rectification is identically inactive in every update. Write `G=J^q`, `A_q=I-ell G`. Measured output at trial N is

\[
y_N^m=\xi_N-\ell\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}G A_q^{N-1-j}\xi_j.
\]

Define bounded linear functionals `F_N:ell_infinity(X)->R` by evaluation of this expression at t_*. Smooth bounded ramps `psi_epsilon` that are zero up to tau and one after `tau+epsilon` belong to X and converge pointwise away from tau to the delayed unit step `D_tau 1`. For each fixed k>=1, the integral operator `G A_q^(k-1)` has a continuous integrable kernel, so dominated convergence gives

\[
[-\ell G A_q^{k-1}\psi_\epsilon](t_*)
\longrightarrow h_{q,k}(t_*-\tau),
\quad h_{q,0}=1,
\]

where `h_{q,k}` are exactly the pure-P initial-shift impulse coefficients of Theorem B in `theorems.md`. For sufficiently small epsilon, `psi_epsilon(t_*)=1` too. For each finite N choose the trial signs that maximize the delayed-step convolution; let epsilon tend to zero after fixing N. Thus

\[
\|F_N\|\ge\sum_{k=0}^N|h_{q,k}(t_*-\tau)|.
\]

Theorem B proves these partial sums unbounded for every q and every nonzero ell. Uniform boundedness on `ell_infinity(X)` therefore supplies a **single** bounded sequence xi for which `sup_N |F_N(xi)|=infinity`. Rescale to any positive prescribed noise radius. Each trial noise is continuous and zero on `[0,tau/2]`. The physical output differs from the measured one by bounded xi_N(t_*), so it too is unbounded. QED.

The proof uses smooth ramps only as finite-horizon approximants. Its infinite witness is in the Banach space of continuous traces, not asserted to be uniformly smooth or equicontinuous. Those additional restrictions were not part of the original arbitrary bounded-noise requirement. This theorem rules out initial-value subtraction as a cure for arbitrary measurement noise; it does not rule out more substantial nonlinear, nonlocal, time-varying or noise-aware learning mechanisms.

For complex `|z|<1`, the generating function is

\[
H_q(z,t)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty h_n(t)z^n
 =\left[(I+\frac{\ell z}{1-z}J^q)^{-1}1\right](t)

where `E_q(w)=Σ_{k>=0} w^k/(qk)!`. The inverse exists by Volterra quasinilpotence. The identity is initially obtained near zero by geometric series and extends throughout the unit disk analytically. Equivalently, it follows by taking the `q`th time derivative and the zero-derivative initial conditions of `H_q`.

We show `Σ_n |h_n(t_*)|=∞`. Absolute summability would make `H_q(·,t_*)` continuous on the closed unit disk, in particular at `z=1`.

* If `ell<0`, let `z` tend to one along the real radius. The argument of `E_q` tends to positive infinity, and its nonnegative power series is unbounded.
* If `ell>0` and `q=1`, the real radial limit is zero. Set `z=iR/(1+iR)`, `R>0`. Then `|z|<1`, `z→1`, `z/(1-z)=iR`, and `|H_1(z,t_*)|=1`. There is no common limit.
* If `ell>0` and `q=2`, on the real radius `H_2(z,t_*)=cos(t_* sqrt(ell z/(1-z)))`, which has no limit.

  \[
  E_q(-R^q)=\frac1q\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\exp(R e^{(2j+1)\pi i/q}).
  \]
  
  The conjugate terms at angles `±π/q` have strictly largest real exponent, so
  
  \[
  E_q(-R^q)=\frac2q e^{R\cos(\pi/q)}\cos(R\sin(\pi/q))
       +O(e^{c_q R}),\quad c_q<\cos(\pi/q).
  \]
  
  Along `R=2πk/sin(π/q)` the main term grows without bound. Thus continuity at one fails.

Now regard `F_i(a)=Σ_{j=0}^i h_{i-j}(t_*)a_j` as bounded real linear functionals on the Banach space `ell∞(N)`. Their norms are exactly `Σ_{n=0}^i |h_n(t_*)|`, which are unbounded. The uniform boundedness principle, by contrapositive, supplies one bounded real `a` for which `sup_i |F_i(a)|=∞`. Scale this sequence to satisfy `|a_i|<=ε0`. This is a single infinite disturbance sequence, not merely a different maximizing sequence for every horizon. QED.

For `ell=0`, choose the feasible target `y_d(t)=t^q/q!`, nominal input `u_d=1`, zero initial states, and `u_0=0`. Actual outputs remain zero, disproving zero-uncertainty convergence. Therefore no constant scalar P0 gain meets the full specification for this class.
The exact quantifier in the nonzero-gain result is `for every t_*>0 there exists a bounded sequence a`; the sequence may depend on `t_*`. No simultaneous divergence at all positive times is claimed.

### Finite witnesses and what they do not establish

For any finite horizon `N`, choosing `a_j=ε0 sign(h_{N-j}(t_*))` for `0<=j<=N` gives exactly

\[
y_N(t_*)=\epsilon_0\sum_{n=0}^N |h_n(t_*)|.
\]

These finite witnesses are easy to reproduce, but by themselves are not a single infinite divergent sequence; the proof above closes that quantifier gap. For `q=1`, one may compute `h_n=L_n(ell t)-L_{n-1}(ell t)` using the Laguerre recurrence, with `h_0=1`. Constant initial error also gives `u_i(0)=-ell i a`, but that input divergence alone would not prove output divergence. The theorem deliberately proves the stronger output claim.
