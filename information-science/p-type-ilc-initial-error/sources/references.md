## Authoritative book statement

**B0.** Sun Mingxuan, “P-type Iterative Learning Control Systems with Relative Degree Not Less Than One” (Chinese title: 相对阶不小于 1 的 P 型迭代学习控制系统), in *10000 Selected Problems in Sciences: Information Science*, Science Press, 2011, pp. 712–715; PDF pages 733–736. Images: `book/pdfpage-0733.png` through `book/pdfpage-0736.png`.

All four images were read before analysis. Page 713 contains equations (1)–(4), including `u_{i+1}=u_i+Le_i`, and explicitly says the initial conditions are uncontrolled. Page 714 ends its technical discussion with the question of robust convergence for P-type systems under arbitrary bounded initial conditions. The reference to a prespecified interval and fixed initial error immediately before that question concerns **D-type** learning, not an already established P-type solution. The input dimension is denoted `r` in the book; the local proof uses `q` for relative degree.

## Primary sources with inspected content

**R1 — 2009 initial rectification, D-type.** X.-D. Li, T. W. S. Chow, J. K. L. Ho, and J. Zhang. “Iterative learning control with initial rectifying action for nonlinear continuous systems.” *IET Control Theory & Applications* 3(1), 49–55 (2009). DOI: [10.1049/iet-cta:20070486](https://doi.org/10.1049/iet-cta:20070486). [Author-hosted primary PDF](https://www.ee.cityu.edu.hk/~twschow/pubs/papers/50.pdf). **Full text.** Snapshot: `li-chow-2009.reader.txt`.

The introduction explicitly identifies a **D-type** technique. Equations (5)–(7) use an error derivative and initial states from adjacent trials. Theorem 1 requires `sup||I-CBK||<1` and a right-invertible, estimable `B(t)` on `[0,h]`. The claimed bound concerns `[h,T]`; the claim of zero tail error requires exact `B` on that initial interval. This is neither full-interval zero tracking under arbitrary shifts nor P0. **Proof-audit caution:** the paper explicitly extends auxiliary equations (15)–(16), originally obtained for `[h,T]`, to `[0,T]` despite their not being equivalent there, and then transfers conclusions back. That transfer is not justified merely by agreement of formulas on a tail when the formulas integrate over the initial interval. Its theorem is not imported into our proof or counted as independently certified.

**R2 — 2017 fractional P-type with initial-state learning.** Xianghu Liu and Yanfang Li. “The convergence analysis of P-type iterative learning control with initial state error for some fractional system.” *Journal of Inequalities and Applications* 2017, article 29. DOI: [10.1186/s13660-017-1302-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13660-017-1302-6). [Primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5285440/). **Full text.** Snapshot: `fractional-2017.reader.txt`.

The plant uses a Caputo derivative of order `0<α<1`. Equations (5)–(6) combine open-/closed-loop P updates with an explicitly constrained/learned initial state. Theorem 3.1 depends on that initialization rule and its stated contraction conditions, not arbitrary uncontrolled trial starts. As printed, equation (6) has `x_{k+1}(0)=x_k(0)+BL_1 e_k(t)`, with a free time variable on the right; the proof uses that expression. We do not silently repair it or import its convergence theorem. It is a verified example of why an attractive title alone does not settle the book's ordinary-integer-order problem.

**R3 — 2018 Sun's error-trajectory/dead-zone method.** M. Sun, T. Wu, L. Chen, and G. Zhang. “Neural AILC for Error Tracking Against Arbitrary Initial Shifts.” *IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks and Learning Systems* 29(7), 2705–2716 (2018; online 2017). DOI: [10.1109/TNNLS.2017.2698507](https://doi.org/10.1109/TNNLS.2017.2698507). [Author abstract, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28534792/). **Abstract verified; full theorem assumptions not fetched.**

The abstract specifies a desired **error trajectory** whose initial value is matched to the actual trial error; it does not constrain the actual starting state. It claims convergence to a prespecified neighborhood with uniform boundedness and uses a dead-zone-modified Lyapunov functional for neural approximation error. It is adaptive neural/error-trajectory control, not evidence of the book's pointwise P0 guarantee. Full author given names beyond the indexed initials have not been independently checked.

**R4 — 2021 vector-relative-degree continuous D-type robustness.** Yun-Shan Wei and Xiao-Dong Li. “Robust iterative learning control for linear continuous systems with vector relative degree under varying input trail lengths and random initial state shifts.” *International Journal of Robust and Nonlinear Control* 31(2), 609–622 (2021; online 2020). DOI: [10.1002/rnc.5300](https://doi.org/10.1002/rnc.5300). **Publisher-deposited abstract verified via DOI/Crossref; Wiley full text inaccessible (403).**

The primary abstract explicitly says **iteratively moving average operator based D-type ILC**, with an initial rectifying interval, a tail-error bound proportional to initial shifts, and zero tail error for fixed shifts. Thus the arbitrary-relative-degree result is not a pure-P result and changes the tracking interval.

**R5 — 2022 adaptive high-order nonlinear arbitrary shifts.** G. Li, T. Lu, Y. Han, and Z. Xu. “Adaptive iterative learning control for high-order nonlinear systems with random initial state shifts.” *ISA Transactions* 130, 205–215 (2022). DOI: [10.1016/j.isatra.2022.04.028](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isatra.2022.04.028). [Primary author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35570151/). **Abstract verified; full proof not fetched.**

The abstract claims order-dependent initial-shift rectification, bounded signals, and complete tracking **over a specified/preset interval** as iteration increases. It explicitly describes adaptive control and uses an arctangent term to avoid flutter. This supports progress for a distinct adaptive/tail-tracking formulation, not equivalence to arbitrary-noise, whole-interval P0 robustness.

**R6 — 2023 field-status survey.** Dongjie Chen, Tiantian Lu, and Guojun Li. “A survey of methods for handling initial state shifts in iterative learning control.” *Heliyon* 9(12), e22492 (2023). DOI: [10.1016/j.heliyon.2023.e22492](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2023.e22492). [Primary survey text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10686873/). **Full text.** Snapshot: `survey-2023.reader.txt`.

Sections 2.4–2.5 separate fixed from arbitrary shifts, and distinguish PID-type corrections from the adaptive/attractor methods in Section 3. Section 2.2 discusses 2-D analysis. Section 3.3.1 discusses time-decaying boundary layers; Sections 3.3.2–3.3.3 discuss finite-time/initial-error transformations. Section 5 says specified-interval accurate tracking under random initial errors is achievable with adaptive controllers/attractor methods, while leaving other initial-correction topics open. Its introductory sentence that the “initial shift problem” is theoretically solved is too broad to be imported as a solution of every controller class and norm; the article's own distinctions matter. This survey is a primary source for its taxonomy but is secondary evidence for other papers' theorems.

**R7 — 2023 Sun's barrier-function learning.** Mingxuan Sun. “Fractional Barrier Lyapunov Functions with Application to Learning Control.” [arXiv:2306.06646v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06646), 11 June 2023. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.06646). **Full text, preprint.** Snapshot: `sun-2023.reader.txt`.

Section IV-A states that “the actual initial state is set to be the same as the desired one,” `x_k(0)=x_d(0)` for all trials. Theorems 1–2 concern saturated learning, barrier constraints, zero residual versus neighborhood tracking. Thus this later work does **not** resolve arbitrary initial shifts. Here “fractional barrier” describes the barrier-function expression, not a fractional-order plant.

**R8 — 2024 fractional initialization and noise.** Xiaofeng Xu, Jiangang Lu, and Jinshui Chen. “Convergence Analysis of Iterative Learning Control for Initialized Fractional Order Systems.” *Fractal and Fractional* 8(3), 168 (2024). DOI: [10.3390/fractalfract8030168](https://doi.org/10.3390/fractalfract8030168). [Publisher page](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-3110/8/3/168). **Publisher-deposited abstract verified via DOI/Crossref; full HTML/XML inaccessible (403).**

The abstract claims robust convergence under nonrepetitive initialization functions, with bounds depending on initialization shift; it also considers nonlinearity, initial error, and channel noise. Perfect tracking is associated with a proposed **initialization-learning strategy**. These fractional-memory/controlled-initialization assumptions do not establish the ordinary strictly proper P0 theorem asked here.

**R9 — 2025 discrete-time random shifts.** Yun-Shan Wei, Xu-Jun Bao, Wenli Shang, and Jia-Zheng Liao. “Iterative Learning Control for Non-Linear Discrete-Time Systems with Iterative Varying Reference Trajectory and Varying Trail Lengths Under Random Initial State Shifts.” *Journal of Intelligent & Robotic Systems* 111, article 58 (2025). DOI: [10.1007/s10846-025-02256-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10846-025-02256-x). [Primary publisher page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10846-025-02256-x). **Abstract verified.**

It claims bounded modified tracking error for varying starts/references and convergence to zero when initial state and reference are fixed. It is explicitly discrete-time. A discrete lifted matrix with shifted output sampling is not the continuous Volterra operator; no continuous-time P0 conclusion follows.

**R10 — 2026 high-relative-degree adaptive discrete systems.** Shuai Gao, Dong Shen, and Abdelhamid Tayebi. “Robust Adaptive Learning Control for a Class of Non-affine Nonlinear Systems.” [arXiv:2602.00968v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00968), 1 February 2026. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00968v1). **Full text, preprint.** Snapshot: `gao-shen-tayebi-2026.reader.txt`.

The displayed plant is `x_k(t+ρ)=F(X_k(t),u_k(t),t)+w_k(t)`: discrete-time, parameterized nonaffine systems, not the book's continuous-time plant. Assumptions include a nonvanishing input derivative, global Lipschitz conditions, bounded disturbances, and known bounded initial values. The proposed adaptive/implicit control is claimed to give a disturbance neighborhood and exact tracking without disturbance. This is pertinent current work but not the book's P-type solution.

## Mathematical sources for the rectifier boundary proof

The continuation's initial-only positive theorem imports the following exact public mathematical formulas, read directly on 2026-10-14:

* NIST DLMF [18.10.9, Laguerre–Bessel integral](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.10.E9.tex): the integral representation of `L_n^(alpha)` with `alpha=0,2`.
* NIST DLMF [10.17.3, fixed-order Bessel asymptotic](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.17.E3.tex): together with continuity, it supplies `sup_{s>0} sqrt(s)|J_alpha(s)|<infinity` for those two fixed orders.
* NIST DLMF [5.11.12, gamma-ratio asymptotic](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.11.E12.tex): the two fixed gamma ratios yield the exponents `-5/4` and `-1/4`. The proof does **not** use a fixed-positive-x Laguerre asymptotic as if uniform at zero.

The inspected survey R6 Section 2.4 displays a derivative-bearing initial-rectification law, and Section 2.5 discusses derivative/state-informed and averaged corrections. Targeted P-type/initial-output/rectification searches did not produce a verified primary theorem matching the explicit F1 law and quantifiers; this is **not** a novelty certificate. F1 is a limited initial-only degree-one result. F2 proves why this particular initial-output subtraction cannot be upgraded to arbitrary continuous measurement-noise robustness at any degree.
