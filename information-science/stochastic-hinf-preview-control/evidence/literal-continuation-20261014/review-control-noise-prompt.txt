# Literal mixed-norm continuation: exact separation and an explicit subclass

Date: 2026-10-14 (PT). This file uses the book's actual norm, not F1 of
`theory.md`. It gives a constant-matrix finite-horizon solvability separation
(Theorem L2) and a complete necessary-and-sufficient result for a separately
labelled time-gated subclass (Theorem L3). Neither is the general book solution.
These are written proofs; novelty is unestablished and no Lean certification is
claimed. New evidence is in `../evidence/literal-continuation-20261014/`.

## 0. Contract and information

For an interval I, write

    N1(f; I) = E (integral_I |f(t)|^2 dt)^(1/2),
    N2(f; I) = (E integral_I |f(t)|^2 dt)^(1/2).

The induced gain is the supremum of Np(z)/Np(w) over nonzero admissible inputs;
strict feasibility at gamma means this supremum is strictly below gamma.
The two expectations in N1 are never replaced by N2.

W is a standard scalar Brownian motion for a filtration (F_t), with independent
future increments relative to F_t. Independent initial randomization is allowed;
future Brownian information is not. Inputs are progressively measurable. Unless
specified otherwise, they satisfy E integral |w|^2 < infinity, as in old F2.
Every N1 upper bound below also holds on the larger class N1(w) < infinity.
This means pathwise L2 and finite expected pathwise L2 norm, not merely E|w(t)|
finite at individual times. Outside the counted interval w is zero. x(0)=0.

Controllers may be nonlinear and history-dependent, but must be nonanticipative
and give a well-posed closed loop. The impossibility in Theorem L2 holds even for
any F_t-adapted control, a larger information class than the book's
u(t)=F(x(t), w|[t-h,t]). Its feasible controller u=0 has the book's form and needs
no additional information. Thus enlarging information does not weaken either
assertion. Theorem L3 also specifies an explicit controller of the book's form.
The words **deterministic** and **adapted** below denote different disturbance
classes, never an implicit interchange of their suprema.

Only finite horizons are asserted in L1--L3. In particular, the feasible u=0 in
L2 is not internally mean-square stable on the infinite horizon. No statement
about infinite-horizon solvability follows from that finite-horizon example.

## 1. An adapted-input convolution estimate

**Lemma L1.** Let a<0, sigma real, q=2a+sigma^2, L>0, h>=0, T=h+L, and

    dx(t) = [a x(t) + w(t-h)] dt + sigma x(t) dW(t),   x(0)=0.

Let

    J_q(delta) = integral_0^delta exp(q r) dr,
    H_delta = sqrt(J_q(delta))/(1-exp(a delta)),      delta>0.

Then, for every adapted input with N1(w;[0,L]) finite,

    N1(x;[0,T]) <= sqrt(L) H_delta N1(w;[0,L]).                 (L1)

This remains true for deterministic inputs, and is uniform over all adapted
input laws. No mean-square stability assumption q<0 is needed.

*Proof.* The homogeneous transition is positive:

    Phi(t,s)=exp((a-sigma^2/2)(t-s)+sigma(W_t-W_s)),  t>=s.

The pathwise variation-of-constants formula is

    x(t)=integral_0^L 1{t>=s+h} Phi(t,s+h) w(s) ds.

For N1 inputs this formula defines the locally well-posed strong solution:
on each finite interval continuous Phi and its inverse are pathwise bounded,
and pathwise L2 inputs are L1 in time. Localization gives the usual Itô equation.
Minkowski in L2([0,T]), followed by Tonelli, gives

    N1(x) <= integral_0^L E[ |w(s)| R_s ] ds,
    R_s = (integral_(s+h)^T Phi(t,s+h)^2 dt)^(1/2).

R_s depends only on Brownian increments strictly after s+h, so is independent of
F_(s+h) and hence of w(s). This is exactly where adaptedness is used; it would
fail for an anticipating disturbance. Let G(r)=exp((a-sigma^2/2)r+sigma W_r).
The expectations of R_s are bounded by M=E||G||_L2(0,infinity).
Split the latter time axis into blocks of length delta. Pointwise, the L2 norm
over the union is at most the sum of block L2 norms. The n-th block norm is
G(n delta) times a fresh, independent block norm. Therefore

    M <= sum_(n>=0) exp(a n delta) E||G||_L2(0,delta)
      <= sum_(n>=0) exp(a n delta) sqrt(integral_0^delta E G(r)^2 dr)
       = H_delta < infinity.

Here E G(r)=exp(a r) and E G(r)^2=exp(q r), directly from the normal moment
formula. Tonelli justifies the sum, also before its finiteness is established.
Finally integral_0^L E|w(s)| ds <= sqrt(L) N1(w). This proves (L1). The same
proof covers inputs with infinite second moments but finite N1. QED.

**Separate Hilbert estimate.** For the undelayed input-to-state operator S on
[0,T], Minkowski in L2(Omega x [0,T]), independent future increments, and
Cauchy--Schwarz in the input time give

    N2(Su) <= M_T N2(u),
    M_T^2 = integral_0^T J_q(T-s) ds
          = (exp(qT)-1-qT)/q^2             if q != 0,
          = T^2/2                         if q = 0.            (L2)

This is an upper bound, not a claim that the operator norm equals M_T.
Indeed ||1{t>=s} Phi(t,s)u(s)||_(L2(Omega x time))
= sqrt(E|u(s)|^2 J_q(T-s)); integration and Cauchy--Schwarz prove it.

## 2. Exact constant-matrix solvability separation, with nonzero control

**Theorem L2 (finite horizon, adapted or deterministic inputs).** Consider the
book's constant-matrix system with h=1, [a,b]=[0,5], counted input interval [0,4],
and

    dx(t) = [-x(t) + u(t) + w(t-1)] dt + 2x(t) dW(t),
    z(t)  = ( x(t), 100u(t) ),                 x(0)=0.          (L3)

Thus A=-1, B=C=1, A0=2, B0=0, D0=(1,0)^T, D1=(0,100)^T.
In particular the control is effective (B is nonzero), D1^T D1=10000, and
ordinary state controllability is not removed. The plant is even mean-square
stabilizable, for example u=-2x makes 2(A+B(-2))+A0^2=-2<0.

1. The literal book problem is strictly feasible at gamma=6 for **all adapted
   disturbances**, including the larger N1-input class: u=0 gives

       g_literal(u=0) <= 2 sqrt((e^2-1)/2)/(1-e^(-1)) < 6.     (L4)

2. No admissible controller, even nonlinear and allowed the whole Brownian
   filtration, has mean-square gain below 14. Consequently the mean-square
   problem at gamma=6 is infeasible. The lower bound uses just one deterministic
   input, so it holds with either deterministic or adapted disturbances.

This separates **existence of controllers**, not just two ratios for one law or
one controller. It also refutes necessity of the mean-square Riccati condition
for the literal finite-horizon problem within the printed constant-matrix model.
It does not prove or disprove sufficiency of that condition for arbitrary
adapted literal inputs.

*Proof of 1.* Apply L1 with a=-1, sigma=2, L=4, delta=1. Since u=0, N1(z)=N1(x).
This gives (L4), uniformly in w. The exact inequality follows already from
8/3 < e < 11/4: its squared upper bound is

    4 (( (11/4)^2-1 )/2) / (1-3/8)^2 = 168/5 < 36.

Hence no numerical sampling or unproved exponential-functional identity is used.

*Proof of 2.* Let w=1_[0,1], so N2(w)=1, and let d be the response with u=0.
Before time 1 it is zero. On [1,2], use r=t-1. Its first two moments obey

    m'=-m+1,       m(0)=0,
    s'=2s+2m,     s(0)=0.

Thus m(r)=1-e^(-r) and

    s(r)=e^(2r)/3 - 1 + (2/3)e^(-r).

After time 2, its second moment grows by e^(2(t-2)). Therefore

    N2(d)^2 >= D := (e^2/3 - 1 + 2/(3e)) (e^6-1)/2.          (L5)

For any candidate adapted u the plant's linearity gives x=d+Su, even if the
rule generating u is nonlinear. Here S acts on the entire interval [0,5],
including the time before the delayed disturbance arrives; preview pre-action
is therefore fully included, not excluded. L2 with q=2 gives

    N2(Su) <= M N2(u),       M^2=(e^10-11)/4.

If the candidate output has finite mean-square energy, its separate 100u
component implies u is square integrable. Otherwise it already fails. The
triangle inequality followed by two-dimensional Cauchy--Schwarz yields

    N2(d) <= N2(x)+M N2(u)
          <= sqrt(1+M^2/10000) sqrt(N2(x)^2+10000 N2(u)^2)
           = sqrt(1+M^2/10000) N2(z).

Consequently every control satisfies

    N2(z)/N2(w) >= sqrt(D/(1+(e^10-11)/40000)) > 14.          (L6)

The final strict inequality is an exact rational consequence of Taylor-series
lower/upper bounds for e, implemented with Fraction arithmetic in the verifier;
its value is about 14.89. The floating-point value is not the proof. QED.

**Corollary L2b (control-dependent noise is allowed).** Replace (L3) by

    dx = [-x+u+w(t-1)] dt + (2x+u) dW,   z=(x,10000u),

with the same h=1, T=5 and input class. Again u=0 has literal gain <6, but
every adapted controller has mean-square gain >17. Here B0=1, so both state-
and control-dependent diffusion are present. The homogeneous feedback u=-2x
even cancels the diffusion and gives drift -3x.

*Proof.* The uncontrolled response d and the N1 estimate are unchanged. By
linearity x=d+y, where y is the control-only response from zero. Itô's formula
and 6yu <= y^2+9u^2 give, with p(t)=E y(t)^2,

    p' = 2p+6E(yu)+E u^2 <= 3p+10E u^2.

For adapted square-integrable u, localization and the usual second-moment
estimate justify this inequality; equivalently first prove it for bounded u
and pass to L2 by continuous dependence for a linear SDE. Gronwall and Tonelli
give

    N2(y)^2 <= (10/3)(e^15-1) N2(u)^2.

The same triangle/Cauchy--Schwarz argument as in L2 therefore gives

    N2(z)/N2(w) >= sqrt(D/(1+(10/3)(e^15-1)/10^8)) > 17.     (L6b)

The last inequality is certified by the same exact rational exponential
bracket. It is approximately 17.60. No proposed controller's linearity is
assumed. QED.

**What changes with the horizon.** The L1 bound scales at most as sqrt(L) for
fixed a<0, sigma, whereas for q>0 the response to a fixed early deterministic
pulse has mean-square energy growing exponentially in L. This is a bound for
finite horizons. It supplies no finite infinite-horizon induced gain: neither
one may send L to infinity in sqrt(L), nor may one identify the expected energy
of a single pulse with the induced norm over all long inputs.

## 3. A fully characterized adapted mixed-norm subclass

This section is explicitly **outside the book's constant-coefficient class**.
It supplies an exact N&S benchmark without defining an optimum through an
unresolved Bellman equation. Set h=0, T=4, x(0)=0. With endpoints immaterial,

    dx = w dt                      on [0,1],
    dx = x dW                      on [1,2],
    dx = w dt                      on [2,3],
    dx = 0                         on [3,4].

Write d(t)=1_[3,4](t), and take z(t)=(d(t)x(t)+u(t), u(t)).
Here B=B0=0, D1=(1,1)^T and D1^T D1=2. Inputs on the two inactive intervals
only increase their norm and cannot help; nevertheless the upper bounds allow
them. The controller has no state actuation in this benchmark, unlike L2.

**Theorem L3 (exact literal synthesis for the gated subclass).** Over either
all deterministic inputs or all adapted inputs, the optimal literal induced
gain is exactly 1. The literal problem is strictly feasible iff gamma>1. The
explicit admissible controller

    u*(t)=-d(t)x(t)/2

attains gain 1. This holds on the N1 input space and on its square-integrable
subspace. For comparison, the optimal mean-square gains are

    adapted:       sqrt((1+e)/2),
    deterministic: sqrt((e+1+sqrt((e-1)^2+4))/4).              (L7)

*Proof.* Pointwise completion of an ordinary Euclidean square, not stochastic
completion of squares, gives

    |z|^2 = (d x)^2/2 + 2(u+d x/2)^2.

Therefore u* minimizes the output norm for every sample path and every input;
no controller can do better. Put X=integral_0^1 w, V=integral_2^3 w and
G=exp(W_2-W_1-1/2). On the output interval x=GX+V. X is F_1 measurable, G is
independent of F_1, EG=1 and EG^2=e. Pathwise input energy is at least X^2+V^2.
For arbitrary adapted V (including information through time 3),

    E|GX+V| <= E(G|X|)+E|V| = E|X|+E|V|
             <= sqrt(2) E sqrt(X^2+V^2) <= sqrt(2) N1(w).

Thus u* gives N1(z)<=N1(w). The deterministic input w=1 on [0,1] and [2,3],
zero elsewhere, gives X=V=1, N1(w)=sqrt(2), and
N1(z)=E(G+1)/sqrt(2)=sqrt(2). Equality proves necessity, attainability and the
strict-feasibility statement, without any linear-controller restriction.

For adapted mean-square inputs, let U=||X||_L2(Omega), V2=||V||_L2(Omega).
Independence gives ||GX||_L2=sqrt(e) U. Hence

    ||GX+V||_L2 <= sqrt(e) U+V2 <= sqrt(e+1) sqrt(U^2+V2^2).

It is attained by w=1 on [0,1], w=G/e on [2,3], zero elsewhere. This second
value is known at time 2 and is therefore adapted; X=1, V=G/e make both
inequalities equalities. The output factor is 1/sqrt(2), proving the first
formula in (L7). For deterministic inputs the exact energy quadratic form is
[X,V] [[e,1],[1,1]] [X,V]^T, while the input energy is at least X^2+V^2 with
equality for constant inputs on each active interval. Its largest eigenvalue,
divided by 2 for the output, gives the second formula. QED.

## 4. Limits, old-proof audit and reproduction

- **Original finite horizon:** L2 is a genuine original-model theorem, valid
  for all adapted inputs and including arbitrary nonlinear-controller
  impossibility for the substitute. General N&S literal synthesis, exact
  optimal levels, singular D1 and general B0 remain unsettled.
- **Original infinite horizon:** not solved here. In particular, stationary
  independent-block arguments require their own positivity, reset/forgetting,
  and controller-memory justification. A finite-horizon separation cannot be
  extrapolated into an infinite-horizon separation.
- **Old mean-square Theorems 1--2:** L2 does not contradict them under F1. It
  proves that their necessity cannot be carried over after deleting F1. The
  L2 impossibility proof uses the same adapted-input information convention as
  F2, and allows preview pre-action; it does not arise from a changed controller
  information set. The old README's Monte Carlo suggestion that the Riccati
  condition was necessarily sufficient for the printed random-input norm was
  unsupported. Jensen compares each output with its own RMS norm, but also
  changes the denominator in the wrong direction for such an inference.
- **N&S benchmark:** L3 is complete only in its explicitly gated scalar class.
  It is not a proof of the constant-matrix general problem.
- **Evidence labels:** analytic proof plus exact arithmetic and independent
  moment-ODE countercheck; not novelty certification or machine-checked proof.
