# A complete strong-stability theorem with an explicit expensive control clock

Date: 2026-10-14 (America/Los_Angeles). Book-level verdict: **PARTIAL**.
Evidence class: classical mathematical derivation / Certification candidate;
not Lean-kernel verified, not a novelty claim. This document does not claim to
close the book's simple, distributed, memoryless-policy requirement.

## 1. Exact target and the deliberately visible gap

The scanned book, printed pp. 731–734 (PDF 752–755), is authoritative.
Printed p. 732 defines strong stability as

\[
 \limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\sum_i\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
       \mathbb E[Q_i(t)]<\infty. \tag{B2}
\]

It also says the scheduler must be “简单的、分布式的、无记忆的策略”.
Printed p. 733 identifies the then-unresolved problem as
“在时变链路速率情况下的基于队列的随机接入算法及其稳定性还未被人们解决。”

The theorem below proves the stronger conclusion
\(\sup_t\mathbb E\sum_iQ_i(t)<\infty\), for every strictly admissible
load and every finite conflict graph, **under synchronous block-fading and an
unbounded predetermined control-clock architecture**. It uses only a link's
own current queue, its current channel, local carrier sensing, and the shared
slot counter. It does not use a central MaxWeight oracle, global queue maximum,
arrival rate, channel transition law, graph size, or a known arrival/channel
bound. It is not time-homogeneous: the common clock accelerates forever.
Consequently this is a scoped full theorem but only a partial answer to the
printed scheduling problem. It is also not a theorem about channels changing
continuously during the control phase, as in Yun–Shin–Yi.

## 2. Model, quantifiers, and controller

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be any finite undirected graph, \(n=|V|\ge1\), and
\(\mathcal I(G)\subset\{0,1\}^n\) its independent-set schedules, including
zero. No restriction is imposed on degree, graph class, or computational
complexity of maximum independent set.

Time consists of unit slots \(t=0,1,\ldots\). At the start of slot \(t\),
\(C(t)\) is a finite-state irreducible Markov chain with transition matrix
\(P\), stationary distribution \(\pi\), and components in \([0,c_*]\),
where \(c_*<\infty\). Periodicity is allowed. It is exogenous and independent
of arrivals and control randomness; its value is constant throughout the slot.
Each transmitter observes only its own component immediately and without error.
Different links' channel components need not be independent.

Arrival vectors \(A(t)\) are iid over slots, independent of channels and
control randomness, with \(0\le A_i(t)\le a_*<\infty\) and mean
\(\lambda_i\). Components of an arrival vector can be dependent. Arrivals are
added at slot end, and the initial queue vector is any deterministic finite
vector. Queue lengths and service are divisible work, not necessarily integer
packets. Dummy control signals may be sent even by an empty queue.

Define the ideal no-control-overhead service region by

\[
 \mathcal C=\left\{\sum_c\pi(c)\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal I(G)}
       \alpha_c(\sigma)(c_i\sigma_i)_{i=1}^n:
       \alpha_c\text{ a probability distribution}\right\}.
\]

**Strict admissibility** means that there exist \(\epsilon>0\) and such
\(\alpha_c\) with \(r_i\ge\lambda_i+\epsilon\) for every \(i\).
This is the usual coordinatewise interior condition, not a graph-dependent
fraction of capacity. The comparison distributions are used only in the proof.

### Controller: synchronized shrinking-window heat-bath access

In slot \(t\), set

\[
 \delta_t=\frac1{t+2},\qquad R_t=\exp((t+1)^2),\qquad
 w_i=Q_i(t)C_i(t),\qquad p_i=\frac{e^{w_i}}{1+e^{w_i}}. \tag{1}
\]

All links reset their reservation bits to zero. During the control interval
\([t,t+\delta_t)\), link \(i\) has an independent Poisson clock of rate
\(R_t\). At each ring it senses all its conflict neighbors. If any neighbor
has reservation bit 1, it sets its bit to 0; otherwise it sets its bit to 1
with probability \(p_i\), and to 0 with probability \(1-p_i\), independently.
The weights are frozen at their slot-start values.

At the common control-window end all links freeze their bits, obtaining
\(\sigma(t)\in\mathcal I(G)\), and transmit at rate \(C_i(t)\sigma_i(t)\)
for the remaining \(1-\delta_t\) units. Potential service is

\[
 S_i(t)=(1-\delta_t)C_i(t)\sigma_i(t),\qquad
 Q_i(t+1)=[Q_i(t)-S_i(t)]^++A_i(t). \tag{2}
\]

**Physical assumptions and costs.** Reservation-bit changes and carrier sensing
are instantaneous and perfect on exactly the conflict edges; continuous
independent clocks have no simultaneous rings almost surely. There are no
hidden terminals, propagation delays, collisions, capture errors, packet-size
minimums, or retransmissions. Control time costs the full \(\delta_t\) fraction
of the slot; it is not declared free. Shared slot boundaries, a common epoch,
and the slot counter are required. Each update is local and uses constant
arithmetic plus carrier sensing, but the *expected number* of control rings is

\[
 nR_t\delta_t=\frac{n\exp((t+1)^2)}{t+2}. \tag{3}
\]

This quantity is finite for each slot, so the process is nonexplosive on every
finite time interval, but grows superexponentially in the slot index. For
example at \(t=10\) it is \(n e^{121}/12\). Arbitrarily fine timing,
unbounded clock rates and real-number probability operations are essential.
No claim of efficient implementation, finite precision robustness, or bounded
hardware rate is made. The controller needs neither \(n,a_*,c_*\) nor a
mixing-time estimate; these enter the mathematical bound, not the code executed
by a link.

## 3. The theorem

**Theorem 1.** For every graph, channel chain, arrival law, finite deterministic
initial queue vector and strictly admissible \(\lambda\) satisfying Section 2,
the controller (1)–(2) obeys

\[
 \sup_{t\ge0}\mathbb E\exp(\theta\|Q(t)\|_2)<\infty
 \quad\text{for some }\theta>0. \tag{4}
\]

In particular \(\sup_t\mathbb E\sum_iQ_i(t)<\infty\), and the exact book
strong-stability condition (B2) follows. This is an existence theorem under the
stated ideal architecture, not an asymptotic simulation inference.

### Lemma 1: stationary distribution of the frozen control process

For fixed \(q,c\), its stationary law is

\[
 \nu_{q,c}(\sigma)=\frac{\exp(\sum_iq_ic_i\sigma_i)}
    {Z(q,c)},\quad
 Z(q,c)=\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal I(G)}\exp(\sum_iq_ic_i\rho_i). \tag{5}
\]

**Proof.** If adding vertex \(i\) is feasible, the forward and reverse rates
are \(R p_i\) and \(R(1-p_i)\); their ratio is \(e^{q_ic_i}\). This is
exactly the probability ratio in (5), so detailed balance holds on every
one-bit edge. Removal paths connect every state to zero and addition paths
connect zero to every independent set. All relevant rates are positive and
finite. The finite chain is irreducible and (5) is its unique stationary law.
Self-rings do not affect this argument. ∎

### Lemma 2: explicit mixing, without a time-scale separation assumption

For arbitrary fixed weights \(w_i\ge0\), all initial states, and duration
\(u\),

\[
 \|\mathcal L(\sigma(u))-\nu_w\|_{\rm TV}
 \le(1-p(w))^{\lfloor Ru\rfloor}
 \le\exp[-p(w)\lfloor Ru\rfloor],\qquad
 p(w)=e^{-n}\prod_i\frac1{1+e^{w_i}}. \tag{6}
\]

**Proof.** Couple all initial states with the same vertex clocks and independent
uniform marks at their rings. A ring uses its mark to set the vertex to zero
whenever the mark exceeds \(p_i\), regardless of the neighbors; otherwise it
sets 1 only if feasible. In an interval of length \(1/R\), the event that
every vertex rings exactly once has probability \(e^{-n}\). Independently,
all these marks force zero with probability \(\prod_i(1-p_i)\). On their
intersection, every coupled state is zero at the interval end, regardless of
the order of rings, since each vertex is updated exactly once. Coupling then
persists forever. The events in disjoint intervals are independent. Couple
one starting state to a chain with initial law \(\nu_w\), and apply the
coupling inequality. Any leftover partial interval cannot separate coupled
chains. This proves (6), with no spectral-gap or adiabatic lemma omitted. ∎

### Lemma 3: a uniform additive MaxWeight loss, including overhead

There is a finite deterministic \(K\), depending on the model and initial
queues but not on \(t\), such that conditionally on the full past and the
slot-start \(q,c\),

\[
 q\cdot\mathbb E[S(t)\mid\mathcal F_t]
 \ge\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal I(G)}\sum_iq_ic_i\sigma_i-K. \tag{7}
\]

Here \(\mathcal F_t\) includes \(Q(t),C(t)\), not this slot's control
randomness or arrivals.

**Proof.** Put \(q_* =\max_iQ_i(0)\) and
\(H_t=nc_*(q_*+a_*t)\). Pathwise \(Q_i(t)\le q_*+a_*t\), so the schedule
weight \(W(\sigma)=\sum_iq_ic_i\sigma_i\) lies in \([0,H_t]\), and
\(\sum_iw_i\le H_t\). Since \(1+e^{w_i}\le2e^{w_i}\), (6) gives the
uniform conditional total-variation bound

\[
 \eta_t=\exp\left[-(2e)^{-n}e^{-H_t}
         \left\lfloor\frac{e^{(t+1)^2}}{t+2}\right\rfloor\right]. \tag{8}
\]

For every probability law (5), its Shannon entropy satisfies
\(\mathsf H(\nu)=\log Z-\mathbb E_\nu W\le\log|\mathcal I(G)|\),
whereas \(\log Z\ge W_{\max}\). Consequently
\(\mathbb E_\nu W\ge W_{\max}-\log|\mathcal I(G)|\). The total-variation
error for a function with range \([0,H_t]\) is at most \(H_t\eta_t\).
Multiplying by the data duration gives

\[
 q\cdot\mathbb E S(t)
 \ge W_{\max}-n\log2-H_t(\eta_t+\delta_t). \tag{9}
\]

Now \(\sup_tH_t\delta_t<\infty\). Also, since \(H_t\) is affine in
\(t\), the logarithm of the positive exponent inside (8) is bounded below by

\[
 (t+1)^2-H_t-n\log(2e)-\log(2(t+2))\longrightarrow+\infty. \tag{10}
\]

For this inequality use \(\lfloor x\rfloor\ge x/2\) for \(x\ge1\);
\(e^{(t+1)^2}/(t+2)\ge1\) for every integer \(t\ge0\). The right side is
a quadratic minus an affine and logarithmic expression. In particular it is
at least \(t^2/2\) for all sufficiently large \(t\), whence
\(H_t\eta_t\le H_t\exp(-e^{t^2/2})\to0\). Thus the explicit constant

\[
 K=n\log2+\sup_{t\in\mathbb Z_+}H_t(\eta_t+\delta_t)<\infty \tag{11}
\]

makes (7) valid, including the finitely many early slots. No runtime procedure
needs to evaluate this supremum. ∎

### Lemma 4: uniform multi-slot quadratic drift for Markov channels

Let \(L(q)=\tfrac12\|q\|_2^2\), \(D=a_*+c_*\), and
\(B=n(a_*^2+c_*^2)/2\). There exist an integer \(T\ge1\) and finite
\(K_T\) such that at every starting time \(t\),

\[
 \mathbb E[L(Q(t+T))-L(Q(t))\mid\mathcal F_t]
 \le K_T-\frac{T\epsilon}{2}\sum_iQ_i(t). \tag{12}
\]

**Proof.** Take comparison distributions witnessing strict admissibility and
write \(s_i(c)=\sum_\sigma\alpha_c(\sigma)c_i\sigma_i\), so
\(r_i=\sum_c\pi(c)s_i(c)\ge\lambda_i+\epsilon\). A finite irreducible
Markov chain admits a real solution of

\[
 h_i-Ph_i=s_i-r_i\mathbf1. \tag{13}
\]

For completeness, the nullspace of \(I-P\) consists of constants: a real
harmonic vector attains its maximum, and the averaging equality propagates
that maximum along every positive-probability transition, hence everywhere by
irreducibility. Thus \(\operatorname{rank}(I-P)=m-1\) for \(m\) channel
states. Its left nullspace is spanned by \(\pi\). The right side of (13)
has zero \(\pi\)-mean and hence lies in the range. This proves solvability
even for periodic chains. Let \(H=\max_i\|h_i\|_\infty<\infty\).
Telescoping (13) yields, conditional on any initial channel state,

\[
 \mathbb E\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}s_i(C(t+k))
 =Tr_i+h_i(C(t))-\mathbb Eh_i(C(t+T))\ge Tr_i-2H. \tag{14}
\]

The scalar queue identity gives
\(Q_i(t+1)^2\le Q_i(t)^2+A_i(t)^2+S_i(t)^2
+2Q_i(t)(A_i(t)-S_i(t))\). Combining conditional independence of arrivals
with (7), and using \(W_{\max}\ge q\cdot s(c)\), gives

\[
 \mathbb E[\Delta L(t)\mid\mathcal F_t]
 \le B+K+\sum_iQ_i(t)(\lambda_i-s_i(C(t))). \tag{15}
\]

Pathwise \(|Q_i(t+k)-Q_i(t)|\le Dk\) and
\(|\lambda_i-s_i(c)|\le D\). Sum (15), take the conditional expectation
at \(t\), replace \(Q_i(t+k)\) with \(Q_i(t)\), and bound the resulting
error by \(nD^2T(T-1)/2\). The exogenous channel property allows (14) even
when conditioning on the queue history. Choose
\(T\ge\max(1,\lceil4H/\epsilon\rceil)\), and set

\[
 K_T=T(B+K)+\frac{nD^2T(T-1)}2.
\]

Then \(T\epsilon-2H\ge T\epsilon/2\), giving (12). ∎

### Lemma 5: the drift implies a uniformly bounded exponential queue moment

This step is included because rate stability, positive recurrence, or a bound
on \(\mathbb E\log\log Q\) does not by itself prove (B2).

Set \(\beta=T\epsilon/2>0\), \(d=\sqrt nDT\), and
\(r_0=\max(1,2K_T/\beta)\). Strict admissibility ensures \(c_*>0\), so
\(d>0\). Let \(X=\|Q(t)\|_2\), \(X'=\|Q(t+T)\|_2\). The elementary
identity
\(2X(X'-X)\le (X')^2-X^2\), together with (12), implies for \(X>0\)

\[
 \mathbb E[X'-X\mid\mathcal F_t]
 \le\frac{K_T}{X}-\beta\frac{\|Q(t)\|_1}{X}
 \le\frac{K_T}{X}-\beta. \tag{16}
\]

Thus for \(X\ge r_0\), the conditional drift is at most \(-\beta/2\).
Also \(|X'-X|\le d\) pathwise. Choose

\[
 0<\theta\le\min\left\{\frac1d,\frac{\beta}{2e d^2},
                         \frac1\beta\right\},\qquad
 \rho=1-\frac{\theta\beta}4\in(0,1). \tag{17}
\]

For \(|y|\le d\), Taylor's theorem gives
\(e^{\theta y}\le1+\theta y+\theta^2d^2e^{\theta d}/2\).
Hence outside \(X<r_0\),
\(\mathbb E[e^{\theta(X'-X)}\mid\mathcal F_t]\le\rho\).
Inside that set, \(e^{\theta X'}\le b:=e^{\theta(r_0+d)}\).
Therefore, uniformly in time and the channel,

\[
 \mathbb E[e^{\theta X'}\mid\mathcal F_t]
 \le \rho e^{\theta X}+b. \tag{18}
\]

Iteration on the deterministic grid \(0,T,2T,\ldots\) yields

\[
 \sup_{m\ge0}\mathbb E e^{\theta\|Q(mT)\|_2}
 \le e^{\theta\|Q(0)\|_2}+\frac b{1-\rho}=:M<\infty.
\]

Between grid points \(\|Q(mT+k)\|_2\le\|Q(mT)\|_2+d\), so
\(\sup_t\mathbb E e^{\theta\|Q(t)\|_2}\le e^{\theta d}M\).
Jensen's inequality and \(\|q\|_1\le\sqrt n\|q\|_2\) give the explicit
finite upper bound

\[
 \sup_t\mathbb E\sum_iQ_i(t)
 \le\sqrt n\left(d+\frac{\log M}{\theta}\right). \tag{19}
\]

This proves Theorem 1, without asserting a stationary distribution for a
time-inhomogeneous process. ∎

## 4. Why this result is meaningful, and why it is not the book solution

The result closes, in this explicit expensive architecture, every bridge from
local random bits to finite expected backlog: implementable local rates,
finite-time mixing from every initial reservation state, the channel-dependent
Gibbs weight, paid control overhead, arbitrary Markov correlation, and a
uniform exponential-moment Foster argument. It separates a genuine issue
(the attainable control speed and information architecture) from an avoidable
proof gap. It does not assume instantaneous Gibbs sampling or hide a maximum
independent-set oracle. All graphs and all strict interior loads are included.

However, the predetermined common counter is external nonlocal timing
information; the rate schedule is time-inhomogeneous; control work per slot is
(3); the fading is frozen within a slot. These are substantial additional
assumptions. “Simple local update” is not “simple low-cost scheduler”. The
book's memoryless language does not explicitly authorize this architecture.
No full book resolution, efficiency theorem, or novelty claim is warranted.

For an autonomous local-queue/local-channel A-CSMA controller in continuously
changing finite-state Markov channels, what is still missing here is a service
estimate strong enough for a *uniform* moment drift, not just pathwise
throughput. One sufficient missing estimate would be, for some fixed finite
\(T\), constants \(K_T<\infty,\beta>0\), and every admissible joint state,

\[
 \mathbb E[L(Q(t+T))-L(Q(t))\mid\mathcal F_t]
 \le K_T-\beta\|Q(t)\|_1. \tag{20}
\]

To derive it by this proof one needs an actual control/channel tracking bound
with additive weighted-service error, uniform in the initial schedule and
queues. The frozen product law (5) is not the invariant law of a joint process
whose channel keeps changing. Log-log queue weights also do not automatically
supply the linear-queue negative drift in (20). The existing rate-stability
statement is not an estimate of (20). Different Lyapunov functions or
state-dependent sampling could work, but require their own moment argument.
This is the precise mathematical/information-architecture blocker; neither
more small-graph simulation nor a missing citation alone closes it.

## 5. Two exact negative controls and an assumption-gap example

### 5.1 Continuous channel variation invalidates instantaneous product form

Take a single conflict edge, channel states \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\), switching
symmetrically at rate 1. Use rate-1 heat-bath clocks and activity 9 at the good
link, 1 at the bad link. The joint chain has six states. If \(a,b,d\) denote
the probability of an empty, good-active, or bad-active schedule in one given
channel state, symmetry and stationarity give

\[
 -\tfrac75a+\tfrac1{10}b+\tfrac12d=0,\quad
 \tfrac9{10}a-\tfrac{11}{10}b+d=0,\quad
 \tfrac12a+b-\tfrac32d=0,\quad a+b+d=\tfrac12.
\]

The unique solution is \((a,b,d)=(13,37,29)/158\). Actual total useful service
is therefore \(37/79\), not the instantaneous-Gibbs prediction \(9/11\).
The exact rational checker reconstructs the generator and stationarity,
rejects the product candidate, and repeats with faster clocks. This is not an
impossibility theorem for adaptive CSMA; it is a falsification of a specific
invalid stationary-law shortcut.

### 5.2 A fixed positive control fraction loses capacity

For one always-good unit-capacity link and \(\delta=1/4\), service is at most
\(3/4\) per slot under any policy using that architecture. Constant arrivals
\(7/8\) are strictly below the ideal capacity 1 but force at least \(t/8\)
backlog growth. This explains why the shrinking window is load-bearing. It is
an architecture-limited impossibility, not a universal distributed-policy
impossibility.

### 5.3 Printed long-run means alone are insufficient—even with bounded arrivals

This is **only an assumption gap**, not a solution of the intended question.
For one link of constant service 1, run deterministic cycles \(k=1,2,\ldots\):
arrivals 2 for \(k\) slots, then 0 for \(3k\) slots. The long-run arrival
mean exists and is \(1/2\): complete cycles have exactly that mean, and the
current cycle has length \(O(k)\) versus elapsed time \(\Theta(k^2)\).
All arrival moments are bounded, so this is not a heavy-tail trick.

Even the work-conserving central scheduler is not strongly stable. Its queue
is zero at each cycle start. Over cycle \(k\), the sum of pre-slot backlogs is
\(k^2+2k\). Through cycle \(K\), time is \(2K(K+1)\) and the exact
average backlog is

\[
 \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K(k^2+2k)}{2K(K+1)}=\frac{2K+7}{12}\to\infty.
\]

The maximum queue in cycle \(k\) is \(k+1\), so \(Q(t)/t\to0\).
Thus bounded increments plus rate stability still do not imply the book's
condition. The failure is temporal regularity, not decentralized information.
Theorem 1 explicitly assumes iid arrivals and does not purport to repair this
unrestricted-arrival formulation.

“有别于确定性调度算法，随机接入方式是另一类可以分布式逼近问题（3）的解的一类算法。最近，人们发现节点接入概率可以基于局部队列信息自适应调整，同时达到镇定队列、降低冲突的目的[5]。这类算法，网络节点接入概率取决于局部队列长度，而节点接入概率影响链路服务速率，从而影响队列动态演变，这种相互作用致使其成为一个关联系统，其网络镇定问题归结为寻找合适的Lyapunov函数刻画该关联系统的稳定性。目前，结果大多集中在假设式（1）中的μ（链路物理带宽，受信道状态影响）为常值。在时变链路速率情况下的基于队列的随机接入算法及其稳定性还未被人们解决。”
Translation: Unlike deterministic scheduling, random access can approximate (3) in a distributed manner. Access probabilities can adapt to local queue information to stabilize queues and reduce collisions. The feedback between access probabilities, service rates, and queues yields an interconnected system, whose stabilization requires an appropriate Lyapunov function. Most existing results assume that μ in (1), the physical link bandwidth affected by channel state, is constant. Queue-based random access algorithms and their stability under time-varying link rates have not yet been resolved.

The subsequent future directions, distinct from the main target, ask for (1) fast or finite-time convergence; (2) distributed scheduling with delayed/inaccurate channel information, contrasting it with currently known instantaneous CSI; (3) retransmissions, real-time behavior and reliability.

Source-model limits: these four pages specify expected long-run arrival/service means and the strong-stability formula, but no iid assumption, no finite arrival bound, no finite-state Markov law, no sensing latency, no control clock bound, and no within-slot fading law. Those assumptions must therefore be declared additions, not attributed to the book. The faithful local-channel idealization is motivated by the book's explicit contrast with delayed/imperfect CSI, not a claim that actual radios provide exact CSI.
