PROVED (C). Nonuniform reweighting cannot rescue an FD-consistent subgroup code. More precisely, let K be a subgroup of a finite product A_1 × ... × A_10, with the graph FDs holding on K. Every distribution supported on any coset of K satisfies Phi >= 0. This includes arbitrary subsets and reweightings of linear or abelian-group codes.

PROVED (entropy minimization lemma). Without assuming the FDs, write pi_v for coordinate projection. Then

  min_{p supported on K} Phi(p)
    = min_{J <= K} [2 sum_v log|pi_v(J)| - 3 log|J|].                 (1)

Here every J on the right is a subgroup.

To prove this, choose a minimizing distribution p having maximum joint entropy among all minimizers. Translate it so that the identity belongs to its support. Let Y,Y' be independent with law p, and put T = YY', multiplying coordinatewise. Chain rules give the exact identity

  2 Phi(Y)
    = Phi(T) + E_t Phi(Y' | T=t)
      + 2 sum_v I(Y'_v ; T_{-v} | T_v).                            (2)

Indeed, both (Y,Y') -> (T,Y') and their coordinate versions are bijections.

If the minimum is m, every entropy functional on the right of (2) is at least m. Consequently Phi(T)=m. Also

  H(T) >= H(T | Y') = H(Y).

Maximum entropy among minimizers forces equality. Thus T and Y' are independent. Since the identity has positive probability under p, every right translate of p by an element of its support equals p. Therefore p is uniform on the subgroup generated by its support. This proves (1).

PROVED (uniform subgroup bound). Every subgroup J <= K inherits the graph FDs. Under its uniform law, each coordinate has a normal kernel N_v in J.

Condition on Z from G2 and translate the resulting coset to

  L = intersection_{z in Z} N_z.

For U=3 and V=10, put A=L intersect N_3 and B=L intersect N_10. These are normal subgroups of L, so AB is a subgroup. The coset variable W modulo AB is a function of either U or V, and

  H(W) = log(|L|/|AB|)
       = log(|L| |A intersect B| / (|A||B|))
       = I(U;V),

with all quantities evaluated in the fixed conditional slice.

The required Ingleton inequality follows directly from this common information. For any W determined by both U and V,

  I(U;V|P) >= H(W|P),
  I(U;V|Q) >= H(W|Q),

and

  H(W|P) + H(W|Q) + I(P;Q) - H(W)
    = I(P;Q|W) + H(W|P,Q) >= 0.

Taking P=4 and Q=8 proves Ing >= 0 in every slice. G2 therefore gives Phi >= 0 for the uniform law on every J. Equation (1) proves the stated obstruction for every reweighting. Coordinatewise translation handles cosets.

PROVED (a second obstruction: correlated proper gadgets). Suppose vertex symbols are tuples

  X_v = (Y_v^1,...,Y_v^r),

and, for each a, the projected component code on Y^a satisfies the graph FDs and has a constant coordinate. Allow arbitrary correlations between components and arbitrary restrictions of their product support.

Let Y^{<a} denote all earlier components at all vertices. Chain rules give

  Phi(X)
    = sum_a E[Phi(Y^a | Y^{<a})]
      + 2 sum_{a,v}
          I(Y_v^a ; Y_{-v}^{<a} | Y_v^{<a})
    >= 0.                                                        (3)

Every conditional component law still satisfies the FDs and has a constant coordinate, so its Phi is nonnegative by G4. Thus even arbitrary correlated reweightings of products of proper-vertex gadgets cannot beat the target.

PROVED (exact computer checks).

1. For a code C with group-valued columns, choose c in C and enumerate the subgroup K generated by c^{-1}C. Check, for every v,

     kernel(pi_{N(v)} restricted to K)
       <= kernel(pi_v restricted to K).

   If all ten inclusions hold, every distribution on C is excluded by the first obstruction.

2. Over a finite field, form a row-basis matrix M for span(C-c). The preceding test becomes ten exact rank equalities:

     rank M_{N(v) union {v}} = rank M_{N(v)}.

   Consequently any counterexample over a finite field must lose at least one graph FD upon taking its affine span.

3. For the gadget obstruction, verify that each vertex symbol is recoverable from its proposed tuple representation. Enumerate each component’s projected support, check its ten FDs, and check that at least one coordinate is constant. These finite checks certify (3) for every weighting.

PROVED (scope). These obstructions leave general nonlinear codes and general coset constructions with nonnormal coordinate subgroups undecided. They establish C, without resolving A or supplying B.
