# Exact scalar benchmarks for the printed stochastic norm

Date: 2026-10-14 (America/Los_Angeles). Status: candidate analytic certification, pending independent hostile review. These results concern **deterministic disturbances**, not the all-adapted disturbance class. No novelty or full book-problem resolution is claimed.

## Contract

Consider constant coefficients

\[
dx(t)=(a x(t)+w(t))dt+\sigma x(t)dW(t),\qquad x(0)=0,\quad z=x.
\]

For horizon T, let

\[
g_1(T)=\sup_{0\ne w\in L^2(0,T)\text{ deterministic}}
 \frac{E\|x_w\|_{L^2(0,T)}}{\|w\|_{L^2(0,T)}},\qquad
g_2(T)=\sup_w\frac{(E\|x_w\|_{L^2(0,T)}^2)^{1/2}}{\|w\|_{L^2(0,T)}}.
\]

The printed book criterion uses g1, while its mean-square replacement uses g2. To include a regular dummy control, set B=B0=0, D0=(1,0)^T, D1=(0,1)^T and z=(x,u). Then D1^T D1=1 and u=0 minimizes both output norms pathwise; no admissible controller can improve these gains. Thus this is a genuine constant-matrix subclass, not a switched/time-gated plant. With preview h<T, the deterministic-input subclass reduces by time shift to active horizon T-h. On the infinite horizon a fixed finite delay changes neither gain.

## Theorem F: strict finite-horizon norm separation

For every finite T>0, every real a and every nonzero sigma,

\[
0<g_1(T)<g_2(T)<\infty.
\]

**Proof.** Write

\[
(\mathcal T w)(t)=\int_0^t\Phi(t,s)w(s)ds,\quad
\Phi(t,s)=\exp((a-\sigma^2/2)(t-s)+\sigma(W_t-W_s)).
\]

The random kernel lies in L2(Omega x {(s,t):0<s<t<T}), since E Phi(t,s)^2=exp((2a+sigma^2)(t-s)). Therefore the operator from deterministic L2(0,T) into the Hilbert space L2(Omega;L2(0,T)) is Hilbert-Schmidt and compact. The functional f(v)=E||v||L2 is Lipschitz in that Hilbert norm. A maximizing input sequence in the unit ball has a weakly convergent subsequence, whose compact images converge strongly. Consequently the g1 maximum is attained. It is positive, so its maximizer has norm one. Positivity of Phi implies |T w|<=T|w| pathwise, so a nonnegative maximizer w* can be chosen.

For nonzero nonnegative w*, the random variable Y=||T w*||L2 is nonconstant. Indeed the map from a continuous Brownian path to Y is continuous in the uniform topology, and along the Cameron-Martin paths W_t=k t its kernel is exp((a-sigma^2/2+sigma k)(t-s)). Increasing sigma k strictly increases the output on a set of positive time measure, because w* is nonnegative and nonzero. Two such paths have different Y values. Wiener measure has positive mass in every uniform neighborhood of these paths, so Y is not almost surely constant. Since E Y^2<infinity, strict Cauchy-Schwarz gives E Y<(E Y^2)^(1/2). At the g1 maximizer this yields g1<||T w*||L2(Omega;L2)<=g2. Finiteness follows from the Hilbert-Schmidt bound. QED.

This proves that some gamma strictly between the two thresholds gives opposite solvability verdicts for this deterministic-disturbance interpretation. It does **not** establish opposite verdicts when disturbances may be adapted random processes: that larger class can change both gains.

### Independently computable mean-square operator

Let c=2a+sigma^2. Direct Gaussian increments give, for s<=r<=t,

\[
E[\Phi(t,s)\Phi(t,r)]=e^{a(r-s)+c(t-r)}.
\]

Hence g2(T)^2 is the largest eigenvalue of the positive compact integral operator with symmetric kernel

\[
K_T(s,r)=e^{a|s-r|}\int_{\max(s,r)}^T e^{c(t-\max(s,r))}dt.
\]

The independent moment equations are m'=a m+w, p'=c p+2w m, with zero initial data and output energy integral of p. Numerical agreement of these representations is evidence for the implementation, not a proof of Theorem F or an exact numerical value of g1.

## Theorem I: exact infinite-horizon deterministic-input gain

Write g1(infinity),g2(infinity) for the same deterministic-input gains over [0,infinity). If c=2a+sigma^2<0, then

\[
g_1(\infty)=g_2(\infty)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{a(2a+\sigma^2)}}.
\]

If c>=0, both gains are infinite. This includes the sigma=0 specialization, for which the finite-horizon strict-separation theorem does not apply.

**Proof, upper bound for c<0.** Necessarily a<0. For compactly supported deterministic w, the moment equations and decay after the input stops yield

\[
E\int_0^\infty x^2dt=-\frac2c\int_0^\infty w(t)m(t)dt
 \le -\frac2c\frac1{-a}\|w\|_2^2,
\quad m(t)=\int_0^t e^{a(t-s)}w(s)ds.
\]

The inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz and the L2 convolution bound ||m||2<=||w||2/(-a). The bound extends to all deterministic L2 inputs by density and continuity of the stochastic solution map. Jensen gives g1<=g2<=sqrt(2/(a c)).

**Lower bound, including instability.** For fixed L>0, apply w=1 on [0,N L] and zero afterward. On each of the N consecutive blocks, let x_j^0 be the zero-start response to unit input driven only by that block's Brownian increments, and Y_j=int_0^L (x_j^0)^2dt. The Y_j are independent and identically distributed, with finite expectation for each finite L. The actual state is nonnegative and is at least x_j^0 throughout block j, because both the carried state and Phi are nonnegative. Thus

\[
\frac{E\|x\|_{L^2(0,\infty)}}{\sqrt{NL}}
 \ge E\sqrt{\frac1{NL}\sum_{j=1}^N Y_j}.
\]

The strong law and Fatou imply that the liminf of the right-hand side is at least sqrt(E Y_1/L). This does not require any unproved exchange of expectation and limit. For unit input, m(t)=(exp(a t)-1)/a when a!=0 and m(t)=t when a=0. The equation p'=c p+2m has p>=0. When c<0, p(t) tends to 2/(a c), so E Y_1/L tends to 2/(a c) as L tends to infinity. When c>=0, E Y_1/L tends to infinity: for a<0, m tends to a positive constant, giving linear growth at c=0 and exponential growth at c>0; for a>=0, m grows at least linearly and c>=0, so the same divergence follows. The bounds establish the theorem. QED.

## Scope and unresolved obligations

- Theorems F and I preserve the book's printed norm and constant matrices, but restrict the exogenous disturbance to deterministic inputs and use an uncontrolled scalar channel with a regular dummy control.
- Finite-horizon g1 is characterized here only by strict inequalities, not an explicit sharp number or a synthesis formula for general plants.
- The infinite-horizon exact formula is a solved subclass, not the general preview-control problem. Adapted random inputs, nontrivial optimal controllers, and arbitrary-dimensional synthesis remain unclosed.
- Expected squared-norm bounds do not by Jensen alone imply E||z||<=gamma E||w|| when w is random; the denominator changes too.
- Computational checks below are numerical identity and boundary checks. They do not replace the Wiener-support, compactness, or block-repetition proofs.
