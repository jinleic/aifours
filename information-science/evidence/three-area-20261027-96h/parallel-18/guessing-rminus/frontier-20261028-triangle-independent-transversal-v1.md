# Independent transversals constrain triangle-syndrome slices

**Status: WRITTEN plus exact finite graph checks.** This strengthens the restricted strategy analysis in `frontier-20261028-triangle-two-coset-obstruction-v1.md` (SHA-256 `da26dfb13dfe38bdcc5c07a3d33a90a5b7adba77eebbcb6d36c2141ce4e032b7`). It gives no new unrestricted guessing-number bound and does not rule out an entropy-ratio improvement on a smaller support.

## The theorem

Use a triangle multiset in any simple graph, separate `A`-coordinates for every vertex/triangle occurrence, and the triangle-sum map `pi` over any finite abelian group `A`; write `C=ker(pi)`. Suppose `I` is an independent vertex set and `J` is any set of triangle occurrences each meeting `I`. Let `H` be any subgroup of syndrome vectors supported on `J`. Then any functional-dependence support contained in one affine syndrome slice `pi^{-1}(a+H)` has size at most `|C|`.

For each `t` in `J`, choose a vertex of `I` in that triangle; there is exactly one because `I` is independent. Define `d(z)` for `z` supported on `J` by placing the syndrome entry `z[t]` at the chosen vertex's coordinate for triangle `t` and zero in all other coordinates. This is a homomorphism, `pi(d(z))=z`, and every nonzero `d(z)` has nonzero symbols only at vertices of the independent set `I`. Therefore every two distinct words in `D={d(z):z in H}` have a forbidden difference: any nonzero vertex in `I` is isolated within its support. Thus `D` is a confusability clique and `pi` maps it bijectively to `H`.

The slice is the disjoint union of the `|C|` translates of `D` indexed by `c` in `C`, after a fixed shift: each word has a unique expression `shift+c+d(z)`. A valid functional-dependence support contains at most one member of each translate, giving the stated bound. This argument does not require the full graph to be `K4`-free.

## R-minus consequences

For the fixed ten-triangle, three-occurrences-per-vertex cover in the preceding packet, `|C|=s^20` and `q=s^3`, where `s=|A|`. Number triangles in the following order:

```
0:123   1:145   2:167   3:239   4:2,7,10
5:389   6:4,5,10   7:4,8,10   8:569   9:678
```

Each independent triple in the table meets exactly the other nine triangles. Hence **any** valid support whose syndrome at the listed omitted triangle is a fixed group element has at most `s^20` words, even if it uses arbitrarily many cosets of `C` and is otherwise nonlinear:

| Omitted syndrome index | Independent triple |
|---:|:---|
| 0 | 4,7,9 |
| 1 | 3,6,10 |
| 2 | 2,5,8 |
| 5 | 2,4,6 |
| 7 | 3,5,7 |
| 9 | 1,9,10 |

In particular, a `q=8` support beating `2^20` words must take both binary values in **each** of these six syndrome coordinates. This necessary condition is not sufficient, nor does it address a smaller support with a better nonuniform entropy ratio. The four unlisted triangle coordinates are not covered by this particular nine-triangle-transversal claim.

Every pair of triangles in the chosen cover also has an independent transversal of one or two vertices. Thus, for any pair of triangle coordinates and any fixed values of the other eight, **all** `s^2` cosets in that slice together support at most `s^20` winning words. For binary `A`, this settles the entire four-coset search generated by any two unit syndrome vectors, independently of which subcode one quotients by.
