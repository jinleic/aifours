## 1. What inference is being tested

The legitimate iterated-copy family (Addendum 4) gives

`Delta <= (1+1/n)a+(n+1)d/2`

for every positive integer n, with a=I(A;B|C), d=I(A;C|B)+I(B;C|A), and Delta=I(A;B)-I(A;B|D)-I(C;D).

It is invalid to send n to infinity while discarding the d term unless a sufficient bound on that SAME original-law d has been proved. When d>0, its coefficient grows rather than disappears. The conditional d=0 conclusion is not unrestricted Ingleton.

## 2. Universal elementary four-variable family

For EVERY r,s in (0,1), let U,V be independent Boolean variables with probabilities P(U=1)=r and P(V=1)=s. Define, on their full Boolean domains,

`A=U AND V`, `B=U OR V`, `C=U`, `D=V`.

Every joint assignment of U,V has positive probability by the open-domain assumptions. No probability or entropy value is numerically evaluated.

### 2.1 Vanishing conditional terms

Given C=U:
- if U=0, A is constant;
- if U=1, B is constant.

Thus A and B are conditionally independent given C and I(A;B|C)=0. The identical argument conditioning on D=V gives I(A;B|D)=0. Independence of U,V gives I(C;D)=0.

### 2.2 Strictly positive mutual information

The event (A=1,B=0) is impossible, because AND=1 forces OR=1. But P(A=1)=rs>0 and P(B=0)=(1-r)(1-s)>0. Therefore A and B are not independent.

For finite variables, mutual information is relative entropy against the product of the marginals; equality to zero is equivalent to independence. Hence I(A;B)>0 for EVERY r,s in the stated open domain.

It follows that the right side of the proposed unrestricted Ingleton inequality is zero and the left side is positive throughout this family. This proof is logical and all-variable; it contains no selected-input arithmetic checks.

At degenerate endpoint probabilities the strict-positivity argument is not asserted. No endpoint was evaluated, and no continuity extrapolation is needed for the open-domain theorem.
