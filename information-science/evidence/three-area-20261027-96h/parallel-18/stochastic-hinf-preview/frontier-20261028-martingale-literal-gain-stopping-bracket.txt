1. Physical model and stopping constants

For any finite T>h>=0,S=T-h, use the ACTUAL original scalar law

 dx_t=w_t dW_t,       z_t=x_t,      x0=0,

realized by u=w in the original deterministic preview class. Inputs w are progressive on the actual Brownian filtration, supported in [0,S]; define gamma1,gamma2 using the SAME literal expected-path ratio E||x||_L2[0,T]/E||w||_L2[0,S], with w respectively E1 or E2.

Define two dimensionless Brownian constants, neither claimed here to be explicitly optimized:

 C_term = sup_(bounded natural-Brownian stopping tau, E sqrt(tau)>0)
                    E|B_tau| / E sqrt(tau),
 C_max  = sup_(any Brownian filtered space and finite stopping tau,
                    0<E sqrt(tau)<infinity)
                    E[sup_(0<=s<=tau)|B_s|] / E sqrt(tau).

The upper constant deliberately permits enlarged Brownian filtrations, as required for an arbitrary input's time-changed filtration. Standard Brownian extension after a finite stopped quadratic-variation clock is allowed for this representation; it does not enlarge the original admissible controller class.

3. Lower bound from EVERY bounded stopping time

Let tau<=N be any natural-Brownian stopping time and choose any 0<delta<S. Define the accelerated Brownian `B_s=sqrt(N/delta) W_(delta s/N)` for 0<=s<=N, then let the disturbance be sqrt(N/delta) until the corresponding stopping time and zero thereafter. The stopped input is progressive and E2 with ||w||²=tau, and the actual state is B_(tau wedge Nt/delta) during [0,delta] and B_tau afterward. Consequently

 E||x||/E||w|| >= sqrt(T-delta) E|B_tau|/E sqrt(tau).

Let delta downarrow 0 and take the supremum over every bounded natural-Brownian stop, proving the first inequality of (A). The explicit first-exit family in the pinned source gives

4. Upper bound from the actual martingale clock

For any progressive E1 input, x is a continuous local martingale with quadratic variation

 A_t=integral_0^t |w_s|²ds,  tau=A_S,  E sqrt(tau)=E||w||<infinity.

The Dambis–Dubins–Schwarz theorem represents x_t=B_(A_t) in a Brownian filtration adapted to the inverse clock, with a standard extension if necessary. The terminal clock tau is a stopping time in that filtration. Since A is continuous and x is constant after S,

 ||x||_L2[0,T] <=sqrt(T) sup_(0<=t<=S)|x_t|
                 =sqrt(T) sup_(0<=s<=tau)|B_s|.

Taking expectation and using the universal definition of C_max gives gamma1<=sqrt(T) C_max. The named L1 martingale inequality gives C_max<=8, without replacing E sqrt(tau) by sqrt(E tau). Inclusion E2 subset E1 proves gamma2<=gamma1.
