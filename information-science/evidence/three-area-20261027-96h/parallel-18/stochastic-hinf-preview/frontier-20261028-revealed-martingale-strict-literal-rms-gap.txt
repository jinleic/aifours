1. Exact physical law and strict bounds

Let T>h>=S=T-h>0, scalar W, zero x0 and the actual original pure-delayed law

 dx_t=w_(t-h)dW_t,     z_t=x_t,

realized by u=w_(t-h),B0=D0=1 and all other plant/control taps zero. Every progressive E1 input is supported on [0,S]. The state is zero before h; the entire input path is F_h-measurable and future noise is independent. Let gamma_lit be the actual original E||z||/E||w|| supremum, and separately define gamma_RMS using sqrt(E||z||²/E||w||²) on E2 inputs.

Put

 c=sqrt(2/pi),      kappa=(c+sqrt(2-c²))/2 <1.

Then

 c sqrt(S) <= gamma_lit <= kappa sqrt(S) < sqrt(S)=gamma_RMS. (1)

Thus the true literal gain is STRICTLY smaller than the RMS gain in this actual output-visible stochastic example. Both endpoints of the displayed literal bracket are bounds, not asserted exact gains. E1 and E2 literal suprema agree by the named revelation theorem, but no pointwise strictness claim at gamma_lit follows.

2. Deterministic-path upper via concentration and Jensen

By the named revelation theorem, gamma_lit is the supremum of E||x(v)||/||v|| over deterministic L2 paths v; this is an EXACT reduction of the full progressive E1 class under h>=S. Take ||v||=1 and define the nonnegative energy-location parameter

 m=(1/S) integral_0^S s |v(s)|²ds in [0,1].

For x_t=integral_0^t v(s)dWtilde_s, Itô isometry yields

 E||x||_L2[0,S]²=S(1-m).

Jensen gives the first bound E||x||<=sqrt(S)sqrt(1-m).

Also X=x_S is standard normal, and

 E||x-X||_L2[0,S]²
  =integral_0^S E|integral_t^S v(s)dWtilde_s|²dt
  =integral_0^S s |v(s)|²ds=S m.

Minkowski and Jensen therefore give the second bound

 E||x||<=sqrt(S) E|X|+sqrt(Sm)
        =sqrt(S)[c+sqrt(m)].

For every m in [0,1], the first bound decreases and the second increases; their minimum is at most their unique crossing. Solving sqrt(1-m)=c+sqrt(m) gives

 sqrt(m)=[sqrt(2-c²)-c]/2,
 crossing=(c+sqrt(2-c²))/2=kappa<1.

Thus every deterministic input ratio is <=kappa sqrt(S), and the exact revelation/energy-weighting formula extends this to every progressive E1 input without substituting an RMS denominator.

3. Lower bound and exact RMS comparison

For a deterministic unit L2 pulse concentrated on [0,epsilon], the post-pulse state is standard normal and remains constant. Its literal ratio is at least c sqrt(S-epsilon); epsilon->0 gives gamma_lit>=c sqrt(S).

For E2 inputs, the entire w path is already known at h and independent of the future Brownian motion. Conditional Itô isometry gives

 E||z||²=E integral_0^S (S-s)|w_s|²ds <=S E||w||².

The same deterministic concentrated pulses approach equality, so gamma_RMS=sqrt(S). This is an exact SECOND-MOMENT comparison only; it is not used as the actual H-infinity criterion.
