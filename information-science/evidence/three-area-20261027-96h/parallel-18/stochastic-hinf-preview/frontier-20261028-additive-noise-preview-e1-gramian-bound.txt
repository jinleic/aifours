1. Full preview law and deterministic Gramian bound

Use the original finite-horizon plant, one Brownian W, arbitrary matrices, progressive vector E1 w supported on [0,S], and the full deterministic linear law

 dx=(Ax+Bu+Cw_(t-h))dt+(A0x+B0u)dW,
 z=D0x+D1u,
 u=Kx+L0w_t+Lh w_(t-h)+integral_0^h H(r)w_(t-r)dr,

T>h>=0, S=T-h, x0=0 and H in L2[0,h]. Suppose ONLY that the selected state-feedback cancels state-multiplicative noise:

 Q=A0+B0K=0.                                               (R)

B0L0, B0Lh, B0H and all D1 taps may be nonzero. Set P=A+BK,O=D0+D1K and define a_d,a_s,a_o exactly as in the named source (drift, additive diffusion and direct-output time-operator norm bounds). Define the deterministic finite-horizon positive semidefinite Gramian

 V(v)=integral_0^v exp(P^T t) O^T O exp(Pt)dt,
 kappa_d=(integral_0^T lambda_max[V(v)]dv)^(1/2),
 kappa_s=(lambda_max[V(T)])^(1/2).

Then for every actual progressive E1 input,

 E||z||_L2[0,T] <= (a_o+kappa_d a_d+8 kappa_s a_s)
                      E||w||_L2[0,S].                      (A)

The expression is finite, and gamma strictly above it is a sufficient original literal H-infinity threshold for THIS given full preview law. It is not asserted sharp or strictly feasible at equality, and does not imply any unrestricted controller synthesis or actual-gain endpoint.

2. Deterministic drift and direct-output pieces

As in the named source write the actual closed law

 dx=(P x+f)dt+g dW,   z=O x+o,

where f,g,o are the actual current/delayed/distributed time operators of w and pathwise ||f||_2<=a_d||w||_2, ||g||_2<=a_s||w||_2, ||o||_2<=a_o||w||_2. The deterministic drift part gives the pathwise H_out=L2([0,T];R^p) output

 z_d(t)=integral_0^t O exp[P(t-s)]f_s ds.

For each s let K_s:R^n -> H_out be `(K_s v)(t)=1_(t>=s)O exp[P(t-s)]v`. Its EXACT operator norm squared is lambda_max[V(T-s)]. Hence Bochner–Minkowski and time Cauchy–Schwarz give

 ||z_d||_H_out <= integral_0^T ||K_s||||f_s||ds
                 <= kappa_d ||f||_2.                       (1)

This is pathwise, includes arbitrary signs and vector directions, and does not require an E2 disturbance.

3. Additive stochastic convolution in output-path Hilbert space

The actual noise contribution is

 z_s(t)=integral_0^t O exp[P(t-s)]g_s dW_s.

View it as the terminal value of the H_out-valued martingale

 M_t=integral_0^t K_s g_s dW_s,   M_T=z_s in H_out.       (2)

Stochastic Fubini proves (2) for bounded-bracket localizations and passage to the locally square-integrable limit. The named source's stopping/Itô-isometry argument with constant 8 applies in the separable Hilbert space H_out as well: its proof uses only the Hilbert norm, continuous martingale quadratic variation and Itô isometry (or finite-rank projections and a limit). Therefore

 E||z_s||_H_out <= E sup_(t<=T)||M_t||_H_out
                <= 8 E [integral_0^T ||K_s g_s||_H_out² ds]^(1/2)
                <= 8 kappa_s E||g||_2.                    (3)

The last step uses V(T-s)<=V(T) in positive-semidefinite order. Combining (1), (3), and E||o||<=a_o E||w|| gives (A). The square root stays INSIDE the expectation throughout; no sqrt(E||w||²) enters. Finite-horizon localization covers E1-only g.
