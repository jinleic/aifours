FRONTIER INFO-3: EXPLICIT FINITE E1 GAIN FOR THE FULL DETERMINISTIC LINEAR PREVIEW CLASS

Status: NEW WRITTEN CANDIDATE, UNREVIEWED AND UNADOPTED. Native prewrite observation Wed Oct 28 18:25:42 PDT 2026. This is an independent all-variable gain-finiteness/sufficient-threshold result for the actual original EXPECTED-PATH-L2 criterion. It removes B0L=D1L=0 and the static-tap restrictions of the earlier construction. It does not determine the sharp gain, strictness at that gain, controller synthesis equivalence, infinite horizon or novelty.

1. Full original plant and preview law

Let T>h>=0, S=T-h. Take arbitrary finite real matrices and the actual original SDE

 dx=(Ax+Bu+Cw_(t-h))dt+(A0x+B0u)dW,
 z=D0x+D1u,                      x0=0,

one Brownian W, vector w and u of their natural dimensions, canonical filtration, and w=0 outside [0,S]. Let w be ANY progressive E1 disturbance, E||w||_L2[0,S]<infinity; no E2 moment is assumed. The deterministic linear preview law is

 u_t=Kx_t+L0 w_t+Lh w_(t-h)+integral_0^h H(r)w_(t-r)dr,

where H is square integrable on [0,h] and all fixed matrices are arbitrary. At h=0 the integral is zero; the estimate does not require combining the coincident taps. All controls are actual progressive locally square-integrable processes.

We derive a finite EXPLICIT deterministic C_up such that

 E||z||_L2[0,T] <= C_up E||w||_L2[0,S].                   (A)

This proves the actual fixed-law literal gain is finite, including an E1-only input, and every gamma>C_up is uniformly strictly feasible. C_up is deliberately conservative and generally very loose; equality gamma=C_up is not claimed strict. A feasible selected law does not establish necessity, optimality or unrestricted synthesis.

2. Closed-law coefficients and deterministic time-domain norms

Set

 P=A+BK, Q=A0+B0K, O=D0+D1K,
 F0=BL0, Fh=BLh+C, F(r)=B H(r),
 G0=B0L0, Gh=B0Lh, G(r)=B0 H(r),
 E0=D1L0, Eh=D1Lh, E(r)=D1 H(r).

All matrix norms below are induced Euclidean operator norms. Define the finite deterministic constants

 a_d=||F0||+||Fh||+integral_0^h ||F(r)||dr,
 a_s=||G0||+||Gh||+integral_0^h ||G(r)||dr,
 a_o=||E0||+||Eh||+integral_0^h ||E(r)||dr.             (1)

Square integrability of H and finite h make these integrals finite. Writing f_t=F0w_t+Fh w_(t-h)+integral F(r)w_(t-r)dr, and analogously g_t,o_t, the closed law is

 dx=(P x+f)dt+(Q x+g)dW,     z=O x+o.

Pathwise Young/Minkowski, extension of w by zero, and ||w(.-h)||_L2[0,T]<=||w||_L2[0,S] give

 ||f||_2<=a_d||w||_2,   ||g||_2<=a_s||w||_2,
 ||o||_2<=a_o||w||_2.                                     (2)

3. Completely specified finite constant

Select any positive delta satisfying delta<=T and

 alpha=||P||delta+8||Q||sqrt(delta) <= 1/2.               (3)

For example choose delta as the minimum of T, 1/(4||P||) if P!=0, and 1/(32||Q||)^2 if Q!=0; omit a term when its denominator is zero. Let N=ceil(T/delta) and

 R(alpha,N)=((1-alpha)^(-N)-1)/alpha for alpha>0,
 R(0,N)=N,
 C_up=a_o+||O||sqrt(T)[sqrt(delta)a_d+8a_s] R(alpha,N).  (4)

Every quantity is finite. The constants 8 and alpha do not hide a squared-input expectation: the martingale inequality in section 4 has an expectation of the square root. Any valid delta in (3) yields a correct bound; optimizing this coarse bound is NOT an exact gain computation.

4. The needed L1 martingale inequality, with constant 8

For a continuous finite-dimensional Hilbert martingale M starting at 0, let M*=sup_(t<=T)||M_t|| and [M]_T be the scalar sum of coordinate quadratic variations. Then

 E M* <= 8 E sqrt([M]_T).                                 (5)

A direct stopping-time proof avoids any unspecified numerical BDG constant. For lambda>0 let tau be the first hit of ||M||=lambda, let sigma be the first later hit of ||M-M_tau||=lambda, and let v be the first time [M] reaches lambda²/4. On {M*>2lambda, sqrt([M]_T)<=lambda/2}, tau and sigma occur before T and before the bracket budget is exhausted. Optional stopping and the Hilbert Itô isometry give

 P(M*>2lambda, sqrt([M]_T)<=lambda/2)
       <= (1/4) P(M*>lambda).

Indeed the stopped increment needs norm at least lambda while its expected squared norm is at most (lambda²/4)P(tau<=T). Consequently

 P(M*>2lambda) <= P(sqrt([M]_T)>lambda/2)+(1/4)P(M*>lambda).

Integrating over lambda>0 gives (1/2)EM* <=2E sqrt([M]_T)+(1/4)EM*, which is (5). To justify finite expectations, first stop at a bounded bracket and use L2 Doob/Itô isometry, then let the bracket cutoff increase by monotone convergence. This is an L1 inequality; it does not assume E[M]_T<infinity for the original input.

5. Localized block recurrence for the actual physical state

For t_i=min(i delta,T), i=0,...,N, let Z_i=sup_(0<=t<=t_i)||x_t||. First stop the state at a large norm so Z_i is integrable; all subsequent estimates apply to the stopped drift/diffusion and the fixed input. On each block, use (5) for the actual vector Itô integral, and pathwise Cauchy–Schwarz/Minkowski:

 E Z_i <= E Z_(i-1)
          + (||P||delta+8||Q||sqrt(delta)) E Z_i
          + [sqrt(delta)a_d+8a_s] E||w||_2.

The local input terms are bounded by their full-horizon norms (2), so they remain integrable for E1-only w. By (3), with A_block=sqrt(delta)a_d+8a_s,

 E Z_i <= (1-alpha)^(-1)[E Z_(i-1)+A_block E||w||_2].

Z_0=0. Summing the geometric recurrence yields

 E Z_N <= A_block R(alpha,N) E||w||_2.                     (6)

If alpha=0, read the geometric sum as N. Let the state stopping cutoff tend to infinity; continuous linear SDEs with pathwise L2 input do not explode on a finite horizon, and monotone convergence gives (6) for the original state.

Finally,

 E||z||_2 <= ||O|| sqrt(T) E Z_N+a_o E||w||_2
           <= C_up E||w||_2,

proving (A). The actual law also has E||u||_2<infinity by the same state bound and Young inequality, and is locally path-L2 admissible. Nothing here assumes w in E2, takes a square root AFTER an input expectation, or replaces E||z|| by sqrt(E||z||²).
