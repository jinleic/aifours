1. Actual original model and theorem

Let T>h>=0, S=T-h, and consider the finite-horizon ORIGINAL model with scalar w, scalar Brownian W, any real finite-dimensional coefficient matrices and actual law

 dx=(Ax+Bu+Cw_(t-h))dt+(A0x+B0u)dW,

 B0 ell=0,       D1 ell=0.                                 (R)

They permit arbitrary, possibly noncommuting A0 and B0K, arbitrary D1K, state feedback K and a genuine current-preview tap ell; (R) removes additive control-noise and direct feedthrough from this PARTICULAR law, not from the plant generally. Set

 P=A+BK,  Q=A0+B0K,  b=B ell,  c=C,  D=D0+D1K.

Then the actual closed loop is dx=(Px+bw_t+c w_(t-h))dt+Qx dW, z=Dx. P,Q are completely arbitrary real n x n matrices, b,c are vectors and D has any output dimension. Itô SDE, not a pathwise Stratonovich substitution.

For all nonzero-output cases characterized in section 2, define the exact mean-column Gamma and deterministic second-moment H in section 3. There exists a positive, model-dependent deterministic epsilon explicitly constructed through a positive-probability future event in section 5 such that, for every E1 input,

Thus the true fixed-law literal gain is strictly between 0 and Gamma. Gamma itself is a uniformly strict feasible threshold, but is NOT asserted to be the actual gain. 2. Exact stochastic-reachability zero classification

For v in R^n define V(v) as the smallest subspace containing v and invariant under P and Q: equivalently span{word(P,Q)v}. It is computable by iterating V_(k+1)=V_k+P V_k+Q V_k until stabilization, at most n strict dimension increases. Call v VISIBLE iff D V(v) is nonzero.

Let Phi(t,s) be the actual two-parameter fundamental solution d_t Phi=P Phi dt+Q Phi dW, Phi(s,s)=I. Standard finite-dimensional linear Itô flow gives an almost surely continuous, invertible two-parameter flow, and Phi(t,s) is determined by Brownian increments on [s,t]. The physical output column is

 K_s(t)=1_(t>=s) D Phi(t,s)b + 1_(t>=s+h) D Phi(t,s+h)c,

and z_t=integral_0^S K_s(t) w_s ds.

The actual output operator is identically zero iff (i) h>0 and b,c are both invisible, or (ii) h=0 and b+c is invisible. Proof: if V(v) subset ker D, invariance makes D Phi(t,s)v=0. Conversely, if D Phi(t,s)v vanished a.s. for every t in any nontrivial interval, its continuous Itô semimartingale decomposition forces DQ Phi(t,s)v=DP Phi(t,s)v=0; iterating on the rows and evaluating at t=s gives D word(P,Q)v=0 for all words. Thus any visible v has positive expected integrated output on EVERY nontrivial initial interval. The h>0 immediate b-column is exposed before the delayed c-column; if b is invisible, only c remains. For h=0 the vectors combine.

In a zero case z=0 and every gamma>0 is strictly feasible, while gamma=0 is not strictly feasible for nonzero w. In a nonzero case Gamma,H>0; the actual gain is >0 by the countable-density argument in section 6.

3. Exact second moments and a deterministic positive lower envelope

Set Y_s=||K_s||_L2[0,T], Cbar(s)=E Y_s and Gamma=(integral_0^S Cbar(s)^2ds)^(1/2). The column uses only increments after s and is independent of F_s. For a matrix X define the deterministic covariance semigroup M_t(X) by

 M'_t=P M_t+M_t P^T+Q M_t Q^T,   M_0=X.

Put a_v(t)=tr[D M_t(vv^T)D^T], and

 X_h=M_h(bb^T)+(e^(Ph)b)c^T+c(e^(Ph)b)^T+cc^T,
 a_h(t)=tr[D M_t(X_h)D^T].

X_h=E[(Phi(h,0)b+c)(Phi(h,0)b+c)^T] is positive semidefinite. Independent Brownian increments before and after h give the EXACT formulas

 m2(s)=E Y_s²=integral_0^h a_b(t)dt + integral_0^(S-s) a_h(t)dt,
 H²=integral_0^S m2(s)ds
   =S integral_0^h a_b(t)dt + integral_0^S (S-t)a_h(t)dt.   (1)

For h=0, X_h=(b+c)(b+c)^T. The visibility argument makes m2(s)>0 for every s<S in every nonzero case. Jensen gives Gamma<=H<infinity.

Define the deterministic fourth-moment bound

 M4=8T integral_0^T [E||D Phi(t,0)b||^4+E||D Phi(t,0)c||^4]dt < infinity.

It bounds E Y_s^4 for EVERY s, using (integral |f|²)^2<=T integral |f|^4 and ||v+w||^4<=8(||v||^4+||w||^4). Each fourth moment is obtained from the finite-dimensional tensor ODE with generator sum_i P^(i)+sum_(i<j) Q^(i)Q^(j), contracted with (D^T D) twice. In a nonzero case M4>0. Hölder yields

 Cbar(s) >= m2(s)^(3/2)/sqrt(M4).

Consequently f=Cbar/Gamma is a unit L2[0,S] function satisfying the EXPLICIT deterministic lower envelope

 f(s)>=f0(s):=m2(s)^(3/2)/(sqrt(M4) H)>0 for s<S.          (2)

This fourth-moment step is essential: ||E K_s|| may vanish when noncommuting state noise makes an otherwise hidden response visible.

4. Positive-probability separated stochastic columns

Set delta=S/8, s0=S/4, u0=3S/4. Depending on the nonzero visibility case, choose one visible vector v and common delay dstar:

 * h>0,b visible: v=b, dstar=0, L=min(h/2,S/16);
 * h>0,b invisible,c visible: v=c, dstar=h, L=S/16;
 * h=0,b+c visible: v=b+c, dstar=0, L=S/16.

All L>0. The K_s prefix on [s+dstar,s+dstar+L] is precisely D Phi(t,s+dstar)v: the competing delayed tap has not started in case 1, the immediate output is identically zero in case 2, and the taps combine in case 3. For each anchor s0,u0, section 2 implies this prefix has positive norm with positive probability. Its two Brownian increment intervals are disjoint, so both prefix norms are simultaneously positive with positive probability.

The continuous flow makes s -> K_s and s -> its moving prefix continuous as L2-time functions, and sup_(delta<=s<=S)||K_s|| finite almost surely. A countable choice of shrinking deterministic closed intervals I around s0 and J around u0, positive rational lambda_I,lambda_J and finite rational M0 therefore gives an event E0 of positive probability p, on which

 sup I+L<inf J,
 inf_(s in I) ||K_s 1_[s+dstar,s+dstar+L]|| >= lambda_I,
 inf_(u in J) ||K_u 1_[u+dstar,u+dstar+L]|| >= lambda_J,
 sup_(v in I union J) ||K_v|| <= M0.                        (3)

I,J are contained in (delta,S). This is a countable-existence construction, not an asserted numerical probability or a selected input. E0 depends only on W increments after delta, hence is independent of F_delta. The entire prefix of K_s in (3) lies before the earliest possible support of K_u. Thus for all s in I,u in J on E0,

 |<K_s,K_u>| <= (1-xi)||K_s||||K_u||,
 xi=1-sqrt[1-(lambda_I/M0)^2] >0.                         (4)

The absolute value covers all signs and output directions. In particular, no commutativity, output positivity, bounded global noise path or chosen test disturbance is assumed.

5. Explicit existence of a uniform literal margin

For the deterministic I,J and p,lambda_I,lambda_J,M0 from (3), put

 b0=(integral_0^delta f0(s)^2ds)^(1/2),
 bI=integral_I f0(s)ds,  bJ=integral_J f0(s)ds,
 eta=min{bI/(2sqrt(|I|)), bJ/(2sqrt(|J|))},
 cI=lambda_I bI/2,       cJ=lambda_J bJ/2,
 epsilon=min{p²b0²/[128(1+p)²], p eta²/16,
             xi cI cJ p²/(8H²)} >0.                         (5)

The components derived from (1)–(2) are deterministic matrix ODE/integral quantities; p and the rational event thresholds are model-specific choices guaranteed by (3). This is a rigorously defined positive margin but not a practical evaluated one without quantitative small-ball/flow estimates.

Let U=||w||, O=||integral K_s w_s ds||, R=integral |w_s|Y_s ds. For each fixed s, w_s is F_s measurable and Y_s depends on future increments, so ER=E integral Cbar(s)|w_s|ds<=Gamma EU, even for E1-only inputs. Normalize EU=1, put q=1-EO/Gamma, and set e=|| |w|/U-f || on U>0 (zero when U=0). Then E[U e²]<=2q. The first-delta input norm supplies an F_delta-measurable Lambda with E|U-Lambda|<=sqrt(2q)/b0, whence E[U 1_E0]>=p-(1+p)sqrt(2q)/b0.

On E0 and e<=eta, Cauchy–Schwarz and f>=f0 imply integral_I |w_s|Y_s ds>=cI U and integral_J |w_s|Y_s ds>=cJ U. The vector Hilbert pairwise deficit and (4) give

 R²-O² >=2xi cI cJ U².                                    (6)

The U-weight of e>eta is <=2q/eta², and that of R>(4H/p)U is <=p/4. If q is below the first two terms in (5), at least p/2 U-weight remains in E0 ∩ {e<=eta} ∩ {R<=(4H/p)U}. Equation (6) then implies q>=xi cI cJ p²/(8H²). If either preliminary inequality fails, q already exceeds epsilon. This proves (A), without a maximizing input, E2 moment, or an illicit future-conditioned disturbance.

6. Positive true gain and limits

Every nonzero visibility case has positive expected column-prefix energy on a positive-length input interval, by section 2 and stationarity. Thus the random Hilbert–Schmidt operator is nonzero with positive probability. A countable dense family of deterministic scalar L2 inputs cannot all map to zero on that event; one member has positive-probability nonzero output. The E1 bound makes its expectation finite, so the true progressive literal gain is >0. No particular input is selected or optimized. The result proves a strict gap BELOW Gamma, not that Gamma or H equals the actual endpoint.
