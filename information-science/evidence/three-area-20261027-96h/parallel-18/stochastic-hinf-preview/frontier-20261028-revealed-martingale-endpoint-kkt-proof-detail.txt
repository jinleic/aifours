1. Compact relaxation and physical clocks

For a probability measure nu on [0,1], define

 Y_nu=integral_0^1 B_s² nu(ds),   F(nu)=E sqrt(Y_nu).

For a deterministic unit L2 input of the actual pure-delayed dx=w_(t-h)dW,z=x law, Brownian time change gives the gain divided by sqrt(S) as F(A_#Leb), where A is absolutely continuous, nondecreasing, A(0)=0,A(1)=1. Conversely any such A has an L2 input sqrt(A').

For any nu, its nondecreasing quantile function can be approximated almost everywhere by monotone piecewise-linear A_j with these endpoints. Then A_j#Leb converges weakly to nu. For any weakly convergent nu_j, Brownian-path continuity gives Y_nuj->Y_nu almost surely, dominated by sup_(s<=1)|B_s|²; the square roots are dominated by the integrable Brownian maximum. Therefore F is continuous on the compact probability-measure space and the actual gain equals sqrt(S) max F, using the named input-revelation theorem for the full E1/E2 class.

2. Strict concavity and uniqueness

For distinct probabilities nu1,nu2 put eta=nu1-nu2. Its total mass is zero. Gaussian covariance gives

 Var(Y_nu1-Y_nu2)=4 integral_0^1 r [eta([r,1])]^2dr.

If this were zero, the bounded-variation tail would vanish almost everywhere away from 0; eta would be supported only at 0 and its total mass would force eta=0. Hence the two path functionals differ with positive probability. Pointwise strict concavity of sqrt on unequal nonnegative arguments makes F strictly concave. The compact maximizer nu* is unique.

3. The maximizer has two positive support points

Let c=sqrt(2/pi). For nu_epsilon=(1-epsilon)delta_1+epsilon delta_(1/2), the nonzero covariance eigenvalues of the 2D Gaussian norm have sum 1-epsilon/2 and product epsilon(1-epsilon)/4. Hence lambda_max>=1-epsilon and lambda_min>=epsilon(1-epsilon)/4. For independent standard normals X,Z and s=sqrt(epsilon)/2,

 F(nu_epsilon)>=sqrt(1-epsilon) E sqrt(X²+s²Z²).

On `1<=|Z|<=2` and `s<=|X|<=1`,

 sqrt(X²+s²Z²)-|X| >= s²/[(sqrt(5)+1)|X|].

The two independent Gaussian density lower bounds give

 E[sqrt(X²+s²Z²)-|X|]
 >= C s² log(1/s), C=2e^(-5/2)/[pi(sqrt(5)+1)]>0.

Since sqrt(1-epsilon)>=1-epsilon, taking epsilon<=1/2 with log(2/sqrt(epsilon))>8c/C makes F(nu_epsilon)>c=F(delta_1). A probability supported at a single t has value c sqrt(t)<=c. Also nu* cannot have an atom at 0: moving it to 1 strictly increases Y since B1!=0 almost surely. Hence nu* has at least TWO distinct support points strictly above 0.

4. Inverse moment and KKT continuity

Choose separated intervals I1,I2 around two such points with 0<inf I1<=sup I1<inf I2 and positive masses p1,p2. Put

 Z_i=(1/p_i) integral_(I_i) B_s nu*(ds),
 Y*=integral B_s² nu*(ds).

Jensen on each interval gives Y*>=p1 Z1²+p2 Z2². The pair (Z1,Z2) is a nondegenerate centered Gaussian: the second average contains an independent Brownian increment after I1, while the first has positive variance. A two-dimensional Gaussian has finite E||(Z1,Z2)||^(-3/2), so

 E[Y*^(-3/4)]<infinity.                                    (5)

This is stronger than the inverse-half moment needed below. For each t, f(t)=E[B_t²/sqrt(Y*)] is finite by Hölder. Also

 |f(t)-f(s)| <=(E|B_t²-B_s²|³)^(1/3) (E Y*^(-3/4))^(2/3),

so f is continuous and f(0)=0. For epsilon<=1/2 the directional quotient of F along `(1-epsilon)nu*+epsilon delta_t` is bounded in absolute value by an integrable multiple of `(B_t²+Y*)/sqrt(Y*)`. Dominated convergence gives the derivative

 (1/2)[f(t)-F(nu*)].

At the maximum this derivative is nonpositive for every t. Moreover Tonelli gives integral f(t)nu*(dt)=E sqrt(Y*)=F(nu*). Continuity therefore implies f=F(nu*) on the FULL support of nu*.

5. Strict actual endpoint from the gap at zero

A physical energy clock A is continuous and A(0)=0; it spends a positive amount of physical time in [0,a), hence its pushforward measure cannot equal nu*.
