It is a BOUND on the user's literal expected-path gain; it does NOT replace that objective by RMS, assert equivalence of feasibility criteria, identify a sharp gain, or decide the pointwise endpoint.

1. Exact physical scope and comparison

For finite T>h>=S=T-h>0, vector progressive E1 w supported [0,S], scalar Brownian W and x0=0, use the actual original pure-delayed law

 dx=(Ax+Bu+Cw_(t-h))dt+(A0x+B0u)dW,
 z=D0x+D1u,               u=Kx+Lh w_(t-h),

with arbitrary finite matrices and the ONLY extra restriction Q=A0+B0K=0. Additive input diffusion G=B0Lh and direct output E=D1Lh may be nonzero. Define

 P=A+BK, F=BLh+C, O=D0+D1K,
 W(v)=integral_0^v exp(P^T t)O^T O exp(Pt)dt,
 (Top v)(t)=E v(t)+integral_0^t O exp[P(t-s)]Fv(s)ds.

For deterministic v define the auxiliary Hilbert-space second moment

 R(v)^2=||Top v||_L2[0,S]^2
         +integral_0^S v(s)^T G^T W(S-s)G v(s)ds,
 Gamma_R=sup_(v!=0) R(v)/||v||_L2.

This is a COMPUTABLE comparison quantity, not the user's objective. The actual original literal gains obey

 sqrt(2/pi) Gamma_R <= gamma1=gamma2 <= Gamma_R.           (1)

The equality is same-law E1/E2 gain equality only; (1) does NOT give pointwise strictness at any gamma or a necessary-and-sufficient H-infinity criterion.

2. Why revealed input makes the comparison valid for the actual progressive class

Before h the state/output are zero and the whole w path is F_S subset F_h measurable. The post-h Brownian increments are independent of F_h. For each deterministic input path v, the post-h output is the Hilbert-space Gaussian

 z(v)=Top v + [t -> integral_0^t O exp[P(t-s)]Gv_s dWtilde_s].

Itô isometry/Fubini proves E||z(v)||²=R(v)². The named revelation theorem proves the actual E1/E2 literal gain is exactly `sup_v E||z(v)||/||v||`, including random inputs with correlated amplitudes and shapes. Therefore an all-Gaussian bound for each deterministic path transfers to the actual progressive gain without conditioning on future noise or replacing E||w|| by sqrt(E||w||²).

3. Sharp Hilbert Gaussian reverse Jensen inequality

For ANY real separable Hilbert Gaussian Y with deterministic mean m and trace-class covariance Sigma,

 sqrt(2/pi) sqrt(E||Y||²) <= E||Y|| <=sqrt(E||Y||²).      (2)

The upper side is Jensen. For the lower, let A=tr Sigma, B=||m||² and diagonalize Sigma. For t>0 its norm-square Laplace transform satisfies

 E exp(-t||Y||²)
 =det(I+2t Sigma)^(-1/2)
    exp[-t <m,(I+2t Sigma)^(-1)m>]
 <=(1+2t(A+B))^(-1/2).                                  (3)

Indeed the determinant satisfies det(I+2t Sigma)>=1+2tA, and the mean quadratic form is at least B/(1+2tA). Thus the log of the inverse-square bound is at least `log(1+2tA)+2tB/(1+2tA)`, which is >=log[1+2t(A+B)] by log(1+x)<=x. These inequalities hold for trace-class limits by monotone eigenvalue approximation.

Use the exact positive integral identity

 sqrt(x)=(1/(2sqrt(pi))) integral_0^infinity (1-e^(-tx))t^(-3/2)dt.

Tonelli and (3) give E||Y||>=E|N(0,A+B)|=sqrt(2/pi)sqrt(A+B), proving (2). Equality in the lower bound occurs for a centered one-dimensional Gaussian, so sqrt(2/pi) is SHARP as a universal Hilbert-Gaussian constant. A physical short-pulse limit of `dx=v dW,z=x` approximates that rank-one output geometry, but no equality of the OPTIMIZED physical gains is asserted.

4. Derivation of the literal-gain bracket

For every deterministic v, (2) with Y=z(v) gives

 sqrt(2/pi) R(v) <= E||z(v)|| <= R(v).

Divide by ||v|| and take the supremum; the revelation identity then yields (1). Gamma_R² is the operator norm of the deterministic positive self-adjoint operator `Top^* Top+M_R`, where `M_R` is multiplication by the matrix `G^T W(S-s)G`. Its computation is a deterministic Hilbert spectral problem; no claim is made that the optimum is an eigenvalue or that the user's literal gain has the same value.
