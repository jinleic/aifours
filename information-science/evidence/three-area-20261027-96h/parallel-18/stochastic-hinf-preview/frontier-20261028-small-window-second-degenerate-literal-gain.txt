1. Original law and exact order

Fix finite T>0, S=T-h>0 and arbitrary real original matrices, scalar W. Use the actual zero controller and zero state:

 dx=(Ax+Cw_(t-h))dt+A0x dW,    z=D0x.

Inputs are progressive E1 or E2 paths supported [0,S], with the SAME literal expected-path ratio. Assume

 D0 C=0,        D0 A0 C=0.                                (R2)

Put Md=D0 A C and Mq=D0 A0² C. On the unit window define the deterministic-input L1 output operator

 (T2 v)(t)=Md integral_0^t integral_0^s v(r)dr ds
     +Mq integral_0^t [integral_0^s (integral_0^r v(u)du)dB_r]dB_s,
 c2=||T2||_(L2_input -> L1(Omega;L2_time)).

If S<=h and alpha(S)=||A||S+8||A0||sqrt(S)<=1/2, BOTH actual literal gains satisfy

 |gamma_i(S)-c2 S²|
 <=2||D0||||C|| S [ ||A||S alpha(S)
      +8||A0||sqrt(S)(||A||S+8||A0||sqrt(S)alpha(S)) ],
 i=1,2.                                                    (1)

Thus gamma_i(S)=c2 S²+O(S^(5/2)); c2 is finite, attained by a deterministic unit input when positive, and c2>0 iff Md!=0 or Mq!=0. If both vanish, (1) states only the absolute O(S^(5/2)) remainder.

2. Leading two physical contributions

For S<=h, the entire w path is F_h measurable before the state starts, and the shifted post-h Brownian motion is independent of it. Let

 x0(t)=C integral_0^t w_r dr,
 x1(t)=integral_0^t A0 x0(s)dWtilde_s,
 x2(t)=integral_0^t A x0(s)ds+integral_0^t A0 x1(s)dWtilde_s.

Under (R2), D0x0=D0x1=0, so the leading output is D0x2. For a revealed input path scaled by v(u)=sqrt(S)w(Su), Brownian scaling gives

 ||D0x2||_L2[0,S]=S²||T2 v||_L2[0,1],  ||v||=||w||.

Conditioning on F_h makes every random E1 input's leading expected ratio an energy-weighted average of deterministic-v ratios; deterministic inputs are admissible to E1/E2. Therefore the LEADING physical operator has exactly c2 S² as its E1 and E2 gain. The two stochastic integrals in T2 use the SAME Brownian path, and no square root of an expected input energy is substituted.

3. Uniform E1 remainder

Put Z=E sup_(t<=S)||x_t||. The prior source gives Z<=2||C||sqrt(S) E||w||. The same L1 maximal inequality yields

 E sup||x-x0|| <=alpha Z,
 E sup||x-x0-x1|| <=[||A||S+8||A0||sqrt(S)alpha] Z.

The exact remaining state is

 x-x0-x1-x2=integral A(x-x0)dt
                       +integral A0(x-x0-x1)dWtilde.

Multiply its expected supremum bound by ||D0||sqrt(S), substitute the two displayed inequalities and Z, and obtain the explicit right side of (1). This holds for EVERY progressive E1 input, without an E2 moment. The gain comparison follows from triangle inequalities and a deterministic near-maximizer of T2.

4. Compactness and nonzero criterion

The unit-interval T2 factors through the compact deterministic Volterra map `v -> integral_0^. v`: its remaining deterministic integration and double Itô map are bounded from L2 time to L1(Omega;L2 time), by Itô isometry and Jensen. Thus T2 is compact and attains a positive norm on the Hilbert unit ball.

If T2 is identically zero, its mean output is `Md V²v`, so Md=0. Then the second-chaos term must also vanish; Itô isometry with deterministic v forces Mq=0. Conversely both matrices zero make T2 zero. Hence c2>0 iff Md or Mq is nonzero. This is an exact all-matrix condition, not a claim of a closed formula for c2.

5. Exact three-stage stochastic example

Take n=3,m=p=1,A=0,C=e1,D0=e3^T and A0 the nilpotent matrix with A0 e1=e2,A0 e2=e3,A0 e3=0. The actual post-h equations are

Then D0C=D0A0C=0,Md=0,Mq=1, and the T2 term is the ENTIRE output. For every S<=h, gamma1=gamma2=c2 S² with c2>0. No numerical value is asserted and this is not an unrestricted synthesis result.
