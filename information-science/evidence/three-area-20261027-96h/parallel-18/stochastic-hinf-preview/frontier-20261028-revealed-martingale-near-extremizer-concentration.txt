1. Actual physical law and normalized clocks

Fix T>h>=S=T-h>0 and the actual original pure-delayed scalar law `dx=w_(t-h)dW,z=x`, x0=0. Let w_j be ANY sequence of nonzero progressive E1 inputs supported [0,S] whose original literal ratios tend to the actual gain `sqrt(S) kappa`. The named source gives a unique maximizing probability nu* for

 F(nu)=E [integral_0^1 B_s² nu(ds)]^(1/2),

and proves support(nu*) subset [a,1] for some a>0.

For each nonzero input path define U_j=||w_j||_L2[0,S] and the normalized cumulative-energy clock

 A_j(u)=U_j^(-2) integral_0^(Su) |w_j(t)|²dt, 0<=u<=1,
 nu_j=(A_j)_#Lebesgue[0,1].

A_j is absolutely continuous, nondecreasing and A_j(0)=0,A_j(1)=1 on U_j>0. Define the ENERGY-SIZE-BIASED probability on the input history

 dQ_j=(U_j/E U_j)dP.

This is a mathematical change of measure for reporting a ratio; it is NOT a change in the original plant, admissibility or noise law. Under Q_j, U_j>0 almost surely.

2. Exact concentration statement

For every weak neighborhood V of nu*,

 Q_j(nu_j not in V) ->0.                                  (1)

In particular, for every b in (0,a) and epsilon>0, if

 tau_b,j=inf{u:A_j(u)>b},

then

 Q_j(tau_b,j>epsilon)->0.                                  (2)

Thus a fixed positive FRACTION b of total input energy must be spent arbitrarily close to the beginning for an energy-weighted near-extremizer. Equation (2) does NOT assert convergence under the original P: rare high-energy input histories may carry most of the E1 ratio.

3. Proof by uniqueness and energy weighting

The revealed-input theorem gives the exact identity

 E||z(w_j)||/(sqrt(S) E U_j)=E_Qj F(nu_j).

The left side tends to kappa, while F(nu)<=kappa. For any weak neighborhood V of nu*, its complement is compact and excludes the UNIQUE maximizer, so

 d_V=kappa-sup_(nu not in V)F(nu)>0.

Therefore

 Q_j(nu_j not in V) <= [kappa-E_Qj F(nu_j)]/d_V ->0,

proving (1). For b<a, the map nu->nu([0,b]) is upper semicontinuous; hence the set `{nu:nu([0,b])>=epsilon}` is closed and excludes nu*. Its Q_j probability tends to zero. Since A_j is continuous/nondecreasing, Lebesgue measure of `{u:A_j(u)<=b}` is at least tau_b,j, proving (2).

4. Consequence for deterministic maximizing sequences

If each w_j is deterministic and normalized to ||w_j||=1, Q_j=P trivially and (2) gives `integral_0^(S delta)|w_j(t)|²dt>=b` eventually for every fixed delta>0 and every b<a. Thus if a subsequence converges weakly in L2 to v,

 ||v||_L2² <=1-b <1.

Indeed the energy outside [0,S delta] is at most 1-b eventually; weak lower semicontinuity on that interval and delta downarrow 0 give the bound. In particular no normalized maximizing sequence can converge strongly to an admissible unit input, matching the exact nonattainment result but supplying a more precise initial-concentration mechanism.
