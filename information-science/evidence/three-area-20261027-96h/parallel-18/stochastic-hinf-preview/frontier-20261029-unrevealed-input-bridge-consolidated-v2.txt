1. Exact lagged-information reduction (T1)

Put R=(W_r)_(0<=r<=h) and B_u=(W_(h+Su)-W_h)/sqrt(S), 0<=u<=1; R and B are independent and B is standard Brownian. For an input w set v(u)=sqrt(S)w(Su); ||v||_L2[0,1]=||w||_L2[0,S], and

 v(u) is G_u-measurable, G_u:=sigma(R) v sigma(B_r: r<=(u-eta)^+),   (1.1)

because F_(Su) is in sigma(R) when Su<=h and equals sigma(R) v sigma(B_r:r<=u-eta) when Su>h; conversely every G-progressive v defines an admissible w. The state vanishes before h, x_(h+Su)=X(u):=integral_0^u v dB, and ||z||_L2[0,T]=sqrt(S)||X||_L2[0,1]. Since G_u is in sigma(R) v sigma(B_r:r<=u), with respect to which B is Brownian, X is a continuous local martingale with clock A(u)=integral_0^u v^2. kappa_i(eta) is the supremum of E||X||/E||v|| over nonzero G-progressive v of class Ei; gamma_i(h)=sqrt(S)kappa_i(eta). The objective is always the literal ratio of expected path norms.

2. E1 and E2 give the same gain (T2)

Lenglart's domination inequality (constant (2-k)/(1-k) at k=1/2) gives E sup_t|M_t|<=3E[<M>_infinity^(1/2)] for continuous local martingales with M_0=0. For nonzero E1 v put v_n=v 1_(|v|<=n): G-progressive, bounded, E||v-v_n||->0 by dominated convergence, and |E||X(v)||-E||X(v_n)|||<=3E||v-v_n||->0 while E||v_n||->E||v||>0. Hence kappa_1=kappa_2.

 |E sqrt(Y_A1)-E sqrt(Y_A2)| <= (integral_0^1 E|A1(u)-A2(u)|du)^(1/2).   (4.1)

6. nu* is not a two-atom law (T6)

Suppose nu*=p delta_a+(1-p)delta_1 with 0<p<1, 0<a<1. Write X=B_a, Z=B_1, Delta=Z-X (independent of X, variance 1-a), Y=pX^2+(1-p)Z^2. Adopted KKT: f(a)=f(1)=kappa=E sqrt(Y) and f<=kappa.

(i) Interior KKT function. For t in (a,1), lambda=(t-a)/(1-a), the Brownian bridge gives E[B_t^2|X,Z]=((1-lambda)X+lambda Z)^2+lambda(1-lambda)(1-a), hence

 f(t)=kappa+lambda(1-lambda)(Gamma-2kappa),  Gamma=2E[XZ/sqrt(Y)]+(1-a)E[Y^(-1/2)].

(ii) Gaussian integration by parts in Delta (for fixed X!=0 the integrands are smooth in Delta since Y>=pX^2; all expectations below are of functions homogeneous of degree 0 or -1 in the nondegenerate 2-dimensional Gaussian (X,Z) and Y>=min(p,1-p)(X^2+Z^2), hence finite):

 E[X Delta Y^(-1/2)]=-(1-a)(1-p)E[XZ Y^(-3/2)],
 E[Delta^2 Y^(-1/2)]=(1-a)E[Y^(-1/2)-(1-p)Delta Z Y^(-3/2)].

Therefore f(a)-f(1)=E[(X^2-Z^2)Y^(-1/2)]=(1-a)E[((1-p)XZ-pX^2)Y^(-3/2)], and f(a)=f(1) gives

 E[pX^2 Y^(-3/2)]=(1-p)E[XZ Y^(-3/2)].   (6.1)

Using E[X^2Y^(-1/2)]=f(a)=kappa and (6.1),

 Gamma-2kappa = 2E[X Delta Y^(-1/2)]+(1-a)E[Y^(-1/2)]
              = (1-a)E[(pX^2+(1-p)Z^2-2(1-p)XZ)Y^(-3/2)]
              = (1-a)(1-p)E[Z Delta Y^(-3/2)].

(iii) Positivity. Conditionally on Z=z, X is N(az, a(1-a)). Put g_z(x)=(px^2+(1-p)z^2)^(-3/2) and m(z)=E[(z-X)g_z(X)|Z=z]. Stein's identity for N(az,a(1-a)) gives, for z>0,

 m(z)=(1-a)z E[g_z(X)|Z=z]+3p a(1-a)E[X(pX^2+(1-p)z^2)^(-5/2)|Z=z]>0:

the first term is positive, and the second is the expectation of an odd function that is positive on (0,infinity) under a normal law with positive mean az, which is positive because the density at x exceeds that at -x for x>0. Symmetry gives m(-z)=-m(z). Hence E[Z Delta Y^(-3/2)]=E[Z m(Z)]>0, so Gamma>2kappa and f(t)>kappa for every t in (a,1), contradicting f<=kappa. Therefore nu* is not a two-atom law. Since nu* is also not a single atom and supp nu* is contained in [a,1] with 1 in the support, supp nu* contains a point t1 in (a,1) in addition to 1: it meets (a,1] in at least two points.

7. Late lag: the revealed value persists below h=S (T7)

7.1 Reduction for eta>=1/2. Put c=1-eta<=1/2<=eta. For u<=eta, G_u=sigma(R); for eta<u<=1, G_u is contained in sigma(R) v sigma(B_r:r<=c), which is contained in F_eta:=sigma(R) v sigma(B_r:r<=eta). So v1=v 1_[0,eta] is sigma(R)-measurable, and v2=v 1_(eta,1] is F_eta-measurable with W:=||v2||^2 measurable with respect to H:=sigma(R) v sigma(B_r:r<=c). Let X1=integral v1 dB and Y_0=integral_0^1 X1(u)^2du (the output of v1 alone). Since B-increments after eta are independent of F_eta, conditionally on F_eta, M(u)=integral_eta^u v2 dB is a centred Wiener integral; hence

 E[||X||^2|F_eta]=Y_0+integral_eta^1(1-r)v2(r)^2dr<=Y_0+cW,  E||X||<=E sqrt(Y_0+cW).   (7.1)

Conditioning on R, it suffices to show, for every deterministic v1 on [0,eta] and every H-measurable W>=0 not both zero,

 E sqrt(Y_0+cW) < kappa E sqrt(||v1||^2+W).   (7.2)

If v1=0 this reads sqrt(c)E sqrt(W)<kappa E sqrt(W), true for c<kappa^2. Otherwise normalize ||v1||=1; let A_1 be its (deterministic, absolutely continuous) clock, nu=(A_1)_#Leb, and beta the Brownian path of X1=beta_(A_1). Then E sqrt(Y_0)=F(nu)<kappa (physical clocks cannot produce nu*), and conditionally on H the law of Y_0 depends only on beta on [0,A_1(c)].

7.2 Two elementary bounds. For y>0, w>=0, 0<c<=1:

 sqrt(y+cw)-sqrt(y) <= max(sqrt(c), c/sqrt(y)) (sqrt(1+w)-1),   (7.3)

(if y>=c, y+cw>=c(1+w) and sqrt(y)>=sqrt(c); if y<c, y+cw>=y(1+w); in both cases compare cw/(sqrt(y+cw)+sqrt(y)) with w/(sqrt(1+w)+1)); and, for c<kappa^2,

 sup_(w>=0)[sqrt(cw)-kappa(sqrt(1+w)-1)] = kappa-sqrt(kappa^2-c).   (7.4)

Write E sqrt(Y_0+cW)-kappa E sqrt(1+W)=(F(nu)-kappa)+E[psi_H(W)] with psi_H(w)=E[sqrt(Y_0+cw)-sqrt(Y_0)|H]-kappa(sqrt(1+w)-1). By (7.3), psi_H(w)<=(Lambda_H-kappa)(sqrt(1+w)-1) with Lambda_H<=sqrt(c)+cE[Y_0^(-1/2)|H]; by (7.4), psi_H<=kappa-sqrt(kappa^2-c) always.

8. The phase transition (T8)

By T3 monotonicity, {eta: kappa(eta)>kappa} is an initial segment of [0,1). By T4 it contains [0,max(m1,1-m1)); by T7 it excludes [eta_dagger,infinity). Let eta_star be its supremum; then max(m1,1-m1)<=eta_star<=eta_dagger<1, kappa(eta)>kappa for eta<eta_star and kappa(eta)=kappa for eta>eta_star. With h=T eta/(1+eta), h_star is in [T/3,T/2). The revealed formula sqrt(T-h)kappa is thus not special to h>=S: it holds for all h in (h_star,T), and fails exactly below h_star. Behaviour at eta_star itself, the pointwise endpoint for eta in (eta_star,eta_dagger), and attainment for eta<eta_star remain open.
