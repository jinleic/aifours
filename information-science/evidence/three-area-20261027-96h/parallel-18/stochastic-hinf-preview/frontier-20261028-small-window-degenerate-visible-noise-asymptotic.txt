1. Original physical law and asymptotic

Fix finite T>0 and arbitrary finite original matrices; for h=T-S use the actual zero-controller plant

 dx=(Ax+Cw_(t-h))dt+A0 x dW,    z=D0 x,    x0=0,

with progressive vector E1/E2 inputs supported [0,S]. Assume

 D0 C=0.                                                    (R)

Set M=D0 A0 C, and define the deterministic-input Banach operator on the unit interval

 (T0 v)(t)=M integral_0^t [integral_0^s v(r)dr] dB_s,
 cstar=||T0||_(L2[0,1] -> L1(Omega;L2[0,1])).

For S<=T/2 and alpha(S)=||A||S+8||A0||sqrt(S)<=1/2, BOTH actual literal gains satisfy

 |gamma_i(S)-cstar S^(3/2)|
 <=2||D0||||C|| S [ ||A||S+8||A0||sqrt(S) alpha(S) ],
 i=1,2.                                                    (1)

In particular gamma_i(S)=cstar S^(3/2)+O(S²). The scalar-W stochastic constant cstar is finite, exactly defined and attained by a deterministic unit input when M!=0; it is NOT asserted to have a closed numerical form. cstar>0 iff M!=0. If M=0, (1) only gives the absolute O(S²) remainder and makes no claim about the next term.

2. Actual post-h state and exact leading stochastic operator

For S<=T/2 the entire input path w[0,S] is F_S subset F_h measurable before the physical state starts at h, and post-h Brownian increments are independent of F_h. Shift to [0,S] as in the named sources:

 dx=A xdt+Cw_tdt+A0 x dWtilde,   z=D0x,   x0=0.

Define x0(t)=C integral_0^t w_s ds. Since D0C=0,

 z1(t)=D0 integral_0^t A0 x0(s)dWtilde_s
      =M integral_0^t [integral_0^s w_r dr]dWtilde_s

is the first potentially visible output. For each input path, scale `v(u)=sqrt(S)w(Su)` and `B_u=Wtilde_(Su)/sqrt(S)`. Conditional on F_h, B is an independent standard Brownian motion and

 ||z1||_L2[0,S] = S^(3/2) ||T0 v||_L2[0,1],
 ||v||_L2[0,1]=||w||_L2[0,S].

Conditional expectation therefore makes every random input's leading ratio an energy-weighted average of deterministic v ratios, and deterministic v are admissible. Both E1 and E2 gains of this LEADING physical operator are exactly cstar S^(3/2); no future-noise lookahead or RMS gain is used.

3. Uniform E1 error through order S²

Let Z=sup_(t<=S)||x_t||. The named L1 maximal-inequality estimate gives E Z<=2||C||sqrt(S) E||w|| whenever alpha<=1/2. Further,

 x-x0=integral A x dt+integral A0 x dWtilde,
 E sup||x-x0|| <=alpha(S) E Z.

Subtracting the leading z1 gives the exact physical remainder

 z-z1=D0[integral A x dt+integral A0(x-x0)dWtilde].

Hence for EVERY E1 input,

 E||z-z1|| <=2||D0||||C|| S
                   [||A||S+8||A0||sqrt(S) alpha(S)] E||w||.

The same inequality is valid on the E2 subclass. It bounds the absolute difference of the two actual gain suprema, because deterministic near/maximizers of the leading operator are admissible to BOTH classes. This proves (1).

4. Why cstar is attained and nonzero precisely when M is nonzero

T0 is the composition of the compact deterministic Volterra operator `v -> integral_0^t v` with a bounded deterministic-integrand Itô map into L1(Omega;L2 time). Indeed Itô isometry/Jensen bounds the latter by the L2 norm. Hence T0 is compact. A compact map from a Hilbert input space to a Banach output space attains a positive operator norm on the weakly compact unit ball. If M!=0, choose any deterministic v with Mv nonzero; Itô isometry gives a nonzero output with positive probability, so cstar>0. If M=0, cstar=0.
