1. Physical family and polynomial systems

Fix the original finite-dimensional real plant matrices A,A0,B,B0,C,D0,D1 and scalar W. For each S=T-h<=h use the ACTUAL pure-delayed original law

 u_t=Kx_t+Lw_(t-h),     x0=0,

with vector progressive E1/E2 inputs supported [0,S], and set

 P(K)=A+BK,  Q(K)=A0+B0K,  O(K)=D0+D1K.

K and L are constant real matrices that may be chosen freely but MUST be held fixed as S downarrow 0. For integer k>=1 define the finite polynomial feasibility problem P_k in unknown entries of K,L:

 D1 L=0;
 O(K) W(P(K),Q(K)) B0 L=0
                   for every P/Q word W with weight <=k-2;
 O(K) W(P(K),Q(K)) (B L+C)=0
                   for every word W with weight <=k-3.      (1)

Word weight is `2#P+#Q`, including the empty word at weight0; negative cutoffs give no equations. P_0 is always feasible and contains no equations. Each P_k is a finite set of REAL POLYNOMIAL equalities; their feasible sets are nested.

2. Exact best constant-law exponent

By the pinned operator-zero and gain-expansion sources, P_k is feasible iff some actual pure-delayed K,L law has T0=...=T_(k-1)=0 in the revealed-input expansion.

* If P_(2n+1) is feasible, any solution produces UNIVERSALLY ZERO OUTPUT for every progressive input and every finite T>h, where n is the state dimension.
* Otherwise let kstar be the largest k<=2n with P_k feasible. Every solution of P_kstar has first nonzero coefficient exactly kstar, and BOTH literal gains satisfy

 gamma_i(S)=c_kstar S^(kstar/2)+O(S^((kstar+1)/2)),
 c_kstar>0,  i=1,2.                                       (2)

No other FIXED constant pair (K,L) has a strictly better exponent: a pair violating P_kstar has an earlier nonzero coefficient, and P_(kstar+1) is infeasible. Feasibility of P_kstar certifies existence of a best-exponent fixed pair, but does not choose among leading constants or assert gain-norm attainment when additive input diffusion is present.

For rational/algebraic original matrices, this finite list of polynomial feasibility questions is decidable IN PRINCIPLE in the real closed field. The theorem does not run a quantifier-elimination solver, claim practical complexity or produce explicit numerical K,L without solving (1). For arbitrary real data it is an exact semialgebraic characterization relative to that data.
