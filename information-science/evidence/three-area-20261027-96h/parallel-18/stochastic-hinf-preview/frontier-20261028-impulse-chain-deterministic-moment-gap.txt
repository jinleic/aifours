2. Deterministic moment bound

For any nonzero a define

 I2(a)=E Y(a),
 U4(a)=T integral_0^T E||O X_t^a||^4dt.

Both are finite and nonnegative; Cauchy in time gives E[Y²]<=U4. If I2>0 then U4>0. The Cauchy–Paley estimate P(Y>=I2/2)>=I2²/(4U4) implies the entirely deterministic lower bound

 L(a) >= d(a) + [sqrt(d(a)²+I2(a)/2)-d(a)] I2(a)²/[4U4(a)].  (3)

If I2=0, use the valid bound L(a)>=d(a), with no division by zero. Therefore (3)/||a|| is a universal lower bound on BOTH actual original literal gains. Unlike an RMS substitution, the lower bound is derived FROM the original E sqrt[d²+Y], by a probability lower estimate.

3. Exact moment ODEs and finite visibility test

Let M2(t)=E[X_t X_t^T], M4(t)=E[X_t^(tensor 4)]. Between impulse times they satisfy deterministic finite-dimensional linear ODEs

 M2'=P M2+M2 P^T+Q M2 Q^T,
 M4'=[sum_i P^(i)+sum_(i<j) Q^(i)Q^(j)] M4.

At tj with increment gj xi_j independent of the pre-jump state,

 M2^+=M2^-+gj gj^T,
 M4^+=M4^- + sum_(six slot-pairs) Insert(M2^-,gj,gj) +3 gj^(tensor 4).

The six inserts put two copies of gj into the chosen tensor slots and M2^- into the remaining ordered slots. The odd-Gaussian terms vanish. Contracting M2 with O^T O and M4 with two copies of O^T O, and integrating, gives I2 and U4 exactly as deterministic ODE/integral quantities; no Monte Carlo is required to DEFINE them.

Equivalently, using the second-moment semigroup M_t(V) for homogeneous P,Q,

 I2(a)=sum_j integral_0^(T-tj)
           tr[O M_t(gj gj^T)O^T]dt.                        (4)

All tj<T. Let N be the largest common P,Q-invariant subspace in ker O. The standard zero-semimartingale induction shows each integral in (4) is positive iff gj is outside N. Thus I2(a)>0 iff at least one impulse gj is outside N; this is a finite common-invariant-subspace test, even when O gj=0 initially.
