3. Proof of output noise hiding

Let xd(w) be the deterministic drift response solving dxd=(P xd+f(w))dt, xd(0)=0 for each input path. Cayley–Hamilton makes Rd P-invariant, so xd(t) lies in Rd pathwise. The actual residual y=x-xd solves

 dy=P y dt+[Q y+Q xd+g(w)]dW,   y0=0.

By (R), Q xd+g(w) lies in N almost everywhere; P,Q preserve N. Strong uniqueness keeps y in N for any progressive path-L2 input, including E1-only. Since O N=0,

 z=O xd+o(w)=T w                                             (2)

pathwise, where T is deterministic. The direct atoms E0,Eh vanish; its regular time kernel consists of bounded matrix exponentials convolved with F0,Fh,F(r), plus E(r). H in L2 and finite T make that kernel square-integrable, so T is Hilbert–Schmidt and compact. For random w, (2) holds sample by sample.

Compactness gives a deterministic singular input attaining ||T|| whenever T!=0. It belongs to both E1 and E2, while the pathwise operator bound applies to every E1 input. Thus gamma1=gamma2=||T|| and the gain is attained; if T=0 the gain is 0. Equality gamma=||T|| cannot be pointwise strict for nonzero T, and gamma=0 cannot be strict even when T=0, proving (1).
