1. Original plant, actual preview law, and exact scope

For T>h>=0, S=T-h, a,sigma,b,ell,ch real, and d!=0, consider the ORIGINAL finite-horizon stochastic model and actual static preview law

 dx_t=(a x_t+b u_t+ch w_(t-h))dt+sigma x_t dW_t,
 z_t=d x_t,                    u_t=ell w_t,

with x0=0, w=0 outside [0,S] and progressive E1 disturbance E||w||_L2[0,S]<infinity. Put c0=b ell. Assume c0,ch>=0 and c0+ch>0. The same result also applies if both coefficients are nonpositive, by extracting their common sign. In particular, c0>0 and ch>0 with h>0 is a GENUINELY nonzero preview control: u_t reads w_t while the plant's physical disturbance enters as w_(t-h). Here B0=D1=0; no claim is made for a stochastic control diffusion or direct feedthrough.

We give deterministic exact constants Gamma,H and an explicit epsilon>0 such that, for EVERY progressive E1 (and E2) disturbance,

 E||z||_L2[0,T] <= |d|(1-epsilon) Gamma E||w||_L2[0,S]
                  <= |d|(1-epsilon) H E||w||_L2[0,S].        (A)

Thus the actual original ratio is uniformly strictly below |d| Gamma, and in particular gamma=|d|Gamma is a strictly feasible physical threshold. If one wants an elementary computable sufficient value without Gamma, every gamma>|d|(1-epsilon)H is strictly feasible. This does NOT assert that Gamma equals the true fixed-law gain, does NOT close E1/E2 strict endpoint transfer at that gain, does NOT prove general matrix synthesis and does NOT allow opposite-sign tap cancellation.

2. Exact stochastic two-tap kernel and H

Let alpha=a-sigma²/2, rho=2a+sigma², and Phi(t,s)=exp[alpha(t-s)+sigma(W_t-W_s)]. For 0<=s<=S, 0<=t<=T define the actual pathwise column

 K_s(t)=c0 1_(t>=s) Phi(t,s) + ch 1_(t>=s+h) Phi(t,s+h).

The physical integrating-factor solution is x_t=integral_0^S K_s(t)w_s ds. K_s depends only on W increments after s, so its L2-time norm is independent of F_s. Set

 C(s)=E||K_s||_L2[0,T],     Gamma=(integral_0^S C(s)^2ds)^(1/2),
 ell0(s)=||E K_s||_L2[0,T],
 H=(integral_0^S E||K_s||_L2[0,T]^2ds)^(1/2).

0<Gamma<=H<infinity, ell0(s)<=C(s), and ell0(s)>0 on [0,S). Explicitly, writing

 J_q(x)=integral_0^x e^(qv)dv,
 F_q(x)=integral_0^x (x-v)e^(qv)dv,
 D=ch²+2c0 ch exp(a h),

one has

 ell0(s)^2 = c0² J_(2a)(T-s) + D J_(2a)(S-s),
 H² = c0² [F_rho(T)-F_rho(h)] + D F_rho(S).              (1)

F_q(x)=(e^(qx)-1-qx)/q² for q!=0, with continuous value x²/2 for q=0. To derive the mixed coefficient in H², for t>=s+h write Phi(t,s)=Phi(s+h,s)Phi(t,s+h). Independent increments give

 E[Phi(t,s)Phi(t,s+h)]=e^(a h)e^(rho(t-s-h)).

The same mixed coefficient in ell0² follows by expanding the square of the deterministic mean kernel. These formulas also include h=0, when the two taps combine as c0+ch.

3. Explicit deterministic gap

Define delta=S/8, I=[S/4,S/3], J=[2S/3,3S/4], choose any r>0, and set

 f0(s)=ell0(s)/H,      b0=(integral_0^delta f0(s)^2ds)^(1/2),
 bI=integral_I f0(s)ds,   bJ=integral_J f0(s)ds,
 eta=min{bI/(2 sqrt(|I|)), bJ/(2 sqrt(|J|))},
 p=P(sup_(0<=v<=T-delta)|B_v|<=r)>0,
 m=exp[-(|alpha|T+2|sigma|r)],      M=1/m,
 Q=c0+ch,     cstar=c0 if c0>0, and cstar=ch if c0=0,
 xi=1-sqrt[1-cstar² m² S/(3 Q² M² T)],
 cI=Q m sqrt(2S/3) bI/2,      cJ=Q m sqrt(S/4) bJ/2,
 epsilon=min{p² b0²/[128(1+p)²], p eta²/16,
             xi cI cJ p²/(8H²)}.                         (2)

All terms are strictly positive, and epsilon<1. All quantities are deterministic; p is a standard Brownian finite-time small-ball probability. If desired it has the convergent series

 p=(4/pi) sum_(j>=0) (-1)^j/(2j+1)
         exp[-(2j+1)²pi²(T-delta)/(8r²)].

4. Literal E1 upper bound and almost-known early amplitude

Write U=||w||, O=||integral K_s w_s ds||, R=integral_0^S |w_s| ||K_s|| ds. Pathwise O<=R. Since w_s is F_s measurable and ||K_s|| depends only on future increments,

 E R = E integral_0^S C(s)|w_s|ds <= Gamma E U.              (3)

All quantities are integrable even for E1-only inputs. Normalize EU=1, put f=C/Gamma, and on {U>0} put v=|w|/U, e=||v-f||; on {U=0} put e=0. Let q=1-EO/Gamma>=0. As f and v have unit L2 norms,

 Gamma-E R = Gamma E(U e²)/2, so E(U e²)<=2q.               (4)

The random variable Lambda=||w 1_[0,delta]||/||f 1_[0,delta]|| is F_delta measurable; ||f 1_[0,delta]||>=b0. Hence E|U-Lambda|<=sqrt(2q)/b0. For the independent future event

 H_event={sup_(delta<=t<=T)|W_t-W_delta|<=r},

we get E[U 1_H_event]>=p-(1+p)sqrt(2q)/b0. The bounded below, future-independent mass uses nonanticipation; it would be false for an arbitrary input depending on the entire future W path.

5. Exact separated-column loss on H_event

For delta<=s<=t<=T, m<=Phi(t,s)<=M. On H_event the physical kernels obey ||K_s||<=Q M sqrt(T). For s in I and u in J, K_u vanishes before u+dstar, where dstar=0 if c0>0 and dstar=h if c0=0. Meanwhile K_s is at least cstar m on [s+dstar,u+dstar), an interval of length at least S/3. Thus

 <K_s,K_u> <= (1-xi)||K_s|| ||K_u||.                       (5)

The coefficients have a common sign, so no inter-tap cancellation occurs. Further, when s is in I or J, BOTH taps are present on [s+h,T], giving ||K_s||>=Q m sqrt(S-s). On {e<=eta}, these bounds and f>=f0 imply

 integral_I |w_s|||K_s||ds>=cI U,
 integral_J |w_s|||K_s||ds>=cJ U.                           (6)

Arbitrary signs of w can only enlarge the cross-angle deficit. Integrating the pairwise Hilbert inner-product identity over I x J and J x I yields

 R²-O² >= 2 xi cI cJ U²                                  (7)

on H_event ∩ {e<=eta}. No pointwise input sign restriction is used.

6. Uniform margin

Let R0=4H/p. The U-weight of {e>eta} is <=2q/eta² by (4). The U-weight of {R>R0U} is <=E R/R0<=p/4 by (3). If q is no larger than the first two terms in (2), then H_event ∩ {e<=eta} ∩ {R<=R0U} has U-weight at least p/2. On this event, (7) and R+O<=2R<=2R0U give R-O >= xi cI cJ U/R0. Therefore

 Gamma q>=E(R-O)>=xi cI cJ p/(2R0),
 q>=xi cI cJ p²/(8H²).

If either preliminary bound on q fails, q is already at least the corresponding first or second term in (2). Thus always q>=epsilon, proving (A). The reasoning is all-input, without an optimizer, selected reference disturbance, or E2 moment assumption.
