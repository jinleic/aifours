1. The upper bound on kappa: tangent inequality

For y0>0 and y>=0, concavity gives sqrt(y)<=sqrt(y0)+(y-y0)/(2sqrt(y0)) = (sqrt(y0)+y/sqrt(y0))/2. Fix a probability nu0 with Y0:=integral B^2 dnu0>0 a.s. For any probability nu put Y=integral B^2 dnu. Taking expectations and using Tonelli (all terms nonnegative):

 F(nu) <= (F(nu0)+integral f0 dnu)/2 <= (F(nu0)+sup_[0,1] f0)/2,  f0(t):=E[B_t^2/sqrt(Y0)].   (1.1)

Hence kappa<=(F(nu0)+sup f0)/2. Also kappa>=F(nu0). No property of the maximizer is used.

The explicit nu0 is supported on s=(0.70, 0.76, 0.81, 0.85, 0.885, 0.915, 0.94, 0.96, 0.975, 0.988, 1), with weights p=(127, 174, 229, 260, 293, 320, 337, 342, 349, 434, 7135)/10000; the weights sum to 1. 2. Laplace representations

For y>0: sqrt(y)=pi^(-1/2) integral_0^infinity (1-e^(-u^2 y))u^(-2)du and y^(-1/2)=2pi^(-1/2) integral_0^infinity e^(-u^2 y)du.

Let X=(B_s1,...,B_sK), K=11, with covariance Sigma_ij=min(s_i,s_j); let D=diag(p) and Y0=X^T D X. Gaussian integration gives:
- E e^(-u^2 Y0) = (det Sigma det T(u))^(-1/2), with T(u)=Sigma^(-1)+2u^2 D. This matrix is tridiagonal, with diagonal 1/g_i+1/g_(i+1) (the last entry is 1/g_K), off-diagonal -1/g_(i+1), and gaps g_1=s_1, g_i=s_i-s_(i-1).
- By Gaussian tilting, E[(a^T X)^2 e^(-u^2 Y0)] = E[e^(-u^2 Y0)] a^T T(u)^(-1) a.

Tonelli then gives F(nu0)=pi^(-1/2) integral g, with g(u)=(1-E e^(-u^2 Y0))/u^2; E[Y0^(-1/2)]=2pi^(-1/2) integral E e^(-u^2Y0); and E[(a^TX)^2 Y0^(-1/2)]=2pi^(-1/2) integral E[(a^TX)^2 e^(-u^2Y0)] for a=e_j and a=e_j+-e_(j+1).

Every integrand is nonincreasing in u, because e^(-u^2y) and (1-e^(-u^2y))/u^2 are nonincreasing in u for each y>=0. At u=0 the integrands take the values E Y0, 1 and E(a^TX)^2 respectively, and these are also their suprema.

3. sup f0 by Brownian-bridge interpolation

Given X, the value B_t for t in [s_j,s_(j+1)] equals (1-lambda)X_j+lambda X_(j+1)+xi, where L=s_(j+1)-s_j and lambda=(t-s_j)/L. The variable xi ~ N(0,L lambda(1-lambda)) is independent of X (Brownian-bridge decomposition and Markov property). Therefore

 f0(t)=(1-lambda)^2 A_j + 2lambda(1-lambda) Bj + lambda^2 A_(j+1) + L lambda(1-lambda) E,

with A_j=E[X_j^2 Y0^(-1/2)], Bj=(E[(X_j+X_(j+1))^2 Y0^(-1/2)]-E[(X_j-X_(j+1))^2 Y0^(-1/2)])/4, and E=E[Y0^(-1/2)]. On [0,s_1] the formula is f0=lambda^2 A_1+s_1 lambda(1-lambda)E. All weights are nonnegative on lambda in [0,1]. So substituting upper bounds of A, Bj and E bounds each quadratic from above, and its maximum on [0,1] is computed exactly from the endpoints and the vertex.

5. The explicit physical witness for C2

Fix eta0=37/50, theta=9/40, c=27/100. Assume h<=eta0 S, and let 0<eps<=(1-eta0)S/2. Put u2:=max(eta0 S,h+eps) and G:=eps^(-1/2)(W_(h+eps)-W_h) ~ N(0,1). Define

 w(u)=eps^(-1/2) on [0,eps);  w(u)=(c/eps)^(1/2) 1(|G|<theta) on [u2,u2+eps);  w=0 elsewhere on [0,S].

Admissibility. G is F_(h+eps)-measurable, and F_(h+eps) is contained in F_(u2). Also u2+eps<=eta0 S+2eps<=S. So w is a bounded simple progressive input supported in [0,S]; it belongs to both the E1 and the E2 class. Its energy is ||w||^2=1+c 1_in, with 1_in:=1(|G|<theta). So E||w||=1+(sqrt(1+c)-1)P(|G|<theta)=:D, exactly and for every eps.

The state is:
- x=0 on [0,h];
- x_t=eps^(-1/2)(W_t-W_h) on [h,h+eps];
- x=G on [h+eps,u2+h];
- x_t=G+1_in(c/eps)^(1/2)(W_t-W_(u2+h)) on [u2+h,u2+h+eps];
- x=G+1_in sqrt(c)G' afterwards, where G':=eps^(-1/2)(W_(u2+h+eps)-W_(u2+h)) ~ N(0,1) is independent of F_(u2+h), which contains sigma(G).

Note that u2+h+eps<=T. Put Q=(G+1_in sqrt(c)G')^2 and let R_eps be the integral of x^2 over the two transition windows. Then ||z||^2=R_eps+(u2-eps)G^2+(S-u2-eps)Q. The transition term is small: E R_eps<=eps(1/2+2+2c).

Also, the main term differs from L_0:=eta0 S G^2+(1-eta0)S Q by at most 2eps(G^2+Q). This holds both when u2=eta0 S and when u2=h+eps with h=eta0 S.

Using |sqrt(a+b)-sqrt(a)|<=sqrt(b) and |sqrt(a)-sqrt(a')|<=sqrt|a-a'| (for a, a', b>=0) and Cauchy-Schwarz, E||z|| -> E sqrt(L_0)=sqrt(S) N as eps->0, where

 N:=E sqrt(eta0 G^2+(1-eta0)Q) = 2phi(theta)+T2,  T2:=E[sqrt(eta0G^2+(1-eta0)(G+sqrt(c)G')^2); |G|<theta].

Here 2phi(theta)=E[|G|;|G|>=theta], because on {|G|>=theta} we have Q=G^2. Hence gamma_i(h)>=sqrt(S) N/D for i=1,2.

Interpretation: the controller injects the whole unit of energy at the start; it reads the state level G after the lag; on the event that the level is small, it injects extra energy c whose effect occupies the last fraction (1-eta0) of the output window. The same limit applies for every lag h<=eta0 S, because the second injection may always be postponed to u2=eta0 S. No monotonicity lemma is used.

6. Rigorous evaluation of N/D

Integrating out G' exactly, with alpha=u^2(1-eta0):

 E[e^(-alpha(G+sqrt(c)G')^2)|G]=(1+2alpha c)^(-1/2) exp(-alpha G^2/(1+2alpha c)).

With K(u)=2(u^2 eta0+alpha/(1+2alpha c)) this gives

 E[e^(-u^2 Q2);|G|<theta] = (1+2alpha c)^(-1/2)(1+K)^(-1/2) erf(theta sqrt((1+K)/2)),

where Q2 is the quantity inside the root in T2. Therefore T2=pi^(-1/2) integral_0^infinity [P_in - E(e^(-u^2Q2);|G|<theta)]u^(-2)du, with P_in=erf(theta/sqrt2). The integrand is nonincreasing in u. It is bounded below at the right Darboux endpoint (6018 nodes on [0,128]). The tail is bounded with E[e^(-u^2Q2);in]<=(1+2u^2 eta0)^(-1/2)<=(2eta0)^(-1/2)/u.

The error function is enclosed through erf(sqrt y)=2pi^(-1/2) sqrt(y) S(y), where S(y)=integral_0^1 e^(-y s^2)ds = sum (-1)^n y^n/(n!(2n+1)):
- S is decreasing in y, so y may be rounded down dyadically for the upper bound;
- the alternating terms decrease for n>y, so consecutive partial sums bracket S;
- for y>9 the bound erf<=1 is used.

e^(-theta^2/2) is enclosed by alternating partial sums. Output: T2>=0.0451169, 2phi(theta)>=0.7779415, D<=1.0225985, and N/D>=0.8048697.
