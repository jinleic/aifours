2. New normal equation for EVERY bounded progressive multiplier

 (ii) E <p,Z(v)>_I = gamma E <u,v>_J
      = gamma E integral_J alpha(t)|w(t)|^2/U dt,                 (N)

with the final integrand defined as zero on U=0. The output map here is the actual original SDE caller, not an auxiliary operator or an assumed time-multiplier commutation.

Proof. For |epsilon| small enough, w+epsilon v=(1+epsilon alpha)w remains a progressive E1 input with positive expected norm. Strong SDE linearity/local uniqueness gives the actual output Z(w+epsilon v)=Z(w)+epsilon Z(v). Since gamma is the E1 operator bound,

 F(epsilon)=gamma E||w+epsilon v||-E||Z(w)+epsilon Z(v)|| >=0,

Let n=E<u,v>, m=E<p,Z(v)>, and q=E[1_{V=0}||Z(v)||]. On U=0, v=0; hence the input norm has the same left/right derivative n. On V>0 the output norm derivative is the inner product with p; on V=0 its right derivative is +||Z(v)|| and its left derivative is -||Z(v)||. Each difference quotient is dominated by the corresponding integrable input/output path norm by the triangle inequality, so dominated convergence justifies taking expectations. Since zero is a minimum,

 gamma n-m-q >=0,    gamma n-m+q <=0.

Thus q=0 and gamma n=m, proving both assertions. No expectation of a second moment, optional projection of a costate, or adjoint/stochastic-Hamiltonian theorem is used. The identity is a necessary stationarity condition at an ACTUAL E1 norm attainer, not claimed sufficient.
