1. The pulse states and exact variation-of-constants identity

For fixed h>0, chain coefficient a and epsilon pulses w_epsilon from the source, write the actual closed SDE

 dx_epsilon=(P x_epsilon+f_epsilon)dt+(Q x_epsilon+g_epsilon)dW,
 z_epsilon=O x_epsilon+atom_epsilon+regular_epsilon.

The pulse input is deterministic. Let F_t solve dF=P Fdt+Q FdW,F0=I. Its inverse solves the usual globally linear matrix SDE, so finite moments of sup||F||,sup||F^(-1)|| and their short increments exist on every finite horizon. Exact Itô variation of constants is

 x_epsilon(t)=F_t [ integral_0^t F_s^(-1)(f_epsilon(s)-Q g_epsilon(s))ds
                 + integral_0^t F_s^(-1)g_epsilon(s)dW_s ]. (1)

The correction -Q g is essential; it is NOT dropped. All f_epsilon,g_epsilon are deterministic here because the physical controller taps are deterministic and w_epsilon is deterministic; the state feedback is already in P,Q.

2. Remainder estimates and the apparent impulse state

For n fixed pulse coefficients, the pulse family's deterministic L1 norm is sqrt(epsilon) sum_j||aj||, while its L2 norm is ||a||. The direct drift and direct diffusion atoms have L1 norm O(sqrt(epsilon)); Young gives every H*w_epsilon term L2 norm O(sqrt(epsilon)). Thus

 ||f_epsilon||_L1=O(sqrt(epsilon)),
 ||g_epsilon - sum_j Gj 1_[tj,tj+epsilon]/sqrt(epsilon)||_L2=O(sqrt(epsilon)),
 ||g_epsilon||_L1=O(sqrt(epsilon)).                          (2)

The same estimates apply to the regular distributed output. Use (1), Itô/BDG and the finite sup moments of F,F^(-1). For each pulse,

 integral_tj^(tj+epsilon) F_s^(-1)Gj dW_s/sqrt(epsilon)
   = F_tj^(-1)Gj xi_j^epsilon + error_j,
 xi_j^epsilon=(W_(tj+epsilon)-W_tj)/sqrt(epsilon),
 E||error_j||² ->0.                                        (3)

Indeed Itô isometry and the short-increment moment estimate for F^(-1) bound the squared error by O(epsilon); higher moments control multiplication by F_t. The drift correction in (1) vanishes by the L1 bounds in (2), and the regular stochastic integral vanishes by the L2 bound. A standard E2 state-sup estimate, uniform because ||w_epsilon||_2=||a||, controls the union of pulse windows, whose total measure is O(epsilon).

Consequently, in expected L2-time norm,

 x_epsilon(t) - sum_(j:t>=tj) Phi(t,tj)Gj xi_j^epsilon ->0.  (4)

The right side may look anticipative INSIDE a pulse window; that is harmless in (4) because the union of those windows has vanishing measure and uniformly bounded second moments. Outside them the corresponding xi_j^epsilon has already been revealed.

3. Exact independence in the weak limit

The finite vector xi^epsilon consists of independent standard Gaussians. Jointly with the Brownian path W in C[0,T], it is a centered Gaussian object and

 |Cov(xi_j^epsilon,W_t)| <= sqrt(epsilon)  for every t.

Consequently `(W,xi^epsilon)` converges weakly in C[0,T] x R^(n+1) to `(Wbar,xi)` where Wbar is Brownian and xi_j are independent standard Gaussians independent of Wbar. The Brownian path marginal and finite Gaussian vectors are tight, and all finite-dimensional cross covariances tend to zero.

For ONE Brownian driver and constant P,Q, the joint fundamental flow Phi(t,s) is continuous as a function of the uniform driving path after the standard transformation F_t=exp(QW_t)Z_t with

 Z'_t=exp(-QW_t)(P-Q²/2)exp(QW_t)Z_t.

The continuous mapping theorem therefore sends the apparent impulse process in (4), as an L2-time-valued random element, to the named source's independent-Gaussian impulse state X^a. Finite n and standard uniform E2 state/flow moment bounds give uniform integrability of its path norm. No finite-dimensional projection is asserted to prove a path-space statement on its own.

4. Restoring direct atoms and the expected path norm

The actual direct atoms on the n+1 pulse windows have exact squared path norm sum_j||Dj||². Outside those windows they vanish. The actual state output on the windows and the regular distributed output on ALL times tend to zero in expected path norm by the estimates above. Therefore the whole actual z_epsilon has the same limiting expected norm as

 [sum_j||Dj||²+integral_0^T||O X_t^a||²dt]^(1/2).

Its expectation converges by the uniform integrability in section 3, establishing the full limit, not merely a liminf. The h=0 case uses one combined atom/one Gaussian and the same argument. Each w_epsilon is deterministic E2 and hence E1; the bound applies to both original expected-path gain suprema without a pointwise endpoint transfer inference.
