1. Actual original law and universal strict gap

For T>h>=S=T-h>0, scalar Brownian W, progressive vector E1/E2 w supported [0,S] and zero x0, use the original pure-delayed law `u=Kx+Lh w_(t-h)`. Write its closed coefficients P,Q,F,G,O,E as in the named source and assume

 P=Q=0,    E=0,    O F=0,    J:=O G !=0.                (R)

J is an arbitrary real p x m matrix of finite rank r>=1. The ACTUAL output is

 z_t=integral_0^(t-h) J w_s dW_(h+s),  h<=t<=T,

and zero before h. Let gamma_lit be the true progressive E1/E2 expected-path gain; let gamma_RMS denote separately the second-moment ratio on E2 inputs. Define

 c_r=E||Z||_R^r/sqrt(r)=sqrt(2) Gamma((r+1)/2)/[sqrt(r) Gamma(r/2)],
 kappa_r=[c_r+sqrt(2-c_r²)]/2.

Then

 sqrt(2/pi)||J||op sqrt(S) <= gamma_lit
   <=kappa_r ||J||op sqrt(S) < ||J||op sqrt(S)=gamma_RMS. (1)

The strict comparison is a statement ABOUT the user's literal gain; no RMS substitution or equality of feasibility criteria is asserted. For J=0 both gains are 0 and the strict comparison is inapplicable. The true literal gain, its pointwise endpoint and its maximizer are NOT computed for rank>1.

2. A Gaussian vector norm bound at fixed trace

For any centered Gaussian Y in an r-dimensional Euclidean subspace with covariance eigenvalues lambda_i>=0,

 E||Y|| <=c_r sqrt(sum_i lambda_i).                         (2)

Indeed `f(lambda)=E sqrt(sum lambda_i Z_i²)` is symmetric and concave on the nonnegative cone. Averaging over all permutations of lambda and applying concavity shows it is maximized at equal eigenvalues, giving (2). Since chi_r is nonconstant, strict Jensen gives c_r<1. The formula for c_r follows by the elementary chi-distribution integral.

3. Uniform true-literal upper, preserving conditional information

By the named revelation theorem, the actual progressive E1 literal gain is the supremum over deterministic input paths v. Take ||v||_L2[0,S]=1 and define

 m=(1/S) integral_0^S s||v_s||²ds in [0,1],
 X=z_S=integral_0^S J v_s dWtilde_s.

Itô isometry yields

 E||z||_L2²=integral_0^S(S-s)||Jv_s||²ds
            <=||J||op² S(1-m),
 E||z-X||_L2²=integral_0^S s||Jv_s||²ds
              <=||J||op² S m.

X is centered Gaussian in range J, of dimension r, with trace covariance <=||J||op². By (2), E||X||<=c_r||J||op. Jensen/Minkowski therefore give

 E||z||/(||J||op sqrt(S)) <=min{sqrt(1-m), c_r+sqrt(m)}.

The two curves cross at `sqrt(m)=[sqrt(2-c_r²)-c_r]/2`; their minimum is universally <=kappa_r<1. Conditioning on the whole revealed random input path transfers this bound to EVERY actual progressive E1 input in the original ratio, proving the upper in (1).

4. Lower and exact RMS value

Take a top right singular vector of J and deterministic L2 pulses near input time0. After each pulse the state is a scalar normal times J times that vector, giving a literal ratio approaching sqrt(2/pi)||J||op sqrt(S). This proves the lower in (1). Conditional Itô isometry gives E||z||²<=S||J||op² E||w||² for E2 w, and the same pulses approach equality, hence gamma_RMS=||J||op sqrt(S). This second-moment value is used only to demonstrate non-equivalence with the actual literal criterion.

5. Boundaries and imports

Named full-input revelation, finite-dimensional Gaussian norm concavity, chi moments, Itô isometry/Jensen/Minkowski. P=Q=0, E=OF=0, scalar W, h>=S and finite r are required. This does not identify the true literal gain or pointwise endpoint when rank>1; it does not cover output-visible state multiplicative noise/general preview, infinite horizon, selected-reference calibration, held source, external model/API, independent/formal review or novelty.
