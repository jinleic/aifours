1. Exact closed constant for the actual original law

For the actual original stochastic law dx=w dW,z=x, u=w, x0=0, with any finite T>h>=0 and disturbance support [0,T-h], let tau be the first exit of standard Brownian B from (-1,1). The named source proves

both in the SAME original expected-path ratio. Let Catalan's constant be the absolutely convergent alternating series

 G=sum_(k>=0) (-1)^k/(2k+1)^2.

Then the exact exit-time half moment is

and hence

 gamma_prog,2 >= [pi²/(4 sqrt(2pi) G)] sqrt(T)
              > (10000/9306) sqrt(T) > gamma_det.          (2)

2. Brownian exit-series derivation

The standard killed-heat-equation eigenfunction expansion for Brownian survival in (-1,1), started at 0, is

 P(tau>t)=(4/pi) sum_(k>=0) [(-1)^k/(2k+1)]
                 exp[-(2k+1)^2 pi² t/8],  t>0.

The tail-integral identity E sqrt(tau)=integral_0^infinity P(tau>t)/(2 sqrt(t))dt and the gamma integral give

 integral_0^infinity exp[-(2k+1)^2 pi² t/8]/(2sqrt(t))dt
   =sqrt(2pi)/[pi(2k+1)].

The sum of the ABSOLUTE values of these integrated terms is finite, because sum 1/(2k+1)² converges; Tonelli/Fubini is therefore legitimate despite the alternating pointwise expansion. Substitution yields (1). The killed-heat spectral expansion is an explicitly named classical import, not an independent proof of the user's larger problem.
