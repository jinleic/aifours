1. Formal operator spaces

Fix input/output finite dimensions and a single Brownian path on [0,1]. For k=0 let U0 be the finite-dimensional space of multiplication operators v->E v by constant matrices. For k>=1 let Uk be the span of ALL unit-window operators with arbitrary constant matrix coefficients built from:

 * an additive-noise base `integral Gv dB` followed by nested time drifts and Q-Itô integrals of combined weight k-1;
 * a drift base `integral Fv dt` followed by nested time drifts and Q-Itô integrals of combined weight k-2.

A time drift has weight2 and an Itô integration weight1. The Brownian noise is the SAME in every nested integral. For each finite k only finitely many words and coefficient entries exist, so Uk is a finite-dimensional subspace of B(L2_input,L1(Omega;L2_output)). Identical kernels within one Uk are automatically quotiented by taking the span.

Claim: for every finite K, U0+...+UK is a DIRECT sum.

2. Separate noise counts and base types

Suppose a finite sum of operators from the Uk is identically zero. Test deterministic inputs; their outputs are finite sums of homogeneous Wiener chaoses, so L2 orthogonality forces each noise-count j component to vanish separately. The coefficient kernels have finite L2 moments for each deterministic input even though the operator norm is L1.

For j>=1 and `0<t1<...<tj<t<1`, stochastic Fubini yields the ordered Itô kernel

 P_G(t1,...,tj,t) v(t1)
 + integral_0^t1 P_F(r,t1,...,tj,t)v(r)dr.                (4)

P_G and P_F are finite matrix polynomials in the chronological time gaps. To separate them, use the COMPLEXIFIED zero operator on `v_omega(r)=a exp(i omega r)` for any fixed input vector a. For each simplex point, the P_F integral tends to zero by Riemann–Lebesgue; polynomial boundedness on the finite simplex gives L2 domination. The L2 norm of the P_G term is independent of omega, because |exp(i omega t1)|=1. The zero sum therefore forces P_G a=0 in L2, for every a, hence P_G=0. The real zero-operator premise implies its complexification is zero; no complex physical disturbance is required.

With P_G=0, (4) has zero Volterra integral for every deterministic v. A countable dense L2 input family plus Fubini makes P_F(r,t1,...,tj,t)=0 almost everywhere before the input integration. The j=0 case is analogous: high frequency first isolates any direct multiplication U0, then the ordinary Volterra kernel vanishes.

3. Different weighted degrees are independent

In the additive-noise base with total chaos count j, the first stochastic time is t1; a weight-k term has drift count i=(k-j)/2 and its gap polynomial P_G is HOMOGENEOUS of total degree i in `t2-t1,...,t-tj`. In the drift base the input time r precedes t1; a weight-k term has i=(k-j-2)/2 and P_F is homogeneous of degree i in `t1-r,...,t-tj`. Invalid counts are absent.

For fixed chaos and base type, distinct weights have distinct total polynomial degrees. The gap variables range over an open positive simplex with sum strictly below1; there is no fixed-sum identity such as `sum gaps=1`. A finite polynomial vanishing there has every homogeneous component zero. The j=0 drift kernels use the same argument in the single gap t-r. Therefore every Uk component of the original zero sum is zero. This proves the direct sum, without assuming individual matrix words within one weight are linearly independent.

4. The precise quantitative implication

Equip each Uk with its inherited operator norm L2_input -> L1(Omega;L2_output). Because the finite sum is direct, the continuous sum map from the finite-dimensional product to its image has a bounded inverse. Equivalently

 kappa_K=min{ ||sum_(k=0)^K u_k|| : u_k in Uk, max_k||u_k||=1 } >0.

The minimizing set is compact and directness excludes zero. Thus for every r in [0,1] and u_k in Uk,

 ||sum_(k=0)^K r^k u_k|| >= kappa_K max_k r^k||u_k||.    (5)

The constant depends only on K and input/output dimensions through the chosen universal spaces; no numerical value is claimed. Applying it to the actual P,Q,F,G,O,E weighted coefficients gives exactly the uniform separation used in the named bounded-controller theorem. The Itô-Picard remainder and compact parameter separation are independent obligations in that source; this addendum does not replace them.
