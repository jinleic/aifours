1. Fixed-K physical class and the h=0 residual

Use the actual original scalar-W full matrix law

 dx=(Ax+Bu+Cw_(t-h))dt+(A0x+B0u)dW,
 z=D0x+D1u,
 u=Kx+L0 w_t+Lh w_(t-h)+integral_0^h H(r)w_(t-r)dr,

with zero initial state, progressive vector E1 input supported on [0,T-h], fixed finite real matrices and fixed K, T>h>0. B0,D1,Q=A0+B0K and all tap directions are arbitrary.

Let P=A+BK, O=D0+D1K, N=maximal common (P,Q)-invariant subspace of ker O, Pi=orthogonal projection onto N^perp, Astar=[D1;Pi B0], Z=orthonormal basis of ker Astar and Bbar=BZ. Define the projection residual

 J=I-(Pi Bbar)(Pi Bbar)^dagger,
 eta=||J Pi C||_F.                                          (1)

The precursor proves that eta=0 is EXACTLY the fixed-K h=0 universal zero-output feasibility condition. Suppose eta>0, so no h=0 law in this class has universally zero output. The positive-h feasibility may or may not hold; the bound below is necessary whenever it does.

2. Exact finite-h necessary trade-off

For EVERY positive h and every fixed-K full-preview law that yields z=0 for all progressive E1 disturbances,

 eta <= ||J Pi (exp(Ph)-I)B||op ||L0||_F
        + [integral_0^h ||J Pi exp(Pt)B||op²dt]^(1/2)
             ||H||_L2([0,h];F).                             (2)

Here ||H||_L2,F²=integral_0^h ||H(r)||_F²dr. The delayed atom Lh does not appear because its visible contribution is constrained to range(Pi Bbar); unbounded Lh alone cannot reduce eta.

In particular, for 0<h<=1,

 eta <= exp(||P||h)||B||op [ h||P||op ||L0||_F
                              +sqrt(h)||H||_L2,F ].         (3)

Therefore no family of exact zero-output laws for h down to 0 can keep BOTH ||L0||_F and ||H||_L2,F uniformly bounded: their maximum tends to infinity along every sequence h->0 on which such laws are chosen. They need not individually diverge, since the burden can alternate. This conclusion concerns only exact rejection; it does not establish any lower bound on the norm of a positive-gain approximately feasible controller.

3. Derivation from the exact full-preview endpoint

Universal zero output implies the imported exact atom conditions Astar L0=Astar Lh=0 and finite endpoint condition

 Pi[exp(Ph)B L0+B Lh+C
     +integral_0^h exp(P(h-r))B H(r)dr]=0.                 (4)

Since L0,Lh lie in ker Astar=range Z, J Pi B L0=J Pi B Lh=0. Apply J to (4), write exp(Ph)B L0=B L0+(exp(Ph)-I)B L0, and take Frobenius/operator norms. Cauchy–Schwarz on the last integral yields (2). The matrix exponential estimates ||exp(Ph)-I||<=h||P||e^(||P||h) and ||exp(Pt)||<=e^(||P||h) give (3). No stochastic optimization, endpoint-gain equality or selected reference is used.

4. Exact example and two different ways to pay

For the pinned horizon source's physical example

 P=[[0,1],[0,0]], Q=0, B=[[0,0],[1,0]], C=e1,
 O=[1,0], D1=[0,1], B0=0, K=0,

one has N=0, Pi=I, Bbar=e2, eta=1. Equation (2) sharpens exactly to

 1 <= h |(L0)_1| + h^(3/2)/sqrt(3) ||H1||_L2[0,h].       (5)

Two explicit zero-output laws for every h>0 demonstrate the trade-off:

 * Kernel-heavy: L0=Lh=0, H1(t)=-6/h²+12t/h³, H2(t)=3(t/h)²-2(t/h)³. Its exact squared kernel norm is 12/h³+13h/35.
 * Atom-heavy: L0=[-1/h,0]^T, Lh=[1/h,0]^T, H1=0,H2(t)=t/h. Its auxiliary state m(t)=[-t/h,-1/h]^T has early output m1+H2=0 and endpoint m(h)+B Lh+C=0. This kernel's squared norm is h/3, but BOTH atom norms grow as 1/h.

Thus it would be false to claim that every distributed kernel must itself diverge; the correct universal statement is the joint trade-off (2)–(3).
