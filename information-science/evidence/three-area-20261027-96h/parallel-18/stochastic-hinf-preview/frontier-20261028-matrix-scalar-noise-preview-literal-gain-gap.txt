1. Actual original matrix plant and feedback/preview law

Take any n>=1, scalar disturbance w, p-output z, control dimension r>=1, T>h>=0, S=T-h>0, real constant matrices A(n x n), B(n x r), K(r x n), D(p x n), vectors ell(r x 1), c(n x 1), and real sigma. For the original finite-horizon model and actual law

 dx=(Ax+Bu+c w_(t-h))dt+sigma I_n x dW,
 z=Dx,            u=Kx+ell w_t,

use x0=0, w supported in [0,S], canonical Brownian filtration and every nonzero progressive E1 disturbance E||w||_L2[0,S]<infinity (hence also every E2 input). The matrix drift/feedback A,B,K and output D are unrestricted; static preview ell and delayed vector c may point in arbitrary directions and can destructively interfere. Restrictions: ONE scalar disturbance, common scalar multiplicative state noise A0=sigma I, B0=0, D1=0; no distributed preview tap or direct output feedthrough is asserted.

Put P=A+BK and b=B ell. In every nonzero physical-output case, explicit deterministic Gamma,H and epsilon>0 below satisfy the ACTUAL literal criterion

 E||z||_L2[0,T] <= (1-epsilon) Gamma E||w||_L2[0,S]
                 <= (1-epsilon) H E||w||_L2[0,S].          (A)

Thus gamma=Gamma is uniformly strictly feasible for this actual feedback/preview law, and any gamma>(1-epsilon)H is an explicit sufficient threshold. 2. Exact matrix-column kernel and visibility

Define deterministic vector functions g_v(t)=D exp(Pt)v for v in R^n, and G_v(x)=(integral_0^x ||g_v(t)||_R^p²dt)^(1/2). Call v VISIBLE iff D P^j v!=0 for at least one j in {0,...,n-1}; by analyticity and Cayley–Hamilton this is equivalent to G_v(x)>0 for EVERY x>0. All norms in this paragraph are real Euclidean.

Because sigma I commutes with P, the actual state transition is exp(P(t-s)) nu(t,s), where

 nu(t,s)=exp[-sigma²(t-s)/2+sigma(W_t-W_s)].

For 0<=s<=S, define the physical output column in L2([0,T];R^p)

 K_s(t)=1_(t>=s) nu(t,s) g_b(t-s)
       +1_(t>=s+h) nu(t,s+h) g_c(t-s-h).

The actual original output is z_t=integral_0^S K_s(t) w_s ds. The column uses only Brownian increments after s and is independent of F_s. It is a vector-valued time function, not a scalar positive kernel.

The output operator vanishes for every input exactly in these cases:

 * h>0 and BOTH b and c are invisible;
 * h=0 and b+c is invisible.

Indeed h>0 exposes the b-column on its separate [s,s+h) prefix; if it is invisible only the delayed c-column remains. If h=0 the immediate columns combine before applying D exp(Pt). Conversely invisible transfer vectors vanish identically by Cayley–Hamilton. In a zero case, gamma>0 is strictly feasible and gamma=0 is not strictly feasible for nonzero inputs. In every nonzero case the literal gain is >0, as proved in section 6.

3. Nonnegative all-matrix Gaussian bound

Define C(s)=E||K_s||, Gamma=(integral_0^S C(s)^2ds)^(1/2), ell0(s)=||E K_s|| and H=(integral_0^S E||K_s||²ds)^(1/2). Jensen gives 0<ell0(s)<=C(s), s<S in a nonzero case, and Gamma<=H<infinity. The nonnegative expressions are

 ell0(s)² = integral_0^h ||g_b(v)||²dv
            + integral_0^(S-s) ||g_b(h+v)+g_c(v)||²dv,

 H² = S integral_0^h e^(sigma² v)||g_b(v)||²dv
      + integral_0^S (S-v)e^(sigma² v)
          [||g_b(h+v)+g_c(v)||²
           +(e^(sigma² h)-1)||g_b(h+v)||²]dv.              (1)

For h=0, these use g_(b+c) throughout. To derive (1), for t>=s+h factor nu(t,s)=nu(t,s+h)nu(s+h,s), then use E nu(s+h,s)=1 and E nu(s+h,s)²=e^(sigma²h). The two increment blocks are independent. The early segment 0<=t-s<h contributes the first integral. No sign, commutation beyond sigma I, approximation, or hidden E2 input assumption is used.

4. Explicit gap constants

In each nonzero case define Q,P0,lambda_I,lambda_J as follows:

 * h>0 and b visible: Q=G_b(T)+G_c(T), P0=m G_b(min(h,S/3)), lambda_I=lambda_J=m G_b(h).
 * h>0, b invisible, c visible: Q=G_c(T), P0=m G_c(S/3), lambda_I=m G_c(2S/3), lambda_J=m G_c(S/4).
 * h=0, b+c visible: Q=G_(b+c)(T), P0=m G_(b+c)(S/3), lambda_I=m G_(b+c)(2S/3), lambda_J=m G_(b+c)(S/4).

 xi=1-sqrt[1-P0²/(M² Q²)],

 epsilon=min{p0² b0²/[128(1+p0)²], p0 eta²/16,

The formula is explicitly computable from deterministic matrix exponentials, one-dimensional integrals and the Brownian small-ball probability. The norm/shape estimates below ensure P0<=M Q and epsilon<1. No smallness or sign condition on the plant matrices is required.

5. Separated vector-column proof

On H_event={sup_(delta<=t<=T)|W_t-W_delta|<=r0}, m<=nu(t,s)<=M whenever delta<=s<=t<=T. Also ||K_s||<=M Q by the actual vector-column triangle inequality. For s in I, u in J, the L2 time-supports have a uniform nonzero gap:

 * h>0,b visible: K_u=0 before u, while K_s(t)=nu(t,s)g_b(t-s) on the first interval of length min(h,u-s)>=min(h,S/3). Its L2 norm there is at least P0. Its whole norm is >=m G_b(h), even if later b/c terms cancel.
 * h>0,b invisible: the only column is delayed c; before u+h the s-column alone has length at least S/3 and norm >=P0. Its whole norm on I or J is at least the respective lambda above.
 * h=0: combine b+c and use the corresponding ordinary Volterra support prefix and lambda bounds.

Thus, even for p>1 and arbitrary tap directions,

 |<K_s,K_u>| <= (1-xi)||K_s||||K_u||.                      (3)

This follows by projecting the first column onto the time support of the second; the ABSOLUTE inner product permits arbitrary signs and vector orientations. On e=|| |w|/||w|| - C/Gamma ||<=eta, the deterministic inequality C/Gamma>=f0 implies

 integral_I |w_s|||K_s||ds>=cI ||w||,
 integral_J |w_s|||K_s||ds>=cJ ||w||.

Therefore R²-O²>=2xi cI cJ ||w||² for R=integral |w_s|||K_s||ds and O=||integral K_s w_s ds||. The double integral of the vector Hilbert inner-product deficit is nonnegative for every pair, including opposite scalar input signs.

6. Progressive E1 proof and zero/nonzero gain

Column/future independence gives exactly ER=E integral C(s)|w_s|ds<=Gamma E||w||, so O is integrable even for E1-only inputs. Normalize E||w||=1 and set q=1-EO/Gamma. The predecessor's elementary Hilbert identity gives E[||w|| e²]<=2q. Its first-delta amplitude is F_delta measurable and is L1-close to ||w|| by <=sqrt(2q)/b0; independent future H_event therefore has ||w||-weighted mass at least p0-(1+p0)sqrt(2q)/b0. Markov gives U-weight <=2q/eta² to e>eta and <=p0/4 to R>(4H/p0)||w||. As shown explicitly in the predecessor, if q is below the first two terms of (2), at least p0/2 U-weight remains where (3) supplies R-O>=xi cI cJ U/(4H/p0). Hence q is at least the third term in (2); if either preliminary bound fails it is already at least epsilon. This proves (A), including all E1 disturbances and without requiring a maximizer.

In a nonzero visibility case the random vector-column kernel is a nonzero Hilbert–Schmidt operator on every Brownian path because its nonzero visible transfer prefix has positive time measure. A fixed countable dense family of deterministic scalar L2 inputs cannot all map to zero on such a path; one member has positive-probability nonzero output. The established E1 bound makes its expectation finite, so the actual literal gain is strictly positive. This is a general density argument, not an optimized selected witness.
