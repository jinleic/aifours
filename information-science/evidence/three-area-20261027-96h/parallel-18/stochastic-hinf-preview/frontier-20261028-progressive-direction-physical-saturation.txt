FRONTIER INFO-3: PATHWISE CAUCHY SATURATION CANNOT PRODUCE AN E1-ONLY INPUT IN A SCALAR PHYSICAL SDE

Status: NEW WRITTEN CANDIDATE, UNREVIEWED. Actual native prewrite clock Wed Oct 28 17:50:34 PDT 2026. This is a precise physical exclusion of one prospective endpoint-counterexample mechanism; it does NOT prove that a literal expected-path-L2 endpoint attainer must saturate the displayed pathwise inequality, and does NOT solve general synthesis or the full E1/E2 question.

1. A general canonical-filtration direction lemma

Let the completed natural Brownian filtration on [0,T] have trivial F0. Let q be ANY real vector-valued progressive E2 process such that q(t,omega) is nonzero dt x P-almost everywhere. Let w be a progressive E1 process and suppose there is one finite random scalar Lambda with

 w_t(omega)=Lambda(omega) q_t(omega) for dt x P-a.e. (t,omega).   (1)

Then Lambda is deterministic almost surely and w is E2. If w is nonzero, Lambda is a nonzero deterministic scalar.

Proof. Fubini gives a full-Lebesgue-measure set of times at which (1) holds almost surely and q_t is nonzero almost surely. Choose a deterministic sequence of such times t_n decreasing to zero. At each t_n,

 Lambda=<w_(t_n),q_(t_n)>/|q_(t_n)|^2

is F_(t_n)-measurable up to null sets. Therefore Lambda is F_(0+)-measurable; usual right-continuity and the canonical Brownian zero-one property make it a deterministic constant. Since q is E2 and the constant is finite, w is E2. The proof permits a different null set for each chosen time and does not require an uncountable simultaneous path equality or any division on q=0.

The result is deliberately stronger than the previously supported revealed-amplitude/future-shape subclass in a DIFFERENT direction: Lambda is permitted to be any terminal random variable before the proof, and the signal need not start after a positive reveal time. The price is the explicit pathwise collinearity with an E2 direction that is already nonzero at arbitrarily early times. No such collinearity is assumed for a general endpoint attainer.

2. The actual original stochastic plant and exact solution

For arbitrary real a,sigma, nonzero real input vector c, and finite T>0, take the ORIGINAL scalar-state, vector-input h=0 plant

 dx=(a x+c^T w)dt+sigma x dW,  x(0)=0,  z=d x,

with any fixed d!=0 and fixed law u=0 (B=B0=D1=0,C=c^T,D0=d). This is a genuine member of the original constant-coefficient linear SDE class; for sigma!=0 its physical state and output have observable Brownian diffusion.

Define the strictly positive stochastic exponential

 Phi_t=exp[(a-sigma^2/2)t+sigma W_t],
 q_t=Phi_t^(-1)c.

Itô's product rule, applied to Phi^(-1)x and localized for an E1 input, gives the ACTUAL solution

 x_t=Phi_t integral_0^t Phi_s^(-1)c^T w_s ds.                    (2)

No quotient at zero is used. Phi and its inverse are continuous and finite on a compact horizon; pathwise E1 input energy is finite, so (2) is well-defined. The local product calculation/strong uniqueness identifies it with the same original state.

For EVERY sample and t<=T, ordinary time-path Cauchy-Schwarz gives

 |x_t| <= Phi_t [integral_0^t Phi_s^(-2)|c|^2 ds]^(1/2)
                    [integral_0^t |w_s|^2 ds]^(1/2).            (3)

At final t=T the noise in the kernel is genuinely visible in the original x/z; (3) is a pathwise estimate, not the printed expected-path-norm gain.

3. Exact exclusion of a heavy endpoint under pathwise saturation

Suppose a nonzero progressive E1 input saturates (3) almost surely at t=T. Since Phi_T>0, c!=0 and q is nonzero on the time domain, equality in Hilbert Cauchy-Schwarz forces

 w_s(omega)=Lambda(omega) Phi_s^(-1)c

for one finite real scalar Lambda on each path (zero-input paths use Lambda=0). The direction q=Phi^(-1)c is progressive, nonzero everywhere and E2: its EXACT expected energy is

 E integral_0^T |q_s|^2ds
   = |c|^2 integral_0^T exp[(-2a+3sigma^2)s] ds < infinity.     (4)

Equation (4) follows from the ordinary Gaussian exponential moment and is valid for every real a,sigma, including the zero-exponent case where the integral is T. By the direction lemma Lambda is deterministic and w∈E2.

Therefore **no E1-only progressive input can realize pathwise final-time Cauchy saturation** for ANY plant in this scalar physical family. In particular, if a proposed proof of an E1-only literal endpoint attainer requires this very saturation, that mechanism is impossible here. Conversely every deterministic nonzero scalar multiple of q is a genuine E2 input saturating (3) for all times t, with actual state x given by (2).

This statement does NOT assert that the original gain is given by the random pathwise bound (3), or that a true expected-path-norm maximizing input must saturate (3). Averages of nonlinear norms can have a different optimizer. The actual literal endpoint transfer for this observable-noise family is therefore still OPEN.

4. Why finite positive preview is not silently included

At h>0 the original drift forcing is c^T w(t-h). The terminal integral's input direction becomes Phi_(r+h)^(-1)c on r∈[0,T-h]. This involves Brownian information AFTER input time r and is generally not progressively measurable at r. Thus the F0/direction lemma above cannot simply be applied to that direction. No positive-delay theorem, ad hoc anticipative disturbance or invalid time relabeling is claimed.
