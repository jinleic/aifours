2. A rank-two Gaussian lower estimate

The input's normalized quadratic-energy clock A increases linearly 0->1/2 on [0,delta], stays 1/2 until epsilon-delta, increases 1/2->1 by epsilon and stays1 afterward. First compare it with the relaxed step clock `Abar=1/2` on [0,epsilon] and `Abar=1` afterward. The relaxed output functional equals

 Fbar=E sqrt[(1-epsilon) B1²+epsilon B_(1/2)²].

Its two Gaussian covariance eigenvalues have trace 1-epsilon/2 and determinant epsilon(1-epsilon)/4. Consequently lambda_max>=1-epsilon and lambda_min>=epsilon(1-epsilon)/4. If X,Z are independent standard normals and s=sqrt(epsilon)/2,

 Fbar>=sqrt(1-epsilon) E sqrt(X²+s²Z²).                    (2)

On `|Z|<=2` and `2s<=|X|<=1`, the difference from |X| is at least `s² Z²/(3|X|)`: here s|Z|<=|X| and sqrt(2)+1<3.

The elementary Gaussian tail estimate `int_2^infinity phi(x)dx<=phi(2)/2` gives
