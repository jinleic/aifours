1. Actual original law and statement

For all T>h>=0, S=T-h>0, a,sigma,b,ell,ch real, d!=0, consider the actual original finite-horizon scalar plant

 dx_t=(a x_t+b u_t+ch w_(t-h))dt+sigma x_t dW_t,
 z_t=d x_t,                  u_t=ell w_t,

x0=0, w supported in [0,S], canonical Brownian filtration. Write c0=b ell. Any signs of c0,ch are now permitted. Disturbances are all progressive E1 inputs, E||w||_L2[0,S]<infinity, including E2.

Except for the explicitly classified zero-operator case below, there is an explicitly specified epsilon>0 for which

 E||z|| <= |d|(1-epsilon) Gamma E||w||
         <= |d|(1-epsilon) H E||w||.                        (A)

Gamma is the genuine norm of mean stochastic kernel-column magnitudes; H is an elementary, rigorously finite Gaussian bound. Consequently the actual literal progressive gain is strictly positive and uniformly <|d| Gamma; gamma=|d|Gamma is strictly feasible for this FIXED actual preview law. No exact actual gain, actual-gain endpoint transfer, dynamic controller synthesis, unrestricted matrices or infinite horizon is claimed.

The physical operator is identically zero precisely when (i) h=0 and c0+ch=0, or (ii) h>0 and c0=ch=0. Then z=0 for every input and every gamma>0 is strictly feasible; gamma=0 is NOT strictly feasible for nonzero w. In every other case Gamma,H>0 and epsilon below is defined.

2. Exact mean and Gaussian kernel magnitudes, with cancellation intact

Let alpha=a-sigma²/2, rho=2a+sigma², and Phi(t,s)=exp[alpha(t-s)+sigma(W_t-W_s)]. The physical solution is x_t=integral_0^S K_s(t)w_s ds with

 K_s(t)=c0 1_(t>=s)Phi(t,s)+ch 1_(t>=s+h)Phi(t,s+h).

The kernel column and its norm use only increments after s and are independent of F_s. Define

 C(s)=E||K_s||_L2[0,T],  Gamma=(integral_0^S C(s)^2ds)^(1/2),
 ell0(s)=||E K_s||_L2[0,T],  H=(integral_0^S E||K_s||²ds)^(1/2),
 J_q(x)=integral_0^x e^(qv)dv,  F_q(x)=integral_0^x(x-v)e^(qv)dv.

Set A=c0 exp(a h)+ch and V=c0² exp(2a h)(exp(sigma²h)-1). Their signs need no assumptions; V>=0. Exact nonnegative expressions valid also for h=0 are

 ell0(s)² = c0² J_(2a)(h)+A² J_(2a)(S-s),
 H² = c0² S J_rho(h)+(A²+V) F_rho(S).                     (1)

F_q(x)=(exp(qx)-1-qx)/q² (q!=0), F_0(x)=x²/2. For h=0, A=c0+ch,V=0 and the immediate taps combine before squaring. For h>0, the first term in (1) is the noncancellable early-column segment. Jensen gives ell0<=C and Gamma<=H. In every nonzero case ell0(s)>0 for s<S, so Gamma,H are positive.

The exact factorization for t>=s+h is

 K_s(t)=Phi(t,s+h)[c0 Phi(s+h,s)+ch].                       (2)

Independent increments and Gaussian exponential moments yield

 E[c0 Phi(s+h,s)+ch]²=A²+V.                               (3)

3. Exact limited no-cancellation result

Fix a deterministic s<S. If h>0, sigma!=0 and c0!=0, the continuous lognormal law of Phi(s+h,s) implies P(c0 Phi(s+h,s)+ch=0)=0 for EVERY real ch. Equivalently V>0 in (3). Therefore no deterministic static preview coefficient can annihilate the INDIVIDUAL delayed-time column tail almost surely. This is an all-variable physical statement, not an optimization of a selected reference disturbance. When sigma=0, the tail vanishes exactly when c0 exp(a h)+ch=0. The preliminary t in [s,s+h) column segment c0 Phi(t,s) still prevents total physical zero response when c0!=0, even in that deterministic cancellation case.

This is a column-wise statement only: it does not establish lack of cancellation among different input times, exclude general controllers, or determine the literal optimum.

4. Explicit gap constants for all signs

Let delta=S/8, I=[S/4,S/3], J=[2S/3,3S/4], and fix r>0. Define

 f0(s)=ell0(s)/H,
 b0=(integral_0^delta f0(s)^2ds)^(1/2),
 bI=integral_I f0(s)ds,   bJ=integral_J f0(s)ds,
 eta=min{bI/(2 sqrt(|I|)), bJ/(2 sqrt(|J|))},
 p=P(sup_(0<=v<=T-delta)|B_v|<=r)>0,
 m=exp[-(|alpha|T+2|sigma|r)],  M=1/m.

In each nonzero case define Q,zeta,L,lambda_I,lambda_J as follows:

* If h>0 and c0!=0: Q=|c0|+|ch|, zeta=|c0|, L=min(h,S/3), and lambda_I=lambda_J=|c0| m sqrt(h).
* If h>0 and c0=0 (thus ch!=0): Q=zeta=|ch|, L=S/3, lambda_I=Q m sqrt(2S/3), lambda_J=Q m sqrt(S/4).
* If h=0 (thus c0+ch!=0): Q=zeta=|c0+ch|, L=S/3, lambda_I=Q m sqrt(2S/3), lambda_J=Q m sqrt(S/4).

Put

 xi=1-sqrt[1-zeta² m² L/(Q² M² T)],
 cI=lambda_I bI/2,  cJ=lambda_J bJ/2,
 epsilon=min{p² b0²/[128(1+p)²], p eta²/16,
             xi cI cJ p²/(8H²)} > 0.                       (4)

All constants are deterministic; H can be evaluated using (1), and p is a standard Brownian small-ball probability. These deliberately conservative quantities are not an asserted best margin.

5. Why signed taps still yield a definite column angle

On the independent future event H_event={sup_(delta<=t<=T)|W_t-W_delta|<=r}, m<=Phi(t,s)<=M for delta<=s<=t<=T, and ||K_s||<=Q M sqrt(T) in every nonzero case.

For s in I and u in J:

* h>0,c0!=0: K_u=0 before u, while K_s=c0 Phi(t,s) on [s,min(s+h,u)); this prefix has length >=min(h,S/3)=L and magnitude >=zeta m.
* h>0,c0=0: K_u=0 before u+h; K_s=ch Phi(t,s+h) on [s+h,u+h), a prefix of length >=S/3=L and magnitude >=zeta m.
* h=0: K_u=0 before u; K_s=(c0+ch)Phi(t,s) on [s,u), also length >=S/3=L.

Thus the support mismatch, without ANY assumption on signs, gives

 |<K_s,K_u>| <= (1-xi)||K_s|| ||K_u||.                     (5)

The absolute value is essential when the delayed coefficient is negative. On H_event, each column norm is bounded below by lambda_I or lambda_J on the respective window: in the first case use the entire [s,s+h) first-tap prefix; in the other cases use the remaining positive-length interval. For normalized input v=|w|/||w||, f=C/Gamma and e=||v-f||<=eta, f>=f0 and Cauchy–Schwarz imply

 integral_I |w_s|||K_s||ds>=cI ||w||,
 integral_J |w_s|||K_s||ds>=cJ ||w||.

For R=integral |w_s|||K_s||ds and O=||integral K_s w_s ds||, the pairwise identity plus (5) yields

 R²-O² >= 2 xi cI cJ ||w||².                              (6)

This is valid even if K_s itself changes sign and for arbitrary input signs.

6. Actual E1 adaptation and positive margin

Since ||K_s|| is independent of F_s, ER=E integral C(s)|w_s|ds<=Gamma E||w||. Normalize E||w||=1 and put q=1-EO/Gamma. As in the named predecessor's sections 4 and 6, the exact CS identity gives E[||w|| e²]<=2q, and the norm of the input's first delta segment is F_delta measurable. Thus the U=||w||-weighted mass of H_event is >=p-(1+p)sqrt(2q)/b0. The e>eta U-weight is <=2q/eta²; the R>(4H/p)U weight is <=p/4. If q is below the first two bounds in (4), the remaining good event has U-weight at least p/2. Its (6) yields R-O>=xi cI cJ U/(4H/p), hence q>=the third bound in (4). If either preliminary bound fails, q already exceeds epsilon. This proves (A) for every E1 input, without an optimizer or E2 moment.

7. Exact zero classification and strict positivity of true gain

When h>0,c0!=0, the kernel is nonzero on the positive-measure prefix {(s,t):0<s<S,s<t<s+h} for every Brownian path. If instead c0=0,ch!=0 it is nonzero on {(s,t):s+h<t<T}. When h=0 and c0+ch!=0 it is nonzero on the usual Volterra triangle. Thus the random Hilbert–Schmidt operator is nonzero a.s. in every nonzero case. A fixed countable dense family of deterministic L2 inputs cannot all map to zero on any such path; countable subadditivity gives one deterministic admissible input with positive-probability nonzero output. The E1 bound makes its expectation finite, proving the actual literal gain is >0. This is an existence argument, not a selected reference/control diagnostic. The remaining cases make K identically zero, proving the exact classification.
