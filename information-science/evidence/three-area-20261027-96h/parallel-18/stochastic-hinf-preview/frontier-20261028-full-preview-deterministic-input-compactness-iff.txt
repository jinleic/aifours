1. Actual full original law and iff

Let T>h>=0,S=T-h, scalar W, x0=0 and arbitrary finite original matrices. Use the registered full deterministic preview law

 dx=(Ax+Bu+Cw_(t-h))dt+(A0x+B0u)dW,
 z=D0x+D1u,
 u=Kx+L0w_t+Lh w_(t-h)+integral_0^h H(r)w_(t-r)dr,

H in L2[0,h], w supported [0,S]. Define P=A+BK,Q=A0+B0K,O=D0+D1K, direct output atoms E0=D1L0,Eh=D1Lh, and direct additive-diffusion atoms G0=B0L0,Gh=B0Lh. Let N be the largest common P,Q-invariant subspace in ker O. For deterministic v in L2[0,S], let T v be the ACTUAL random output path, viewed in L1(Omega;L2[0,T]) with norm E||.||.

**Theorem.** For h>0,

 T is compact iff E0=Eh=0 AND range(G0),range(Gh) subset N. (1)

For h=0 the atoms coincide and the exact condition is

 E0+Eh=0 AND range(G0+Gh) subset N.                         (2)

NO condition is imposed on the distributed H diffusion/output kernels: their deterministic time convolution is compact, even if its stochastic response is output-visible. No condition is imposed on drift taps.

2. Why all regular/delay-drift pieces are compact

The distributed time operators `v -> integral_0^h H(r)v(t-r)dr`, and their B,B0,D1 multiples, have square-integrable kernels on [0,T] x [0,S], hence are Hilbert–Schmidt as deterministic L2 time maps. The map from deterministic additive diffusion forcing g in L2 time to actual stochastic output is bounded into L1(Omega;L2) by standard L2 SDE estimates, so its composition with the distributed time operator is compact. The direct distributed output is compact for the same kernel reason.

The map from deterministic drift forcing f in L2 time to actual output has random kernel `1_(s<=t) O Phi(t,s)` with finite expected Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Finite-rank input projections and dominated convergence, exactly as in the named pure-delay source, make this map compact into L1(Omega;L2). Current, delayed and distributed drift time operators are bounded and compose with it. Thus the only possible noncompact pieces are direct-output atoms E0,Eh and additive-diffusion atoms G0,Gh.

3. Necessity of zero direct atoms

For h>0 choose 0<a<min(h,S) and a deterministic weakly-null normalized trigonometric input sequence supported [0,a]. Its two direct atom outputs occur on disjoint intervals [0,a] and [h,h+a] and have constant norm sqrt(||E0 b||²+||Eh b||²) for any fixed input direction b. The additive-noise response has zero mean for deterministic inputs; the mean of every compact remaining piece tends to zero. Jensen `E||z||>=||Ez||` shows that if either E0 or Eh is nonzero, T does not send this weakly-null sequence to zero and cannot be compact. At h=0 the same argument applies to the combined atom E0+Eh; separate constraints would be incorrect.

4. Necessity that both input-noise atoms be hidden

Assume the direct atoms vanish. If range(G0) is not contained in N, choose b with g=G0 b outside N. Use normalized cosine inputs v_n supported [0,a] with 0<a<btime<h and a<S; inspect output only on [0,btime]. No delayed Gh atom has started there. The G0-driven state has zero mean and covariance equation

 Sigma_n'=P Sigma_n+Sigma_n P^T+Q Sigma_n Q^T+G0 v_n v_n^T G0^T.

As in the pinned pure-delay proof, Riemann–Lebesgue gives a strictly positive limit for E||output||² on [0,btime], because g outside N has positive observed homogeneous-flow energy on every nontrivial interval. Uniform L4 estimates and Hölder convert this to a positive lower bound on E||output||. All regular/drift pieces tend to zero on the weakly-null input by compactness, so T is noncompact.

If range(G0) is contained in N but range(Gh) is not, the G0-driven response stays hidden. Use the same v_n and inspect [h,h+btime] with 0<a<btime<S; the delayed Gh response gives the identical positive L1 lower bound, and all regular/drift pieces again vanish. For h=0 use G0+Gh on the initial interval. Thus the visibility conditions in (1)–(2) are necessary.

5. Sufficiency and important endpoint boundary

If the required direct output atoms vanish and the direct diffusion atoms map into N, their stochastic state responses stay in N and have no output. Every remaining term was proved compact in section 2, so T is compact.

For a PURE delayed law in the special h>=S input-revelation geometry, the named revealed-input theorem can then convert compactness into E1/E2 gain attainment and strict-endpoint failure. For the GENERAL full law here, early current/distributed control can depend on noise while the input is still being generated; deterministic-input compactness alone does NOT equate its gain to the full progressive gain or decide the actual strict endpoint.
