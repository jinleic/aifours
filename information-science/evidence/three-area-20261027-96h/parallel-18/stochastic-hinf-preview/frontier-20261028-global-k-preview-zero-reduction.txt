1. Actual original existence statement

For arbitrary real finite plant matrices and each T>h>=0 consider the original plant and registered deterministic full-preview law

 dx=(Ax+Bu+Cw_(t-h))dt+(A0x+B0u)dW,
 z=D0x+D1u,                x0=0,
 u=Kx+L0w_t+Lh w_(t-h)+integral_0^h H(r)w_(t-r)dr,

with scalar Brownian W, vector progressive E1 disturbance supported on [0,T-h], constant K,L0,Lh and deterministic H in L2[0,h].

**Theorem.** With K FREE to change, the following are equivalent:

 (i) for some T>h>0 a full-preview law makes z=0 almost surely for every progressive E1 input;
 (ii) there exist real constant Khat,L and a subspace M such that

 (A+B Khat)M subset M,   (A0+B0 Khat)M subset M,
 (D0+D1 Khat)M=0,
 range(BL+C),range(B0L) subset M,   D1 L=0;               (S)

 (iii) for EVERY h>=0 and EVERY finite T>h, the PURE delayed law

 u=Khat x+L w_(t-h)

makes z=0 for every such input. In particular exact zero-output existence over arbitrary constant K is preview-horizon independent all the way through h=0. This does NOT assert equality of positive gains, equality of optimal controllers, feasibility at gamma=0, or any result under a fixed K or a bounded/stable-controller requirement.

2. Sufficiency of the pure law

If (S) holds, the zero-start state stays in M for any progressive input: both closed drift and closed diffusion preserve M and both disturbance-forcing ranges lie in M. The output on M and the direct delayed feedthrough are zero. Thus (ii) implies (iii) by actual Itô uniqueness, including h=0. Clearly (iii) implies (i).

3. Necessity: extract a finite state-response subspace from ANY zero-output full law

Assume (i) for some K,L0,Lh,H and h>0. Set P=A+BK,Q=A0+B0K,O=D0+D1K. Let N be the largest common (P,Q)-invariant subspace of ker O and Pi the projection onto N^perp. The pinned exact zero-output source gives

 D1 Lh=0,             B0 Lh has range in N,                (1)

and for the deterministic auxiliary impulse response

 m(t)=exp(Pt)B L0+integral_0^t exp[P(t-r)]B H(r)dr,

it gives the early and late identities

 D1 H(t)+O m(t)=0,
 Pi[B0 H(t)+Q m(t)]=0,                    for a.e. 0<t<h,  (2)
 m(h)+B Lh+C has range in N.                               (3)

These are actual necessary zero-output equations; m is deterministic n x m and absolutely continuous, not the stochastic state.

Let V be the finite span of ALL columns of m(t) for 0<=t<=h. At almost every t, m'(t)=P m(t)+B H(t), the identities (2) hold, and m'(t)'s columns lie in V because m(t) stays in the linear subspace V. Values of m on this full-measure set still span V by continuity. Select finitely many such column/time pairs forming a basis of V, and define a linear map theta:V->R^r by assigning theta(m_j(t_i))=H_j(t_i) on that basis. Then, for EVERY v in V,

 P v+B theta(v) in V,
 Q v+B0 theta(v) in N,
 O v+D1 theta(v)=0.                                        (4)

This is an all-variable linear-span construction; no controller/sample/reference optimization or external input is used.

4. Change K without conflict on the overlapping subspaces

Choose any complement Vc of V intersect N inside V, so V=(V intersect N) direct-sum Vc and M=N direct-sum Vc=N+V. Define a linear DeltaK on M by

 DeltaK n=0 for n in N,        DeltaK v=theta(v) for v in Vc,

and extend it arbitrarily to all of R^n. This is well-defined because N and Vc have zero intersection; it does NOT require theta to vanish on V intersect N. Put Khat=K+DeltaK.

For n in N, the three closed matrices with Khat still map N into N and annihilate it at output. For v in Vc, (4) gives

 (A+B Khat)v=P v+B theta(v) in V subset M,
 (A0+B0 Khat)v=Q v+B0 theta(v) in N subset M,
 (D0+D1 Khat)v=O v+D1 theta(v)=0.

Thus M is common invariant for the two Khat state matrices and output-invisible. From (1), D1 Lh=0 and range(B0 Lh) subset N subset M. From (3), range(B Lh+C) subset N+V=M. Hence (S) holds with L=Lh. This constructs a PURE delayed zero-output law and proves (i) implies (ii).

The choice of complement in this proof is essential: imposing DeltaK=theta on all V while also requiring DeltaK=0 on N could be inconsistent on their intersection. No stability or bounded-feedback property of Khat follows from this linear extension.

5. Exact finite all-K existence test

For arbitrary h>=0 the theorem reduces exact zero-output existence to the following finite polynomial feasibility problem: range over d=0,...,n and real unknown Khat,L,Nbasis,Pn,Qn,Y0,Y1 satisfying

 Nbasis^T Nbasis=I_d,
 (A+B Khat)Nbasis=Nbasis Pn,
 (A0+B0 Khat)Nbasis=Nbasis Qn,
 (D0+D1 Khat)Nbasis=0,
 D1 L=0,   B0 L=Nbasis Y0,   B L+C=Nbasis Y1.            (5)

An orthonormal basis Nbasis represents M; all equations are polynomial in the unknowns. Necessity follows from (S), sufficiency from section 2. For rational/algebraic plant matrices, existence is decidable IN PRINCIPLE by real-closed-field quantifier elimination; no such solver is run and no complexity or practicality claim is made. Fixed K is NOT allowed to change under the precursor's fixed-K theorem, which is why its positive-h vs zero-h separation is not contradicted.

6. Exact conversion illustration

For the pinned fixed-K separation example n=2,r=2,p=m=1, B=[[0,0],[1,0]], D0=[1,0], D1=[0,1], K=0, and V=R²,N=0, the distributed construction has theta=[[0,0],[-1,0]]. The conversion sets Khat=theta and yields D0+D1 Khat=[0,0], so M=R² is output-invisible. Python Fraction arithmetic checked this exact equality. Thus the same example that NEEDS distributed preview at K=0 has a zero-output pure law once K is allowed to change. This illustrates the fixed-K/global distinction; no optimal-gain conclusion follows.
