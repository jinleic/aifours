2. Universal second-order inequality without an inverse-output moment assumption

For bounded alpha define, on U>0 and V>0 respectively,

 C_in(alpha)= [||v||^2-<u,v>^2]/U,
 C_out(alpha)= [||z_v||^2-<p,z_v>^2]/V,

with C_in=0 when U=0 and C_out=0 when V=0. The V=0 convention is justified by z_v=0 there. Both are nonnegative. The new assertion is

 E C_out(alpha) <= gamma E C_in(alpha) < infinity.                 (K)

This is an actual all-variable second-order NECESSARY condition, not a sufficient endpoint criterion.

Proof. For |epsilon| small, w+/-epsilon v have positive literal denominator and F(+/-epsilon)>=0, with F(0)=0. Add the two inequalities. On every nonzero input path, the symmetric norm quotient

 [||w+epsilon v||+||w-epsilon v||-2U]/epsilon^2

The corresponding OUTPUT quotient is nonnegative by convexity of the Hilbert norm. It tends to C_out on V>0; on V=0 it is identically zero because z_v=0 there. Fatou's lemma therefore gives an output-curvature EXPECTATION no larger than the gamma times input limit. This proves (K) without assuming E[1/V] finite, an E2 output for w, a second-moment bound for z_v, or interchange of an unbounded output derivative with expectation.
