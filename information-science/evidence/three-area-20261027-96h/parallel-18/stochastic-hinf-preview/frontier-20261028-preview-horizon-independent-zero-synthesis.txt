1. Actual original full-preview law

For arbitrary finite real matrices, vector progressive E1 disturbance, one scalar Brownian W, zero initial state and T>h>=0, use the original plant and registered full law

 dx=(Ax+Bu+Cw_(t-h))dt+(A0x+B0u)dW,
 z=D0x+D1u,
 u=Kx+L0w_t+Lh w_(t-h)+integral_0^h H(r)w_(t-r)dr,

where K is fixed and H is a freely chosen L2 kernel for EACH given h. Input w is supported on [0,T-h]. B0,D1 and noncommuting state noise are unrestricted. The exact requirement is z=0 a.s. for ALL such inputs; whenever it holds, every gamma>0 meets the original strict expected-path-L2 condition.

2. Finite subspaces independent of h

Use the predecessor's explicit deterministic quantities

 P=A+BK, Q=A0+B0K, O=D0+D1K,
 N=largest common (P,Q)-invariant subspace in ker O,
 Pi=orthogonal projection onto N^perp,
 Astar=[D1;Pi B0], Cstar=[O;Pi Q],
 Z=orthonormal basis of ker Astar, Bbar=B Z,
 X=ker[(I-Astar Astar^dagger) Cstar],
 Pbar=P-B Astar^dagger Cstar.

Let V be the largest (Pbar,Bbar)-controlled invariant subspace of X, computed by V0=X, V_(j+1)=X intersect Pbar^(-1)(Vj+range Bbar) until stable. Choose a linear F:V -> R^k with (Pbar+Bbar F)V subset V and extend it linearly off V. Let W span Ustar={u:Bbar u in V}, put Ac=Pbar+Bbar F, Bc=Bbar W, and define the finite reachable subspace

 Rch=span{Ac^j Bc:0<=j<n}.                                 (1)

All these subspaces depend on the plant and chosen K, but NONE depends on h or T. Different valid friends F and bases W give the same Rch, as shown below.

3. Exact positive-horizon decision and L0 elimination

For every h>0 and every T>h, zero output for ALL progressive E1 inputs is achievable by some actual full-preview L0,Lh,H if and only if

 range(Pi C) subset Pi[Rch+range(Bbar)].                    (2)

Whenever (2) holds, an exact zero-output law exists with **L0=0**. Thus the instantaneous preview atom L0 never changes ZERO-output existence once an unrestricted square-integrable distributed preview kernel over a positive interval is available. It may still affect positive gain, kernel size, stability constraints or other objectives; no such claim is made.

Proof of the simplification: the predecessor allows initial impulse states I=range(Bbar) intersect V and reachable endpoint exp(Ac h)I+Rch. But

 I=range(Bc) subset Rch,

because W spans precisely the controls whose Bbar image lies in V. Rch is Ac-invariant by Cayley–Hamilton, hence exp(Ac h)I subset Rch. The endpoint set is therefore just Rch. A current atom can be set to zero and its contribution reached from zero by the distributed input over any POSITIVE time h. The delayed atom contributes the separate range(Bbar), giving (2). This also proves independence of h and of the choice of friend: both compute the same set of all constrained trajectories reachable from zero.

4. Complete explicit L0=0 controller

When (2) holds, choose matrices Rho with columns in Rch and ah satisfying

 Pi[Rho+Bbar ah+C]=0,

and set L0=0, Lh=Z ah. Let

 Wh=integral_0^h exp(Ac t)Bc Bc^T exp(Ac^T t)dt,
 v(t)=Bc^T exp[Ac^T(h-t)] Wh^dagger Rho,
 m(t)=integral_0^t exp[Ac(t-s)]Bc v(s)ds,
 H(t)=-Astar^dagger Cstar m(t)+Z[F m(t)+W v(t)].            (3)

The finite-horizon Gramian has range Rch for every h>0: its nullspace consists exactly of vectors orthogonal to all Ac^j Bc, by analyticity. Thus m(0)=0, m(h)=Rho, m(t) remains in V subset X, H is a fixed deterministic continuous/L2 function, and the predecessor's early Volterra and late endpoint equations hold. The formula is an actual controller construction, not a feedback on the auxiliary m. If (2) fails, the predecessor's necessity proves that NO fixed-K full deterministic linear preview law produces exact zero output for that h; it does NOT rule out positive-gain feasibility.

5. Exact h=0 boundary and a strict separation example

At h=0 the distributed support is empty and L0,Lh coincide. The exact zero-output condition is ONLY

 range(Pi C) subset Pi range(Bbar).                          (4)

Therefore (2) may hold for every h>0 while (4) fails at h=0. This is an exact statement within the fixed-K subclass, not a claim of a continuous or bounded-control h->0 limit.

The predecessor's fully explicit physical example has n=2,m=p=1,r=2,

 P=[[0,1],[0,0]], Q=0, B=[[0,0],[1,0]], C=[1,0]^T,
 O=[1,0], D1=[0,1], B0=0, K=0.

Here N=0, Bbar=e2, Ac=P and Rch=span{e2,Pe2}=R². Hence (2) holds for EVERY h>0, while (4) fails because C=e1 is not in span(e2). For arbitrary h>0 choose

 L0=Lh=0,
 H1(t)=-6/h²+12t/h³,
 H2(t)=3(t/h)²-2(t/h)³,          0<=t<=h.

The corresponding auxiliary state is

 m1(t)=-3(t/h)²+2(t/h)³,  m2(t)=-6t/h²+6t²/h³,

so m'=P m+B H, early output m1+H2=0, and m(h)+C=0. The actual original output therefore vanishes for every admissible input. The exact kernel energy is

 integral_0^h ||H(t)||²dt = 12/h³+13h/35.

In particular this kernel norm diverges as h down to 0: positive-h feasibility does NOT imply a finite-energy uniform limit at h=0. No bounded-control or infinite-horizon claim follows.
