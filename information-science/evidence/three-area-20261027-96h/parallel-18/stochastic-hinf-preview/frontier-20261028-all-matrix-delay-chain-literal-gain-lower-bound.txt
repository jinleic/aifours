1. Full original physical class and chain bound

For arbitrary finite original matrices, scalar Brownian W, T>h>=0, S=T-h, and every progressive vector E1 w supported on [0,S], take the actual original plant and registered deterministic linear preview law

 dx=(Ax+Bu+Cw_(t-h))dt+(A0x+B0u)dW,
 z=D0x+D1u,
 u=Kx+L0w_t+Lh w_(t-h)+integral_0^h H(r)w_(t-r)dr,

x0=0, H in L2[0,h]. Let gamma1 and gamma2 be the ACTUAL given-law gains over E1 and E2 inputs, respectively, in BOTH cases using the original ratio E||z||_L2/E||w||_L2. Set E0=D1L0, Eh=D1Lh.

For h>0 let n=ceil(S/h), and define B_n:R^(n m)->R^((n+1)p) by

 y0=E0 a0,
 yj=E0 aj+Eh a_(j-1) for 1<=j<n,
 yn=Eh a_(n-1).

Then the universal necessary bounds are

 gamma1,gamma2 >= ||B_n||op   if h>0;
 gamma1,gamma2 >= ||E0+Eh||op if h=0.                      (1)

Therefore any gamma strictly below the relevant bound is IMPOSSIBLE for the original strict inequality, for this given law. Equality is NOT classified. P,Q, stochastic control diffusion, state observability and all distributed H are unrestricted; this is not just a deterministic-output theorem.

2. Simultaneous narrow-pulse proof

For any input coefficient vector a=(a0,...,a_(n-1)) and sufficiently small epsilon>0, define the deterministic progressive input

 w_epsilon(t)=epsilon^(-1/2) a_j on [j h,j h+epsilon], 0<=j<n,
 w_epsilon(t)=0 elsewhere.

Choose epsilon<h/2 and epsilon<S-(n-1)h; the corresponding output windows [j h,j h+epsilon], 0<=j<=n, lie in [0,T] and are disjoint. The input is both E1 and E2 with ||w_epsilon||_2=||a||. On the output windows, the direct atoms E0 w_t+Eh w_(t-h) have exact path norm ||B_n a||.

The named all-matrix source gives a law-dependent finite constant C_state with E sup_t||x_t||<=C_state E||w||_2. Therefore the state output on the union of the n+1 small windows has expected path norm at most ||D0+D1K|| sqrt((n+1)epsilon) C_state||a||, tending to zero.

For the distributed direct-output kernel E(r)=D1H(r), output on those windows sees only lags r in [0,epsilon] or [h-epsilon,h]: a difference of two pulse indices is an integer multiple of h and epsilon<h/2. Young's inequality bounds its path norm there by

 [integral_0^epsilon ||E(r)||dr+integral_(h-epsilon)^h ||E(r)||dr] ||a|| ->0.

Reverse triangle inequality restricted to the output windows yields, for ALL coefficient vectors a,

 liminf_(epsilon->0) E||z(w_epsilon)||/||a|| >= ||B_n a||/||a||.

Taking the supremum over all a proves (1) for both actual gain classes. This is a universal finite-chain proof family, not optimization of a selected reference law or a numerical diagnostic. For h=0 the two atoms coincide and the single-pulse proof gives ||E0+Eh||.

3. Exact small-delay limit of the direct-atom lower bound

Keep T>0 and the matrices E0,Eh FIXED while h downarrow 0. Then n=ceil((T-h)/h)->infinity and

 ||B_n|| -> max_(theta in [0,2pi]) ||E0+e^(i theta)Eh||op.  (2)

For the upper bound, regard B_n as convolution by the two matrix taps on a zero-extended finite sequence and use discrete Parseval. For the lower bound, choose a complex unit vector v and phase z on the unit circle nearly maximizing the symbol, and take a_j=z^j v/sqrt(n). The n-1 interior outputs yield

 ||B_n a|| >= sqrt((n-1)/n)||E0+z^(-1)Eh||op

for v chosen as a maximizing vector. Real matrices have the same operator norm on their complexification, so this proves (2) for the original real problem. It is a limit of the LOWER BOUNDS; actual gains may be larger and the controller matrices must not be varied with h when invoking (2).

At exactly h=0 the atom bound is ||E0+Eh||. These can differ dramatically: E0=1,Eh=-1 gives h=0 bound0, whereas the limit of positive-h bounds is2. This is a real high-frequency/short-delay discontinuity under unrestricted L2 inputs, not a claim that a particular total physical gain converges or that any gain is attained.
