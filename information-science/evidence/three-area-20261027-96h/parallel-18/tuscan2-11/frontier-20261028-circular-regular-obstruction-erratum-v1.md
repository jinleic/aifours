## E1 — accepted FATAL: domain must be odd n ≥ 3 (candidate LF 7, 9–11, 13, 19)

Under the candidate's own definition, n=1 is a counterexample: the single cyclic row `(0,1)` on Z/2 covers each distinct ordered pair once at distance 1; its distance-2 events are the loops `(0,0),(1,1)`, so the at-most-once condition on distinct pairs holds vacuously; and the regular C2 action preserves the row. The counting sentence in LF 7 ("each distance has n(n+1) occurrences, equal to the number of ordered pairs, so both covers are exact") is valid only when distance-2 events join distinct symbols, i.e. N=n+1≥3. **Replace the LF 19 conclusion by: for every odd n≥3, no circular Tuscan-2 n×(n+1) array admits a regular action of any group of order n+1.** The all-odd-n statement in the frozen candidate must not be cited. The n=11 consequences in LF 7 (circular ⇒ linear T2(11)), the regular-action exclusion at n=11, and the prime-order restriction in LF 23 are unaffected.

## E2 — accepted MAJOR: replace the misstated normal-complement import (candidate LF 15)

The parenthetical "a finite group whose Sylow p-subgroup is cyclic, hence lies in the centre of its abelian normalizer, has a normal p-complement" is **false as stated**: for S3 and p=3 the Sylow subgroup is cyclic, its normalizer S3 is nonabelian, and there is no normal 3-complement; even for p=2 the normalizer need not be abelian (the review's 42-element example). Only the p=2 consequence is used, and it holds for the following reason. Let P≅C_{2^k} be a Sylow 2-subgroup of H. The conjugation image N_H(P)/C_H(P) embeds in Aut(P), a group of order 2^{k−1}; it is also a quotient of N_H(P)/P (because P ≤ C_H(P)), which has odd order. Hence it is trivial and **P is central in its normalizer** (not: the normalizer is abelian). A normal 2-complement then follows either from Burnside's normal p-complement theorem with its correct hypothesis P ≤ Z(N_H(P)), or — self-contained and preferred here — from the explicit transfer V:H→P reconstructed in the review (its steps 1–3): V is a homomorphism, V(x)≡x modulo P² for a generator x of P because the generator has an odd number of fixed right cosets, so V is onto and H/ker V ≅ C_{2^k}. The remaining φ-sum contradiction in LF 15–19 is unchanged.

## Corrected statement and status

Circular Tuscan-2 n×(n+1) arrays, odd n≥3: no regular group action on the n+1 symbols is possible. In particular, for n=11 none of the five groups of order 12 can act regularly; prime-order symbol symmetries must be fixed-point-free of order 2 or 3. This remains a falsification of one construction route only. It does not decide the original T2(11) problem, establish that circular arrays exist or not, or claim novelty. The separately reported bounded SAT outcomes for order-2/order-3 symmetric circular types were not reviewed and remain unverified (UNSAT) or UNKNOWN.
