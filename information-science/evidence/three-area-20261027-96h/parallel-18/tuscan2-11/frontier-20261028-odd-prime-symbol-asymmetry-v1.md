# Symbol asymmetry of odd-prime T1 arrays

Native `/bin/date` before writing: **Wed Oct 28 17:44:15 PDT 2026**. **WRITTEN universal necessary condition:** every T1(p) array of odd prime order p has trivial symbol-permutation automorphism, when rows may be permuted but their left-to-right order is retained. In particular every original Tuscan-2(11) witness has this asymmetry. This is an unconditional theorem about **all** actual arrays satisfying the D1 condition, not an assertion that a T2(11) array exists or does not exist. No Latin-column or cyclic-row hypothesis is imposed on an array. No novelty or priority claim is made.

## Definitions and proof

An array here consists of p permutation rows of a p-symbol alphabet, with every ordered adjacent pair distinct across all rows; because there are exactly p(p−1) possible ordered pairs, they are all present. Its symbol automorphism group G consists of the permutations of the alphabet that send its **set of row strings** to itself, allowing row reordering but no column permutation or reversal.

The rows are distinct: two equal rows would duplicate each of their adjacent events. If `g∈G` fixes even one row string, then g fixes every symbol in that permutation row and hence is the identity. Thus G acts **freely** on the p rows. Every row orbit has |G| elements, so `|G|` divides the prime p. If G is nontrivial, it has order p and is cyclic. A nonidentity generator acts on the p symbols as a single p-cycle; after a relabelling, this action is addition of 1 in Z_p. Because its orbit on row strings has size p, all rows are translates of one base permutation `x_0,...,x_(p−1)`.

For a directed adjacency in a translated row, the difference of the second symbol and the first in Z_p is one of `d_i=x_(i+1)−x_i`, i=0,...,p−2. If two d_i agree, a translate carries the first ordered pair to the second, producing the same ordered adjacency in two different rows. Therefore all p−1 differences must be distinct and nonzero, hence they are precisely the nonzero elements of Z_p. Since p is odd, their sum is `1+...+(p−1)=p(p−1)/2≡0 (mod p)`. Telescoping also gives their sum as `x_(p−1)−x_0`, forcing the two endpoints equal, contrary to the base row being a permutation. This contradiction eliminates |G|=p, so G is trivial.

The p=2 boundary is deliberately excluded: the sum of the nonzero residues is not zero and the two rows `01,10` do have the nontrivial symbol-swap automorphism. The proof says nothing about symmetries that simultaneously permute columns, reverse row strings, or use symbol-dependent changes. It is a structural pruning obstruction, not a complete characterization of T1 or T2 arrays and not a universal nonexistence proof.
