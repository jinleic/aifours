## 3. Why the same identities remain strict on the explicit neighborhood

The following is a written perturbation bound; the center identities and multiplier bounds, not the derivative argument, were machine-checked. Throughout the stated neighborhood all nodes and odd-band endpoints lie between \(1/2\) and 30; every relevant node-node or node-root separation exceeds \(1/2000\). For the positive unnormalized weights (4), \(|\partial\log q_i|<20000\) with respect to any of the 11 varying scalars: the elementary-symmetric numerator contributes at most 2, while the root and node difference factors contribute at most six terms bounded by 2000. Consequently a normalized weight has derivative magnitude below 40000.

For (3), \(|F|<27000\), \(|F'|<2700\), and each coefficient of \(E_-(t)\) and its derivative is bounded by 27000 and 2700 respectively. Summing over support nodes and the 11 varying scalars gives a deliberately loose bound \(10^{17}\epsilon\) on the change of any ray-row coefficient. The six band rows in (6), with multipliers below 1000, change by at most \(2\cdot10^7\epsilon\) per coefficient. Thus the residual of an original dual identity after perturbation has every coefficient smaller in absolute value than \(10^{18}\epsilon\).

The first eight rows in (6) form a fixed basis. If the residual is \((r_l,r_u)\), correct the lower-bound multipliers by \(r_l+r_u\) and the width multipliers by \(r_u\). Each correction is at most \(2\cdot10^{18}\epsilon=2\cdot10^{-12}<1/2000\), so all multipliers remain positive and the identities continue to hold exactly. Positivity of the selector, band order, node signs, endpoint coefficient widths and the center example's stability margins also persists under this much smaller-than-margin perturbation.
