# Boundary-only positivity does not bound numerator degree

For the equal-degree problem, the bounded ratio and positive leading limit allow the imaginary-axis strict positivity condition to extend to the right half-plane. That reasoning must **not** be applied without qualification to arbitrary higher-degree polynomial numerators. For any stable denominator \(a\), the literal numerator \(b=a(1+s^4)\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Re}\frac{b(i\omega)}{a(i\omega)}=1+\omega^4>0
\quad(\omega\in\mathbb R),
\]
but \(b/a=1+s^4\) is not positive real throughout the right half-plane and has relative degree \(-4\). Thus boundary-only positivity alone does not give a degree-at-most-\(n+1\) restriction or guarantee a stable higher-degree numerator. This caveat does not alter any equal-degree result in the current packets, and it is not a synthesis verdict or formal verification.
