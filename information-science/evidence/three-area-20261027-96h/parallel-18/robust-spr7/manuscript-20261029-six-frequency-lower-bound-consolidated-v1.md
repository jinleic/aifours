**Theorem.** Let K be a compact independent coefficient box of monic real septics a(s)=s⁷+Σ_{j<7}a_j sʲ, every member strictly Hurwitz. No positive weights μ_i, actual members a_i∈K, and distinct positive squared frequencies t_1<…<t_m with m≤5 — together with any zero/infinity atoms — make all eight aggregate numerator-monomial coordinates strictly negative. Hence such a certificate needs m≥6.

**Standard imports (classical, hypotheses exhibited at use).** (S1) Hermite–Biehler: for real strictly Hurwitz a of degree 7 with a₀>0, E_a and O_a have simple positive strictly interlacing roots ρ₁<σ₁<ρ₂<σ₂<ρ₃<σ₃, and the unwrapped phase φ of a(iω) is continuous with φ(0)=0, strictly increasing, φ(∞)=7π/2. (S2) Minkowski–Weyl: a finitely constrained pointed polyhedral cone is generated by its extreme rays. (S3) Lagrange interpolation and divided differences.

## 1. Setup

Write t=ω², E_a(t)=a₀−a₂t+a₄t²−a₆t³, O_a(t)=a₁−a₃t+a₅t²−t³, so Re(b(iω)·conj(a(iω)))=P_{a,b}(t):=E_a(t)E_b(t)+tO_a(t)O_b(t). A **strict-negative aggregate** is C:=Σᵢμᵢ·row(aᵢ,tᵢ)+w₀a₀⁰e₀+w_∞e₇ with μᵢ,w₀,w_∞>0, aᵢ∈K, distinct positive tᵢ, where row(a,t)∈ℝ⁸ has (row)_{2r}=(−t)^rE_a(t), (row)_{2r+1}=t(−t)^rO_a(t), r=0..3; every coordinate C_j<0. (The compactified row row/(1+t)⁷ is continuous on [0,∞] with values a₀e₀ at 0 and e₇ at ∞; per-atom positive rescaling does not change signs.)

Atoms sharing a node merge — the positively weighted average member stays in K (convex) and is Hurwitz — so assume distinct nodes, one member each. At fixed t the map a↦(E_a(t),O_a(t)) fills the rectangle [E₋(t),E₊(t)]×[O₋(t),O₊(t)] (independent intervals), with E₋=ℓ₀−u₂t+ℓ₄t²−ℓ₆t³, E₊=u₀−ℓ₂t+u₄t²−u₆t³, O₋=ℓ₁−u₃t+ℓ₅t²−u₇t³, O₊=u₁−ℓ₃t+u₅t²−ℓ₇t³ (u₇=ℓ₇=1 for monic K; the variable-leading case ℓ₇≤u₇ arises only in §§8–9 and is carried explicitly).

Every test F below is F(t)=−t(b−t)(e−t) or −t(c−t)(d−t), so F(0)=0 and the even part of F as a numerator has nonnegative coefficients (0, be or cd, b+e or c+d, 1). Hence b_F·C=Σ_j b_{F,2j}C_{2j}<0 for every strict-negative aggregate: **a certificate has strictly negative F-pairing for each such F**, while zero atoms contribute 0 (F(0)=0) and infinity atoms contribute 0 to all finite pairings (only a positive amount to C₇). It remains to contradict this for each word.

## 2. Global bands, classes, placements

**Lemma 1 (bands).** O₋, O₊ each have three positive roots; write them (a,d,e) and (b,c,f). Then a≤b<c≤d<e≤f; every member's odd roots lie in [a,b], [c,d], [e,f] and every member's even roots in (0,a], [b,c], [d,e].
*Proof.* Each corner (E±,O±) is a member; by (S1) the roots of E₊ (and E₋) interlace those of O₋ and of O₊. Also O₊−O₋=δ₀+δ₁t+δ₂t², δⱼ≥0, and O±(0)≥ℓ₁>0. If b<a then on (b,a)⊆(b,c)∩(0,a) we get O₊<0<O₋, impossible; so a≤b, and similarly c≤d, e≤f (test the gaps adjacent to the conflicting roots). The middle root of E₊ lies in (max(a,b),min(c,d))=(b,c), giving b<c and d<e. For a member with O(σ)=0, O₋≤O≤O₊: on (0,a), O₋>0 forces σ₁≥a; on (b,c), O₊<0 forces σ₂∉(b,c); on (d,e), O₋>0 forces σ₂≤d; the mirrored comparisons give the three closed odd bands, and (S1) interlacing then places the ρⱼ in the closed gaps. ∎

For a member a and t>0 with a(iω)≠0 define the **class** q(t)∈{0,…,6} as the quadrant index of φ(t): classes 0..6 have sign pairs (E,O)=(++),(−+),(−−),(+−),(++),(−+),(−−). Both parities never vanish together (that would be a(iω)=0). A single vanishing parity may be assigned either adjacent class; then for tᵢ<tⱼ: (i) qⱼ≥qᵢ−1 always; (ii) if both parity signs differ between the samples, qⱼ≥qᵢ+2. Both follow from strict increase of φ and the quadrant cycle of length 4. With both parities nonzero, class q confines t for that member: q=0: t∈(0,ρ₁); q=1: (ρ₁,σ₁); q=2: (σ₁,ρ₂); q=3: (ρ₂,σ₂); q=4: (σ₂,ρ₃); q=5: (ρ₃,σ₃); q=6: t>σ₃.

**Lemma 2 (placements).** A five-node certificate's class word forces, with all inequalities strict:
A=(2,1,3,2,4): a<t₁<t₂<b, b<t₃<t₄<c, c<t₅<e. B=(2,1,3,5,4): a<t₁<t₂<b, b<t₃<d, d<t₄<t₅<e. C=(2,4,3,5,4): a<t₁<c, c<t₂<t₃<d, d<t₄<t₅<e.
*Proof.* Weights are finite, so O(tᵢ)≠0 and the strict class regions apply. Class 2 at t₁: t₁∈(σ₁⁽¹⁾,ρ₂⁽¹⁾)⊆(a,c). Class 1 at t₂: t₂<σ₁⁽²⁾≤b; with t₁<t₂ this gives a<t₁<t₂<b (A, B). Class 3 at t₃ (A,B): t₃∈(ρ₂,σ₂)⊆(b,d). A: class 2 at t₄ with E flipping + to − between t₃,t₄: t₄<ρ₂⁽⁴⁾≤c while t₃>ρ₂⁽³⁾≥b, so b<t₃<t₄<c; class 4 at t₅: t₅∈(σ₂,ρ₃)⊆(c,e). B: class 5 at t₄: t₄∈(ρ₃,σ₃) with ρ₃≥d; class 4 at t₅ with E flipping − to +: t₅<ρ₃⁽⁵⁾≤e; so d<t₄<t₅<e. ## 3. Moment signs; m≥4; m≥5; classification (K1)

**Lemma 3.** If C<0 coordinatewise, then for z=xᵢ:=μᵢEᵢ(tᵢ) and for z=yᵢ:=μᵢtᵢOᵢ(tᵢ) (finite atoms only): Σz<0, Σtz>0, Σt²z<0, Σt³z>0.

**Lemma 4 (oriented subsequence).** Under Lemma 3's four inequalities, the signed word of the nonzero zᵢ (node order) contains the oriented subsequence (−,+,−,+). Hence m≥4.
*Proof.* Otherwise the word fits four blocks +*−*+*−*. Pick r₁<r₂<r₃ in the corresponding gaps (before the first block, between blocks, after the last, as needed). ## 4. The eight odd-moment rays

Fix one word with odd signs on its five nodes (A: (−,+,−,−,+); B,C: (−,+,−,+,+)). For the certificate's actual members consider 𝒞={w∈ℝ⁵₊: ΣᵢwᵢtᵢOᵢ(tᵢ)(−tᵢ)^r≤0, r=0..3}; the certificate's y-vector lies in 𝒞 by Lemma 3. 𝒞 is pointed. An extreme ray needs active constraints of rank 4. Support s=5 forces all four moments zero; the Vandermonde null vector wᵢ∝1/(tᵢOᵢ(tᵢ)Π_{j≠i}(tᵢ−tⱼ)) has strictly alternating signs, contradicting w≥0 together with the O-sign word. s≤3 is impossible by Lemma 4. So s=4 with exactly three active moments. Dropping one node must leave the O-subsequence (−,+,−,+): for A the supports are (1,2,3,5),(1,2,4,5); for B,C: (1,2,3,4),(1,2,3,5). For support u₁<…<u₄ with H(t)=Π(t−uᵢ) and k∈{0,…,3}, Lagrange interpolation of t^r gives the exact weights
wᵢ^{(k)}=e_{3−k}({uⱼ:j≠i})/(uᵢOᵢ(uᵢ)H′(uᵢ))>0, with moment vector −e_k;
positivity needs exactly the signs (−,+,−,+), which hold. By (S2) every element of 𝒞 is a positive combination of these **eight rays**. Note e_{3−k}(ûᵢ) is nonincreasing in i, constant only for k=3.

Contradiction scheme (§§5–9): show every ray of the relevant word has **strictly positive** F-pairing ΣwᵢEᵢ(uᵢ)F(uᵢ) for every choice of actual members with the required O-signs; positive combinations then inherit this, contradicting §1. Domination principle: a ray term equals const·E(u)F(u)/(uO(u)H′(u)); for fixed sign of E(u)F(u)/(uH′(u)) the term is monotone in 1/O(u), so its minimum over actual O(u) of the required sign is attained at an envelope endpoint (or the limit |O|→0, which only helps when the coefficient has the favorable sign); each case below names its worst endpoints.
