# Any corner-supported SPR obstruction must use a diagonal pair

**Status: WRITTEN candidate, not parent adoption.** This is a universal structural restriction on the actual-box negative-certificate search, not an encoding equivalence and not an unrestricted positive verdict.

Let \(K\) be any monic strictly Hurwitz independent coefficient box of degree \(n\), and name its four **actual** parity corners \(a_{--},a_{-+},a_{+-},a_{++}\), with the first sign choosing the even evaluation extremum and the second sign the odd extremum. Consider any positive-weight finite actual-corner atom list at positive finite frequencies, optionally together with zero and compactified-infinity atoms. If its aggregate numerator-coordinate vector is either zero or strictly negative in every coordinate, then among its **positive-frequency** atoms it must use either **both** \(a_{--}\) and \(a_{++}\), or **both** \(a_{-+}\) and \(a_{+-}\).

In particular, no choice of frequencies or positive weights drawn only from one corner, or from any adjacent corner pair sharing one parity, can yield either obstruction. This applies directly to the original eight-finite-frequency strictly-negative-coordinate certificate, without assuming that the listed frequencies are roots or that the denominator corners are close.

## Proof

If all positive-frequency atoms share one even corner part, their finitely many actual denominators form a compact stable fixed-even family. If they share one odd corner part, they form a compact stable fixed-odd family. In either case the fixed-parity theorem supplies a real equal-degree numerator with strictly positive coefficients and strictly positive pairings at **every** frequency for those denominators. Zero-frequency atoms contribute \(a_0b_0>0\), and compactified-infinity atoms contribute \(b_n>0\), even if their corner labels differ. Positive aggregation therefore evaluates strictly positively on that numerator. This contradicts either an identically zero aggregate vector or one with all coordinates strictly negative.

Consequently the positive-frequency corner labels must cover both even choices and both odd choices. Any subset of a \(2\times2\) corner grid doing so contains a diagonal pair: a two-corner cover is itself diagonal, and every three- or four-corner cover contains a diagonal. This completes the proof. The condition is necessary only: neither a diagonal pair nor the old eight-atom upper support by itself produces a certificate.
