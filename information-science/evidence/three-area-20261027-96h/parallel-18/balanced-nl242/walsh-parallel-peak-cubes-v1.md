## Claim

For any actual balanced peak≤28 f, fix an **arbitrary** 3-dimensional frequency direction H. Among all affine cosets of H that are fully peak, the sign patterns are either all affine characters on H or all non-affine. In the non-affine case there are at most 20 fully peak cosets with direction H, and their four nonzero local-correlation source cosets are the same affine quotient 2-flat for every such peak cube.

This includes the vacuous case of no peak cube. It does not assert that any direction has a peak cube or that either sign type occurs.

## Proof

Put U=H^⊥ and define p_H(y)=|{x∈x_y+U:f(x)=1}| mod 2 on the eight source cosets V/U. This is one pattern belonging to the common actual f, independent of the base a₀ of any peak cube with direction H.

For every fully peak E=a₀+H, the source-coset parity theorem gives p_H=0 if the eight signs on E form an affine character, and otherwise p_H is the indicator of the affine 2-flat on which the eight oriented local correlations have magnitude 14. The two cases are disjoint. Hence different parallel peak cubes cannot have different sign types; in the non-affine case their four active source-coset labels are exactly the common support of p_H.

Now suppose there are r parallel fully peak cosets in the non-affine case. Each corresponds to a different local character η=a₀|_U, because two global frequencies restrict equally to U exactly when they differ by H. Fix any one of the four source cosets in supp p_H. On that 6-flat the unnormalised local Walsh transform of the restricted sign function has squared sum 64²=4096. The r distinct local characters contributed by the peak cubes each have magnitude 14, so 196r≤4096 and r≤20. All choices of H and the source cell were arbitrary; no direction or object is selected or tested. ∎
