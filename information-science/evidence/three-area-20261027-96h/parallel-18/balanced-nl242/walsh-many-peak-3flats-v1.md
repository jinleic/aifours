## Proof

The peak set S lies in an affine 8-space. For each nonzero direction b in its 8-dimensional direction space let e_b be the number of unordered pairs of S separated by b. Let D range over the 127 two-dimensional direction spaces containing b, and let p_D be the number of affine peak 2-flats with direction D. Two distinct b-pairs form one such plane, so

    Σ_{D∋b} p_D = binom(e_b,2).                                  (2)

Any two distinct parallel affine 2-flats with direction D form an affine 3-flat, so for fixed b the number of pairs (D, peak 3-flat) is Σ_D binom(p_D,2). For nonnegative integers p, binom(p,2)≥(p−1)_+, hence

    Σ_{D∋b} binom(p_D,2) ≥ [binom(e_b,2)−127]_+
                            ≥ 9(e_b−16)_+.                        (3)

The last inequality holds for every integer e_b≥0; for e_b≥17 its difference is (e_b−17)(e_b−2)/2, and for e_b≤16 the right side is 0.

Every peak affine 3-flat has 7 two-dimensional direction subspaces and each such direction has 3 nonzero choices of b. Therefore it is counted exactly 21 times when (3) is summed over all b≠0. Since Σ_b e_b=binom(n,2),

    21G ≥ 9Σ_b(e_b−16)_+ ≥ 9·max{0,binom(n,2)−255·16}.

This proves the first bound in (1). The parent-notified n≥92 gives binom(n,2)−4080≥106, so G≥ceil(318/7)=46. These are analytic counts over all directions and all sets, not a generated enumeration or a chosen example. ∎
