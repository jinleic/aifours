## The stronger divisible-by-32 statement

Let f:F₂⁹→{0,1} be any balanced Boolean function, with k=W_f/4, nonzero one-support XOR T, K=T^⊥, and even-half quadratic `q(a)=k(a)/2 mod 2` on K. Let B be its alternating polar and R=rad(B). Define

    Z₈ = {a∈K : k(a)≡0 (mod 8)}.

No peak bound is needed for the following necessary statements:

1. The indicator of Z₈ on the eight-dimensional K has algebraic degree≤6, and therefore `|Z₈|≥4`.
2. If `rank(B)≤4`, that indicator has degree≤5 and `|Z₈|≥8`.
3. If `|Z₈|=4`, then Z₈ is a **linear 2-plane**, contains R, and rank(B)≥6. If rank(B)=6, necessarily **Z₈=R**.

For a balanced peak≤28 target, |k|≤7 and `k≡0 mod8` means k=0. Thus its four-Walsh-zero case is restricted to either rank eight, or rank six with the zero plane **exactly the even-half polar radical**. Rank zero, two and four are excluded in that case. The previously displayed rank-four four-zero formal histogram is therefore impossible at **every** placement, not merely at its shown arrangement.

## Mixed six-flat energy and zero parity

The earlier even-half caller, reconstructed in `frontier-20261028-even-half-four-zero-obstruction-v1.md`, SHA-256 `7344078fb115eb47f9d5e3c731a63d16567cb09d23af4868810c5d45ef38f8c4`, 59 LF, proves q is quadratic. Fix any 5-dimensional L≤K, an arbitrary odd coset B₀=a₀+L with a₀∉K, and any even coset A=a+L. Their union is an affine frequency 6-flat, so the exact affine-energy caller gives

    E(A)+E(B₀) ≡ 0 (mod 32),       E(C)=Σ_{x∈C}k(x)².                       (1)

Thus E(A) modulo 32 is the same for all eight even cosets of L. For completeness, (1) follows directly from quotient Parseval: the spatial annihilator has dimension three; its 8-point signed character sums v_C are even, and their signed sum is one Walsh coefficient, divisible by four because f is balanced. Hence an even number of v_C/2 are odd, `Σ_C v_C²≡0 mod8`, and `Σ_F k²=4Σ_C v_C²≡0 mod32`.

Let `n_q(A)=Σ_{x∈A}q(x)` and `n_z(A)=|A∩Z₈|`. For any even integer k, `k² mod32` is 0 if k≡0 mod8, 16 if k≡4 mod8, and 4 if k≡2 or 6 mod8. Therefore

    E(A) ≡ 16(32−n_q(A)−n_z(A)) + 4 n_q(A)
         ≡ 16[n_q(A)/4 + n_z(A)] (mod 32).                                   (2)

The quotient is integral: a quadratic on five variables has polar rank 0,2, or 4 and weights respectively in `{0,16,32}`, `{8,16,24}`, or `{12,16,20}`. Equations (1)–(2) imply that `n_z(A) mod2 + n_q(A)/4 mod2` is constant over the eight cosets.

## Quadratic coset lemma and polynomial degree

For fixed L, the function `a+L ↦ n_q(a+L)/4 mod2` on K/L is affine. If the polar on L has rank≤2, every listed weight is divisible by eight, so it is identically zero. If the restricted rank is four, its one-dimensional radical is generated by r. The restriction on a+L is balanced of weight 16 exactly when `q(r)+B(a,r)=1`, and otherwise has weight 12 or 20. Hence

    n_q(a+L)/4 ≡ 1+q(r)+B(a,r) (mod 2),                                     (3)

an affine function of the coset; B(r,L)=0 makes it well-defined. Consequently, for every 5-dimensional L the fifth additive derivative of the indicator of Z₈ in the directions of a basis of L is an affine function of its base point. Every seventh derivative vanishes, and the indicator has degree≤6.

If rank(B)≤4, this coset function is actually constant. Restricted rank≤2 is already constant. In the rank-four case, `dim(L∩R)≥5+4−8=1`; the one-dimensional restricted radical must equal L∩R, so its generator is globally radical and `B(a,r)=0`. Thus every sixth derivative of the indicator vanishes and its degree is≤5.

The elementary minimum-support bound for a nonzero degree≤d Boolean polynomial in eight variables is `2^(8−d)`; an induction proof is in the pinned even-half paper. Since 0∈Z₈, the indicator is nonzero. This gives |Z₈|≥4 generally and ≥8 when rank(B)≤4. ∎

## Equality and radical containment

If |Z₈|=4, the degree≤6 bound makes Z₈ an affine 2-plane by a short equality argument. Each derivative of its indicator has degree≤5 and hence is either zero or has weight at least eight; its weight is at most eight. If x,y∈Z₈, the derivative in direction x+y has overlapping support and therefore cannot have weight eight, so x+y is a period. Fixing x₀∈Z₈ shows Z₈ is precisely x₀ plus its period subspace. As 0∈Z₈, it is a **linear** 2-plane.

Suppose some r∈R lies outside Z₈. Choose a 5-dimensional L containing r and disjoint from Z₈: take a six-dimensional complement of Z₈ containing r and then a five-dimensional subspace of it containing r. If q|L has rank≤2, the coset function (3) is constant; if it has rank four, its one-dimensional radical contains the globally radical r and (3) is again constant. Equations (1)–(2) would therefore force the parity of |Z₈∩A| to be the same on every coset A of L. But Z₈ is disjoint from L except at zero, so its four elements occupy four different cosets, giving parity one on those four and zero on the other four. Contradiction. Thus R⊆Z₈; since dim Z₈=2, rank(B)≥6, and rank six makes R=Z₈. ∎
