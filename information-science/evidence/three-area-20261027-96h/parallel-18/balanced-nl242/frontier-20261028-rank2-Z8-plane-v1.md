## Statement

This is a zero-set description conditional on D=20, not a proof that D=20 exists or is impossible. The rank-two D=12 and D=16 branches, rank 4/6/8, and the original witness-or-unrestricted-exclusion gap remain.

## Proof

The pinned profile candidate gives m=122 and z=64 for this branch. On the eight-dimensional even frequency space K, the rank-two quadratic q satisfies q(0)=0. Since its zero count is 64, its radical has dimension six and q vanishes **exactly** on R. Indeed, a nonzero radical linear term would make q balanced (128 zeros); when it vanishes, the quotient is the anisotropic rank-two form with the zero vector as its only zero.

The pinned singular-cubic theorem proves that the Walsh-zero indicator restricted to R is a nonzero Boolean polynomial of degree≤3. It contains 0, so its support has at least `2^(6−3)=8` points. Conversely the even-energy inequality gives

    64·122 ≤16(64−Z)+36·192 = 7936−16Z,

hence Z≤8. Thus Z=8, all zeros lie in R, and equality holds in every coefficientwise energy bound. This gives the asserted |W|=16/24 magnitudes.

A nonzero degree≤3 Boolean polynomial in six variables with weight `2^(6−3)=8` is the indicator of an affine 3-flat. For completeness: for any two support points x,y, its derivative in direction x+y has degree≤2 and weight `2(8−|S∩(S+x+y)|)<16`. A nonzero degree≤2 Boolean polynomial in six variables has weight at least 16, so the derivative vanishes. Therefore the support is invariant under every difference of two support points; translating by a support point shows it is a linear subspace, and its size is eight. Since the actual support contains frequency 0, the affine flat is a linear 3-subspace S⊂R. ∎
