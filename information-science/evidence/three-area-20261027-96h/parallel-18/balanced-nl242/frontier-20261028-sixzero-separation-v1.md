# info-6 frontier: the six-zero case is excluded by affine-energy separation

Owner **InfoBalancedNL242-2-2**. Native `/bin/date` before writing: **2026-10-28T22:00:23-0700 PDT**. **UNREVIEWED AUTHOR CANDIDATE.** This result is independent of the pending four-zero kernel-pair certificate; it does not itself prove an unrestricted target exclusion or supply a witness.

## Statement

Let f:F₂⁹→{0,1} be any balanced Boolean function with nonzero one-support XOR T. Put k=W_f/4, K=T^⊥, and `Z₈={a∈K:k(a)≡0 mod8}`. Then

> **|Z₈|=4 or |Z₈|≥8.**

In particular |Z₈| cannot be six. For a balanced peak≤28 target, T≠0 by the separately supported original caller and Z₈ is exactly the Walsh-zero set. Hence a target cannot have exactly six Walsh zeros. The four-zero possibility is **not** assumed excluded in this proof.

## The parity caller

For any five-dimensional L≤K and any coset A=a+L, let q=k/2 mod2 on K and `n_q(A)=Σ_A q`. The f131-scoped mixed-6-flat energy identity, also reconstructed in `frontier-20261028-four-zero-radical-reduction-v1.md` (SHA-256 `89ca5480f9c4375cc0a82b313fd66c554941bfc7856bdea3e326935a94eeb229`, 63 LF), states

    |A∩Z₈| + n_q(A)/4  is constant modulo 2 across all eight L-cosets.      (1)

A five-variable quadratic has weight divisible by four, so the quotient is integral. If its polar rank is≤2, the weight is divisible by eight. If its polar rank is four and its radical line is generated by r, its quotient parity is `1+q(r)+B(a,r)`; in particular it is constant when r is a global radical vector. If all eight Z₈-cosets have equal parity and |Z₈|<8, they must all be even, because eight odd cosets would already contain at least eight points.

The f131 reduction already proves |Z₈|≥8 when rank(B)≤4. It remains to treat ranks eight and six without assuming |Z₈|=4.

## Rank eight

Suppose rank(B)=8 and 1≤m=|Z₈|<8. Fix a point z₀∈Z₈. Choose an isotropic 3-space D uniformly from the finite set of all such subspaces of the symplectic eight-space, and let L=D^⊥. Then dim L=5 and rank(B|L)=2. The symplectic group is transitive on nonzero vectors, and every L contains 31 nonzero vectors. Double counting gives, for each fixed nonzero v,

    Pr[v∈L] = 31/255.

There are at most m−1≤6 nonzero differences z+z₀ with z∈Z₈\{z₀}. The union bound gives probability at most `6·31/255<1` that L contains any of them. Thus some such L makes `(z₀+L)∩Z₈={z₀}`. Its q restriction has rank two, so (1) forces equal Z₈ parity on all eight cosets; with m<8 all those parities must be even. The singleton coset is a contradiction. Therefore rank eight has |Z₈|≥8. This argument makes no assumption that the small set is a plane or that a target exists.

## Rank six

Suppose rank(B)=6, so its radical R has dimension two, and 1≤m=|Z₈|<8. We show Z₈ is invariant under every r∈R. If not, choose nonzero r∈R and z₀∈Z₈ with z₀+r∉Z₈. Pick a uniformly random five-dimensional L containing r. In the seven-dimensional quotient K/⟨r⟩, L/⟨r⟩ has dimension four; for any fixed difference `d=z+z₀` with z≠z₀, its quotient is nonzero and

    Pr[d∈L]=(2⁴−1)/(2⁷−1)=15/127.

At most six differences occur, and `6·15/127<1`, so some L contains r and none of them. Hence `(z₀+L)∩Z₈={z₀}`. If rank(B|L)≤2, all q-coset weights are divisible by eight. If it is four, its one-dimensional radical contains the globally radical r, so `n_q/4 mod2` is constant across L-cosets. In either case (1) makes all eight Z₈-coset parities equal, impossible below eight because one coset is a singleton.

Thus Z₈ is a union of R-cosets. Its size is a positive multiple of four; since |Z₈|<8 it is four, and because 0∈Z₈ the set is R itself. Combining all ranks proves the statement. ∎
