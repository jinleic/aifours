## 3. Intersection indices and normalizer seven-retention

### 3.1 The fixed original fibre index, LF59–70

Write `V_j=U∩U_j`. Since `[G:V_j]` is divisible by both `7r_i` and `7r_j`, and the private cofactors are coprime, their least common multiple is `7r_i r_j`. Thus `[G:V_j]=7r_i r_j lambda_j` with integer lambda_j. Product-set cardinality gives `|UU_j|/|G|=lambda_j/7`, hence `lambda_j<=7` and `[U:V_j]=r_j lambda_j`.

If lambda_j were seven, the product set UU_j would be all G. In particular the representative of the disjoint coset a_jU_j would belong to UU_j, forcing that left coset to meet U. Hence `1<=lambda_j<=6`. This uses actual subgroups, not the false general assertion that an intersection index divides the product of two subgroup indices. The fibre subgroup and lambda_j are fixed before any enlargement.

### 3.2 Lemma NR, LF72–86

Let U be normal in L and suppose `d=[G:L]` lacks seven. Then `d|r_i` and `gcd(d,7r_j)=1` for every other j. The elementary coprime-index lemma gives `LU_j=G`, so L meets every original a_jU_j and `[L:L∩U_j]=7r_j`.

Projection to P=L/U factors this index as `c_j [U:V_j]=c_j r_j lambda_j`. Thus `c_j lambda_j=7`. Since lambda_j is strictly below seven, it is one and c_j is seven. The projected support avoids identity: any identity-fibre point would give an original intersection with U.

If two projected supports overlap, choose a representative of the common U-fibre. The two original pieces translate into left V_j/V_k cosets inside U of coprime indices r_j,r_k. They intersect by the same coprime-index lemma, contradicting original disjointness. Hence seven disjoint quotient supports each have size |P|/7, exhaust P, yet all avoid identity. Contradiction.

Therefore every such normal overgroup retains seven. Applying this to `L=N_G(U)` is valid because **U is normal in its normalizer**, not because the normalizer is presumed normal in G. Consequently `[G:N]=7s` and `|N/U|` divides r_i. The proof also correctly excludes U normal in G without excluding arbitrary nonnormal overgroups.

## 4. One quotient for ALL actual index-two overgroups, LF90–110

## 6. Elementary-abelian slices and the affine lemma, LF145–177

For any E≤Q elementary abelian, a nonempty intersection of E with xH is an actual affine coset of E∩H, regardless of whether H is normal. Its index is at most `[Q:H]`, by the injection into left Q/H cosets, and is a power of two. Identity avoidance makes that index at least two. Consequently A restrictions have codimension one or two, whereas B/C restrictions are affine hyperplanes avoiding zero, hence negative character halfspaces.

For an A support its defining affine values form a nonzero linear functional ell on its annihilator W_A. A character vanishing on every point of that support is exactly a member of `ker ell` in W_A, of dimension at most one. This correctly distinguishes annihilating the direction space from vanishing on the affine support.

The disjoint-A lemma is valid. Four density-at-least-one-quarter supports would exhaust E while avoiding zero, so there are at most three. Two disjoint affine systems must disagree on some common nonzero character, and therefore their annihilators intersect nontrivially and have total rank at most three. If three supports occur none can be a hyperplane, by density. All three annihilators then have dimension two. Were their total rank at least four, the third would lie outside the rank-three sum of the first two, and its intersections with them must be the same line. That is also their common intersection line. Pairwise disjointness would demand three pairwise different binary offsets on that line's nonzero character, impossible.

The case split LF163–169 preserves all private-three qualifications:

- With at least two A supports, choose one not owning three. All B/C characters vanish on it and hence lie in its at-most-one-dimensional affine vanishing subspace. They add no rank beyond the joint A span, which is at most three.
- With one regular A, the same argument bounds total rank by two.
- With the sole A belonging to the private-three owner, B still adds no rank outside its annihilator. C has rank at most three. A total rank five would make its two independent affine equations and three independent present C characters jointly solvable, giving four colliders. If A were a hyperplane, total rank four would cause the same contradiction. Thus only the codimension-two private-owner case can reach rank four.
- With no A and both B,C present, every regular B is impossible because two negative hyperplanes in a binary vector space always meet, whereas the corresponding fibre gcd is one. Thus B is only the possible private-three owner. Its character cannot add a fourth independent direction to three present C characters, or four negative halfspaces would meet. The combined rank is at most three. The one-type cases obey the stated smaller bounds.

The common kernel of all these defining characters is therefore a genuine blind subgroup B_E of codimension at most four, usually at most three. An A support cannot contain a point on which all its defining characters are zero because ell is nonzero; B/C negative halfspaces cannot either. This proves blindness, not merely a bound on aggregate incidences, for **every** elementary abelian E.
