## 6. Two short exact slice lemmas

### 6.1 A nonzero low-magnitude row costs at least four sites

For an outer row put beta_t=phi(p_t). It is supported exactly at its c_t magnitude1 positions. At every L in M, (9) and the empty column give

    H4 beta_t(L)=0 modulo3.                         (11)

If c_t=1, its transform is always±1, impossible. If c_t=2, its transform is0 or±2, with zero set one affine hyperplane. That cannot contain the full-affine-rank M. If c_t=3, divisibility by3 means its three signed characters all agree; two independent character equations give an affine two-plane of four points, too small to contain M. Hence

    c_t=0 OR c_t>=4.                                (12)

There is also an exact equality classification when c_t=4. Four support points have affine rank2 or3. In rank3, affine/sign gauge reduces their transform to1+chi_1+chi_2+chi_3; its zeros lie in an affine hyperplane, again unable to contain M. In rank2, signs of product−1 give transform magnitude2 everywhere and no zeros; signs of product+1 are an affine character on that plane. Thus **c_t=4 requires beta_t to be a character-signed affine two-plane**, whose transform is supported on an affine two-plane contained in the ten nonempty columns. This is not a sufficient completion condition.

### 6.2 All-magnitude3 rows are spikes or four-site6 rows

If c_t=0, write p_t=3epsilon with epsilon a Boolean sign on16 points. Every f_t(L) is even, because each column of (10) has even cardinality. Equation (9) implies3H4epsilon=4f_t, so every coefficient of H4epsilon is divisible by8. Parseval gives exactly two possibilities:

- one coefficient±16, giving f_t with one entry±12;
- four coefficients±8, giving f_t with four entries±6.

In the second case those four support sites form an affine two-plane: the inverse four-point signed support must have transform magnitude2 everywhere, which is impossible for four affine-independent points and forces the affine-plane, negative-sign-product case. Thus the second type is geometrically constrained, not just any four entries.

A spike can occur only in a size12 column, and a column cannot contain two spikes because its squared Fourier norm is192<2*144. If a size12 column contains a spike, it has no coefficient±6: for a support S of XOR0,

    f_t(L)=sum A(a,L) modulo4 for EVERY t.           (13)

Indeed its change from t=0 is−2 times a signed halfspace count, whose parity is t dot XOR(S)=0. A spike makes the common residue0 modulo4.

More generally, if any coefficient is±6, (13) makes all16 coefficients2 modulo4. Column Parseval is sum_t f_t(L)^2=16m. Consequently the number of magnitude6 coefficients is at most4 in a size12 column, at most3 in a size10 column, and at most2 in a size8 column. This bound counts all rows, not only the all3 rows.

### Proof
