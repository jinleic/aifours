## 2. Two basic tools

### 2.1 Minimum weight for low polar rank

For `rank B≤4`, item 1 puts `1_Z` in RM(5,8). A nonzero word there has minimum weight `2^(8−5)=8`; every minimum-weight word is an affine 3-flat. Since `0∈Z`, **Z is a linear 3-space**. This uses no D/m profile list. Also, q vanishes on Z, so Z is totally singular: `B(x,y)=q(x+y)+q(x)+q(y)=0` for x,y∈Z.

Conversely, for `rank B≤4`, every linear 3-space is compatible with (P\*) before the requirement `q|Z=0`: any 5-space L meets R nontrivially. If its restricted polar rank is 4, the one-dimensional restricted radical lies in R, so the slope `B(·,r)` is zero; otherwise the parity is already constant. A 3-space meets every L-coset in either zero or `2^d` points, where `d=dim(Z∩L)`. If d=0 the eight counts are all 1; otherwise every count is even. Hence (P\*) alone accepts exactly the 97,155 linear 3-spaces at each such polar rank.

### 2.2 Whole odd-half identity

Let `n_j` count odd-half `|k|=j` and put `D=n₁+n₃`. Define the odd sign `g=(-1)^Q`, `h=1_{|k|∈{3,5}}`, and `δ` by

`k=g(12h−7)−8δ`, with `δ∈{−1,0,1}`, `wt(δ)=D`, and `Σδg=n₃−n₁`.

Let m be the number of agreeing unordered T-pairs. It is even by balance. If `v=Walsh(k)/128`, then v is ternary and has exactly m zeros. For `rank B=2t`, the Walsh transform of g is `2^(8−t)σ` on an affine set Y of size `2^(2t)`, and zero outside. Put `S=Σ_{y∈Y}σ(y)v(y)`. Odd Parseval gives the exact whole-half identities

`Σkg = 32(m−116−D/2) = 2^(7−t)S`, hence `m=116+D/2+2^(2−t)S`, with `|S|≤2^(2t)`.

For completeness, the first equality follows by substituting the four permitted odd magnitudes into the decomposition and using `8(m−60)=6n₁+5n₃+3n₅`; the second is character orthogonality. No residue `m≡1−D (mod 3)` is introduced.

The even-half energy bound is

`64m ≤ 16(z−8)+36(256−z)`,

because the eight zeros are among q's z zero positions. The nondegenerate quotient weights give z=128 or 256 at rank 0; z=64,128 or 192 at rank 2; and z=96,128 or 160 at rank 4.

## 3. Ranks 0, 2 and 4

**Rank 0.** §2.2 gives `m≥112`; the even bound gives `m≤62` or `m≤102`. Contradiction.

**Rank 2.** §2.2 gives `m≥108+D/2`, so z=128 and z=192 are incompatible with the even bounds `m≤102` and `m≤82`. Thus z=64 and q vanishes exactly on R. By §2.1, Z is a 3-space inside R. The remaining direct bounds are `D≡0 (mod 4)`, `D≤28`, and `108+D/2≤m≤122`; they are bounds, not a claimed profile roster. There are `[6 choose 3]_2=1,395` abstract choices of Z for fixed q.

**Rank 4: exclude z=160 and z=128 without P1.** The whole-half identity yields `m≥100+D/2`; z=160 gives `m≤92`. At z=128 the even bound gives `m≤102` and hence `D≤4`; D is even. We exclude D=0,2,4 as follows.

Modulo 3, `128v=Walsh(k)` and `Walsh(g)=64σ1_Y` imply

`v ≡ σ1_Y − Walsh(δ) (mod 3)`, `|Y|=16`.

Outside Y, `v=0` exactly when `Walsh(δ)=0 (mod 3)`. With D=0 this gives at least 240 zeros of v. With D=2 the two distinct character terms cancel on exactly 128 points, at least 112 of them outside Y. Both contradict `m≤102`.

For D=4, `m≥102` forces `m=102`, `S=−16`, and therefore v has no zero on Y. Four distinct signed characters have exactly 0, 96 or 192 zeros modulo 3 over F₂⁸. To see this, compare the three character ratios to the first. If they are independent, 3/8 of all patterns give zero. If their rank is 2, their product is constant: one sign syndrome gives zero on 3/4 of points, the other gives none. With no zeros of v on Y, 96 or fewer zeros of `Walsh(δ)` give `m≤96`, while 192 give `m≥192−16=176`. Both contradict m=102.

Thus z=96, q vanishes on the four-dimensional R and descends to an elliptic 4-dimensional form. The direct bound is `D≤24` with D even; **no complete D/m roster is claimed**. A totally singular 3-space Z projects to a totally singular subspace of the elliptic 4-space, whose Witt index is 1. Therefore `dim(Z∩R)≥2`. There are `[4 choose 3]_2=15` choices inside R and `5·[4 choose 2]_2·4=700` with one-dimensional projection, for a total of **715** at fixed q. These are only parity/quadric candidates.

## 4. Rank 8: parity excludes all eight-point sets

Assume B is nondegenerate. For any 3-dimensional totally isotropic D, set `L=D^{⊥_B}`. Then `dim L=5` and `rank(B|L)=2`, so (P\*) makes the parity on D* constant, either all zero or all one. For x,y∈D,

`G(x,y):=Σ_{z∈Z} B(z,x)B(z,y)=0`.

Indeed every quadratic monomial on D* has even total weight, including when every coset parity is one. Every B-orthogonal pair extends to such D. Hence G vanishes on every pair with B(x,y)=0. For fixed nonzero x, `G(x,·)` is a multiple of `B(x,·)`; linearity in x shows the multiple is one global c, so `G=cB`.

Let W have columns `w_z=B(z,·)`. Its column for z=0 is zero, and the other seven sum to zero because `⊕Z=0`, so `rank G≤rank W≤6<8`. Thus c=0 and `WWᵀ=0`. The row space of W is self-orthogonal and has a fixed zero coordinate, hence is a self-orthogonal code of length 7 and has dimension at most 3. B is nondegenerate, so the eight columns w_z are distinct. They must therefore be **all** points of a 3-dimensional linear space; Z is itself a linear 3-space. Because q vanishes there, it is totally isotropic.

Extend a basis `e₁,e₂,e₃` of Z to a symplectic basis `e₁,…,e₄,f₁,…,f₄`, and choose

`L=⟨f₁,f₂,f₃,e₄+e₁,f₄+e₂⟩`.

Then `L∩Z=0`, so every coset has one zero. But `rank(B|L)=4` and its radical is generated by f₃, so (P\*) requires the nonconstant slope `B(·,f₃)` across K/L. A constant parity vector cannot have that slope. Contradiction. ∎

## 5. Rank 6: exact parity classification

Let R have dimension 2 and let `V=K/R` be a nondegenerate symplectic 6-space. Fix a complement so elements are `(ρ,s)∈R⊕V`. The following classifies **all** eight-element sets satisfying (P\*), before imposing q or Booleanity.

### 5.1 Linear three-spaces cannot satisfy (P\*)

Suppose W⊂K is any 3-space. Set `t=dim(W∩R)` and `P=π(W)`, so `dim P=3−t≥1`. Pick a hyperplane H of V not containing P. The intersection `P∩H` has dimension `2−t`. Write the fibres of W over P as cosets of `W∩R`. Choose a linear map `ℓ:H→R` whose induced difference from W's section is injective on `P∩H` into `R/(W∩R)`; the dimensions on both sides are `2−t`. Then the graph L of ℓ has dimension 5 and `L∩W=0`.

Since H is a hyperplane in a nondegenerate symplectic 6-space, `rank(B|H)=4` and its radical is `H^⊥`, a nonzero line. Thus `rank(B|L)=4` and its radical generator is outside R. It induces a nonconstant slope on K/L. But W occupies every L-coset once. This violates (P\*). ∎

### 5.2 Every R-fibre has even intersection with Z

For any 3-space H⊂V, `L=R⊕H` has restricted polar rank at most 2, so the mod-2 multiplicity function w of the projection of Z has constant parity on the eight cosets of H. Every monomial of degree at most 2 on V factors through some 3-dimensional quotient and has even sum over that quotient. Therefore `w⊥RM(2,6)`, i.e. `w∈RM(3,6)`. Its weight is at most 8. By the minimum-weight theorem, either w=0 or its support is an affine 3-flat.

In the second case there are exactly eight occupied fibres, one zero in each. Since `0∈Z`, the affine flat is a linear 3-space P and Z is a section `Z={(σ(s),s):s∈P}` with `σ(0)=0`.

Fix nonzero v∈P. There exists a totally isotropic 2-space U⊂V such that `U^⊥∩P=⟨v⟩`. To construct it, the restriction map `v^⊥→(P/⟨v⟩)*` is surjective. Choose two preimages with independent restrictions; choose the first outside P, and add an element of `P^⊥` to the second if necessary to make their pairing zero. The resulting U has the required kernel on P.

For each line `⟨r⟩⊂R`, set `L=⟨r⟩⊕U^⊥`. This is a 5-space of restricted rank 2. The four quotient values of P each have two preimages s,s+v. Their two zero points have either the same or different R/⟨r⟩ labels. Since (P\*) requires all eight coset parities to be equal, `σ(s+v)+σ(s) mod ⟨r⟩` is independent of s. Taking two different lines in R shows the vector difference itself is independent of s. This holds for every v, and `σ(0)=0`, so σ is linear. Then Z is a 3-space, contradicting §5.1. Hence **w=0**: every R-fibre has 0, 2 or 4 zeros.

### 5.3 The fibre pattern and the nonlinear graph

For any line `⟨r⟩⊂R` and isotropic 2-space U⊂V, the same rank-two L makes all coset parities equal. A full R-fibre contributes even counts to both R/⟨r⟩ labels. A two-point fibre, with difference d∈R∖0, contributes nothing mod 2 if d=r and one point to each label if d≠r.

The possibilities for eight zeros are `(4,4)`, `(4,2,2)`, and `(2,2,2,2)` across distinct R-fibres. A `(4,4)` set is a 3-space and fails by §5.1. With `(4,2,2)`, choose r equal to one pair difference if the differences differ, or different from the common difference if they agree. In the first situation exactly one pair splits; in the second, two split and an isotropic U can be chosen to separate their distinct projected fibre locations. Either choice gives nonconstant coset parity.

For `(2,2,2,2)`, let n_r count pairs of difference r. For fixed r, the number of split pairs is `4−n_r`; constant coset parity forces this number even, so every n_r is even. If two differences occur twice, choose r equal to one difference and choose U to separate the two remaining fibre locations, a contradiction. Thus **all four pairs have the same nonzero difference r₀**.

For r≠r₀ all four pairs split. On V/U^⊥, the parity of their four projected locations must be constant. It follows that their XOR pairs trivially with every vector of U. Every vector of V belongs to an isotropic 2-space, so the four locations sum to zero. They include 0 and are distinct, hence form a linear 2-plane P.

After choosing `r₁∈R∖⟨r₀⟩` and basis `p₁,p₂` of P, the four zero pairs have the form

`Z={t r₀+x a+y b+xy r₁ : t,x,y∈F₂}`.                         (★)

Here a,b are lifts of `p₁,p₂`. An affine/linear section would give a 3-space and is excluded by §5.1; every nonlinear Boolean function on P with value 0 at the origin is `xy` plus a linear function, absorbed into the lifts. Thus (★) is necessary.

### 5.4 Sufficiency for the parity abstraction and its count

Conversely, every set (★) satisfies (P\*) for B, **without** asserting q=0 or a target spectrum. Let L be a 5-space.

- If `r₀∈L`, every coset has even intersection with Z. If `rank(B|L)=4`, its one-dimensional radical includes r₀ and is global, so its (P\*) slope is zero.
- If `r₀∉L` but `dim(L∩R)=1`, the image of R in K/L is one-dimensional. Modulo that image, the projection of (★) is the linear map `(x,y)↦xπ(a)+yπ(b)`. Each coset has even parity, or all have odd parity. If the restricted rank is 4, its radical is `L∩R`, again with zero slope.
- If `L∩R=0`, its projection H is a hyperplane of V, its restricted rank is 4, and its radical generator has nonzero V-component in H^⊥. The image of R in K/L has dimension 2, generated by the independent images C,D of r₀,r₁. If P⊂H, modulo C the four quadratic values `αx+βy+xy` have odd counts of both values, so parity is one on exactly the four cosets in the image of R. If P is not contained in H, choose a in H and b outside H. At y=0 the x increment is A=π(a); at y=1 it is A+D. Exactly one of A and A+D lies outside ⟨C⟩, so precisely one of the two R-image cosets has odd parity. In either subcase the parity is an affine nonconstant function with slope `B(·,rad(B|L))`, as required.

For fixed B, choose one of 3 lines r₀, one of `[6 choose 2]_2=651` planes P, and one of 4 nonlinear sections P→R/⟨r₀⟩. The line, plane and section are uniquely determined by Z, giving **3·651·4=7,812** parity-compatible sets. This is an exact classification of the parity abstraction, not of Boolean spectra. ∎

## 6. Rank-six quadratic compatibility and its counts

Write `q(ρ,s)=λ(ρ)+Q(s)` with λ linear on R and Q a nondegenerate quadratic on V. Since r₀∈Z, `λ(r₀)=0`. In (★), q vanishes on a,b and on `a+b+r₁`, hence

`λ(r₁)=B(a,b)=B̄(p₁,p₂)`.                                      (†)

If λ=0 then P must be totally singular for Q. If λ≠0 then `⟨r₀⟩=ker λ` and P must be a nondegenerate 2-plane. For a fixed such q, every nondegenerate P has exactly one section whose two points in each fibre satisfy q=0; its quadratic term is nonzero by (†). The number of such P is `63·32/6=336`.

If λ=0 and Q is elliptic, it has 28 zeros and 45 totally singular 2-planes. For each P, all three r₀ lines and four nonlinear sections obey q=0, giving `45·3·4=540` necessary zero sets. The 45 count follows by choosing one of 27 nonzero singular vectors, then one of 5 nonzero singular vectors of its elliptic 4-dimensional quotient, and dividing by the 3 nonzero vectors per plane.

If λ=0 and Q is hyperbolic, P is totally singular but the case is excluded by the new moment argument in §7. Before that argument the parity/quadric abstraction allows `105·3·4=1,260` sets. None of these counts asserts feasibility of the original Boolean target.

## 7. New rank-six moment obstruction: singular radical, hyperbolic quotient

Assume the λ=0, hyperbolic-Q case, and let Z have form (★). Put t=r₁, so `t∈R∖⟨r₀⟩`. For the support M of f define

`M^t={x∈M:t·x=1}`, `N^t(a)=#{x∈M^t:a·x=1}`, and `τ^t=⊕M^t`.

For every a, with the usual a=0 or a=t exception understood modulo 8,

`k(a+t)−k(a) ≡ |M^t|−2(a·τ^t)−4 C(N^t(a),2)  (mod 8)`.

The last term is a Boolean polynomial of degree ≤2. Since q is R-invariant, `k(a+t)≡k(a) (mod 4)` on K. At a=0 this makes `|M^t|` divisible by 4; the same congruence for every a∈K gives `τ^t∈K^⊥=⟨T⟩`. Therefore on K the mod-8 difference divided by 4 is a quadratic function φ_t.

On q=0, k is 0 or ±4. Thus `φ_t(a)=1_Z(a)+1_Z(a+t)`. The set (★) has one ⟨r₀⟩-coset in each fibre over P, so translation by t swaps its zero and nonzero cosets there; outside P neither is a zero. Restricting φ_t to V gives a degree-≤2 Boolean polynomial p satisfying

`p(s)=1_P(s)` for every `s∈{Q=0}`.                              (‡)

We show (‡) is impossible for hyperbolic Q.

For each u∈P, the affine derivative `D_u p(s)=p(s+u)+p(s)` vanishes on `{Q=0}∩u^⊥`, because translation by u preserves both Q=0 and membership in P. It has zero constant term. The singular vectors in that set span u^⊥: extend u to a hyperbolic symplectic basis, where a basis of u^⊥ consists of singular vectors. Thus `D_u p` is either 0 or `B̄(u,·)`. Comparing the derivatives for two independent u,v∈P and u+v shows the coefficient is the same c for every u∈P. Consequently `p':=p+cQ` is invariant under all P-translations.

For s∉P^⊥, Q is a nonconstant affine function on s+P, so that coset contains a singular point outside P. At that point (‡) gives `p'=0`; P-invariance then gives `p'(s)=0`. Hence `supp(p')⊂P^⊥`, a 4-space of size 16. But p' has degree ≤2 and `p'(0)=1`, so the RM(2,6) minimum-weight bound forces `supp(p')=P^⊥`. Hyperbolicity extends P to a singular 3-space, giving a singular point in `P^⊥∖P`; at that point (‡) requires p'=0, contradiction. ∎

This argument uses actual support moments in addition to (P\*). It does **not** rule out the elliptic or λ≠0 rank-six cases.
