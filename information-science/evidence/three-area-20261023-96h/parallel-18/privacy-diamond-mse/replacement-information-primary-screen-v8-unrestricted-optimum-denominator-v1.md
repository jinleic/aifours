## 2. A universal private-leaf forcing lemma — no promised guards

**Lemma PF — WRITTEN; imports only the endpoint-inclusive missed-mass objective and elementary leaf geodesics. Scope:** any finite simple connected unweighted base graph G_0 of n_0 vertices, any specified subset S of k vertices with 1<=k<=n_0, and the ordinary unrestricted exact budget k. Attach L=n_0+1 distinct new pendant leaves to EACH vertex of S. All new and old vertices remain eligible.

Then S is the UNIQUE minimum-beta selection in the enlarged graph.

**Proof.** If C=S, every pair with a selected endpoint has zero avoidance. Every pair involving a new leaf also has zero avoidance: every path from that leaf to another vertex contains its selected attachment center. Only pairs among the n_0-k old unselected vertices can remain missed, so

```
beta(S)<=binom(n_0-k,2).                              (PF1)
```

For any other k-element C, some center u in S is omitted, since including every center would already use the entire budget. At most k of u's L private leaves can be selected. Hence at least

```
L-k=n_0-k+1
```

of those leaves are unselected. For any two of them the UNIQUE shortest path has length two and is leaf-u-leaf. Its endpoints and its center are all unselected, and no selected vertex elsewhere can lie on that path. Every such pair contributes exactly 1, giving

```
beta(C)>=binom(n_0-k+1,2)>binom(n_0-k,2)              (PF2)
```

when n_0-k>=1. If n_0=k, the same stated construction needs a small endpoint clarification: an omitted center u has at most k selected private leaves, but equality leaves only one unselected leaf. In this boundary case some unselected leaf and u itself give a missed pair of mass 1, because u is also unselected, whereas beta(S)=0. Thus strict inequality still follows. Alternatively the present application below has n_0-k=2m+1>=1 and uses only PF1/PF2.

All competitors are included: other centers, cycle vertices, portals and private leaves. There is NO compulsory-site promise, candidate set, weight or external consistency condition. Attaching pendant edges preserves the cactus property whenever the base graph is a cactus. The lemma is a written elementary forcing argument; no claim of literature novelty is made.
