## 6. Positive exact PRIOR equivalence for the two selector comparisons

Primary source actually used: Amir and Rietzke, [*A Comment on “Monotone Comparative Statics”*, author-posted February14,2025 manuscript](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/229183/1/23292-2_3_.pdf). Its complete12-page body, definitions, Lemma1, Theorem1(i) and proof, and Theorem2/proof were read. Final publication is authenticated separately as [Econometrica93(4), July2025,1481-1490, DOI10.3982/ECTA23292](https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/2025/07/01/A-Comment-on-Monotone-Comparative-Statics). The public author manuscript, not inaccessible final PDF typography, is the exact theorem text used below.

Their strong set order A≤sB means a∧b∈A and a∨b∈B for a∈A,b∈B. Theorem1(i) gives strong-set monotonicity of argmax under QSM dominance. Lemma1(iii) supplies that dominance when one objective is QSM and there is single-crossing dominance. Their definitions explicitly allow derivative/ID comparisons as a sufficient route to single-crossing dominance.

Every separable objective is modular under coordinate min/max, hence QSM, even if it is nonlinear in each coordinate. The E problems have strictly convex separable minima, so the corresponding maxima of their negatives are unique. Thus strong-set ordering of their singleton argmax sets gives componentwise ordering.

### 6.1 E Section4.1 is a direct binary-comparison corollary

Fix an interval of unchanged active indices. Write C(y) for E's endpoint-envelope polytope and h_y(s) for its positive weighted cubic objective. The verified bound movements give

```
C(y2) <=s C(y1),   y1<y2.
```

The offset constraints are a sublattice after subtracting i from coordinate i. Both negative objectives are modular on a common product box containing the polytopes. Their gradients obey

```
∂(-h_y1)/∂s_i - ∂(-h_y2)/∂s_i
= a_i(y2²-y1²) >=0.
```

So -h_y2 is ID/single-crossing dominated by -h_y1; modularity and Lemma1(iii) give QSM dominance. Theorem1(i) yields s*(y2)≤s*(y1). This is exactly the selector's nonincreasing comparison.

### 6.2 E Section4.2 is the same prior theorem after shifting

Put delta=y2-y1. Let A(z)=h_y1(z+delta) be the shifted old objective and B(z)=h_y2(z) the new one. Their choice sets satisfy S_A≤sS_B because their upper bounds coincide and the new lower bounds are higher.

Use the common product lattice

```
X = product_i [t_i-delta, u_i(y2)].
```

It contains both choice polytopes. Since each index is active at y2, every z∈X has z_i≥t_i-delta≥y1. With c_i=0 for head coordinates and c_L=m≥0,

```
∂(-B)/∂z_i - ∂(-A)/∂z_i
= 2a_i*delta*(z_i+c_i-y1) >=0 on ALL of X.
```

The product box is convex, so integrating this ordered gradient along comparable points gives ID/single-crossing dominance on that whole comparison domain. Both objectives are modular. The same Theorem1(i) therefore gives

```
s*(y1)-delta <= s*(y2).
```

Together with Section6.1 this proves the within-interval1-Lipschitz estimate. The ambient-domain sign restriction is verified here; it is not assumed from a local derivative at one optimizer.
