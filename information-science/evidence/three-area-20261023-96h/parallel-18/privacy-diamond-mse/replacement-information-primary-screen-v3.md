## 2. Current2026 theorem: what its supplied structure really includes

Direct source: [Mauro E. S. Morales, *The Hidden Subgroup Problem in Semidirect Products and Quasi-Hamiltonian Groups*, arXiv2608.05321v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05321v2), header28-Sep2026. This continuation reads the reference list and AppendixA comparison plus the actual Section4 construction/encoding paragraphs, in addition to v2's TheoremC and input-model coverage.

TheoremC assumes a structured quasi-Hamiltonian presentation. For each relevant non-Hamiltonian Sylow p-factor, the source assumes

```
P=A<b>, A normal and Abelian,
P/A=<bA>, |P:A|=q=p^m,
b^(-1)*a*b=a^r for every a in A, r=1+p^s,
```

with actual generators/coordinates and the action data available. This is a special decomposition, not an arbitrary list of relators or a generic composition series.

The Section4.2 encoding uses the ability to write actual P elements as `b^j*a`, with0<=j<q and a in Abelian coordinates. Crucially the source only has `b^q in A`; it does NOT require `b^q=1`. In its associated Abelian group B the new formal generator c satisfies `c^q=a0`, with a0 constructed from that actual extension element. Therefore a proposed recognition bridge cannot silently replace the extension by a split semidirect product or throw away the intersection/extension relation.

These are source-definition comparisons only. No power, Smith form, coordinate, finite group, matrix, algorithm or circuit was computed or tested here. The theorem's proof is not re-certified by this selection note.

## 3. Older primary source makes the generic preprocessing PRIOR

### 3.1 Watrous: actual outputs and a warning against over-claiming structure

[John Watrous, *Quantum algorithms for solvable groups*, arXivquant-ph/0011023v2](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0011023v2),26-Jan2001, was directly opened. Actual read includes the abstract, Theorem1, introduction and the unique-encoding/oracle/solvable-series definitions.

Theorem1 gives a polynomial-time quantum algorithm for the order of a solvable group and an approximate uniform subgroup state, with explicit encoding/error dependence. The introduction also states membership, subgroup equality and normality tests, and explains how Abelian factor-group algorithms can be used despite non-unique quotient representatives. It notes that a polynomial-length cyclic-factor solvable series can be obtained from arbitrary generators by a classical Monte Carlo routine.

This is already more than a bare order oracle, but it is NOT by itself the special `A,b,p,s` modular-group recognition theorem. Its explicit correction to an earlier version is instructive: isomorphic factors of a derived series do not imply isomorphic groups, so an earlier claimed solvable-group isomorphism consequence was withdrawn. Accordingly, possessing a generic series/factor description cannot simply be ASSERTED to reveal all extension data or the special quasi-Hamiltonian structure.

This source is used at its directly stated outputs; no independent verification of its entire quantum algorithm is claimed.

### 3.2 Ivanyos–Magniez–Santha: generic presentation, center and Sylow are also PRIOR

[Gábor Ivanyos, Frédéric Magniez and Miklos Santha, *Efficient quantum algorithms for some instances of the non-Abelian hidden subgroup problem*, arXivquant-ph/0102014v1](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0102014v1),2-Feb2001, was directly opened. Actual coverage includes the abstract/introduction/model, Section3 Theorem4 and Corollary5, and Section4.1 Theorem6. The relevant statements were reread as full raw ranges, not inferred from a search snippet.

Its Theorem4, quoting the Beals–Babai structure algorithms, supplies under declared prime/order/discrete-log/elementary-Abelian constructive-membership facilities:

1. membership;
2. group order AND a presentation;
3. center generators;
4. a composition series with nice representations of factors;

The source defines “presentation” explicitly as a generator list plus relators whose normal closure is the kernel of the free-group map. That generic output is NOT automatically the special Abelian-normal/cyclic-factor presentation above.

Corollary5 states quantum implementations of the tasks given element-order and elementary-Abelian constructive-membership oracles. Theorem6 then supplies those facilities for UNIQUE encodings, with total running time polynomial in input size plus its parameter nu(G). The paper states nu(G)=1 for solvable groups. Finite quasi-Hamiltonian groups are nilpotent, hence solvable at the2026 source's classification boundary. Consequently, in this model generic group presentation, center, composition and Sylow computation are not new open subproblems.

The source also carefully distinguishes normal-hidden-subgroup recovery in suitable ambient classes from unrestricted HSP, and the costs/conditions of non-unique quotient representations. No general non-Abelian Fourier transform or arbitrary-group polynomial algorithm is silently obtained from Theorem6.

It is a different promise problem, and ambient encoding/algorithmic parameters must be specified separately. Switching to it would be another target choice, not completing the QH-RECOGNITION bridge by a change of words.

The prior normal-HSP algorithms in the2001 source also carry ambient-class/oracle conditions. They must not be used as unrestricted arbitrary-group subroutines merely because a permutable subgroup is close to normal in an abstract subgroup-lattice sense.
