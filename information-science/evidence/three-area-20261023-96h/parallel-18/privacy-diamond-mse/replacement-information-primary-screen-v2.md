## 3. TEX533 — unconditional decidability of the real exponential field

### 3.1 Literal book quantifiers

The printed533 article **Tarski指数函数问题**, author 詹乃军, explicitly defines the finite first-order language with variables, constants0/1, addition/subtraction/multiplication, exp, equality/order, Boolean connectives and quantifiers. Its question is whether there is an effective method deciding truth of ANY closed sentence in the real exponential structure in FINITELY many steps.

A precise modern statement preserving that question is:

```
Does there exist a total algorithm D such that, for EVERY finitely
encoded closed first-order sentence phi in
{0,1,+,-,*,exp,<,=}, D halts and returns TRUE iff
(R;0,1,+,-,*,exp,<,=) satisfies phi?
```

All finite quantifier patterns are included. No arbitrary uncomputable real parameters are added as input constants. No polynomial-time bound is requested by the book: DECIDABILITY itself is the target. Bounded interval approximation, polynomial-only real closed fields, a fixed quantifier fragment or a conditional Schanuel theorem is not a full answer.

Printed534 already distinguishes model completeness from full decidability and records a conditional Macintyre–Wilkie result. Its statement that a Schanuel assumption would suffice is not permission to assume that conjecture in a new unconditional solution.

## 4. HSP545 — general non-Abelian hidden-subgroup recovery

### 4.1 Exact promise and efficiency quantifiers

Printed545, **非阿贝尔群上的隐子群问题**, author 孙晓明, states the promise directly: a black-box f:G->X is constant on each left coset of a hidden subgroup K and distinct on different cosets; recover K by an effective polynomial-time quantum algorithm. Printed546 explicitly says the algorithm need only work on inputs satisfying the hidden-subgroup promise and need not recognize violations. It also describes output of generators for graph automorphism groups, rather than an exponentially long subgroup listing.

For a meaningful standard finite-group formalization:

```
For EVERY finite-group input G in a fixed efficient reversible
black-box encoding, and EVERY oracle f hiding left cosets of H<=G,
output a generating set for H with success probability at least2/3
in time polynomial in the complete input/oracle encoding size;
element encoding length is polynomial in log|G|.
```

The group-operation/encoding convention must be frozen. Polynomial in a full Cayley table of size comparable to |G| is not the intended exponential quantum speedup. The source book's black-box f promise alone does not supply every group-operation primitive or an efficient non-Abelian Fourier transform for free. The current primary below gives an explicit oracle model that can serve as the model-comparison reference; it is not a license to add extra structural data silently.

This is different from the already rejected PG516 polynomial graph-game solver: HSP asks for a quantum recovery algorithm from a group-valued coset oracle, not a parity objective on a given graph. It is also not the roster's Sun coset inequality or R-minus guessing problem.

### 4.2 Latest dated primary and its exact positive restrictions

[Mauro E. S. Morales, *The Hidden Subgroup Problem in Semidirect Products and Quasi-Hamiltonian Groups*, arXiv2608.05321v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05321v2), CURRENT header **28 Sep2026**, was opened directly. Actual coverage includes the abstract/introduction/main results TheoremsA–C, exact Section2.1.3 black-box model and Section4 input-model paragraph. Truncated search excerpts were explicitly reread raw.

The abstract AND introduction explicitly say no polynomial-time quantum algorithm is known for the GENERAL non-Abelian HSP. The paper separates polynomial QUERY complexity from polynomial TOTAL runtime: even enough dihedral Fourier samples do not supply an efficient recovery/postprocessing algorithm, and efficient implementation of a joint pretty-good measurement is a separate issue.

Its positive claims have load-bearing restrictions:

- TheoremA treats cyclic normal factor Z_N with prime-power complement, under its N/p polylog condition.
- TheoremB treats a bounded-generator-rank Abelian normal factor and SCALAR action, with Exp(A)/p polynomial in log|G|.
- TheoremC treats ALL finite quasi-Hamiltonian groups **given a STRUCTURED quasi-Hamiltonian presentation**, not arbitrary black-box generators alone.

For TheoremC the input supplies the Sylow factorization and the structural description of each factor. In the non-Hamiltonian case it supplies `P=A<b>`, A's generators/coordinates and the action parameters p,s for b's power action. Section4 explicitly states that this is additional computational information and does NOT address the constructive-recognition problem of recovering that presentation from arbitrary generators.

Section2.1.3 also distinguishes non-unique quotient encodings from unique encodings required by certain subroutines, and describes state-preparation/solvability conditions. No new proposal may silently assume those conversions at no cost.

**Dated status:** a directly matched28-Sep2026 primary OPEN assertion for general non-Abelian HSP; no full resolution located by the bounded search. The positive listed families are PRIOR at their stated promises, not solutions of all G.

## 5. COV472 — covariance estimation from nonhomogeneous radar samples

### 5.1 Actual printed questions and missing model choices

Printed472–473, **非均匀样本条件下的协方差矩阵估计问题**, 廖桂生、李明, concerns range-dependent radar clutter distributions, particularly non-side-looking/bistatic airborne radar. Printed473 asks two questions:

1. Can a linear transformation make data from ALL range cells with different space-time distributions have a common space-time distribution?
2. Can covariance be accurately estimated directly from nonhomogeneous samples?

These are actual questions, not a claim that the chapter already supplies a single minimax/statistical decision problem. The wording does not fully fix:

- one COMMON transformation versus a range-indexed family produced by one rule;
- invertibility or another target/signal-information-preservation constraint;
- known physical geometry/covariances versus transformations estimated from the same finite data;
- Gaussian/elliptical/texture/mixture families, dependence and contamination assumptions;
- the target covariance (e.g. a particular cell under test) and how other cells' laws are related to it;
- exact equality of DISTRIBUTIONS versus merely covariance equality;
- finite-sample exact recovery, unbiasedness, consistency, minimax loss or a specified downstream detection/SINR criterion.

Without those choices, there is no unique quantified mathematical acceptance statement. A strict screen must not silently replace “all nonhomogeneous samples” by i.i.d. training snapshots from one structured covariance model.

### 5.2 Written model diagnostic — not a new full solution

The transformation ambiguity is consequential, not cosmetic:

- If arbitrary information-destroying linear maps are permitted, the zero map produces one common degenerate output law. That is not a meaningful radar solution.
- A SINGLE COMMON invertible transformation cannot turn two different input laws into one common law: applying its inverse to equal pushforwards would make the original laws equal. Even covariance equality under one common invertible congruence would already force equal original covariances.
- If a DIFFERENT known transformation is permitted at every range cell, known nonsingular Gaussian covariances can be whitened/transported by standard linear algebra. This does not solve unknown-covariance estimation or homogenize arbitrary non-Gaussian laws.

These are WRITTEN definition checks only, not an executed calculation, newly discovered theorem or claim that the whole article is trivially solved. They show why a useful full answer must include information retention and available-knowledge quantifiers.

Likewise, arbitrary unrelated training-cell laws do not identify an unobserved target-cell covariance merely by being called nonhomogeneous samples. A consistent/risk theorem needs an explicit relation to the target law. The chapter does not specify that relation sufficiently to select a universal new estimator theorem.
