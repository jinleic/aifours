`★ Insight ─────────────────────────────────────`
- **Why A and Λ separate at finite N.** The common pilot's two-step response carries the full mean-field gain (A+Λ)B. A private pilot's two-step response carries only (A+Λ/N)B. Inverting these two equations has determinant −(N−1)/N. That is why N=1 is unidentifiable (C4), and why the error-amplification constants 3 and 4 are largest at N=2.
- **The extra pilot control matters.** Without the (m_k+1)-th probe, the last lag-two sample would include an exploitation input that is correlated with the pilots. This would add an O(1/m_k) bias. Consistency would survive, but the exact finite-N identity (5.2) would not.
- **How the lower bound handles explosive competitors.** The deviation lower bound puts −M_T and εV_T inside one exponential supermartingale. Because of this, it covers competitors whose energy V_T is unbounded. A plain martingale strong law would need h to be bounded.
