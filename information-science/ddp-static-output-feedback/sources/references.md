# Primary-source evidence and literature boundary

Retrieved and checked on 2026-10-14 (America/Los_Angeles). A verified result below means the cited primary text was fetched and its stated scope checked; it does not mean this project formally verified every proof in that source. Failed retrieval and metadata-only checks are explicitly separated. PDF hashes and retrieval URLs are in [source-downloads.json](../evidence/source-downloads.json).

## Decisive sources

1. **Book problem.** Chen Benmei, "静态输出反馈下的干扰解耦" ("Disturbance Decoupling Problem with Static Output Feedback"), in *10000 Selected Problems in Sciences: Information Science*, Science Press, 2011, printed pp. 631-632; PDF pp. 652-653. Authoritative local images: [page 631](book/pdfpage-0652.png), [page 632](book/pdfpage-0653.png). **Verified from both images.** The printed definition of Sigma_Q appears to use B instead of E; the plant equation, dimensions, and Chen et al. 2000 define Sigma_Q=(A,E,C1,D1). We use E and disclose this apparent typographical error. D11=0 is the explicitly adopted model, not an unexamined claim about arbitrary algebraic feedback loops.

2. **Ben M. Chen, Iven M. Y. Mareels, Yufan Zheng, Cishen Zhang (2000).** "Solutions to disturbance decoupling problem with constant measurement feedback for linear systems." *Automatica* 36(11), 1717-1724. DOI: [10.1016/S0005-1098(00)00090-X](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0005-1098(00)00090-X). [Local full PDF](papers/chen-2000.pdf); [archived primary paper](https://web.archive.org/web/20240829235344if_/https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?repid=rep1&type=pdf&doi=5f87022c9d5caa1678532dcb62d33a3c8462fe61). **Full primary paper verified**, after publisher HTTP 403 and a CiteSeer redirect. Corollaries 5-6 characterize all gains by linear equations under left/right invertibility; Theorem 4 and Theorem 7 reduce the general problem and handle invertible reduced plants. Crucially, printed p. 1722 explicitly says the remaining problem is a finite-step QEPCAD computation and mentions Groebner bases plus QEPCAD for both DDPCM and DDPCMS. Appendix A, equation (A.7), uses Markov parameters through order n-1. This directly rules out claiming the current generic exact algebraic algorithm as a new solution. The paper's Theorem 2 uses a related common-gain linear matrix equation for necessary geometric constraints; our fixed-V statement should also not receive a priority claim.

3. **Amir Ali Ahmadi, Abraar Chaudhry, Ijay Narang, Yukai Tang (2026).** "Complexity of Output Feedback Stabilization." [arXiv:2609.20636v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20636v2), v1 submitted September 17; v2 HTML dated September 20. [Full HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20636v2), [local PDF](papers/sof-hardness-2026-v2.pdf). **Primary statement and proof context verified; preprint, not peer-review certification.** Theorem 1 establishes strong NP-hardness for unrestricted continuous-time static-output-feedback stabilization, even one measured output; C is binary, |A_ij| <= 256 N^2, |B_ij| <= 6. The introduction explicitly distinguishes this result from the older bounded-gain theorem. We rely only on the theorem statement plus our proved zero-channel embedding into stable DDP. No independent reconstruction of its full 3SAT reduction is claimed.

4. **Vincent Blondel and John N. Tsitsiklis (1997).** "NP-hardness of some linear control design problems." *SIAM Journal on Control and Optimization* 35(6), 2118-2127. DOI: [10.1137/S0363012994272630](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0363012994272630). [Author-hosted PDF](https://www.mit.edu/~jnt/Papers/J067-97-vb-contr.pdf), [local copy](papers/blondel-tsitsiklis-1997.pdf). **Full primary text verified**, especially pp. 2119-2120. It proves hardness for bounded gains and simultaneous/decentralized variants, and explicitly does not settle unrestricted SOF complexity. It also explicitly describes real quantifier-elimination decision algorithms. Do not retroactively attribute unrestricted hardness to this paper.

5. **Saugata Basu (2014).** "Algorithms in Real Algebraic Geometry: A Survey." [arXiv:1409.1534v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.1534v1), [local PDF](papers/basu-survey-2014-v1.pdf). **Primary survey fetched.** Definition 1.1 distinguishes arithmetic and bit complexity; Theorem 2.4 gives CAD complexity (sd)^(2^O(k)); Section 2.2 and Theorem 2.16 discuss stronger singly exponential algorithms for existential problems. This supports exact termination and a conservative complexity ceiling, not practicality. No full CAD implementation is supplied here; Z3 nonlinear real arithmetic is the exercised exact backend.

## Post-book developments and scope exclusions

6. **N. Otsuka, G. Conte, A. M. Perdon (2015).** "Disturbance decoupling via static-output feedback for uncertain switched linear systems." *Far East Journal of Dynamical Systems* 26(2), 99-124. DOI: [10.17654/FJDSJun2015_099_124](https://doi.org/10.17654/FJDSJun2015_099_124). [Publisher full-text page](https://pphmjopenaccess.com/fejds/article/view/5594), [local PDF](papers/otsuka-conte-perdon-2015.pdf). **Primary paper verified**, especially Lemma 2.7, Theorem 3.2 and Corollary 3.3. It has y=C_sigma x, z=D_sigma x, no book-style direct-feedthrough terms, and mode-dependent static gains. Theorem 3.2 gives sufficient conditions with fixed B or fixed C across uncertainty vertices; Corollary 3.3 gives necessity and sufficiency when both are fixed. N=N1=1 recovers the classical zero-feedthrough LTI subspace formulation. It is not a complete efficient answer for the book's arbitrary-feedthrough, internally stable problem.

7. **Thomas Berger (2017).** "Disturbance decoupling by behavioral feedback for linear differential-algebraic systems." *Automatica* 80, 272-283. DOI: [10.1016/j.automatica.2017.01.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.automatica.2017.01.012). [Author-hosted preprint, October 5, 2016](https://math.uni-paderborn.de/fileadmin-eim/mathematik/ag-systemtheorie/Berg-161005.pdf), [local PDF](papers/berger-2017-preprint.pdf). **Primary preprint verified**, especially Introduction, Definitions 1-2 and behavioral control law K1 x+K2 u=0. It gives geometric characterizations and resolves an output-uniqueness problem for possibly nonregular DAEs. This feedback need not be solvable for u and can constrain full state; it is not the same controller class as u=K(C1x+D1w). It therefore cannot be imported as a solution to the book problem.

8. **Luca Claude Gino Lebon, Johan Lindberg, Claudio Altafini (2026).** "Minimal Input Cardinality Disturbance Decoupling of Coupled Oscillators via Output Feedback with Application to Power Networks." [arXiv:2604.14977v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14977v2), version dated August 17. [Local PDF](papers/lebon-lindberg-altafini-2026-v2.pdf). **Primary abstract verified.** It concerns a specified class of coupled oscillator networks linearized around stable phase-locked synchronization, minimal actuator placement, and a power-grid application. No full theorem audit is claimed; this class-restricted result is not evidence that arbitrary plants have a new general synthesis method.

9. **Ruirui Ma and Sarah H. Q. Li (2026).** "On the Gap Between H2 Optimal Control and Disturbance Decoupling." [arXiv:2603.20438v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20438v3), v3 PDF dated June 30. **Full primary text fetched and scope checked.** Section III explicitly restricts feedback to u=Fx (state feedback), and Section V assumes V precomputed and provides local BMI/DC synthesis initialized at feasibility. This does not solve arbitrary static measurement-feedback feasibility; a small numerical H2 value is not a zero-transfer certificate. We do not rely on its optimization or convergence claims as proved facts in our own argument.

## Search audit and limits

Searches included exact static-output/static-measurement DDP titles; 2011-2026 filters; Otsuka/Conte/Perdon; Hamano-Furuta; (C,A,B)-pairs; behavioral DDP; structured/generic measurement feedback; and unrestricted SOF complexity. Initial generic web queries were noisy, so Crossref title queries were used to recover correct DOI records and publisher/author PDFs. The survey is scoped, not exhaustive. Failure to retrieve a paper or discover a newer result is not evidence of nonexistence. The defensible decisive conclusion is positive: exact real-algebraic decision for both variants was explicitly described in 2000, while the 2026 source supplies an unrestricted stable-case complexity barrier.

## Continuation: controller-graph geometry

17. **Elisa Gorla and Joachim Rosenthal (2009).** "Pole Placement with Fields of Positive Characteristic." [arXiv:0912.2932v1](https://arxiv.org/html/0912.2932v1), [local PDF](papers/gorla-rosenthal-2009-v1.pdf). **Primary Section 2 read**, especially equations **(14)-(15)**: the closed-loop characteristic polynomial is a linear combination of controller Plucker coordinates and a plant-dependent polynomial coefficient vector; the pole-placement map is a central projection restricted to a Grassmannian. This is decisive prior art for the geometric machinery, not a theorem solving arbitrary feedthrough DDP. The continuation supplies its own bordered-determinant proof so it does not depend on minimality or genericity hypotheses of a coprime-factor representation.

18. **Alexandre Eremenko and Andrei Gabrielov.** "Pole Placement by Static Output Feedback for Generic Linear Systems." [Author PDF](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~eremenko/dvi/polep72.pdf), [local copy](papers/eremenko-gabrielov-real-feedback.pdf). **Primary Sections 1-2 read**, especially **(2.2)-(2.5)** and the real-versus-complex qualification. The controller graph is one big Grassmannian cell; generic complex pole-placement existence is not a decision for every real plant, and poles of a nonminimal transfer realization need not be all state eigenvalues. The continuation retains the full state pencil rather than assuming a minimal factorization.

On 2026-10-14, additional searches combined "disturbance decoupling" with "Plucker", "Grassmann", "quadratic form", and "two inputs" with "static". They located the above geometric baseline but did not establish a prior publication of the exact arbitrary-order **m=p=2, full-feedthrough, unstabilized** quadric corollary. This is a limited **no-match search, not a novelty certificate**. The general book problem is not classified SOLVED-BY-PRIOR-WORK on this evidence; only its already-known algebraic decision component is. The continuation proof and exact scope are in [work/continuation-structural.md](../work/continuation-structural.md).

19. **Nicos Karcanias and John Leventides (2016; online 2015).** "Solution of the determinantal assignment problem using the Grassmann matrices." *International Journal of Control* 89(2), 352-367. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/00207179.2015.1077525), [accepted manuscript repository](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/12469/), [local full manuscript](papers/karcanias-leventides-2016-accepted.pdf). **Primary Sections 1-3 and 7 read in the continuation**, following the independent referee's lead. Equations **(2)-(4)** explicitly use Binet-Cauchy to split constant determinant assignment into plant-derived linear coefficient equations and controller decomposability; the latter is described by quadratic Plucker relations and real Grassmannian intersections. Section 7, **(26)-(29)**, formulates a nonlinear optimization and a power-method-like iteration with numerical stopping criteria. This establishes the classical status of the representation; it does not provide a universal exact infeasibility certificate from unsuccessful local iteration. The continuation's general theorem is a direct instance of this framework, and its m=p=2 one-quadric synthesis is an elementary corollary, **likely routine rather than a novel general method**.

Retrieval was not inferred from a search summary: Crossref resolved the DOI; the publisher returned HTTP 403; institutional search returned HTTP 429; OpenAlex exposed the accepted-manuscript PDF, which was fetched and read. The [A-DAP author project report](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/329084/reporting) was also read and corroborates the linear-variety/Grassmannian-intersection framing, but the mathematical comparison above relies on the actual paper. This later positive source match supersedes any implication that not finding a literal DDP title was evidence of new geometric machinery.
