## Load-bearing literature

1. **Restivo, Salemi, Sportelli (1989), Completing codes.** [Original Numdam PDF](https://www.numdam.org/item/ITA_1989__23_2_135_0.pdf). Retained as `restivo-salemi-sportelli1989.pdf` and `.txt`. The scanned p.145 was rendered to `rss1989-page145.png` and its formula/proof checked visually. The lemma and Theorem 4 on pp.145–146 establish the necessary completion-to-factorization bridge; the following corollary does not claim a converse. The paper identifies the exact Shor pair and leaves its embedding unresolved. Its Conjecture 2 must not be treated as a theorem, particularly in light of Sands 2007 below.
2. **Cordero (2019), A Note with Computer Exploration on the Triangle Conjecture.** [Author PDF](https://www.normalesup.org/~cordero/Publications/A_Note_with_Computer_Exploration_on_the_Triangle_Conjecture.pdf); [HAL hal-03682699](https://hal.science/hal-03682699), DOI [10.1007/978-3-030-13435-8_30](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-13435-8_30). Retained as `cordero2019.pdf` and `.txt`. The author-hosted full text was recovered after HAL's nominal PDF URL returned HTML. The exact Shor code is (2), also `Y1`. Theorem 1 is the necessary factorization bridge; Theorem 2 is the multiplier theorem; §2.2 table gives `n=330*k`, `k>=4`. Therefore `n>=1320` is explicitly prior art. The `3R` collision in the preceding paragraph has a literal typo, `8+3*0=0+3*1`; replacing the first 8 by 3 gives the actual valid collision. `../proof.md` does not silently repeat the error.
3. **Cordero (2019), About the Triangle Conjecture, GdR IM poster.** [Requested source](http://www.normalesup.org/~cordero/Presentations/2019/GdR_IM.pdf). Retained from HTTPS as `cordero2019-poster.pdf` and `.txt`. It explicitly says the exact code's inclusion in a finite maximal code is not known and gives the 330-divisibility argument. The full paper is preferable for precise hypotheses and the already-known lower bound 1320.
4. **De Felice (2022), Finite maximal codes and factorizations of cyclic groups.** [arXiv 2202.09675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09675), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.09675), retained PDF/text `defelice2022.*`. The introduction distinguishes factorization, triangle, and inclusion conjectures. Proposition 2.1 bounds the size of a bayonet subcode by the pure-letter order. Proposition 7.5 and Corollary 7.1 cover at most two prime factors, not arbitrary multiples of 660.
5. **Cordero (2023 preprint), Factorizations of Cyclic Groups and Bayonet Codes.** [arXiv 2301.13566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13566), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.13566), [author-hosted version](https://www.normalesup.org/~cordero/Publications/Factorizations_and_Bayonet_Codes.pdf). Retained arXiv PDF/text `cordero2023.*`. Definition 1.5 explains that `n`-cbc completeness is a bayonet cardinality condition, not the book's finite-code completeness. The paragraph after it explicitly says the general realization in finite maximal codes is unknown. Corollary 6.3 is restricted to cbc Hajós orders. Theorem 6.5 covers products of at most three primes and orders `p^k q`; Theorem 7.2 proves multiplier/compatibility constraints, not Shor noncompletion. The author's [publication page](https://www.normalesup.org/~cordero/) lists this work in *Journal of Automata, Languages and Combinatorics* as 2023. Do not substitute an aggregator's publication year for the retained preprint date.
6. **Sands (2000), Replacement of factors by subgroups in the factorization of abelian groups.** DOI [10.1112/S0024609300007189](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0024609300007189). Original publisher PDF returned HTTP 403. Cordero 2019 states the precise multiplier result used here. `../proof.md` supplies a group-ring derivation, so the obstruction is not resting on an inaccessible proof. A [2005 corrigendum](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0024609304003686) reports an error in the proofs of Theorems 6 and 8 (a coset was incorrectly called a subgroup); its abstract was read, but its full PDF was not obtained.
7. **Sands (2007), A question concerning the factorization of cyclic groups.** DOI [10.1142/S0218196707004293](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218196707004293). The DOI/Crossref primary abstract was read: a Restivo–Salemi–Sportelli conjecture asserting impossibility of certain cyclic factorizations is false. Publisher full text and publisher abstract endpoint returned HTTP 403. The precise counterexample construction was not obtained, so no claim is made that it embeds this exact Shor pair. Cordero 2023 cites it as a possible source of non-Hajós/non-Rédei constructions.

## Novelty boundary

The 330 divisor and lower bound 1320 are reproduction. The doubled-left collision proves an additional factor of two in the necessary divisor using the same existing method, but its novelty has not been established. There is no full-resolution claim to prioritize. The unresolved all-order gap is stated in `../proof.md` and must not be replaced by finite-order UNSAT results or a conjectural bridge.
