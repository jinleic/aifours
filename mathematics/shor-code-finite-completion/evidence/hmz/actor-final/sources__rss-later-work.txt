Conjecture 2 concerns (T,R)=({0,p,k},{0,1}), p prime, p not dividing k.
The authors note that Shor's pair contains ({0,3,8},{0,1}), and conjecture
noncompletion on this basis. The exact pair is not decided in that paper.

2. Szabo (2006), Completing codes and the Redei property of groups,
Theoretical Computer Science 359, pp.449-454.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2006.02.002
Author-uploaded primary PDF:
https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Sandor-Szabo-6/publication/220149955_Completing_codes_and_the_Redei_property_of_groups/links/61ca1e78da5d105e55ff8a32/Completing-codes-and-the-Redei-property-of-groups.pdf

Page 450 restates RSS Conjecture 2 as Conjecture 1 with factors reversed.
Page 453, Theorem 7, gives the following implication (paraphrase):

  C' a finite completion of Shor's code; a^n in C'
    => exists distinct primes p1,p2,p3: 330*p1*p2*p3 divides n.

Verbatim parsed hypothesis and conclusion fragments, the explicitly
labelled readable paraphrase, URL, retrieval time and page labels are
retained in szabo-2006-theorem7-excerpt.txt. The parser's control-character
artifacts are documented there; no visually audited local PDF is claimed.

The proof uses the associated pair
  R={0,3,8,11}, S={0,1,7,13,14},
the 330 divisor of the right-factor cardinality, and Theorem 3:
in a normalized full-rank cyclic factorization, a factor cardinality
cannot have at most two distinct prime divisors. The resulting lower bound
is 9900. This is an earlier primary publication of the condition indexed
as Szabo-Sands (2009), Theorem 10.5, not an all-order noncompletion result.
The review correction in proof.md Section 3 combines this theorem with
8 dividing n to give the partial corollary n >= 19800. Its novelty is
unaudited: the full 2009 book Section 10 pages and Cordero's thesis were
not read. No additional divisor, lower-bound or finite-order search was
performed.

3. Sands (2007), A question concerning the factorization of cyclic groups,
International Journal of Algebra and Computation 17(8), pp.1573-1575.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218196707004293
Publisher-provided first-page preview:
https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220169752_A_QUESTION_CONCERNING_THE_FACTORIZATION_OF_CYCLIC_GROUPS

The preview identifies the conjecture as that of Restivo, Salemi and
Sportelli. Its exact final abstract sentence is:
  "In this note it is shown that in fact such factorizations do exist."

Thus a general RSS no-embedding conjecture is refuted according to this
published primary abstract. The construction on pages 1574-1575 was not
retrieved or independently checked. This evidence does not identify which
particular p,k are realized and does not establish an embedding of Shor's
full pair.

Access attempts: publisher DOI/abs, DOI/pdf and DOI/epdf endpoints did not
