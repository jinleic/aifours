                                                                                     Clelia De Felice ∗




arXiv:2202.09675v1 [cs.FL] 19 Feb 2022
                                                                                          Abstract
                                                     Variable-length codes are the bases of the free submonoids of a free monoid. There are
                                                 some important longstanding open questions about the structure of finite maximal codes,
                                                 namely the factorization conjecture and the triangle conjecture, proposed by Perrin and
                                                 Schützemberger. The latter concerns finite codes Y which are subsets of a∗ Ba∗ , where a
                                                 is a letter and B is an alphabet not containing a. A structural property of finite maximal
                                                 codes has recently been shown by Zhang and Shum. It exhibits a relationship between finite
                                                 maximal codes and factorizations of cyclic groups. With the aim of highlighting the links
                                                 between this result and other older ones on maximal and factorizing codes, we give a simpler
                                                 and a new proof of this result. As a consequence, we prove that for any finite maximal code
                                                 X ⊆ (B ∪ {a})∗ containing the word apq , where p, q are prime numbers, X ∩ a∗ Ba∗ satisfies
                                                 the triangle conjecture. Let n be a positive integer that is a product of at most two prime
                                                 numbers. We also prove that it is decidable whether a finite code Y ∪ an ⊆ a∗ Ba∗ ∪ a∗ is
                                                 included in a finite maximal code and that, if this holds, Y ∪ an is included in a code that
                                                 also satisfies the factorization conjecture.

                                             Keywords: Formal languages, variable-length codes, finite maximal codes, factorizations of
                                         cyclic groups.

                                         1       Introduction
                                         The theory of variable-length codes takes its origin in the framework of the theory of information,
                                         since Shannon’s early works in the 1950’s. An algebraic theory of codes was subsequently initi-
                                         ated by Schützenberger, who proposed in [37] the semigroup theory as a mathematical setting
                                         for the study of these objects. In this context the theory of codes has been extensively devel-
                                         oped, showing strong relations with automata theory, combinatorics on words, formal languages
                                         and the theory of semigroups (see [1] for a complete treatment of this topic). In this paper we
                                         follow this algebraic approach and codes are defined as the bases of the free submonoids of a
                                         free monoid.
                                              We are interested in some important longstanding open questions about the structure of
                                         finite maximal codes (maximal objects in the class of codes for the order of set inclusion). One
                                         of these conjectures asks whether any finite maximal code X is (positively) factorizing [36], that
                                         is if there always exist finite subsets P , S of A∗ such that

                                                                                  X − 1 = P (A − 1)S                                             (1.1)

                                         (here 1 is the empty word and X denotes the characteristic polynomial of a finite language X,
                                         i.e., the formal sum of its elements).

    The above conjecture was formulated by Schützenberger but, as far as we know, it does not
appear explicitly in any of his papers. It was quoted as the factorization conjecture in [27] for
the first time and then also reported in [1]. The major contribution to this conjecture is due to
Reutenauer [33, 34]. In particular, he proved that for any finite maximal code X over A, there
exist polynomials P, S ∈ ZhAi such that Eq. (1.1) holds, that is, X − 1 = P (A − 1)S. Other
partial results concerning this conjecture may be found in [2, 3, 4, 11, 18, 20, 32, 44]. Some of
them show a relationship between factorizations of finite cyclic groups and factorizing codes. We
recall that a pair (R, T ) of subsets of N is a factorization of Z/nZ if for each z ∈ {0, . . . , n − 1}
there exists a unique pair (r, t), with r ∈ R and t ∈ T , such that r + t = z (mod n). The
simplest example is provided by the Krasner factorizations, where for each z one has r + t = z
[24].
    The factorization conjecture is still open and weaker forms of it have been proposed and
reported below.
    Two words x, y are commutatively equivalent if the symbols of y can be reordered to make x.
Two sets X, Y are commutatively equivalent if there is a bijection φ from X onto Y such that
for every x ∈ X, x and φ(x) are commutative equivalent. A well known class of codes is that of
prefix codes, i.e., codes such that none of their words is a left factor of another. A code X ⊆ A∗
is commutatively prefix if there exists a prefix code Y ⊆ A∗ which is commutatively equivalent
to X.
    It is conjectured that every finite maximal code is commutatively prefix. This is the commu-
tative equivalence conjecture, due Schützenberger [39] and inspired by a problem of information
theory [29]. Any factorizing code is commutatively prefix. Partial results on the commutative
equivalence conjecture have been proved in [29, 26].
    A third conjecture takes into account bayonet codes, i.e., codes such that each of their words
has the form ai baj , a ∈ A, b ∈ A \ {a}. It is conjectured that for any finite bayonet code X
which can be embedded in a finite maximal code, one has

                                     Card(X) ≤ max{|x| | x ∈ X}                                        (1.2)

This is the triangle conjecture, due to Perrin and Schützemberger [30]. If X is a finite maximal
code and X is commutatively prefix, then X ∩ a∗ ba∗ verifies the triangle conjecture, for any
a, b ∈ A. Partial results on the triangle conjecture have been proved in [9, 23, 31, 45].
     Originally the three conjectures were proposed for codes with no additional hypothesis. In
1985 Shor found a bayonet code X such that Card(X) > max{|x| | x ∈ X} [40]. Other
counterexamples may be found in [7]. Thus the conjectures were restricted as above to the
smaller class of finite maximal codes and its subsets.
     Notice that there are finite codes which are not contained in any finite maximal code [32].
The inclusion problem, for a finite code X, is the existence of a finite maximal code containing
X. The inclusion conjecture claims that the inclusion problem is decidable.
     The aim of this paper is on the one hand to highlight the links between a recent result on finite
maximal codes and other less recent ones on maximal and factorizing codes, on the other hand to
deduce from these connections new results on the aforementioned conjectures. More specifically,
the starting point of our research is a structural property of the maximal finite codes which has
recently been established by Zhang and Shum in [45, 46]. Let X be a finite maximal code over A
such that an ∈ X, where a ∈ A. Set B = A \ {a}. Zhang and Shum considered sets Xw of words
of the form ai waj , where w ∈ B(a∗ B)∗ , and ai waj ∈ X ∗ \ [an (a∗ wa∗ ∩ X ∗ ) ∪ (X ∗ ∩ a∗ wa∗ )an ].
They proved that the words in Xw can be arranged in a matrix (arp,q wavp,q )1≤p≤m,1≤q≤ℓ such
that for each Rp = {rp,q | q ∈ {1, . . . , ℓ}} and each Tq = {vp,q | p ∈ {1, . . . , m}}, the pair (Rp , Tq )
is a factorization of the finite cyclic group of order n (Theorem 3.1). Our first result is a new

simpler proof of the property established by Zhang and Shum (Theorem 4.1). It is based on
the so-called weak form of the factorization conjecture proved by Reutenauer in [34] and then
by Zhang and Gu in [43] (Theorem 3.2). Both in the proof of Zhang and Shum and in the
one presented in this article, a crucial role is played by the existence of a special “universal”
factorization of a cyclic group associated with all Xw , called here a companion factorization of

Krasner factorization is equivalent to a special arrangement of the words of Xw , named a good
arrangement in [15] where it was introduced for a factorizing code X and w ∈ B = A \ {a}
(Proposition 7.2).
    In the same article [45], Zhang and Shum have proposed a stronger version of the triangle
conjecture, that is, they ask whether Eq. (1.2) holds for all Xw when we change w to b. As a
corollary of their result, they obtained that if X has a Krasner pair as a companion factorization,
then X satisfies this stronger version of the conjecture. Again, we give another proof of this
result, based on the properties of a good arrangement (Proposition 7.8).
    It is known that if X has a Krasner pair as a companion factorization, then the factorizations
(Rp , Tq ) are of a special type, named Hajós factorizations (Section 6). Not all factorizations of
Z/nZ are of this type but they certainly are for some values of n which have been exactly
identified [41]. The simplest case is n = p or n = pq, with p and q prime numbers. We
wondered if the condition that all (Rp , Tq ) are Hajós factorizations guarantees that the triangle
conjecture (“basic” or stronger version) is true for Xw . This is an open problem and a main
issue arising from this research. However, Zhang and Shum proved that the answer is positive
if n = p and we have proved that the same is true for n = pq. That is, if X is a finite maximal
code and apq ∈ X with p, q prime numbers, then X ∩ a∗ Ba∗ satisfies the triangle conjecture
(Corollary 7.1). Actually, for these codes we have proved more. Namely, let n be a positive
integer that is a product of at most two prime numbers. In Proposition 7.5, we prove that a
finite code Y ∪ an ⊆ a∗ Ba∗ ∪ a∗ is included in a factorizing code if and only if it is included in
a finite maximal code and both conditions are equivalent to a decidable property for Y ∪ an .
Consequently we state that it is decidable whether Y ∪ an is included in a finite maximal code.
    This paper is organized as follows. Basics on words, codes and related notions are mainly
collected in Section 2. Other more specific definitions and known results are presented just
before their use in Sections 3, 5 and 6. Sections 4 and 7 are devoted to the proofs of our results.
Precisely, in Section 4 we present the new proof of the previously mentioned result of Zhang and
Shum. Then, we prove our new results in Section 7. Future research directions are discussed
in Section 8. Finally, we have gathered in an appendix (Section 9) additional information on
known results and definitions that are quickly and informally mentioned in the article, for the
convenience of the reader who wants to give a deeper look to the problem.

2.4    Composition
Composition is a partially binary operation on codes. We recall below the definition of this
operation and of the converse notion of decomposition.
    Let Z ⊆ A∗ , Y ⊆ B ∗ be two codes such that B = alph(Y ) (i.e., each letter b ∈ B is a factor
of at least one word in Y ). Then the codes Y and Z are composable if there is a bijection from B
onto Z. If β is such a bijection, then Y and Z are called composable through β. Then β defines
a morphism from B ∗ into A∗ which is injective since Z is a code. The set

                                            X = β(Y ) ⊆ Z ∗

is a code over A. We denote it by
                                               X =Y ◦Z
and we say that X is obtained by composition of Y and Z. The words in X are obtained just by
replacing, in the words of Y , each letter b by the word β(b) ∈ Z. The following result, proved
in [1, Proposition 2.2.6], underlines a second aspect of the composition operation, namely the
decomposition of a code into simpler ones.

Proposition 2.3 Let X, Z ⊆ A∗ be codes. There exists a code Y such that X = Y ◦ Z if and
only if
                            X ⊆ Z ∗ and alphZ (X) = Z
where the second condition above means that all words in Z appear in at least one factorization
of a word in X as product of words in Z.

2.6   Factorizations of cyclic groups
The notion of factorization of a finite abelian group was introduced for the first time by Hajós
when he solved one of Minkowski’s conjectures by giving it a group-theoretical formulation
[22, 42]. Let G be a finite abelian group where the composition law is written additively. Given
two subsets R, T of G, we write R + T = {r + t | r ∈ R, t ∈ T }. The sum R + T is direct if for any
element g in G, there exists at most one pair (r, t) with r ∈ R and t ∈ T such that g = r + t. A
sequence S1 , . . . , Sk of subsets of G is a factorization of G (or G is the direct sum of its subsets
Si ) if each element of G may be written uniquely as a sum with just one term from each Si .
Then Si is called a factor of G.
     We shall be interested in factorizations of cyclic groups. As usual, we realize the cyclic group
of order n as the factor group Z/nZ of the integers modulo n. For the relation with codes, we
will always consider positive representatives of its classes. Moreover, we will focus our attention
on the factorizations of Z/nZ by two factors, as in the following definition.

Definition 2.1 A pair (R, T ) of subsets of N is a factorization of Z/nZ if for any z ∈ {0, . . . , n−
1} there exists a unique pair (r, t), with r ∈ R and t ∈ T , such that r + t = z (mod n).
    Here, the classical hypotheses R, T ⊆ {0, . . . , n − 1} and 0 ∈ R ∩ T are not implicitly
assumed to hold, as we consider factorizations of Z/nZ in relation to codes. Note that if (R, T )
is a factorization of Z/nZ, then Card(R+T ) = Card(R) Card(T ) = n. The following proposition
is more or less classically known [12].

Proposition 2.4 A pair (R, T ) of subsets of N is a factorization of Z/nZ if and only if there
exists a finite subset H of N such that
                                 an − 1                     an − 1
                     aR aT = (          )(1 + aH (a − 1)) =        + aH (an − 1)
                                  a−1                       a−1
    The general structure of the factorizations (R, T ) of Z/nZ is still unknown but two simple
families of these pairs can be recursively constructed: Krasner factorizations and Hajós factor-
izations. We recall that a Krasner factorization (of order n) is a pair (I, J) of subsets of N such
that for any z ∈ {0, . . . , n − 1} there exists a unique (i, j), with i ∈ I and j ∈ J, such that
i + j = z. The pairs (I, J) as above have been completely described in [24]. Hajós factorizations
will be taken into account in Section 6.

2.7   The factorization conjecture
Given a finite maximal code X, a factorization (P, S) for X is a pair of polynomials P, S ∈ ZhAi
such that X = P (A − 1)S + 1. Theorem 2.3 is from [34]. It shows that any finite maximal code
has a factorization.

Theorem 2.3 Let X ∈ NhAi, with (X, 1) = 0, and let P, S ∈ ZhAi be such that X = P (A −
1)S + 1. Then, X is the characteristic polynomial of a finite maximal code. Furthermore, if
P, S ∈ NhAi, then P, S are polynomials with coefficients 0, 1. Conversely, for any finite maximal
code X there exist P, S ∈ ZhAi such that X = P (A − 1)S + 1.
   Let X be a finite maximal code. A pair (P, S) of subsets of A∗ is a positive factorization for
X if (P , S) is a factorization for X. Then X is called a (positively) factorizing code.
   Conjecture 2.1 is among the most difficult, unsolved problems in the theory of codes. This
conjecture was formulated by Schützenberger but, as far as we know, it does not appear explicitly


                                                   6
in any of his papers. It was quoted as the factorization conjecture in [27] for the first time and
then also reported in [1].

Conjecture 2.1 [36] Any finite maximal code is a positively factorizing code.
    Finite maximal prefix codes are the simplest examples of positively factorizing codes. Indeed,
X is a finite maximal prefix code if and only if X = P (A − 1) + 1 for a finite subset P of A∗ [1].
In the previous relation, P is the set of the proper prefixes of the words in X.
    As already said in Section 1, the factorization conjecture is still open and weaker forms of it
have been proposed. In particular, the triangle conjecture will be recalled in Section 5 and new
results on it will be presented in Section 7.

6     Hajós factorizations and good arrangements
In this section we recall the definitions of Hajós factorizations and of special arrangements of
words, named good arrangements, with the main related results. This material will be used in
Section 7. We will also provide relationships between results proved for factorizing codes and
results demonstrated in Section 4.

6.1    Hajós factorizations
Let G be a finite abelian group. We recall that a subset S of G is periodic if there exists g
in G \ 0 such that g + S = S. A factorization (T, R) of G is periodic if at least one factor
is periodic. When we consider the particular case of the cyclic groups, one has the following
obvious characterization of the periodic factorizations, which we need in the sequel [21].

Lemma 6.1 A pair (T, R) is a periodic factorization of Z/nZ if and only if there exists a
divisor p of n, p 6= n, and a factorization (T, S) of Z/pZ such that R is the direct sum of S and
{0, 1, . . . , (n/p − 1)}p.
    Let (T, R) be a periodic factorization of Z/nZ. A divisor p of n, p 6= n, is a period of R
if R is the direct sum of S and {0, 1, . . . , (n/p − 1)}p, where (T, S) is a factorization of Z/pZ.
Hajós conjectured that any factorization of a finite abelian group was periodic. This conjecture
was false. Thereafter, cyclic groups were classed in good groups and bad groups. A group is
good (or it has the Hajós property) if any of its factorization is periodic, otherwise it is bad.
The integer n is said to be a Hajós number if the group Z/nZ has the Hajós property [1]. In
a sequence of different papers, good cyclic groups were characterized [41]. They are Z/pn qZ,
Z/p2 q 2 Z, Z/p2 qrZ, Z/pqrsZ and their subgroups, where p, q, r, s are different primes.
    In [22], Hajós gave a method, slightly corrected later by Sands in [8], for the construction
of a class of periodic factorizations which contains all factorizations of a good group. As done
in [12], we report this method for the cyclic group Z/nZ of order n (Definition 6.1). The
corresponding factorizations will be named Hajós factorizations. The operation ◦ intervenes:
for subsets S = {s1 , . . . , sq }, T of Z/nZ, S ◦ T denotes the family of subsets of Z/nZ having
the form {si + ti | i ∈ {1, . . . , q}}, where {t1 , . . . , tq } is any multiset of elements of T having the
same cardinality as S.

7     New results
Let X be a finite maximal code containing an . We begin this section by showing that the case
where a companion factorization of X is a Krasner factorization is equivalent to the existence

arrangement for Y is a decidable property.

8    Future Perspectives
In this section we summarize the main issues arising from this research. Obviously the aim is
the solution of the three conjectures presented in Section 1. A preliminary step in this direction
could be to extend some results of this paper.
    First, in Proposition 7.3, we state that X always has a companion factorization which is a
Krasner factorization of Z/nZ when the order n of a is a product of at most two prime numbers.
This result guarantees that the triangle conjecture (“basic” or stronger version) is true for
Xw . We wondered if Proposition 7.3 can be extended to the case where X has a companion
factorization which is a Hajós factorization of Z/nZ, or, at least, when n is a Hajós number.
    Next, once again if n is a prime number or a product of two (not necessarily distinct) prime
numbers, by using Corollary 7.1 in [17], we state that a finite code Y ∪ an ⊆ a∗ Ba∗ ∪ a∗ is
included in a factorizing code if and only if it is included in a finite maximal code. The proof
of Corollary 7.1 in [17] provides a construction of this factorizing code, if it exists. One might
ask if this construction could be extended to provide all the finite maximal codes in this small
family, namely that of finite maximal codes such that the order of a is a product of at most two
prime numbers.
    Then, all partial results on the triangle conjecture require that the factorizations of cyclic
groups associated with Xw are Hajós factorizations. Is there a finite maximal code for which
this does not happen? If there were, it would be a counterexample to the factorizing conjecture.
    Finally, in Section 4 we have given a proof of Theorem 3.1 different from that in [45]. In
both proofs there are pairs of sets of integers defined starting from strongly right completable
words (with respect to a finite maximal code). It would be interesting to investigate whether
there is a relationship between the pairs of sets (P, Q) defined in [45] and those (T, R) defined
here.
