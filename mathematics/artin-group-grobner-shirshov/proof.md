## 0. Formulation, alphabet and monomial order

**Book problem (3).** Give Gröbner–Shirshov (GS) bases of the finite-type Artin groups in the
Artin–Garside generators $\sigma_i,\Delta,\Delta^{-1}$ (book p. 33; type $A$ was done by Bokut,
arXiv:0806.1125, J. Symbolic Comput. 43 (2008) 397–405).

**Setting.** $(W,S)$ is a finite Coxeter system, $S=\{s_1,\dots,s_n\}$, $m_{st}$ the Coxeter
matrix, $N=\ell(w_0)$ the number of positive roots. $A=A(W)$ is the Artin group and $A^+$ the
Artin monoid, generated by (lifts of) $S$ with relations $\Pi_{m_{st}}(s,t)=\Pi_{m_{st}}(t,s)$,
where $\Pi_k(s,t)=sts\cdots$ ($k$ letters). $\Delta\in A^+$ is the lift of $w_0$ and
$\varphi(s)=w_0sw_0$. The alphabet is the book's exact Artin–Garside alphabet
$$X=S\cup\{\Delta,\Delta^{-1}\},$$
and $A$ is presented as a *semigroup* on $X$ by the braid relations, $\Delta=w_\Delta$ (any
positive word for $\Delta$), $\Delta\Delta^{-1}=1=\Delta^{-1}\Delta$ (every $s$ is then
invertible: $s\cdot(s^{-1}\Delta)\Delta^{-1}=1$ with $s^{-1}\Delta\in A^+$).

**Monomial order (a choice, disclosed).** The book does not fix a monomial order. Throughout we
use the deg-lex order on $X^*$ induced by a letter order
$$\Delta^{-1}<\Delta<s_1<\dots<s_n,$$
where the order of $S$ is arbitrary (it is part of the data; results depend on it). This is
exactly Bokut's convention for type $A$. Nothing below is claimed for other monomial orders.

**Not this problem.** Convergent (quadratic) rewriting systems for Garside/Artin monoids on the
alphabet of *all simple elements* (Garside generators) are a different formulation on a different
alphabet (see README, State of the art). Here the alphabet has $n+2$ letters.

Letters in examples and code: generator $k$ of the chosen order is `a`,`b`,`c`,…; `1`$=\Delta$,
`0`$=\Delta^{-1}$.

## 1. Background facts used (standard, not reproved)

* (G1) $A^+$ is cancellative and embeds in $A$ (Brieskorn–Saito 1972; Deligne 1972).
* (G2) Any two elements of $A^+$ have a left lcm $a\vee b$ (least common right multiple w.r.t.
  left divisibility $\preceq$); for atoms $s\ne t$, $s\vee t=\Pi_{m_{st}}(s,t)=\Pi_{m_{st}}(t,s)$
  (Brieskorn–Saito).
* (G3) The simple elements (left divisors of $\Delta$) are the positive lifts of the elements of
  $W$; for simples, $\preceq$ is the right weak order of $W$; $s\preceq g$ for $g$ simple iff
  $s$ is a left descent of $g$.
* (G4) $s\Delta=\Delta\varphi(s)$ for $s\in S$; $\varphi$ is an involutive diagram automorphism.
* (G5) Every $g\in A$ is uniquely $g=\Delta^k p$ with $k\in\mathbb Z$, $p\in A^+$, $\Delta\not\preceq p$
  (Garside 1969; Brieskorn–Saito).
* (G6) $M(t):=\sum_{p\in A^+}t^{\ell(p)}=1/D(t)$, $D(t)=\sum_{T\subseteq S}(-1)^{|T|}t^{N_T}$, $N_T=\ell(w_0(W_T))$
  (Deligne/Saito). Short proof: for $p\ne1$, $\sum_{T\subseteq L(p)}(-1)^{|T|}=0$ where
  $L(p)=\{s:s\preceq p\}$; $T\subseteq L(p)\iff\Delta_T\preceq p$ by (G2); summing
  $t^{\ell(p)}$ and writing $p=\Delta_Tq$ (G1) gives $D(t)M(t)=1$.

For $a,b\in A^+$ write $a\backslash b:=a^{-1}(a\vee b)$ (right complement). Then
$$b\preceq ah\iff a\backslash b\preceq h,\qquad (gh)\backslash c=h\backslash(g\backslash c),\qquad c\preceq c'\Rightarrow a\backslash c\preceq a\backslash c'.\tag{1.1}$$
For atoms $x\ne t$: $x\backslash t=\Pi_{m_{tx}-1}(t,x)$ (alternating word of length
$m_{tx}-1$ beginning with $t$). If $c$ is simple then $y\backslash c\preceq y^{-1}\Delta$ is simple.

## 2. GS bases of semigroup presentations: the criterion we use

Let a semigroup $G$ be presented on a finite alphabet $X$, $<$ a monomial well order, and
$\mathrm{NF}\subset X^*$ the set of $<$-least representatives. Let $S$ be a set of pairs $(u,v)$
with $u=v$ in $G$ and $v<u$ (leading word $\bar u=u$).

**Lemma 1.** (a) $\mathrm{NF}\subseteq\mathrm{Irr}(S)$. (b) The following are equivalent:
(i) $S$ is a GS basis (all compositions trivial) and $\langle X\mid S\rangle\to G$ is an isomorphism;
(ii) $\mathrm{Irr}(S)=\mathrm{NF}$; (iii) every word $u\notin\mathrm{NF}$ all of whose proper factors
lie in NF is a leading word of $S$; (iv) $|\mathrm{Irr}(S)\cap X^L|=|\mathrm{NF}\cap X^L|$ for all $L$.
(c) Consequently the *reduced* GS basis is $\{u\to\mathrm{NF}(u): u\in\mathrm{MinRed}\}$,
$\mathrm{MinRed}$ the set of words $u\notin\mathrm{NF}$ whose proper factors are all in NF, and
**every** GS basis of $G$ on $X$ for $<$ has at least $|\mathrm{MinRed}|$ elements.

*Proof.* (a) If $w\in\mathrm{NF}$ contained a leading word $u$, replacing it by $v<u$ gives a
smaller word for the same element. (ii)⇒(i): $\mathrm{Irr}(S)$ spans $k\langle X|S\rangle$ (every word
reduces to an irreducible one), and its image in $k[G]$ is a set of distinct group elements,
hence linearly independent; by the Composition–Diamond lemma (Shirshov 1962; Bokut 1976;
Bergman 1978) $S$ is a GS basis, and two words equal in $G$ reduce to the same irreducible word,
so the congruences of $S$ and of $G$ coincide. (i)⇒(ii): by the CD lemma $\mathrm{Irr}(S)$ is a basis
of $k\langle X|S\rangle=k[G]$, so distinct irreducible words are distinct elements; with (a) and the
fact that each element has a least word, $\mathrm{Irr}(S)=\mathrm{NF}$. (ii)⇔(iii): NF is closed
under factors; a word outside NF contains a minimal non-normal factor, which by (iii) is a leading
word; conversely if $u\in\mathrm{MinRed}$ and $\mathrm{Irr}(S)=\mathrm{NF}$ then $u$ contains a
leading word, and proper factors of $u$ are normal hence irreducible, so the leading word is $u$.
(iv)⇔(ii) by (a). (c) follows from (iii). ∎

## 3. Normal forms on the Artin–Garside alphabet

**Lemma 2.** Assume $N\ge2$. For $g=\Delta^kp$ as in (G5) the geodesic length of $g$ over $X$ is
$|k|+\ell(p)$, and the deg-lex normal form is
$$\mathrm{NF}(g)=\Delta^{(k)}\,\mathrm{lexmin}(p),\qquad \Delta^{(k)}=\Delta^k\ (k\ge0),\ (\Delta^{-1})^{|k|}\ (k<0),$$
where $\mathrm{lexmin}(p)$ is the deg-lex least positive word of $p$. Hence the growth series of
$A$ over $X$ is $G(t)=(1-t^N)M(t)\frac{1+t}{1-t}$.

*Proof.* Let $w$ represent $g$ with $e_\pm$ letters $\Delta^{\pm1}$ and positive part $P$. By
(G4) $w=\Delta^{m}P'$ in $A$ with $m=e_+-e_-$ and $P'$ positive, $|P'|=|P|$. Then
$P'=\Delta^{k-m}p$ with $P'\in A^+$; if $k<m$ then $p=\Delta^{m-k}P'$ would be divisible by
$\Delta$, so $k\ge m$ and $|P'|=(k-m)N+\ell(p)$ (homogeneity). Thus
$|w|\ge|m|+(k-m)N+\ell(p)\ge|k|+\ell(p)$, with equality only if $m=k$ (as $N\ge2$) and
$e_++e_-=|m|$. So geodesics consist of $|k|$ letters $\Delta^{\operatorname{sgn}k}$ and a positive
part of length $\ell(p)$; since $\Delta^{\pm1}$ are the smallest letters, the lex-least one puts
all of them first, followed by the least positive word of $p$. The count: elements of length $L$
are pairs $(k,p)$ with $|k|+\ell(p)=L$; $\sum_{p\ \Delta\text{-free}}t^{\ell(p)}=(1-t^N)M(t)$ by
(G5) applied in $A^+$. ∎

Call a positive word *normal* if it is lexmin and its element is not divisible by $\Delta$.

## 4. Theorem A (structure of the reduced GS basis) — PROVED

**Theorem A.** For every finite $W$ with $N\ge2$ and every order of $S$, the reduced GS basis of
$A(W)$ on $X$ (deg-lex, $\Delta^{-1}<\Delta<S$) is the disjoint union of
* $I=\{\Delta\Delta^{-1}\to1,\ \Delta^{-1}\Delta\to1\}$,
* $T=\{s\Delta\to\Delta\varphi(s),\ s\Delta^{-1}\to\Delta^{-1}\varphi(s): s\in S\}$,
* $P=\{u\to\mathrm{NF}(u): u\in S^+\text{ positive},\ u\text{ not normal},\ \text{all proper factors of }u\text{ normal}\}$.

For $u\in P$: $\mathrm{NF}(u)=\mathrm{lexmin}([u])$ if $[u]$ is $\Delta$-free, and otherwise
$\mathrm{NF}(u)=\Delta\,\mathrm{lexmin}(\Delta^{-1}[u])$ with $\Delta^{-1}[u]$ $\Delta$-free.

*Proof.* By Lemma 1(c) we must determine MinRed. By Lemma 2 the words in NF are exactly
$\Delta^{(k)}w$ with $w$ a normal positive word. Let $u\in\mathrm{MinRed}$ contain a letter
$\Delta^{\pm1}$. If $u$ has a factor $\Delta\Delta^{-1}$ or $\Delta^{-1}\Delta$ (not normal: it
represents $1$) or $s\Delta^{\pm1}$ (not normal: its normal form is $\Delta^{\pm1}\varphi(s)$ by
(G4) and Lemma 2), minimality forces $u$ to be that factor. Otherwise all $\Delta$-letters of $u$
have the same sign and precede all positive letters, i.e. $u=\Delta^{(k)}w$ with $k\ne0$ and $w$
positive; since $u\notin\mathrm{NF}$, Lemma 2 shows that $w$ is not normal, but $w$ is a proper
factor of $u$ — contradiction. Hence the non-positive part of MinRed is $I\cup T$ (each of these
words is non-normal with normal proper factors), and the positive part is $P$ by definition.
For $u=xv\in P$ ($x\in S$) the factor $v$ is normal. If $\Delta^2\preceq[u]=x[v]$ then by (1.1)
$x^{-1}\Delta^2=x\backslash\Delta^2\preceq[v]$; but $x^{-1}\Delta\cdot\Delta=\Delta\cdot\varphi(x^{-1}\Delta)$ by (G4),
so $\Delta\preceq[v]$, contradicting normality of $v$. Thus $[u]=\Delta^kp$ with $k\in\{0,1\}$ and
Lemma 2 gives the stated normal form. ∎

**Lemma 3 (parabolic closure).** For $J\subsetneq S$ and $u\in J^*$: $u$ is normal iff $u$ is the
deg-lex least word of its element in the parabolic monoid $A^+_J$. Hence
$P\cap J^*=\mathrm{MinRed}(A_J^+)$, the leading words of the reduced deg-lex GS basis of the
*monoid* $A^+_J$ with the induced order.

*Proof.* Braid relations preserve the set of letters occurring in a word, so every word of
$[u]$ lies in $J^*$; lexmin in $A^+$ and in $A^+_J$ coincide. An element of $A^+_J$ is never
divisible by $\Delta$ when $J\ne S$ (every word for $\Delta$ contains all letters of $S$). ∎

## 5. Lemma 4 and Theorem B (finite-automaton description, all finite types) — PROVED

**Lemma 4.** A positive word $v=v_1\cdots v_L$ is normal iff for every position $k$:
(L) $\Pi_{m-1}(t,v_k)\not\preceq[v_{k+1}\cdots v_L]$ for every $t<v_k$ with $m=m_{tv_k}$, and
(D) $v_k^{-1}\Delta\not\preceq[v_{k+1}\cdots v_L]$.
Consequently $u=xv\in P$ ($x\in S$) iff $v$ and $xv'$ are normal and
$\Pi_{m_{tx}-1}(t,x)\preceq[v]$ for some $t<x$, or $x^{-1}\Delta\preceq[v]$.

*Proof.* $\mathrm{lexmin}(g)=s\cdot\mathrm{lexmin}(s^{-1}g)$ with $s=\min L(g)$, so $v$ is lexmin iff
$v_k=\min L([v_k\cdots v_L])$ for every $k$, iff no $t<v_k$ divides $[v_k\cdots v_L]$; by (1.1)
$t\preceq v_kh\iff v_k\backslash t=\Pi_{m-1}(t,v_k)\preceq h$. $\Delta$-freeness of $[v]$ is
inherited by all factors (if $[w]=\Delta w'$ then $[pws]=\Delta\varphi([p])w'[s]$ by (G4)), and
$\Delta\preceq v_kh\iff v_k^{-1}\Delta\preceq h$. The second statement is the first applied to
$xv'$, $v$ and $xv$. ∎

**Theorem B.** For every finite $W$ with $N\ge2$ (i.e. not of type $A_1$, which Theorem C treats
separately) and every order of $S$, the set of leading words of the reduced GS basis is a regular
language, recognised by an explicit finite automaton built from $W$:
states are antichains (w.r.t. $\preceq$) of simple elements ("obligations"); reading $y\in S$ maps
the state $O$ to the set of minimal elements of
$\{y\backslash c: c\in O\}\cup\{\Pi_{m_{ty}-1}(t,y): t<y\}\cup\{y^{-1}\Delta\}$, and the word dies
if some $y\backslash c=1$. A positive word is normal iff its run from $\emptyset$ never dies;
$P$-leading words are $u$ with $u_{[1,L-1]}$ and $u_{[2,L]}$ normal and $u$ not normal
(pair automaton). Together with $I\cup T$ this describes the reduced GS basis for every finite type.

*Proof.* Induction on $k$ with the invariant: after reading $v_1\cdots v_k$ the state is (the minimal
elements of) $\{[v_{j+1}\cdots v_k]\backslash c\}$ over all obligations $c$ created at positions
$j\le k$, by (1.1) ($c\not\preceq[v_{j+1}\cdots v_kh]\iff[v_{j+1}\cdots v_k]\backslash c\not\preceq h$
when the complement is $\ne1$). Dropping non-minimal obligations is sound by the monotonicity in
(1.1). All obligations are simple, so there are finitely many states. Lemma 4 finishes. ∎

normal form (`work/gs_oracle.py`) up to length 15 for $A_3,B_3,H_3$ (several orders); see README.

## 6. Rank $\le 2$: the explicit finite basis — PROVED

**Theorem C.** Let $W=I_2(m)$, $m\ge2$ ($m=2$: $A_1\times A_1$; $m=3,4,5,6$: $A_2,B_2,H_2,G_2$), with
order $a<b$ (the other order is symmetric). Put $\langle ab\rangle_k=\Pi_k(a,b)$. The reduced GS
basis of $A(W)$ on $\{\Delta^{-1}<\Delta<a<b\}$ consists of the 8 relations
$$\begin{aligned}
&R_1:\ \langle ab\rangle_m\to\Delta,\quad R_2:\ \langle ba\rangle_m\to\Delta,\\
&R_3^x:\ x\Delta\to\Delta\varphi(x),\quad R_4^x:\ x\Delta^{-1}\to\Delta^{-1}\varphi(x)\quad(x\in\{a,b\}),\\
&R_5:\ \Delta\Delta^{-1}\to1,\quad R_6:\ \Delta^{-1}\Delta\to1,
\end{aligned}$$
$\varphi=\mathrm{id}$ for $m$ even, $\varphi=(a\,b)$ for $m$ odd. The normal forms are
$\Delta^{(k)}w$, $w\in\{a,b\}^*$ without alternating factor of length $m$ (= Garside normal form).
For rank 1 ($A_1$, $N=1$, $\Delta=a$) the reduced basis is $\{a\to\Delta,\Delta\Delta^{-1}\to1,\Delta^{-1}\Delta\to1\}$.
For the literal empty Coxeter system ($S=\varnothing$, $N=0$, $\Delta=1$), the reduced basis
is $\{\Delta\to\epsilon,\ \Delta^{-1}\to\epsilon\}$. Both rules decrease length and every
nonempty word reduces to $\epsilon$; all compositions are trivial. This separate boundary
case was omitted from the original “every finite Coxeter system” wording.

*Proof (termination and confluence, self-contained).* Validity: $R_1,R_2$ define $\Delta$;
$R_3$ is (G4); $R_4$ follows from $R_3$ ($x\Delta=\Delta\varphi(x)\Rightarrow\Delta^{-1}x=\varphi(x)\Delta^{-1}$,
then replace $x$ by $\varphi(x)$). Each right side is deg-lex smaller (shorter, or same length with
first letter $\Delta^{\pm1}<x$); deg-lex is a monomial well order, so rewriting terminates.
No leading word is a factor of another (positive words of length $m$ vs. length-2 words containing
$\Delta^{\pm1}$), so there are no inclusion compositions. All intersection compositions
(overlaps $w=\bar f c=d\bar g$, $|\bar f|+|\bar g|>|w|$):

1. $R_i/R_j$, $i,j\in\{1,2\}$: a proper suffix of an alternating word is a prefix of an alternating
   word iff their letters agree, so $w$ is an alternating word of length $m+j$, $1\le j\le m-1$.
   Write $w=w'\,\bar g=\bar f\,w''$ with $|w'|=|w''|=j$. Then $w\to\Delta w''$ and
   $w\to w'\Delta\to^*\Delta\varphi(w')$ by $j$ applications of $R_3$. Positions $i$ and $i+m$ of an
   alternating word carry the same letter iff $m$ is even, so $w''=\varphi(w')$: joinable.
2. $R_{1,2}/R_3$: $w=\bar f\Delta$ with $\bar f=f'x$ alternating of length $m$:
   $w\to\Delta\Delta$; $w\to f'\Delta\varphi(x)\to^*\Delta\varphi(f')\varphi(x)=\Delta\varphi(\bar f)\to\Delta\Delta$
   ($\varphi(\bar f)$ is alternating of length $m$).
3. $R_{1,2}/R_4$: $w=\bar f\Delta^{-1}\to\Delta\Delta^{-1}\to1$;
   $w\to f'\Delta^{-1}\varphi(x)\to^*\Delta^{-1}\varphi(\bar f)\to\Delta^{-1}\Delta\to1$.
4. $R_3/R_5$: $x\Delta\Delta^{-1}\to x$; $x\Delta\Delta^{-1}\to\Delta\varphi(x)\Delta^{-1}\to\Delta\Delta^{-1}x\to x$.
5. $R_4/R_6$: symmetric to 4.
6. $R_5/R_6$, $R_6/R_5$: $\Delta\Delta^{-1}\Delta\to\Delta$ both ways; $\Delta^{-1}\Delta\Delta^{-1}\to\Delta^{-1}$ both ways.

These are all overlaps: suffixes of leading words that are prefixes of leading words are a letter
$x$ (suffix of $R_{1,2}$; prefixes of $R_{1,2},R_3^x,R_4^x$), $\Delta$ (suffix of $R_3,R_6$; prefix
of $R_5$) and $\Delta^{-1}$ (suffix of $R_4,R_5$; prefix of $R_6$), plus longer alternating
suffixes in case 1. By the Composition–Diamond lemma $S$ is a GS basis; the original relations
are consequences of $S$, and $S$ holds in $A$, so $\langle X|S\rangle=A$. The irreducible words are
$\Delta^{(k)}w$ with $w$ avoiding $\langle ab\rangle_m,\langle ba\rangle_m$, and $\Delta$-letters
first, as stated. Rank 1 is immediate. ∎

**Theorem D (finiteness).** For finite $W$ (any order of $S$, deg-lex with $\Delta^{-1}<\Delta<S$),
$A(W)$ has a finite GS basis on the Artin–Garside alphabet iff $\operatorname{rank}W\le2$.

*Proof.* "If": Theorem C. "Only if": by Lemma 1(c) it suffices that MinRed is infinite.

(a) Some $s<t$ has $m=m_{st}\ge3$. Put $u_i=t\,v_i$ with $v_i=s^i\langle st\rangle_m$, $i\ge1$. We
show $u_i\in\mathrm{MinRed}(A^+_{\{s,t\}})$; since $|S|\ge3$, Lemma 3 then gives $u_i\in P(W)$.
An alternating word of length $<m$ contains no side of a braid relation, so it is the only word
of its element; the same holds for any word without alternating factor of length $m$.
 * $v_i$ is lexmin: the only letter with a smaller letter is $t$; after an occurrence of $t$ in
   $v_i$ the suffix is an alternating word of length $\le m-2$, the only word of its element, so
   it is not divisible by $\Pi_{m-1}(s,t)$ (length $m-1$); Lemma 4(L) holds everywhere.
 * $t\,v_i'$ is lexmin, $v_i'=s^i\langle st\rangle_{m-1}$: $v_i'$ has no alternating factor of length $m$,
   so it is the only word of its element; it begins with $ss$ while $\Pi_{m-1}(s,t)$ begins with
   $st$ ($m-1\ge2$), so $\Pi_{m-1}(s,t)\not\preceq[v_i']$; the other occurrences of $t$ are handled as for $v_i$.
 * $u_i$ is not lexmin: $[v_i]=[s^i]\,\Delta_{st}=\Delta_{st}\,\varphi_{st}([s^i])$, so
   $\Pi_{m-1}(s,t)\preceq\Delta_{st}\preceq[v_i]$, i.e. $s\preceq[u_i]$ by (1.1).

Proper factors of $u_i$ are factors of $v_i$ or of $tv_i'$, hence lexmin; so $u_i\in\mathrm{MinRed}(A^+_{\{s,t\}})$.

(b) All $m_{st}=2$ and $n\ge3$ (order $s_1<\dots<s_n$): $\Delta=s_1\cdots s_n$, the lexmin words are
$s_1^{k_1}\cdots s_n^{k_n}$ and the normal ones are those missing a letter. $u_i=s_1s_2^{\,i}s_3\cdots s_n$
($i\ge1$) is $\Delta$-divisible, and its maximal proper factors $s_1s_2^{\,i}s_3\cdots s_{n-1}$ and
$s_2^{\,i}s_3\cdots s_n$ are normal. ∎

(The words $u_i$ for $m=3$ are Bokut–Fong–Ke–Shiao's family $a_2a_1^la_2a_1$, arXiv:0806.1118, Lemma 4.1.)

## 8. Growth of the reduced basis: exact classification — COMPUTED+VERIFIED

For a trim deterministic automaton $\mathcal A$ let $c(L)$ be the number of accepted words of
length $L$. (i) If no SCC contains a cycle, the language is finite. (ii) If some SCC contains a
vertex with two internal out-edges, there are two distinct cycles $C_1,C_2$ through it, and the
$2^k$ concatenations of $C_1^{|C_2|}$ and $C_2^{|C_1|}$ give $2^k$ distinct accepted words of one
length (determinism), so $\limsup_L c(L)^{1/L}>1$: exponential growth along an arithmetic
progression of lengths (not necessarily at every length, e.g. $(aa|bb)^*$). (iii) Otherwise every
cyclic SCC is a simple cycle; with $d\ge1$ the maximal number of cyclic SCCs on an accepting path,
$c(L)=O(L^{d-1})$, and $c(L)\ge cL^{d-1}$ for $L$ in an arithmetic progression (independent
repetition counts of $d$ cycles). We call (iii) "polynomial of degree $d-1$". Applying this to the
exact pair automaton of Theorem B (`work/classify.py`, trimmed to co-accessible states) gives, for

## 11. Uniform explicit right sides: a rational GS basis for every finite type

This continuation replaces the normal-form oracle in Theorem B's right sides by a finite,
explicit output table. Its result is a GS basis, **not in general a reduced GS basis**.
The book asks for a GS basis; the stronger reduced-basis problem is distinguished below.
Only the alphabet $S\cup\{\Delta,\Delta^{-1}\}$ is used as algebra generators. Simple
elements occur as finite *indices*, never as additional algebra generators.

Fix $N\ge2$, a total order on $S$, and for each $w\in W$ a reduced Coxeter word $\rho(w)$,
with $\rho(1)=\epsilon$. For definiteness choose the lex-least reduced Coxeter word: repeatedly
remove the least left descent. This is a computation in the finite group $W$, not an
Artin-group normal-form oracle. The positive lift of $w$ is independent of the chosen
reduced word by Matsumoto's theorem. For simples $u,v$, use the finite weak-order join to define
$u\backslash v=u^{-1}(u\vee v)\in W$; its positive lift is the right complement in (1.1).

**Theorem G (uniform rational GS basis).** For every finite Coxeter system with $N\ge2$ and
every order of its atoms, add the following rules to the twists and cancellations of Theorem A. Choose an
atom $x$ and a target $q$, where either $q=t\in S$ with $t<x$, or $q=\Delta$ (indexed by $w_0$).
Set $e_0=x\backslash q$ and, for a nonempty atom word $v=y_1\cdots y_k$, set
$$e_i=y_i\backslash e_{i-1},\qquad f_i=e_{i-1}\backslash y_i\quad(1\le i\le k).$$
For **every** such sequence satisfying $e_i\ne1$ for $0\le i<k$ and $e_k=1$, include
$$
 \boxed{\quad x\,y_1\cdots y_k\ \longrightarrow\
          q\,\rho(q\backslash x)\,\rho(f_1)\cdots\rho(f_k).\quad} \tag{11.1}
$$
Here the first $q$ on the right is the single letter $t$ or $\Delta$, not a word for $w_0$.
All complement calculations use only the finite weak-order table of $W$. Formula (11.1),
including its right side, is therefore an explicit finite-state family with arbitrary-length
paths; it does not mean "$u\to\mathrm{NF}(u)$" and does not invoke an Artin normal form.

The resulting monic binomials form a GS basis over any field for deg-lex with
$\Delta^{-1}<\Delta<S$. Their irreducibles are exactly the normal forms of Lemma 2.
One may optionally discard every (11.1) whose left side is not a *minimal* non-normal positive
word (using the finite automaton of Theorem B): the resulting subset is also a GS basis.
Its leading words are minimal, but its tails need not be irreducible and distinct targets
can give several binomials with the same leading word.

*Proof, for all ranks and all word lengths.* The lcm identity gives
$e_{i-1}f_i=y_i e_i$ in $A^+$. Induction yields
$e_0f_1\cdots f_k=y_1\cdots y_k e_k=v$. Also $xe_0=q(q\backslash x)$, so
the two sides of (11.1) are equal. When $q=t<x$, the equality is homogeneous and its right
side starts with $t$, hence strictly decreases deg-lex. When $q=\Delta$, $q\backslash x=1$,
and homogeneity in atom length gives $\sum_i\ell(f_i)=k+1-N$. The right side has $X$-length
$k+2-N<k+1$ since $N\ge2$. Thus every rule strictly decreases a monomial well order.

Any left side is non-normal. Conversely consider a minimal non-normal positive word $xv$.
Its proper factors are normal, so Lemma 4 supplies a target $q=t<x$ or $\Delta$ with
$x\backslash q\preceq[v]$. The iterated-complement identity (1.1) implies $e_k=1$.
If $e_i=1$ earlier, the proper prefix $xy_1\cdots y_i$ would have a smaller representative
(target $t$) or be $\Delta$-divisible (target $\Delta$), contradicting minimality.
Consequently (11.1) contains a rule for every minimal non-normal positive word.
Together with the twists/cancellations it contains a rule for every MinRed word, and Lemma 1
proves the GS assertion, the group presentation, and the normal-form assertion.

For explicit composition control: an intersection or inclusion ambiguity $w$ has two one-step
descendants $a,b<w$ representing the same group element. Termination reduces each to the
unique irreducible word of that element just established. Every intermediate word remains
$<w$ by monomiality. Subtracting these two reduction chains expresses the composition as
a sum $\sum_i\lambda_i u_i s_i v_i$ with $u_i\overline{s_i}v_i<w$. Hence **every intersection
and inclusion composition is trivial**, uniformly; no rank-bounded enumeration is used. ∎

**Finite rational-expression form.** Use vertices $e\in W\setminus\{1\}$, plus start and accept.
Initial edges have pair labels $(x,\ q\rho(q\backslash x))$ and target $e_0$; an internal edge
$e\to y\backslash e$ has label $(y,\rho(e\backslash y))$, terminating on reaching $1$.
Paired words multiply coordinatewise, and every path label is precisely a rule (11.1).
The start/accept language is a rational subset of $S^*\times X^*$, expressible by finitely
many union, concatenation and Kleene-star operations. This is a compact GS basis on the
book's alphabet with explicit left and right sides. The same choice of path is used on
both sides; separately projecting the two languages and pairing arbitrary outputs is invalid.
The optional minimal-leading filter is a finite product with Theorem B's pair automaton.

**All-$n$ specialization to $B_n$.** This is a theorem for every $n\ge2$, not an extrapolation
from $B_4$. Take $W$ to be the $2^n n!$ signed permutations with windows $(v_1,\ldots,v_n)$.
For the graph $s_1-\cdots-s_{n-1}\overset4-s_n$, let $s_i$ ($i<n$) swap positions $n-i,n-i+1$
and let $s_n$ change the sign of position 1. Integer Coxeter length is
$$
\ell(v)=\#\{i:v_i<0\}+\#\{i<j:v_i>v_j\}+\#\{i<j:v_i+v_j<0\}.
$$
Compute $u\preceq v$ by $\ell(u^{-1}v)=\ell(v)-\ell(u)$ and compute $u\vee v$ as the unique
least common upper bound in this finite order. These formulas, the chosen order of the $s_i$,
and (11.1) completely specify every generator, state, transition and right side for every $n$.
Here $w_0=(-1,\ldots,-n)$, $N=n^2$, and $\varphi=\mathrm{id}$. No unknown completion or
unknown normal form enters this prescription. Theorem G proves its validity and all
compositions simultaneously for all $n$. The construction likewise applies to $A_n$ under
any order, and to the exceptional and reducible finite types.

This is a **finite-state, generally non-reduced** explicit answer to the fixed-alphabet/
fixed-order version of book question (3). It is not a claim of a new theorem in the literature,
nor a finite list of reduced Bokut-style word templates; the latter stronger presentation
request remains separate. The finite-state formula itself uses only standard lcm/reversing
identities, now packaged as monomial-order-decreasing relations.

  The sole emitted output is $bdbad$, reducible to $bdabd$ by $ba\to ab$.
* $F_4$: the class of $dbcdbc$ is
  $\{bdbcdb,bdbdcb,dbcdbc,dbdbcb,dbdcbc\}$. Its maximal proper prefix class is
  $\{dbcdb,dbdcb\}$ and suffix class is $\{bcdbc,bdbcb,bdcbc\}$.
  The sole emitted output is $bdbdcb$, reducible to $bdbcdb$ by $dc\to cd$.

Each proper-factor representative displayed first is lex-least, and all these word lengths
are below $N=16$ or $24$. Hence the proper factors are normal by Lemma 2, while the whole
input is not. Deleting its sole rule violates Lemma 1(iii). The complete finite classes
and all graph-path outputs are independently checked in `reduced_filter_control.py`.

Commutations alone also fail to normalize every output: $B_4$ has
$daabbdabd\to adaadbdaa$, with no commuting move from the right side but the decreasing
braid move $adaadbdaa\to adaabdbaa$; $F_4$ has $dabdabdb\to bdbdadbd$, with no commuting
move but $bdbdadbd\to bdbadabd$. These are finite obstructions to two particular reductions,
not reduced templates or an impossibility theorem for rational reduced bases.
