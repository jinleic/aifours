                                                                            1. Introduction
                                            In this paper, we are continuing to use a language of the Gröbner-Shirshov
                                         bases for some semigroups presented by defining relations (in papers [31], [32] some
                                         groups have been treated as semigroups). Traditionally in this situation, one use
                                         some other languages like elementary transformations language ([1],[2]), Newman’s
                                         Diamond lemma language ([63]), the rewriting systems language ([34] ), or some
                                         else ([56]). For example, P.M. Cohn used Diamond lemma for semigroups as early
                                         as in 1956 ([41], [42]). After him first author used the same language to prove some
                                         embedding theorems for semigroups [8],[9]. Also first author used a language of
                                         rewriting systems in order to prove that multiplicative semigroups of some rings is
                                         embeddable into groups [12], [13] (see also [16], [20]). But here we formulate our
                                         main results in terms of Gröbner-Shirshov bases.
                                            During the last few years the Gröbner-Shirshov bases method has been used
                                         to study some important examples of associative algebras (quantum enveloping
                                         algebras of the type An , Hecke algebras of type An ), Lie algebras (simple Lie
                                         algebras of the types An , Bn , Cn , Dn , G2 , F4 , E6 , E7 , E8 , Kac-Moody algebras of
                                                      (1)   (1)   (1)  (1)
                                         the types An , Bn , Cn , Dn ), Lie superalgebras (simple Lie superalgebras of the
                                         types An , Bn , Cn , Dn ), modules (irreducible modules over simple Lie algebras of
                                         the type An , Specht modules over Hecke algebras of the type An ), and groups
                                         (Gröbner-Shirshov bases for Coxeter groups of types An , Bn , Dn ) [25] – [31], [53]–
                                         [55], [65] – [68]. Also this method was invented for associative conformal algebras
                                         [21], [22].
                                            In recent paper [32], two of us applied the Gröbner-Shirshov bases method to
                                         study some groups by P.S. Novikov [64] and W.W. Boone [33] with the algorith-
                                         mically unsolvable word problem. They revised some papers [10], [11], [17], [18]
                                         (see also [19], [28]) that used a rewriting systems language (between lines of HNN-
                                         extension theory) using the very language of Gröbner-Shirshov bases. It means
                                         that they eliminated any group theory in proving Novikov’s and Boone’s theorems
                                         on unsolvability.
                                            Some other activities on Gröbner-Shirshov (non-commutative Gröbner, stan-
                                         dard) bases may be found in papers by T. Mora [62], V.N. Latyshev [58], V. Uf-
                                         narovski [73], A.A. Mikhalev and A.A. Zolotyh [61], A.A. Mikhalev and E. Vasilieva

   If a subset S of khXi is not a Gröbner-Shirshov basis then one can add to S all
nontrivial compositions of polynomials of S, and continue this process (infinitely)

Remark 4.6. In view of [49], Theorem 4.2 gives rise to a new algorithm of the

                                                                    +

                                           v = ∆s u,
