I found the exact base parallel classes for Denniston's LKTS(15) and checked by hand, with no compute, that they form an LKTS(15). The Zhou–Chang LKTS(21)/LKTS(39) data are not public: only the abstract can be read, and it gives no generators. As of 2026-10-14 I found nothing on LKTS(57) or LKTS(87) newer than arXiv:2607.21521v1 (2026-07-23). That only means I found nothing; it does not prove the two orders are still open.

## Status as of 2026-10-14
- **Wang §4 (local `sources/wang-2607.21521.txt`):** "an LKTS(v) is known to exist for every v < 200 satisfying v ≡ 3 (mod 6), with the two possible exceptions being 57 and 87."
- **Why the finite-field methods miss them:** 55 = 5·11 and 85 = 5·17 are neither prime powers nor products of prime powers ≡ 1 (mod 6).
- **Wang's suggested routes:** a recursion LKTS(v) → LKTS(4v−3) would give 57 from 15; LKTS(v) → LKTS(4v+3) would give 87 from 21. He also suggests direct search with a prescribed automorphism group.
- **Search scope:**
  - arXiv API sorted by date (all:Kirkman; "Kirkman triple" OR LKTS OR "Sylvester's problem"; abs:"large set" AND triple): the newest relevant entry is 2607.21521v1.
  - Crossref titles containing "Kirkman" since 2025: 11 hits, none about large sets.
  - General web search: nothing newer.
- **RDSQS route (my arithmetic, from statements in Liu–Lei [arXiv:2210.06214](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06214)v2):** Thm 1.1 says RDSQS(v+1) gives LKTS(3v), and the paper says RDSQS(v) needs v ≡ 4 (mod 6). So this route only yields orders ≡ 9 (mod 18). 57 ≡ 3 and 87 ≡ 15 (mod 18) are out of reach.
- **Denniston-style search is consistent in count (my arithmetic):** on ℤ_{v−2}∪{∞1,∞2}, translations act freely on triples because gcd(v−2, 6) = 1. The number of orbits equals the number of blocks of a KTS(v): 532 for v = 57 and 1247 for v = 87. This says nothing about whether such a KTS exists.
- The local book excerpt's "smallest unresolved order is 21" is out of date; Zhou–Chang solved 21 in 2009/2010.

## Exact small-case data
**Denniston LKTS(15)** on ℤ₁₃∪{∞1,∞2}, as given in Wang–Shi [arXiv:1307.3019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.3019) §4. The large set is the 13 translates D_i, with Q_{i,j} = Q_{0,j} + i:
- Q00 = {∞1,∞2,0} {1,4,5} {2,6,11} {3,7,10} {8,9,12}
- Q01 = {∞1,1,6} {∞2,2,8} {0,10,12} {3,5,9} {4,7,11}
- Q02 = {∞1,2,5} {∞2,4,9} {0,3,11} {1,7,12} {6,8,10}
- Q03 = {∞1,3,12} {∞2,5,7} {0,4,6} {1,8,11} {2,9,10}
- Q04 = {∞1,4,10} {∞2,11,12} {0,5,8} {1,2,3} {6,7,9}
- Q05 = {∞1,7,8} {∞2,3,6} {0,1,9} {2,4,12} {5,10,11}
- Q06 = {∞1,9,11} {∞2,1,10} {0,2,7} {3,4,8} {5,6,12}

Cross-check: this is exactly Denniston's first week as given on Wikipedia (raw page), under A…M→0…12, N→∞1, O→∞2. Denniston's Days 7, 6, 3, 4, 5, 1, 2 correspond to Q00…Q06.

What I checked by hand:
- Each class partitions all 15 points.
- D₀ (the KTS of the 7 base classes Q00…Q06) is an STS(15): every point has all 14 partners exactly once.
- Its 35 blocks lie in different ℤ₁₃-orbits: the ∞1 and ∞2 pair-differences each give {±1,…,±6}, and the 22 finite triples give all 22 cyclic gap patterns. So the 13 translates are an LKTS(15).
- No multiplier x↦ax with a ≠ 1 preserves D₀, with the ∞'s fixed or swapped. For a = 3, {1,4,5} goes to {2,3,12}, which is not a block; for a = −1, {2,6,11} goes to {2,7,11}, which is not a block.

**ZCZ q = 67 (v = 69), g = 2, exponents mod 66:**
- Pairs: {13,42} with ∞1; {20,33} with ∞2; {39,38} with 0.
- Triples: {46,36,6} {1,24,32} {0,2,54} {3,16,62} {40,12,8} {19,10,58} {41,60,18} {5,52,7} {17,28,22} {15,64,48} {51,43,57} {25,14,63} {31,49,34} {59,30,35} {9,29,50} {23,53,27} {65,11,26} {4,61,45} {55,21,56} {44,47,37}.
- Hand-checked: these blocks partition ℤ₆₆, and their ordered differences cover ℤ₆₆∖{0,22,44} exactly twice and never hit {0,22,44}. That matches the appendix's difference-family claim.
- The rule that turns this into the full LKTS (Thm 3.2 / Lemma 3.4) is behind the paywall.
