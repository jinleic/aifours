                                                                                               Abstract

                                                         An r-block-coloring, simply r-coloring, of a Steiner triple system STS(v) is a partition
                                                     of the block set into r color classes, each color class being a partial parallel class. The
                                                     chromatic index of STS(v), denoted by χ′ (v), is the smallest r for which an r-coloring
                                                     of an STS(v) exists. A minimum colorable Steiner triple system mcSTS(v) is an STS(v)
                                                     admitting a χ′ (v)-coloring. We generalize the notion of an RDSQS (a Steiner quadruple
                                                     system SQS with resolvable derived designs) to mcDSQS, representing an SQS whose derived
                                                     design at every point is minimum colorable. This is motivated from an application in non-
                                                     binary diameter perfect codes. The purpose of this paper is to display a few recursive
                                                     constructions to produce mcDSQSs via Steiner systems S(3, K, v) with certain properties.
                                                     Among others, a construction for mcDSQSs is developed, which is also new even for RDSQSs;
                                                     special constructions concentrating only on mcDSQS(6n + 2)s are demonstrated as well.
                                                     As the main results, both a new infinite family of RDSQS(6n + 4)s and the first infinite
                                                     family of mcDSQS(6n + 2)s are constructed. To be specific, an RDSQS(22m+1 + 2) and
                                                     an mcDSQS(2 · 9m + 2) are proved to exist, in which the former class gives rise to a new
                                                     infinite family of large sets of Kirkman triple systems. As applications,      the smallest q is

                                           1        Introduction and preliminaries

                                                Let X be a set of v points and K a set of positive integers. Let G be a collection of subsets
                                           (called groups or holes) of X which partition X. A group divisible t-design of order v with block
                                           sizes from K, denoted by GDD(t, K, v), is a triple (X, G, B) with the following properties:

                                            (1) B is a family of subsets of X (called blocks), each having cardinality in K;
                                            (2) each block of B intersects any given group in at most one point;

The type of the GDD is defined to be the multiset T = {|G| : G ∈ G} of group sizes. We also
use g1a1 g2a2 . . . grar to denote the type, which means that there are ai groups of size gi , 1 ≤ i ≤ r.
A GDD(t, {k}, v) is also denoted by GDD(t, k, v). A GDD(t, k, kn) of type nk is known as a
transversal t-design and denoted by TD(t, k, n). If (X, G, B) is a GDD(t, K, v) of type 1v , then
we omit the notation of G and call (X, B) a Steiner t-system, also denoted S(t, K, v). The
notation S(t, k, v) is used for K = {k}. An S(2, 3, v) is a Steiner triple system STS(v) and an
S(3, 4, v) is a Steiner quadruple system SQS(v). It is well-known that an STS(v) exists if and
only if v ≡ 1, 3 (mod 6) [10] and an SQS(v) exists if and only if v ≡ 2, 4 (mod 6) [7].
     Let (X, G, B) be a GDD and Y ⊆ X. A partial parallel class (PPC) of Y is a collection of
pairwise disjoint blocks contained in Y . A parallel class (PC) of Y is a PPC that partitions Y .
We say that (X, G, B) is resolvable, denoted by RGDD, if B can be partitioned into PCs of X.
An RGDD(t, k, v) of type 1v is a resolvable S(t, k, v), denoted by RS(t, k, v). An RS(2, 3, v) is
a Kirkman triple system KTS(v). A KTS(v) exists if and only if v ≡ 3 (mod 6) [12, 15]. Two
KTS(v)s on the same set X are said to be disjoint if they have no blocks in common. A large
set of Kirkman triple system of order v, denoted by LKTS(v), is a partition of all triples of a
v-element set X into v − 2 pairwise disjoint KTS(v)s. Although the problem of the existence of
LKTS(v)s has a long history, it remains pretty much open.
     Let (X, B) be an S(t, k, v). For any point x ∈ X, let Bx = {B \ {x} : x ∈ B, B ∈ B}. Then
(X \ {x}, Bx ) is an S(t − 1, k − 1, v − 1), which is said to be the derived design of (X, B) at the
point x. An S(t, k, v) with resolvable derived designs, abbreviated to RDS(t, k, v), refers to an
S(t, k, v) whose derived design at every point is resolvable. We use the notation RDSQS(v) to
denote an SQS(v) with resolvable derived designs. RDSQSs play an important role in recursive
constructions of large sets of Kirkman triple systems (see [3, 11, 22]).

Theorem 1.1 ([3, 22]). If there exists an RDSQS(v + 1), then there exists an LKTS(3v).

a resolvable derived design, but one may instead ask for a derived design with a partition of the

     If an STS(v) admits a χ′ (v)-coloring, then it is said to be minimum colorable, denoted
by mcSTS(v). If v ≡ 3 (mod 6), then an mcSTS(v) is a KTS(v). An SQS(v) with minimum
colorable derived designs, abbreviated to mcDSQS(v), refers to an SQS(v) whose derived design
at every point is minimum colorable. Therefore, an mcDSQS(v) is the same as an RDSQS(v)
if v ≡ 4 (mod 6). There is a large literature studying RDSQSs [2, 3, 11, 23], while we only
have one known mcDSQS(6n + 2) with n ≥ 2, that is, an mcDSQS(20) [16]. In this work we
introduce the concept of mcDSQSs, not only as a generalization of RDSQSs, but also due to
their applications in diameter perfect codes.
      Diameter perfect codes are a natural generalization of perfect codes. They are based on
the code-anticode bound, a generalization of sphere-packing bound. Code-anticode bound was
first introduced by Delsarte (see [5]) and generalized by Ahlswede et al. (see [1]). Let C be a
code in a metric space V with a transitive automorphism group, where the minimum distance
between the codewords in C is d. Let A be a anticode of V such that the maximum distance
(diameter) between elements in A is D, where D < d. Then
                                                   |C| · |A| ≤ |V|.
If the inequality holds with equality, then C is said to be a D-diameter perfect code or diameter
perfect distance-d code. Let Jq (n, w) denote the set of all words of length n and weight w, over
Zq = {0, 1, . . . , q − 1}, where the Hamming distance is used as the metric. A code C ⊆ Jq (n, w)
is a q-ary constant-weight-w code of length n and it is denoted by (n, |C|, d; w)q code if C has
minimum distance d.

     The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 is devoted to demonstrating recursive
constructions for generic mcDSQSs. In Subsection 2.1, we define a gcDCQS(g n : s) to build an
mcDSQS(gn + s) by filling in holes. Then we demonstrate several recursive constructions to
produce gcDCQS(gn : s) via Steiner systems S(3, K, v) with certain properties. In Subsection
2.2, we concentrate on constructions of mcDSQS(6n+2)s. We propose two new types of colorings
of the derived designs of a CQS(gn : 2) and then present related constructions. In Section 3, we
construct some necessary designs required in our recursive constructions. Then we establish the
existence of a new infinite family of RDSQS(6n + 4)s and an infinite family of mcDSQS(6n + 2)s.
As consequences, we show their applications in diameter perfect codes and large sets of Kirkman
triple systems. In Section 4, we conclude the paper with some open questions.


2    Recursive constructions

     A combinatorial structure named candelabra quadruple system CQS plays a central role
in the recursive constructions of SQSs. In order to produce RDSQSs, candelabra quadruple
systems with resolvable derived designs RDCQSs were introduced and intensively studied in
the last decade. In this section, we introduce several coloring types of the derived designs of a
CQS as a generalization of an RDCQS. Then we display a number of recursive constructions
to produce mcDSQSs. For later use, we introduce or recall some definitions.
     An incomplete Steiner triple system on a v-set X with an h-subset H as a hole, denoted by
STS(v, h), is a triple (X, H, B), where B is a block set of a GDD(2, 3, v) of type 1v−h h1 with H
as the long group. Further let v ≡ 1, 3 (mod 6). If B can be partitioned into r := χ′ (v) PPCs
of X, in which there are s := χ′ (v) − v−h2 PPCs of X \ H, then we say that (X, H, B) is good
colorable, denoted by gcSTS(v, h). It then follows from (1.1) that

    A Kirkman frame F(2, 3, v{m}) (as in [18]), briefly by KF(mv ), is a GDD(2, 3, mv) of type
mv (X, G, B) such that the block set B can be partitioned into subsets Pr , r ∈ R, each Pr being
a PC of X \ G for some group G ∈ G. A KF(mv ) exists if and only if m ≡ 0 (mod 2), v ≥ 4
and m(v − 1) ≡ 0 (mod 3) (see [17]).
     Let (X, G, A) be a GDD(3, 4, mv) of type mv . For any x ∈ X, let Ax = {A\{x} : x ∈
A, A ∈ A}. Then the derived design (X\G, G\{G}, Ax ) is a GDD(2, 3, m(v − 1)) of type mv−1 .
Here we examine two types of coloring of the derived designs. We use RDGDD(3, 4, v{m})
to denote a GDD(3, 4, mv) of type mv with resolvable derived designs, meaning that its de-
rived design at every point is a resolvable GDD(2, 3, m(v − 1)). An RDTD(3, 4, m) denotes an
RDGDD(3, 4, 4{m}). Parallelly, we use FDGDD(3, 4, v{m}) to denote a GDD(3, 4, mv) of type
mv whose derived design at every point is a KF(mv−1 ).

     For a v-set X, take an s-subset S ⊆ X and call it a stem. Let G be a collection of subsets,
called groups, of X \ S which partition X \ S. A candelabra t-system (as in [13]) of order v with
block sizes from a set K, briefly by CS(t, K, v), is a quadruple (X, S, G, A) satisfying that

 (1) A is a family of subsets of X, called blocks, each of cardinality from K;
 (2) each t-subset of X except those contained in S ∪ G for some G ∈ G is contained in exactly

By the type of a CS(t, K, v) we mean the list ({|G| : G ∈ G} : s). Exponential notation is also
applied to denote the type of G. A candelabra system with t = 3 and k = 4 is a candelabra
quadruple system and denoted by CQS.
     It is a standard construction to build an SQS(gn + s) from a CQS(g n : s) by filling in holes.
To produce mcDSQSs, we need to define desirable colorings of the derived designs of CQSs.
The rest of this section is divided into two subsections. In the first subsection, we generalize
known definitions and constructions related with RDSQSs to develop recursive constructions
applicable for generic mcDSQSs. In the second subsection, we turn our attention to mcDSQSs
only of orders 6n+2. We introduce two new types of colorings of derived designs of a CQS(gn : 2)
and display effective recursive constructions.

4    Summary and open problems

      In this paper, we considered the constructions and existence of mcDSQS(v)s. There has
been an amount of work on RDSQS(6n+4)s, while the study on mcDSQS(6n+2)s has just begun
in this paper. We generalized a number of existing recursive constructions about RDSQSs to
produce general mcDSQSs. In particular, a construction for gcDCQSs is developed, which is also
new even for RDCQSs, see Construction 2.4. Moreover, we demonstrated several constructions
aiming only at mcDSQS(6n + 2)s (in Subsection 2.2). As the main results, we obtained a new
infinite family of RDSQS(6n+4)s and displayed a new infinite family of LKTSs. (More families of
RDSQSs and thus more LKTSs could be produced by substituting known orders to the product
construction [3, 24], but we did not study this in depth to avoid distraction.) We obtained the
first infinite family of mcDSQS(6n + 2)s and presented their applications in diameter perfect
constant-weight codes. Because the chromatic index of an STS(v) equals (v + 1)/2 only when
v ≡ 1 (mod 6) and v ≥ 19, direct constructions for auxiliary designs to build mcDSQS(6n + 2)s
usually require significant computation. For future work, we pose the following problems.

 (1) Find more examples of mcDSQS(v)s, for example, an mcDSQS(38) and an RDSQS(52)
     (the smallest unknown mcDSQS(6n + 2)s and the smallest unknown RDSQS(6n + 4)s,
     respectively).
 (2) Find a gcDCQS(v 4 : 0) for v = 20, 32. Apply Corollary 2.6. If a gcDCQS(204 : 0) exists,
     then there exists an mcDSQS(5 · 4n ) for any integer n ≥ 3. In the same way, the existence
