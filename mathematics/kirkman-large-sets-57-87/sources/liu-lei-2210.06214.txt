                                                   Abstract: The existence of large sets of Kirkman triple systems (LKTSs) is
                                                   one of the best-known open problems in combinatorial design theory. Steiner
                                                   quadruple systems with resolvable derived designs (RDSQSs) play an important
                                                   role in the recursive constructions of LKTSs. In this paper, we introduce a
                                                   special combinatorial structure RDSQS∗ (v) and use it to present a construction
                                                   for RDSQS(4v). As a consequence, some new infinite families of LKTSs are given.

                                                   Key words: Large sets, Kirkman triple systems, Steiner quadruple systems,
                                                   Resolvable derived designs


                                           1        Introduction
                                           Let X be a v-element set and K a set of positive integers. A t-wise balanced design
                                           S(t, K, v) is a pair (X, B) where B is a collection of subsets of X (called blocks) with
                                           the property that the size of every block is in the set K and every t-element subset of
                                           X is contained in exactly one block. An S(t, {k}, v) is denoted by S(t, k, v) and called a
                                           Steiner system. An S(2, 3, v) is called a Steiner triple system, denoted by STS(v). An
                                           S(3, 4, v) is called a Steiner quadruple system and denoted by SQS(v). It is well known
                                           that there is an SQS(v) if and only if v ≡ 2, 4 (mod 6) [10].
                                                   Let (X, B) be an S(t, k, v). If there exists a partition Γ = {P1 , P2 , ..., Pr } of B such
                                           that each Pi forms a parallel class, i.e. a partition of X, then the S(t, k, v) is called
                                           resolvable and Γ is called a resolution. A resolvable STS(v) is called a Kirkman triple
                                           system and denoted by KTS(v). It is well known that a KTS(v) exists if and only if
                                           v ≡ 3 (mod 6) [18].
                                                   Two STS(v)s on the same set X are said to be disjoint if they have no triples in
                                           common. A partition of all the triples of a v-element set X into v − 2 disjoint STS(v)s,
                                           denoted by LSTS(v), is called a large set of STS(v)s. An LSTS(v) is known to exist if
                                           and only if v ≡ 1, 3 (mod 6) and v 6= 7 [15, 16, 19].
                                                   Furthermore, if each STS(v) in an LSTS(v) is a KTS(v), then it is called a large set
                                           of Kirkman triple systems and denoted by LKTS(v). The existence of LKTSs remains
                                           an open problem though much work has been done by many researchers. The existence

of an LKTS(15) was shown by Denniston [6], and more small orders were given in
[3, 7, 9, 26, 27, 28]. Denniston [8] used transitive KTSs to give a tripling construction
for LKTSs, which was generalized to a product construction by Lei [12]. Zhang and
Zhu [24, 25] improved Denniston’s tripling construction and Lei’s product construction
by removing the condition of transitive KTS. Lei [13, 14] also displayed a recursive
construction for LKTSs based on 3-wise balanced designs. Lately, Xu and Ji [20] used
a (2, 1)-RSQS∗ (4n) to construct an LKTS(22n+1 + 1). More constructions and results
on LKTSs were displayed in Chang and Zhou’s survey paper [4].
    In the process of studying LKTSs, many combinatorial configurations have been
proposed in order to present some recursive constructions for LKTSs. The Steiner
quadruple system with resolvable derived designs (RDSQS) is one of these special con-
figurations. The RDSQS was firstly used by Chang and Zhou in [4], where its main
application in constructing LKTSs is restated after Yuan and Kang [21].
    Let (X, B) be an S(t, k, v). For any x ∈ X, let Bx = {B \ {x} : x ∈ B, B ∈ B}, then
(X \ {x}, Bx ) is an S(t − 1, k − 1, v − 1), which is said to be the derived design at the
point x. An S(t, k, v) with resolvable derived designs, abbreviated to RDS(t, k, v), refers
to an S(t, k, v) whose derived design at every point is resolvable. An RDSQS(v) refers
to an RDS(3, 4, v), i.e., its derived design at every point is a KTS(v − 1). Necessarily,
the existence of an RDSQS(v) requires that v ≡ 4 (mod 6).

Theorem 1.1. [4, 21] If there exists an RDSQS(v+1), then there exists an LKT S(3v).

    In this paper, three infinite families of RDSQSs are given. As a consequence, some
new infinite families of LKTSs are given.
    The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we use an auxiliary
design named group divisible design with resolvable derived designs to construct an
RDSQS(v). In Section 3, a special combinatorial structure denoted by RDSQS∗ (v) is
introduced. We use it to construct an RDSQS(4v). In the last section we combine the
known constructions to update and expand our results on RDSQSs and LKTSs.


2    RDSQSs and related designs
In this section, we mainly establish some results of RDSQS(v) by using the combina-
torial structure group divisible design with resolvable derived designs (RDGDD). In
what follows, Zv = {0, 1, . . . , v − 1} denotes the integer modulo v residual class additive
group.
    Let v and t be positive integers and K a set of positive integers. A group divisible t-
design of order v with block sizes from K, denoted by GDD(t, K, v), is a triple (X, G, B)
with the following properties:
    (1) X is a set of v elements (called points);

                                             2
   (2) G = {G1 , G2 , . . . } is a set of non-empty subsets (called groups) of X which
partition X;
   (3) B is a family of subsets of X (called blocks), each of cardinality from K, such
that each block intersects any given group in at most one point, and each t-element set
of points from t distinct groups is contained in exactly one block.

   The type of a GDD is defined to be the multiset T = {|G| : G ∈ G} of group
sizes. We also use ai bj ck · · · to denote the type, which means that in the multiset there
are i occurrences of a, j occurrences of b, etc. We often write GDD(t, k, v) instead of
GDD(t, {k}, v). A GDD(t, k, kn) of type nk is also called a transversal t-design and
also denoted by TD(t, k, n).

GDD(t, K , gk) of type g k , then there exists a GDD(t, K , gv) of type g v .
   Let (X, G, B) be a GDD(t, k, gn) of type gn . If B can be partitioned into some
parallel classes, then the GDD(t, k, gn) is called resolvable. For any x ∈ G and G ∈ G,
let Bx = {B\{x} : x ∈ B, B ∈ B}, then (X\G, G\{G}, Bx ) is a GDD(t−1, k−1, g(n−1))
of type g n−1 , which is said to be the derived design at the point x. A GDD(t, k, v)
with resolvable derived designs, denoted by RDGDD(t, k, v), refers to a GDD(t, k, v)
whose derived design at every point is resolvable. Similarly, RDTD(t, k, n) represents
a TD(t, k, n) whose derived design at every point is resolvable.

Lemma 4.1. [23] If there exists an RDSQS(u + 1) and an RDSQS(v + 1), then there
exists an RDSQS(uv + 1).
