Introduction

      In [5] the author raised the question whether each E-unitary regular semig-
roup S is embeddable into a semidirect product of a band by a group. Furthermore,
she presented, for every band variety V, a canonical homomorphism of S into a
semidirect product of a band from V by the greatest group homomorphic image of
S which is an embedding if and only if S is embeddable into a semidirect product
of a band from V by a group. Finally, by applying this construction, she proved
that each E-unitary regular semigroup S with regular band of idempotents is em-
beddable into a semidirect product of a band B by a group where B can be chosen
from the band variety generated by ES . Recently, B. Billhardt [1] and A. A. Isma-
eel [3] proved independently, that if Eω, the closure of the set of idempotents in an
inverse semigroup S is a Clifford semigroup then S is embeddable into a semidirect
product of a Clifford semigroup by a group. The proof in the latter paper is based
on a generalization of our canonical construction in [5].
      In this paper we further generalize this construction. For any orthodox se-
migroup S, for any group congruence θ on S and for any bivariety V of orthodox
semigroups, we define a canonical homomorphism of S into a semidirect product of
a member in V by S/θ which is an embedding if and only if S is embeddable over
θ into a semidirect product of a member in V by a group. Hence we immediately
infer a criterion for the embeddability of S over θ into a semidirect product of a

1. Preliminaries

       In this section we summarize the notions and results needed in the paper. For
the undefined notions and notations the reader is referred to [2].
       Given an orthodox semigroup S, its band of idempotents is denoted by ES
or, if it causes no confusion, then simply by E. The identity element of a group is
denoted by 1.
       If S is an orthodox semigroup and θ is a congruence on S then, as usual, we
denote its kernel {s ∈ S: s θ e for some e ∈ E} by ker θ. It is well known that ker θ
is an orthodox subsemigroup in S. Obviously, we have E ⊆ ker θ. In particular, if
θ is a group congruence then ker θ = {s ∈ S: sθ = 1}. If K is a group variety then
we denote by σK the least congruence θ on S such that S/θ ∈ K. In particular, as
usual, σ is the least group congruence on S.
       If φ: S → T is a homomorphism then the congruence on S induced by φ is
denoted by ≡φ .

      A bivariety of orthodox semigroups is a class of orthodox semigroups closed
with respect to P, Sr and H. In particular, the bivarieties of inverse semigroups
and of orthogroups, and hence those of groups are just the varieties of inverse
semigroups, orthogroups and groups, respectively. Therefore, in these cases, we
will often speak simply about varieties, e. g. about group varieties, and not about
bivarieties. The bivariety of all orthodox semigroups will be denoted by O.
      It is proved in [4] that the bivarieties of orthodox semigroups are just the
classes defined by biidentities. Moreover, the notion of a biinvariant congruence and
that of a bifree object is introduced. Given a bivariety V of orthodox semigroups
and an alphabet A, the biinvariant congruence on A⊕ corresponding to V is

       ρ(V, A) = {(u, v) ∈ A⊕ × A⊕ : the biidentity u ˆ

The bifree object in V on A turns out to be A⊕ /ρ(V, A). Note that if V is
an inverse semigroup variety, in particular, a group variety, then the biinvariant
congruences corresponding to V coincide with the respective fully invariant cong-
ruences.
      Now we recall the basic notions on graphs and semigroupoids needed later.
      A graph X consists of a set of objects denoted by Obj(X) and, for every pair
i, j ∈ Obj(X), a set of arrows from i to j which is denoted by X(i, j) and is called
a hom-set. The different hom-sets are supposed to be disjoint. If a ∈ X(i, j)
then we also write that α(a) = i and ω(a) = j. The set of all arrows will be
denoted by Arr(X). The arrows a, b are called coterminal if a, b ∈ X(i, j) for some
i, j ∈ Obj(X) and are termed consecutive provided ω(a) = α(b).
      A graph with involution consists of a graph X and a mapping ∗ assigning to
any arrow a ∈ X(i, j) (i, j ∈ Obj(X)) an arrow a∗ ∈ X(j, i) such that (a∗ )∗ = a.
      A semigroupoid is a graph C equipped with a composition which assigns to
every pair of consecutive arrows a ∈ C(i, j), b ∈ C(j, k) an arrow ab ∈ C(i, k) such
that the composition is associative, that is, for any arrows a ∈ C(i, j), b ∈ C(j, k)
and c ∈ C(k, l), we have (ab)c = a(bc).
      Observe that C(i, i) is either empty or a semigroup for each i ∈ Obj(C). For
the sake of uniformity, we will consider the empty set also as a semigroup, and we
term C(i, i) the local semigroup of C at i.
      A semigroupoid with involution is a graph C with involution equipped with a
composition which makes C a semigroupoid and which possesses the property that
(ab)∗ = b∗ a∗ for every a ∈ C(i, j), b ∈ C(j, k) (i, j, k ∈ Obj(C)).
      In particular, a set A can be considered as a graph with one object whose uni-
que hom-set is A and, similarly, a semigroup S can be considered as a semigroupoid
with one object whose unique hom-set is S.

      A semigroupoid C is called regular if, for every a ∈ Arr(C), there is x ∈ Arr(C)
with axa = a. In the same way as in the case of a semigroup, one can show that
if such an x exists then a′ = xax has the property that aa′ a = a and a′ aa′ = a′ .
If the latter equalities hold for some a and a′ then we say that a′ is an inverse of
a. The set of inverses of an element a is denoted by V (a). An idempotent in a
semigroupoid C is an arrow satisfying e2 = e. Clearly, each idempotent belongs
to a local semigroup. A regular semigroupoid is termed orthodox if the product of
the idempotents is idempotent. Clearly, a regular semigroupoid is orthodox if and
only if each of its local semigroups is orthodox.
      Let X, Y be two graphs. A graph function f : X → Y consists of an object
function f : Obj(X) → Obj(Y ) and, for every i, j ∈ Obj(X), a hom-set function
f : X(i, j) → Y (if, jf ). If C, D are semigroupoids then by a morphism of semigro-
upoids φ: C → D we mean a graph function φ such that aφ · bφ = (ab)φ for every
pair a, b of consecutive arrows in C. If the hom-set functions are injective then φ
is termed a faithful morphism. If the object function is bijective and the hom-set
functions are surjective then φ is said to be a quotient morphism. If the object and
the hom-set functions are bijective then φ is called an isomorphism.
      By a congruence γ on a semigroupoid C we mean a family

                                γ = {γ(i, j): i, j ∈ Obj(C)}

of equivalence relations γ(i, j) on C(i, j) such that, for every a ∈ C(i, j),
c, d ∈ C(j, k) and b ∈ C(k, l) (i, j, k, l ∈ Obj(C)), the relation c γ(j, k) d implies
ac γ(i, k) ad and cb γ(j, l) db. For simplicity, we will often write γ instead of γ(i, j).
If a ∈ C(i, j) then the equivalence class containing a will be denoted by aγ. Note
that γ(i, i) is a congruence on the local semigroup C(i, i).
      Given a congruence γ on a semigroupoid C, we can define the quotient semig-
roupoid C/γ as follows: Obj(C/γ) = Obj(C), (C/γ)(i, j) is the set of all γ(i, j)-
classes of C(i, j) and the composition rule is given by

                     aγ · bγ = (ab)γ           (a ∈ C(i, j), b ∈ C(j, k)).

Obviously, the congruence γ on C determines a quotient morphism γ ♮ whose object
function is identical and whose hom-set functions assign the respective γ-class to
each arrow. Conversely, if φ: C → D is a quotient morphism then the family of
equivalence relations on the hom-sets in C determined by the hom-set functions is a
congruence on C which we will denote by ≡φ . Moreover, φ induces an isomorphism
ι: C/ ≡φ → D such that φ = ≡♮φ ι.

      Let S be an orthodox semigroup and G a group. Suppose that G acts on
S by automorphisms on the left, that is, for every g ∈ G, an automorphism of S
is given, denoted by g: S → S, s 7→ gs, such that h(gs) = (hg)s holds for every
g, h ∈ G and s ∈ S. Briefly, we will say only that G acts on S. The semidirect
product S ∗ G is defined on the underlying set S × G by the multiplication

                  (s, g)(s, g) = (s · gs, gg) (s, s ∈ S, g, g ∈ G).

The following properties of the semidirect product are straightforward.

Result 1.1. Let S be an orthodox semigroup and G a group acting on S.
  (i) The semidirect product S ∗ G is an orthodox semigroup with ES∗G =
      {(e, 1): e ∈ ES }, and ES∗G is isomorphic to ES .
 (ii) The second projection π2 : S ∗ G → G, (s, g) 7→ g is a homomorphism of S ∗ G
      onto G with ker(≡π2 ) = {(s, 1): s ∈ S} which is isomorphic to S.
