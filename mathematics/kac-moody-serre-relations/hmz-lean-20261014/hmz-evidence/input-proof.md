# Graded Serre-ideal comparison: proofs and finite certification

Date: 2026-10-14 (America/Los_Angeles). **The full book conjecture is not proved or refuted here.** This file establishes the exact algebraic meaning of the finite computation and records bounded/special-degree results only. No all-height extrapolation is made.

## 1. Exact target and conventions

Work over characteristic zero, first Q and then C. A generalized Cartan matrix A=(a_ij) has a_ii=2, a_ij nonpositive integers for i!=j, and a_ij=0 iff a_ji=0. Use the book's full realization: if rank(A)=ell and A is n by n, take an independent Cartan basis h_1,...,h_(2n-ell) and a rank-n matrix C whose first n rows are A. In the preliminary contragredient algebra g_tilde(A), impose

    [e_i,f_j]=delta_ij h_i,   [h_p,h_q]=0,
    [h_p,e_i]=c_pi e_i,      [h_p,f_i]=-c_pi f_i.

The standard triangular decomposition of this preliminary construction is L(f) direct-sum h direct-sum L(e), with free positive and negative Lie algebras. All computations below occur in L=L(e). Its degree is in N^n, with deg(e_i)=epsilon_i and total height |alpha|=sum alpha_i. Full-rank C makes distinct root degrees distinguishable by Cartan weights; in particular h_i are **independent even when A is singular**.

Let tau be the unique maximal ideal of g_tilde(A) intersecting h trivially. Write K_alpha=tau intersect L_alpha. Let I be the free-Lie ideal of L generated by

    s_ij=(ad e_i)^(1-a_ij) e_j,  i!=j.

The full book conjecture says the analogous positive and negative Serre relations generate tau, for every GCM A. Its positive part is I_alpha=K_alpha for every positive multidegree alpha. A discrepancy in one exact-Q multidegree would refute it; agreement through height H proves only that bounded statement. Symmetrizable matrices are already covered by Gabber–Kac. We make no claim of novelty for the basic triangular construction or known symmetrizable theorem.

## 2. Exact lowering operations

Use D_i=[f_i, .]. At height one, D_i(e_j)=-delta_ij h_i. At height at least two the result is in the positive free Lie algebra of degree alpha-epsilon_i, or zero. Extend by the derivation identity

    D_i([u,v])=[D_i(u),v]+[u,D_i(v)].

When a leaf produces -h_i, use

    [-h_i,v]=-(sum_j a_ij deg_j(v))v,
    [u,-h_i]=+(sum_j a_ij deg_j(u))u.

The row index is i: interchanging a_ij and a_ji would change the computation for a nonsymmetrizable matrix. The producer implements this recursive rule.

There is an independent tensor-word formula used by the checker. Expand a positive Lie element x of height at least two in the free associative algebra as x=sum_w b_w w. The enveloping-algebra commutator [f_i,w] inserts -h_i at each occurrence of e_i. Commuting h_i to the right gives

    D_i(x) = -sum_w b_w sum_(p:w_p=i)
                 (sum_(q>p) a_(i,w_q)) (w with position p deleted).

The rightmost-h_i coefficient is -sum_w b_w sum_(p:w_p=i) (w with p deleted). It vanishes on every Lie element of height at least two: it is the associative derivation with e_i mapped to 1 and the other generators mapped to 0, which annihilates every nontrivial Lie bracket. The checker verifies this cancellation explicitly. Thus the word-deletion formula is not an approximation to the recursive lowering rule; it is a separately derived exact formula.

## 3. Proposition: the radical recurrence computes the maximal ideal

Set K_epsilon_i=0. For |alpha|>=2 define recursively

    K_alpha={x in L_alpha : D_i(x) belongs to K_(alpha-epsilon_i) for every i}.

An absent or negative multidegree means the zero space. Then these spaces equal tau intersect L_alpha in every positive degree.

**Proof.** First let K+=direct-sum_alpha K_alpha. By definition it is stable under each f_i; at height one it has no vectors, so lowering never produces a nonzero Cartan component. It is stable under h because each homogeneous component is a weight space.

To show stability under e_j, use induction on the height of x in K_alpha. For every i,

    D_i([e_j,x])=[e_j,D_i(x)]-delta_ij alpha(h_i)x.

The first term lies in K_(alpha+epsilon_j-epsilon_i) by induction applied to the lower-height element D_i(x); the second lies there as well when i=j. Hence [e_j,x] satisfies the defining recurrence for K_(alpha+epsilon_j). The base case has K_epsilon_i=0. Thus K+ is an ideal of g_tilde(A), contained entirely in the positive part. It meets h trivially, so K+ is contained in tau.

Conversely, tau intersect L_epsilon_i=0, because otherwise its bracket with f_i contains the nonzero h_i. For x in tau intersect L_alpha of greater height, each D_i(x) belongs to tau in a lower positive degree. Induction and the recurrence imply x in K_alpha. This proves equality. QED.

This proof uses the ordinary characteristic-zero contragredient construction with full Cartan realization. It does not identify a positive-characteristic group kernel with a Lie ideal. Finite-field computations in this folder are separate rank diagnostics.

## 4. Proposition: the recursive Serre span is exactly I

Let S_alpha be the span of original Serre generators of degree alpha. Then

    I_alpha = S_alpha + sum_i [e_i, I_(alpha-epsilon_i)].

**Proof.** The right-hand recursive family contains the prescribed generators and is stable under bracket with every e_i. Repeated Jacobi identities imply stability under bracket with every element of the Lie algebra generated by the e_i, namely L. It is therefore an ideal containing all s_ij, and contains I by minimality. The reverse containment follows by induction, since every displayed generator/bracket belongs to I. QED.

Moreover I is contained in K+. For r>=1, direct induction from Section 2 gives

    D_i((ad e_i)^r e_j)
      = -r(a_ij+r-1)(ad e_i)^(r-1)e_j.

At r=1-a_ij this is zero. Lowering by f_j gives (ad e_i)^r(-h_j): for r>=2 it is zero after the second commutator; for r=1 we have a_ij=0 and hence a_ji=0, so it is also zero. Lowering by any other f_k is zero. Thus each Serre generator is killed by every lowering operator and belongs to K at its height. Since K+ is an ideal, I is contained in K+.

## 5. Finite algorithm and independent certificate

For each multidegree through a fixed height H, enumerate the standard Lyndon words over the ordered alphabet e_1<e_2<e_3. Bracket a Lyndon word w by splitting off its longest proper Lyndon suffix v and recursively taking [b(u),b(v)]. In its associative expansion the lexicographically least word is w with coefficient 1. This triangularity allows exact conversion from Lie polynomials to Lyndon coordinates.

The number of such words in multidegree alpha is the multigraded Witt dimension

    dim L_alpha = (1/|alpha|) sum_(d | gcd(alpha)) mu(d)
                  (|alpha|/d)! / product_i (alpha_i/d)!.

This identity also follows from the PBW product formula for the enveloping algebra of a free Lie algebra, whose tensor-algebra Hilbert series is 1/(1-sum t_i), by taking logarithms and applying Möbius inversion. The monic triangular expansions give linear independence; the dimension identity gives completeness. These standard free-Lie facts justify the basis, and the checker verifies the dimension identity and triangular expansions in every computed degree.

The producer constructs I_alpha from Section 4. It constructs the map

    L_alpha -> direct-sum_i L_(alpha-epsilon_i)/K_(alpha-epsilon_i),
    x |-> (D_i x)_i,

and obtains K_alpha as its exact kernel by Section 3. It records spanning rows for both ideals, free basis words, and dimensions. Height-one kernels are explicitly zero; they are not inferred by wrongly treating Cartan elements as zero.

The independent script `work/check_certificate.py` imports neither the producer nor its library. It computes lowerings by the associative-word deletion formula of Section 2, then independently converts the result to its monic Lyndon basis for efficient exact elimination. It verifies **zero-defect equality certificates** and explicitly refuses a claimed nonzero defect, which would require a separate witness audit. For every degree it checks:

1. all and only the expected Lyndon words occur; their count is the Witt dimension;
2. certificate ideal rows are independent and span exactly the original Serre generators and brackets with preceding verified ideal rows;
3. certificate radical rows span that same ideal, as asserted by a zero-defect certificate;
4. the induced lowering map on a complementary basis of L_alpha/I_alpha is injective, using an independent exact rational rank computation modulo the previously verified I components;
5. all reported dimensions agree, and no degree with nonzero free-Lie dimension is omitted or duplicated.

The injectivity test proves K_alpha=I_alpha by induction: Section 4 already proves lowering-stability of I, and lower-degree equality identifies the map's targets with the actual maximal quotients. Therefore its kernel is precisely K_alpha/I_alpha. Checking only quotient columns omits no possible extra relation; it avoids rechecking a large ideal whose inclusion was proved algebraically. The simple-root case remains explicitly separate.

Before checking any algebra, the verifier binds the certificate to an independently requested height and matrix-name set, comparing the matrix entries against `work/matrices.json`. Six negative controls change the field, omit a degree, alter a dimension, omit a whole matrix, alter a matrix entry, and change the height. The checker must reject all six. These scope checks repair the first independent referee's finding. The earlier unbound, full tensor-elimination checker is preserved only as `evidence/check_certificate-reviewed.py`; its incomplete runs do not count as acceptance. Source hashes, certificate hashes, and actual run results are recorded in evidence/.

Every coefficient in these finite matrices is rational. Tensoring finite-dimensional spaces and exact sequences with C is exact; therefore verified Q dimensions agree with those for the book's characteristic-zero C realization. A modular rank match by itself would not suffice: ranks may jump at primes, and a modular kernel is not a counterexample over C.

    a_12 a_23 a_31 = a_21 a_32 a_13.

## 7. An all-degree coefficient-one boundary theorem

**Theorem.** Let J be a set of indices and i an additional index. Suppose the principal GCM A_J is symmetrizable. For every positive degree alpha supported on J union {i} with alpha_i=1, the Serre ideal and maximal Cartan-disjoint ideal agree: I_alpha=K_alpha.

This is a consequence of established symmetrizable theory, not a claim to solve the nonsymmetrizable conjecture. It is proved uniformly in all remaining coefficients of alpha and all allowed entries of A.

**Proof by symmetrizable comparison.** We may restrict to the supported principal submatrix: free positive degrees, Serre spans, and lowerings with indices in the support are unchanged, while outside lowerings are zero.

Choose positive integers d_j symmetrizing A_J. Such an integer choice exists after clearing denominators of a positive rational symmetrizer. On J union {i}, construct a GCM B by retaining every row indexed by J and putting

    b_ij=d_j a_ji for j in J,   b_ii=2.

Then diag((d_j)_(j in J),1) symmetrizes B. Its entries are integers with the required signs and symmetric zero pattern.

Let S(A) and S(B) denote the Serre quotients, not the maximal-ideal quotients. Their positive spaces with coefficient of i equal to one are canonically identical. Indeed, all Serre generators whose leading index lies in J are unchanged. If a_ij<0, a Serre generator with leading index i contains at least two occurrences of e_i, so it and all positive descendants cannot contribute to an i-coefficient-one component. If a_ij=0, then a_ji=b_ij=0 and the commutation relation [e_i,e_j]=0 is unchanged. This proves equality of the defining positive ideal components in the entire slice, not just in a truncated degree.

Under this identification, each lowering D_j with j in J is unchanged, because its formula depends only on row j of the matrix. Let W be the image in this slice of the extra ideal tau(A)/I(A). It is graded, stable under all D_j with j in J, and its component at epsilon_i is zero.

By the Gabber–Kac theorem for the symmetrizable B, S(B)=g(B). Its invariant symmetric bilinear form gives a nondegenerate pairing between each nonzero root space g(B)_alpha and its opposite g(B)_(-alpha). For x in W_alpha, every element of the opposite root space is a linear combination of iterated brackets

    ad(f_j1) ... ad(f_jm) f_i,   all j_k in J,

with exactly alpha_j occurrences of each index j in J. This spanning statement follows from Jacobi: a negative bracket word containing exactly one f_i can be rewritten with that f_i innermost and all other factors acting on it.

Move these adjoint operators across the invariant pairing. Up to a sign, the result is a pairing of f_i with a composition of J-lowerings applied to x. All intermediate vectors stay in W, and the final degree is epsilon_i, whose W component is zero. Hence x pairs to zero with the whole opposite root space. Nondegeneracy gives x=0. Thus W=0 in the slice, proving I_alpha=K_alpha. QED.

**Rank-three corollary.** Every rank-two GCM is symmetrizable: for [[2,-p],[-r,2]] with p,r>0, diag(r,p) is a symmetrizer; the disconnected case is immediate. Therefore for every rank-three GCM, equality holds at every degree having a zero or one coordinate. Any additional positive relation must have all three coordinates at least two. This argument alone excludes the entire height-at-most-five range and leaves (2,2,2) at height six as the first candidate. Section 9 separately excludes that candidate by an exact polynomial certificate. Neither argument settles all interior degrees.

The proof does not assume that a singular rank-two Cartan matrix has independent rows. If using the alternative lowest-weight-module proof, a full Levi Cartan realization must be chosen inside the ambient full realization. The comparison proof above avoids that extra representation-theoretic bookkeeping.

## 8. Necessary condition on a first extra relation

Suppose the extra ideal tau/I is nonzero, and choose a nonzero homogeneous element x of least positive height. Then every D_i x is zero, by minimality. In the Serre quotient the adjoint actions of e_i and f_i are locally nilpotent: this holds on each Chevalley/Cartan generator by the Serre relations and the sl2 relations, and hence on every finite bracket by the iterated derivation rule. The sl2-submodule generated by the lowest-weight vector x is therefore finite-dimensional. Its lowest h_i-weight is a nonpositive integer. Consequently

    sum_j a_ij alpha_j <= 0 for every i.

For a rank-three counterexample, combine this with Section 7: a first extra relation can only occur at an interior degree alpha_j>=2 in the fundamental imaginary cone A alpha<=0. This is a necessary condition, not an existence result. The computed examples include such interior degrees rather than testing only the already-excluded boundary.

## 9. Computer-assisted theorem: every rank-three GCM agrees through height six

**Theorem.** For every rank-three GCM in characteristic zero, I_alpha=K_alpha for every positive alpha of total height at most six. In particular no additional defining relation can first occur below height seven in rank three. This is a bounded-height theorem for an unbounded family of matrices, not an all-height theorem for any nonsymmetrizable matrix. Its novelty in the literature has not been established.

**Proof.** Section 7 handles every degree with a coordinate zero or one. The only remaining positive degree of height at most six is alpha=(2,2,2). If some off-diagonal pair is zero, the underlying graph on three vertices is a forest and A is symmetrizable: choose positive diagonal ratios along its edges, independently on components. Gabber–Kac then applies.

Assume all six off-diagonal entries are strictly negative. Order their magnitudes by the edges

    (1,2), (1,3), (2,1), (2,3), (3,1), (3,2).

For each magnitude, either it is 1 or it is 2+x with x a nonnegative integer. These choices partition every such GCM into 64 cells. A mask bit equal to one denotes the fixed magnitude 1; a zero bit denotes 2+x. There is no upper bound on x.

In all degrees beta componentwise at most (2,2,2), the positive Serre ideal depends only on the mask, not on the free parameters. A relation for magnitude 1 is (ad e_i)^2 e_j and can contribute; for magnitude at least 2 it contains at least three e_i and cannot contribute in these degrees or to them by positive ideal generation. The ideal projections are therefore fixed rational linear maps within each cell.

The joint lowering from L_222/I_222 to the three immediate lower quotients has an affine-linear rational matrix M(x): one application of ad(f_i) is linear in the Cartan entries, and the source/target ideal projections are constant. The lower degrees (1,2,2), (2,1,2), (2,2,1) are coefficient-one degrees, so Section 7 already identifies their Serre and maximal quotients. Consequently injectivity of M(x) proves I_222=K_222.

The certificate supplies a maximal square minor and its exact determinant polynomial for each of 16 representative masks:

    0,2,3,5,6,7,10,11,15,22,23,25,27,30,31,63.

For each representative, after a recorded overall sign, every nonzero coefficient of its determinant is positive rational and its constant coefficient is strictly positive. Hence the determinant is positive whenever all free parameters are nonnegative. Each selected minor is therefore nonzero, proving injectivity on that entire parameter cell, not merely at sampled matrices.

`evidence/symbolic-222/permutation-cover.json` supplies, for **each of all 64 masks**, a permutation of the three indices taking it to one of these representative cells. The independent checker verifies every permutation and mask transformation and rejects an omitted cell. Simultaneously permuting e_i, f_i, h_i and the rows/columns of A preserves both ideal constructions, and the degree (2,2,2) is fixed by every such permutation. Therefore the representative certificates cover every cell.

The independent verification is `work/check_interior_cells.py`, with receipt `evidence/symbolic-222/all-cells-independent-check.json`. It does not import the producer or SymPy. It reconstructs the exact Serre ideal spaces and the lowering matrices using the independently derived tensor-word formula, checks every saved affine entry, clears denominators, and computes each determinant by explicit fraction-free Bareiss elimination over FLINT's integer multivariate polynomial ring. Every division is checked to have zero remainder, and the full polynomial coefficient list is compared exactly. Sixteen altered determinant coefficients and an omitted coverage cell are rejected. The receipt binds every representative certificate and the coverage file by SHA-256.

For example, mask 0 (all magnitudes at least 2) has a 14-by-14 minor. Its sign-normalized determinant has 3,699 positive terms, constant 286654464, and total degree 14; the separate `independent-check.json` in that directory verifies it. The other representatives have smaller minors. The proof depends on the full checked identities, not this example or floating-point evaluations. This establishes injectivity in every cell, completing the theorem. QED.

**Failed route preserved.** The first lexicographic minor was not coefficientwise positive in six masks (1,17,21,33,37,49). This is not a counterexample to injectivity. The direct 64-positive-minor checker correctly rejected that incomplete argument. The final proof does not suppress those failures: it replaces their minors by explicitly verified index permutations to successful representatives, with complete coverage still required. The initial reporting exception and the direct-check rejection are retained in evidence.

## 10. A finite-height certificate controls an infinite coefficient band

**Theorem.** Fix an index i, let J be its complement, and suppose B=A_J is invertible over Q with B^(-1) entrywise nonnegative. Put

    v=-B^(-1) a_Ji >=0,    C_i=1+sum_(j in J) v_j.

Suppose I_alpha=K_alpha is known for every positive degree of total height at most H. If k>=0 is an integer satisfying floor(C_i k)<=H, then equality holds at **every** positive degree with alpha_i<=k, with no total-height bound.

**Proof.** Let R=ker(S(A) to g(A)), the extra ideal in the full Serre quotient; use its positive homogeneous components. If there were a defect in this band, choose a nonzero homogeneous x in R with least total height **within the band**. No global minimality outside the band is assumed. All simple lowerings decrease height and remain in the band, and the extra ideal has no simple-root or Cartan component. Thus every lowering kills x. By the integrable sl2 argument of Section 8, A alpha<=0.

The rows indexed by J give B alpha_J<=-a_Ji alpha_i. Multiplying by the entrywise nonnegative inverse preserves the coordinatewise inequality, yielding alpha_J<=v alpha_i. Consequently the integer height is at most floor(C_i alpha_i)<=floor(C_i k)<=H, contradicting the verified lower-height equality. QED.

This is a proved transfer from a finite certificate, not extrapolation from samples. The inverse-nonnegative hypothesis is essential to this argument; a symmetrizable but indefinite deletion need not have it.

For A_one, direct rational inversion gives:

| omitted index i | coordinates v on its ordered complement | C_i |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | (4/3,5/3) on (2,3) | 4 |
| 2 | (3/2,2) on (1,3) | 9/2 |
| 3 | (1,1) on (1,2) | 3 |

The already accepted height-12 certificate therefore gives equality throughout the union of bands alpha_1<=3, alpha_2<=2, or alpha_3<=4. An accepted height-13 extension strengthens the middle band to alpha_2<=3, because floor((9/2)*3)=13. Exact matrix data and conditional bounds are recorded by `work/round2_band_bounds.py`; the height assumption must be supplied by its separate accepted Lie-algebra certificate.

## 11. A purely algebraic coefficient-two theorem for an unbounded family

**Theorem.** For every pair of integers r,s>=1, let

    A(r,s) = [[2,-1,-1],[-1,2,-1],[-r,-s,2]].

Then I_alpha=K_alpha at every positive degree with alpha_3<=2. The family is nonsymmetrizable when r!=s, by the directed cycle-product criterion. Its first two rows and third column are fixed; this is not a theorem for every matrix merely having an A2 deletion.

There are two proofs. First, deleting node 3 gives B=[[2,-1],[-1,2]], v=(1,1), C_3=3. The uniform height-six certificate of Section 9 and the band theorem with k=2 prove the result. The following independent proof does **not** use that computation.

**Purely algebraic proof.** Let S(A) be the Serre quotient, graded by the number of occurrences of e_3, and write S_k for its positive level-k slice. The Levi on nodes 1,2 is sl3. Set v=e_3. The level-one module V=S_1 is cyclic integrable lowest-weight of Levi weight (-1,-1): f_1 v=f_2 v=0, the two Cartan weights are -1, and integrability follows from the Serre relations. The classical irreducibility theorem for integrable highest/lowest-weight modules is used only for this **symmetrizable Levi**; it is explicitly stated in Heckenberger–Kolb, arXiv:math/0605460, Section 2.1, text lines 103–115. Therefore V is the adjoint sl3 module, with v identified with E_31.

Before imposing Serre generators whose leading index is 3, the positive presentation is n_J^+ semidirect the free Lie algebra on V. Indeed, the cyclic n_J^+-module generated by v has exactly the relations (ad e_j)^(1-a_j3)v=0 for j=1,2. Map the two presentations to each other on their named generators; the cyclic-module relations and freeness extend the maps, and both compositions fix every generator. At level two the free Lie algebra on V is Lambda^2 V. The only remaining level-two Serre generators occur when r=1 or s=1, and are represented by v wedge E_1 v and v wedge E_2 v, respectively. Their positive Levi spans are full Levi submodules: they are Cartan weight vectors killed by both Levi lowerings, and reordering Levi generators moves negative generators to the right. Hence

    S_2 = Lambda^2 V /
          (U(sl3)(v wedge E_1 v) if r=1
           + U(sl3)(v wedge E_2 v) if s=1).

In the adjoint matrix model of V, let the Levi generators act by E_12,E_23,E_21,E_32 and the diagonal matrices H_1=E_11-E_22, H_2=E_22-E_33. Write E_j=ad(e_j) and F_j=ad(f_j) for these actions. Define the actual positive-slice vectors

    u=E_1 v,   w=E_2 v,   p=E_1 E_2 v,   q=E_2 E_1 v.

Under V isomorphic to sl3, their images are -E_32, E_21, H_1, -H_2, respectively. These are matrix-model identifications, not equalities of p or q with Cartan elements inside S(A).

The Levi lowerings on (v,u,w,p,q) are

    F_1: (0,v,0,2w,w),    F_2: (0,0,v,u,2u).

Thus t_1=v wedge u, t_2=v wedge w, and

    z=v wedge(p-q)-u wedge w

are nonzero common lowest vectors of weights (0,-3), (-3,0), (-1,-1). Their cyclic simple Levi modules have dimensions 10,10,8: they are Sym^3(C^3), its dual, and the adjoint module. For the first dimension/simplicity, the ten degree-three monomials have distinct Cartan weights; lowering any monomial reaches x_3^3 and raising from it reaches every monomial. The dual is simple by annihilator duality. The adjoint is simple because sl3 is simple. The three nonisomorphic simple submodules are disjoint and their dimensions sum to 28=dim Lambda^2(C^8), so they exhaust Lambda^2 V.

The quotient S_2 retains the 8-dimensional z-summand always, the t_1-summand precisely when r>=2, and the t_2-summand precisely when s>=2. Its common Levi-lowering kernel is exactly the surviving displayed lowest lines. Their full multidegrees are (1,0,2), (0,1,2), and (1,1,2), respectively.

Now use the **original** third-row lowering D=ad(f_3), not a symmetrized replacement. Direct derivation gives

    D t_1=2(r-1)u,   D t_2=2(s-1)w,
    D z=(3r+2s-2)p-(2r+3s-2)q.

For clarity, Dv=-h_3, Du=-r e_1, Dw=-s e_2, Dp=-s[e_1,e_2], Dq=r[e_1,e_2], and [ [e_1,e_2],v ]=p-q; these identities yield the displayed formulas by Jacobi. The first two images are nonzero exactly when their summands survive. The last is nonzero for every r,s>=1 since p,q are independent and its two coefficient magnitudes are positive. The images lie in distinct full multidegrees and cannot cancel. Thus D is injective on the common Levi-lowest subspace of S_2.

By Section 7 the extra ideal has zero level-one part. If it had a nonzero level-two part, choose a homogeneous element with least total Levi degree in that part. The Levi lowerings kill it by minimality, and D maps it to the zero extra level-one part. This contradicts the injectivity just proved. Thus the extra level-two part is zero. Level zero is the symmetrizable Levi and level one is Section 7, completing the theorem. QED.

In particular dim S_2=28-10*1_(r=1)-10*1_(s=1), giving 18 for A_one. This proof also exposes the failed generic comparison route: changing the omitted row changes D, and at level two can add/remove a whole quadratic Serre summand. Negative words containing two f_i cannot be detected using only complementary lowerings of a single f_i. The full detailed audit is `evidence/round2-coefficient-two.md`.

**Exact all-level detection criterion.** More generally, fix a node i with symmetrizable complementary Levi J and an integer k>=2. In the Serre quotient put P_k={x in S_k : ad(f_j)x=0 for every j in J}, where S_k is the positive slice of i-coefficient k, and let R_k be the extra-ideal slice. If R_(k-1)=0, then

    R_k=0  if and only if  D_i restricted to P_k is injective.

Indeed, a nonzero R_k has a homogeneous vector of minimum total J-degree, killed by every complementary lowering; D_i sends it to R_(k-1)=0. Conversely, a nonzero homogeneous vector in P_k killed by D_i has all simple lowerings in the Serre ideal, so a free-Lie representative belongs to K by Section 3 and gives a nonzero extra-ideal vector. Homogeneous components can be taken because all the maps are graded. Since R_0=0 for the symmetrizable Levi and R_1=0 by Section 7, proving this injectivity at every k>=2 would prove the full conjecture for that A. The preceding argument proves k=2 for A(r,s), not arbitrary k.

## 12. Exact degree (4,4,5) and the height-thirteen extension for A_one

## 14. Remaining mathematical gap

Agreement at finite height does not imply I=K in all heights. The recurrence constructs an exact search for a first additional relation, but supplies no uniform bound on where a first relation would occur if one exists. A proof of the book conjecture requires an all-height argument for every nonsymmetrizable GCM, not three matrices and not a finite-dimensional truncation. Conversely a modular discrepancy needs exact characteristic-zero confirmation and explicit witness verification before it can be called a counterexample.
