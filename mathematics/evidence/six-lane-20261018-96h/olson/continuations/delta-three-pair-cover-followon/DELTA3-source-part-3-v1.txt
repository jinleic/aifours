80:## 3. Connectivity of the pattern graph (proved here)
81:
82:For 1 <= m with 2m < k, let F_m be the proper nonempty subsets of the k-cycle with exactly m cyclic intervals; join two
83:patterns by an edge when they differ at exactly one run index and both belong to F_m.
84:
85:**Lemma CONNECT.**  The graph F_m is connected.  Every run index i is toggled by at least one edge of F_m.
86:Proof.  A pattern has exactly 2m boundaries between adjacent run indices, lying at distinct block sites of the k-cycle;
87:these boundaries alternate outside-to-inside and inside-to-outside.  Moving a boundary one site into a free adjacent site
88:without colliding with another boundary toggles exactly one run and leaves the interval count m unchanged.
89:We prove that any two such boundary configurations with the alternating labels are linked by these moves, including a
90:pattern and its complement.
91:
92:Put n=2m.  Mark one outside-to-inside boundary as number 1; in cyclic order mark the other boundaries 2,...,n.  Choose
93:integer lifts of their positions satisfying
94:   x_1 < x_2 < ... < x_n < x_1 + k.
95:Labels alternate with the number.  Every pattern can be represented this way, with boundary 1 chosen at any of its
96:outside-to-inside boundaries.  The feasible lifted configurations form
97:   L = {x in Z^n : x_(i+1)-x_i >= 1 for 1<=i<n, x_1+k-x_n >= 1}.
98:A feasible unit change of one coordinate projects to the allowed boundary move above.  We show L is connected.
99:
100:Given x,y in L, z_i := min(x_i,y_i) also lies in L.  For consecutive coordinates this follows from
101:min(x_(i+1),y_(i+1)) >= min(x_i+1,y_i+1) = z_i+1.  For the wrapping inequality, if z_1=x_1, then z_n<=x_n gives
102:z_1+k-z_n>=x_1+k-x_n>=1; the other case is the same.
103:If x != z, put S={i:x_i>z_i}.  A coordinate i in S can be decreased by one whenever its preceding cyclic gap is >1;
104:this preserves the preceding gap, and enlarges the following one.  If that preceding gap equals 1, its predecessor is
105:also in S: writing x_0=x_n-k and z_0=z_n-k, x_i=x_(i-1)+1 and z_i>=z_(i-1)+1 together with x_i>z_i imply
106:x_(i-1)>z_(i-1).  If no coordinate of S were decreasable, following predecessors would force every coordinate into S and
107:every gap to equal 1.  Their sum would give k=n, contrary to 2m<k.  A decreasable coordinate therefore exists; repeated
108:steps reduce sum_i(x_i-z_i) until x=z.  Likewise y can be decreased to z, giving a unit-move path x to y by reversing
109:that second path.  Thus L, and hence F_m, is connected.  For the complement of a pattern choose boundary 1 at one of its
110:old inside-to-outside boundaries; the same argument applies (no claim that the old labels remain at their old sites).

141:
142:**Lemma LIGHT.**  Let n nonnegative weights be arranged on a cycle, with n>=2f, f>=1.  There is an independent set of f
143:vertices (no two cyclically adjacent) of weight at most f/n times the total weight.  The same conclusion holds for a
144:disjoint union of paths with n vertices (use the vertices in their path order).
145:Proof.  The set {0,2,...,2(f-1)} is independent on the n-cycle: its wrapping gap is n-2(f-1)>=2.  Its n cyclic translates
146:all have f vertices and each vertex belongs to exactly f translates.  Their average weight is f/n times the total, so
147:one is no heavier than that.  For a disjoint union of paths, list the paths consecutively and add edges to turn this list
148:into one cycle; independence in the augmented cycle implies independence in the original paths.  []
