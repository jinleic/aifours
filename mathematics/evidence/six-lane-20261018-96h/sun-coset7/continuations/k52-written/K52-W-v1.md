## 2. Elementary finite reduction, multiplier range and actual inversion
**Lemma R0 (restated).** Take N = intersection_j core_G(U_j). Each core has finite index (kernel of a finite coset action),
so N is normal of finite index and contained in every U_j. Each coset is the full preimage of its image in G/N;
all n_j and disjointness are preserved. Thus work in finite G for the rest of the proof. This is not a least-order frame.

For i != j put g_ij = gcd(n_i,n_j), I_ij = [G:U_i cap U_j], alpha_ij = I_ij / lcm(n_i,n_j).
**Lemma A.** alpha_ij is an integer in {1,...,g_ij-1}. *Proof.* Both n_i,n_j divide I_ij (Lagrange/index chain), so their
lcm divides I_ij. For subgroups H,K the multiplication map H x K -> HK has exactly |H cap K| preimages per product,
whence |HK| = |H||K|/|H cap K|. Therefore |U_i U_j|/|G| = I_ij/(n_i n_j) = alpha_ij/g_ij. Disjointness says
h = g_i^(-1)g_j is not in U_i U_j (the coset intersection criterion, obtained by solving g_i u = g_j v).
Thus U_i U_j != G and alpha_ij/g_ij < 1. QED
In particular alpha_(d_j o)=1, and alpha_(d_1 d_2),alpha_(d_j s) are in {1,2,3}.

**Lemma INV (restated with proof).** Put U_j* = g_j U_j g_j^(-1), g_j* = g_j^(-1).
Then g_j* U_j* = (g_j U_j)^(-1) are pairwise disjoint LEFT cosets with the same n_j, so SHAPE and the class are unchanged.
Let alpha* be their actual multiplier matrix. For each i != j,
  alpha_ij + alpha*_ij <= g_ij.
*Proof.* Inversion is a bijection and conjugation preserves subgroup index. With h = g_i^(-1)g_j,
alpha*_ij = [G:U_i cap h U_j h^(-1)]/lcm(n_i,n_j), by conjugating the intersection by g_i^(-1).
The two (U_i,U_j)-double cosets U_i U_j and U_i h U_j are distinct (h not in U_i U_j), hence disjoint.
Their normalised sizes, by the same product-count formula, are alpha_ij/g_ij and alpha*_ij/g_ij. Their sum is at most 1.
No subgroup property of the products is asserted. Double inversion returns the original configuration. QED
