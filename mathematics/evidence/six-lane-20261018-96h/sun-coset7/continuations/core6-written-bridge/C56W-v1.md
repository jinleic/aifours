## 1. Setting, multipliers, markers
Let G be finite, U_1..U_6 with n_i = [G:U_i] = 5 r_i, r_i pairwise coprime, g_iU_i pairwise disjoint. Every pair has
gcd(n_i, n_j) = 5. Put alpha_ij = [G : U_i cap U_j]/lcm(n_i, n_j).
**Lemma MU.** alpha_ij is an integer in {1, 2, 3, 4}. *Proof.* n_i, n_j divide [G : U_i cap U_j], so lcm does; the
multiplication map U_i x U_j -> U_iU_j has fibres of size |U_i cap U_j|, so |U_iU_j|/|G| = alpha_ij/5; disjointness means
g_i^-1 g_j is not in U_iU_j, so alpha_ij/5 < 1. QED
**Markers.** By pairwise coprimality E2, E3, E5 each occur at most once (any of them may coincide). A vertex is **plain** if
its r is coprime to 6 (it may be E5; r = 1 included).

## 2. Two tools (complete proofs)
**Lemma INV.** The cosets (g_iU_i)^-1 = g_i^-1(g_iU_ig_i^-1) are pairwise disjoint left cosets with the same indices: a
Core5(6) configuration Gamma* with the same r_i and markers. Its multipliers satisfy alpha_ij + alpha*_ij <= 5. *Proof.* As in
C5W/KII-W: inversion is a bijection, conjugates have equal index, alpha*_ij/5 = |U_ihU_j|/|G| with h = g_i^-1 g_j not in U_iU_j,
and the distinct double cosets U_iU_j, U_ihU_j are disjoint. QED. In particular **alpha_ij = 4 implies alpha*_ij = 1**, and
alpha_ij >= 3 implies alpha*_ij <= 2.

**Lemma FC (left-fibre count with Sun(2)-Sun(4) only).** Fix an apex v. For j != v put lambda_j = [U_v : U_v cap U_j] =
r_j alpha_vj and t_j = [U_j : U_j cap U_v] = r_v alpha_vj. Call a set S of neighbours **admissible** if every two members have
gcd(lambda) >= 2, every three contain two with gcd >= 3, and every four contain two with gcd >= 4. If w_j >= 0 and
sum_(j in S) w_j <= 1 for every admissible S, then sum_j w_j alpha_vj/5 <= 1 - 1/n_v < 1.
*Proof.* g_jU_j meets exactly t_j left cosets of U_v, none equal to g_vU_v. For a left coset B = bU_v != g_vU_v the cosets
meeting B have pieces p_j(U_j cap U_v) in B (p_j in g_jU_j cap B), which b^-1 turns into pairwise disjoint left cosets in U_v of
subgroups of indices lambda_j. Sun(2) (if gcd(lambda, lambda') = 1 the two subgroups H, K have [U_v : H cap K] >= lambda lambda',
so |HK| >= |U_v|, HK = U_v and all their cosets meet), Sun(3) and Sun(4) (Zhu) applied in U_v to 2, 3, 4 of the pieces show
that the set of j meeting B is admissible. Summing over the n_v - 1 cosets B != g_vU_v counts j exactly t_j times. QED
(A fibre holds at most five pieces; sets of five are constrained only through their subsets; Sun(5) is never used.)

## 3. Local gcds at an apex v
The r's are pairwise coprime and alpha <= 4, so two labels share only the primes 2 and 3:
gcd(lambda_j, lambda_k) = 2^(min v_2) 3^(min v_3) with v_2(lambda_j) = v_2(r_j) + v_2(alpha_vj), v_3(lambda_j) = v_3(r_j) + [alpha_vj = 3].
(E5 never matters: no alpha has the factor 5.) For a plain neighbour: v_2 = v_2(alpha) in {0, 1, 2}, v_3 = [alpha = 3]. Hence the
plain neighbours split into three mutually incompatible groups:
- **G1** (alpha = 1): isolated (gcd 1 with every other label);
- **G3** (alpha = 3): pairwise gcd 3, so an admissible set holds at most three of them (Sun(4) clause);
- **GE** (alpha in {2, 4}): pairwise gcd 2, except gcd 4 between two 4s. An admissible subset of GE contains at most one
  2 together with any 4s, or exactly two 2s and no 4 (every three members need a 4-4 pair).
Standard weights: 1 on each G1 member (value 1/5 each); on G3, 1/m on each of its m members if m <= 3 (value 3/5) and 1/3
each if m >= 4 (value m/5); on GE (n_2 twos, n_4 fours): **E(a)** weight 1 on one member of largest mass (value >= 2/5, and
4/5 if n_4 >= 1); **E(b)** if n_4 >= 1: 1/n_4 on each 4, 0 on 2s (value 4/5); **E(c)** if n_2 >= 2 and n_4 = 0: 1/2 on each 2
(value n_2/5); **E(d)** if n_2 >= 2 and n_4 >= 1: 1/2 on each 2 and 1/(2 n_4) on each 4 (value n_2/5 + 2/5; feasible because a
set with two 2s has no 4 and a set with at most one 2 carries at most 1/2 + 1/2).

## 4. Lemma P6 (five plain neighbours)
**Lemma P6.** If all five neighbours of v are plain, then either FC fails, or the multipliers at v are, as a multiset,
{4,4,4,4,4}, {2,4,4,4,4} or {2,2,4,4,4}; in particular at least three of them equal 4.
*Proof.* Let n_1, n_2, n_3, n_4 count the multipliers (sum 5); every admissible set lies in one group.
- n_3 >= 4: G3 gives n_3/5; with n_3 = 5 this is 1, and with n_3 = 4 the fifth neighbour adds >= 1/5. Value >= 1.
- 1 <= n_3 <= 3: G3 gives 3/5; the other 5 - n_3 >= 2 neighbours add >= 2/5 (two G1 members, or E(a) on GE). Value >= 1.
- n_3 = 0, n_4 = 0: n_1/5 + (2/5 if n_2 = 1; n_2/5 by E(c) if n_2 >= 2) >= (n_1 + n_2)/5 = 1.
- n_3 = 0, n_4 >= 1, n_1 >= 1: E(b) gives 4/5, plus >= 1/5.
- n_3 = 0, n_1 = 0, n_4 <= 2: then n_2 = 5 - n_4 >= 3 and E(d) gives (n_2 + 2)/5 >= 1.
Every other case has n_1 = n_3 = 0 and n_4 >= 3, i.e. the three listed multisets. QED

## 5. Lemma A6 (the E2 apex when E2 != E3)
**Lemma A6.** Suppose E2 =: a and E3 =: b exist with a != b; the other four vertices form the plain set P. Then either FC at a
fails, or with beta := alpha_ab and the multiset M of alpha_ap (p in P), one of the following holds:
(S-i) beta in {1, 2} and M in {{1,3,3,3}, {3,3,3,3}}; (S-ii) beta = 2 and M in {{4,4,4,4}, {2,4,4,4}};
(S-iii) beta = 4 and M in {{4,4,4,4}, {2,4,4,4}, {2,2,4,4}}.
*Proof.* lambda_b = r_b beta has v_2 = v_2(beta) (r_b odd) and v_3 >= 1. So b has gcd 3 with every G3 member, gcd
2^min(v_2 beta, v_2 alpha) with GE members (compatible iff beta is even; for beta = 2 the gcd is 2, for beta = 4 it is 2 with 2s and 4
with 4s), and gcd 1 with G1 members. Let n_1..n_4 count M (sum 4).
- **n_3 in {1, 2}, or n_3 = 3 with an even entry:** weight 1 on one G3 member p* (compatible only with b and G3, which get 0),
  standard weights elsewhere (G1: 1; GE by E(a)). The non-G3 entries add >= 2/5 (some GE member, or n_1 = 4 - n_3 >= 2). Value
  >= 3/5 + 2/5 = 1.
- **n_3 = 3 and n_1 = 1, or n_3 = 4:** {b} cup G3 is pairwise gcd 3, so an admissible set holds at most three of them; weights
  1/3 on b and on each G3 member, 1 on the G1 member if any. Value beta/15 + n_3/5 + n_1/5 = (beta + 12)/15, which is >= 1 iff
  beta >= 3. For beta in {1, 2} this is (S-i).
- **n_3 = 0, beta odd:** b is isolated: weight 1 (value beta/5 >= 1/5). The four plain entries give, by section 3, at least
  4/5: n_4 >= 1 by E(b) (plus G1); n_4 = 0 gives n_1/5 + (2/5 if n_2 = 1, n_2/5 if n_2 >= 2) >= 4/5. Value >= 1.
- **n_3 = 0, beta = 2:** b has gcd 2 with every GE member and is incompatible with G1, i.e. b behaves exactly as a plain
  neighbour with multiplier 2. The five items {b} cup P form a row as in Lemma P6 with n_2 + 1 twos; Lemma P6's proof applies
  verbatim, leaving only n_1 = 0 and (n_2 + 1, n_4) in {(1, 4), (2, 3)}: (S-ii).
- **n_3 = 0, beta = 4:** likewise b behaves as a plain neighbour with multiplier 4 (gcd 2 with 2s, 4 with 4s); Lemma P6
  leaves n_1 = 0 and (n_2, n_4 + 1) in {(0, 5), (1, 4), (2, 3)}: (S-iii). QED

## 6. Theorem C56W
**Theorem C56W [CANDIDATE_UNVERIFIED].** Core5(6) holds: no finite group has six pairwise disjoint left cosets with indices
5 r_i and r_i pairwise coprime; every placement of E2 (any 2-power), E3 and E5, r_i = 1 and all cofactors are included.
*Proof.* Let Gamma be such a configuration and Gamma* its inverse (Lemma INV).
- **Case 1: E2 and E3 are not two distinct vertices.** Let v = E2 if it exists, else v = E3 if it exists, else any vertex;
  every neighbour of v is plain. By Lemma P6 for Gamma, at least three alpha_vj equal 4; then those alpha*_vj equal 1, so the
  row of v in Gamma* has at least three multipliers 1, which none of the multisets of Lemma P6 has. Lemma P6 for Gamma*
  gives a contradiction.
- **Case 2: E2 = a and E3 = b with a != b.** By Lemma A6, Gamma is in (S-i), (S-ii) or (S-iii) at a, and so is Gamma*.
  If Gamma is in (S-i), at least three alpha_ap equal 3, so at least three alpha*_ap are <= 2; then M* has at most one entry
  >= 3, whereas (S-i) needs three 3s and (S-ii), (S-iii) need at least two 4s. If Gamma is in (S-ii) or (S-iii), at least two
  alpha_ap equal 4, so M* has at least two 1s, whereas every allowed multiset has at most one 1. Contradiction. QED

**Remarks.** (1) E5 and the 2-power class of E2 never enter: E2 is always the apex when it exists. (2) Conditional
observation, NOT claimed: the k6 README's R1(6) (lines 31-43) reduces Sun(6) to Core5(6) plus Core(5), Prop 4.5 and Zhu;
