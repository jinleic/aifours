## 4. The Sun(5) reduction R1 and Theorem S5W (following proof-v4 section 2 lines 53-61)
**Lemma R1 (group by group).** Let G be finite and g_1U_1, ..., g_5U_5 pairwise disjoint with all pairwise index gcds <= 4. Then
G contains either four disjoint cosets with all pairwise gcds 3, or three with all pairwise gcds 2, or four of the
Prop 4.5 shape, or a Core(5) configuration (with the same subgroups).
*Proof.* (1) Each gcd lies in {2, 3, 4}: none is 1 by L0.6 with L0.2. (2) If some n_i is odd, every gcd with it is odd, hence 3,
so 3 divides all five indices and every gcd is 3 (a multiple of 3 that is <= 4): any four cosets give the first alternative.
(3) Otherwise all n_i are even; write n_i = 2^(e_i) m_i with m_i odd. Each gcd is 2 or 4, so the m_i are pairwise coprime and
min(e_i, e_j) <= 2; hence at most one e_i >= 3. If two indices have e = 1, together with any third index all three pairwise gcds
equal 2 (second alternative). If exactly one, say n_1, has e = 1, then n_1 = 2r_1 with r_1 = m_1 odd and, for any three others,
n_i = 4r_i with r_i = 2^(e_i - 2) m_i; these four r's are pairwise coprime (odd parts coprime, at most one e_i >= 3): the Prop 4.5
shape. If no index has e = 1, then n_i = 4r_i with r_i = 2^(e_i - 2) m_i pairwise coprime: a Core(5) configuration. QED
**Theorem S5W (CONDITIONAL on H5).** (Statement as in section 1.) *Proof.* Suppose five pairwise disjoint left cosets of
finite-index subgroups of a group G have all pairwise index gcds <= 4. By R0 (section 6) pass to a finite quotient with the
same indices and disjointness. Lemma R1 yields one of four configurations in that finite group: the first contradicts Zhu
Theorem 1.1 at k = 4, the second Zhu at k = 3, the third Proposition 4.5, the fourth H5. QED (Equal subgroups and every
cofactor are allowed throughout; no minimal-order frame is used.)

## 5. The Sun(6) reduction R1(6) and Theorem S6W (following O16/sun-coset6/README.md lines 31-43 with erratum C1)

**Lemma R1(6) (group by group).** Let G be finite and g_1U_1, ..., g_6U_6 pairwise disjoint with all pairwise index gcds <= 5.
Then G contains one of: four disjoint cosets with all pairwise gcds 3; three with all pairwise gcds 2; four of the Prop 4.5
shape; five forming a Core(5) configuration; or the six forming a Core5(6) configuration.
*Proof.* (1) Each gcd is 2, 3, 4 or 5 (none is 1, by L0.6), so each pair shares exactly one prime of {2, 3, 5} and no other.
(2) With A_p = {i : p | n_i}, each of the 15 pairs lies in exactly one A_p, so sum_p C(|A_p|, 2) = 15, and |A_p cap A_q| <= 1 for
p != q. If every |A_p| <= 5, the binomials lie in {0, 1, 3, 6, 10}, and the only three summing to 15 are 6 + 6 + 3 (no pair
sums to 5, so 10 cannot occur): sizes {4, 4, 3}; two 4-sets meeting in at most one point cover >= 7 > 6 points, impossible.
So one prime p divides all six indices. (3) p = 3: every pair shares only 3 and gcd <= 5, so all fifteen pairwise gcds equal 3
(C1); any four cosets give the first alternative. (4) p = 2: all gcds are 2 or 4; with n_i = 2^(e_i) m_i the m_i are pairwise
coprime and at most one e_i >= 3; exactly as in Lemma R1 (3), two indices with e = 1 give the second alternative, exactly one
gives the Prop 4.5 shape with any three others, and none gives a Core(5) configuration on any five of them. (5) p = 5: all
gcds equal 5, so n_i = 5r_i with gcd(r_i, r_j) = 1: a Core5(6) configuration. QED
**Theorem S6W (CONDITIONAL on H5 and H6).** (Statement as in section 1.) *Proof.* Given six pairwise disjoint cosets with all

## 6. R0 (complete)
For a group G and finitely many finite-index subgroups U_i, core_G(U_i) is the kernel of the action of G on G/U_i, so its
index is at most [G:U_i]!. N = intersection of the cores is normal of finite index and contained in every U_i. Every g_iU_i is
the full preimage of its image in G/N, so indices, intersection indices and pairwise disjointness pass to G/N. The quotient
images of equal subgroups are equal; nothing requires the U_i distinct.
