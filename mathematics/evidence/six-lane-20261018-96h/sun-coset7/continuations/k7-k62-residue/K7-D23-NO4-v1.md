## 0. Exact scope frozen before the claims

K7: n_i = 6 r_i, r_i pairwise coprime; r_i = m_i w_i, m_i the {2,3,5}-part, gcd(w_i,30)=1; the w_i are pairwise
coprime. Assume the 2-marker and 3-marker are on **different vertices z and t**:

  r_z = 2^E 5^Bz w_z,   r_t = 3^A 5^Bt w_t,   E,A >= 1, Bz,Bt >= 0.

A 5-marker, if not on z or t, is on one further vertex p and r_p = 5^Bp w_p, Bp >= 1; there is at most one positive
B. All exponents are actual, unbounded exponents. Every vertex other than z has odd r_i; r_z is not divisible by 3.
Let alpha and alpha* be the symmetric alpha matrices of the configuration and its INV inverse.

A row at z is **4-type** if its six entries lie in {1,2,4}, at most one entry is 1, and at least three are 4.

**Claim D23-CENTER (5-marker anywhere).** No such K7 configuration has BOTH z-rows (alpha and alpha*) 4-type.

**Claim D23-MIX (5-marker absent or on z or t).** When all five vertices W := V \ {z,t} are unmarked, no such K7
configuration has a 4-type z-row in EITHER orientation. More precisely each row is either 4-type or 3-type as
specified in section 2, and the 3/4 and 4/3 combinations are excluded in section 3 (the 4/4 combination by CENTER).

The restriction in D23-MIX is mandatory: it is not asserted here for a 5-marker on a third vertex. Neither claim
addresses 2- and 3-markers together on z plus a separate 5-marker y.

## 1. Proof of D23-CENTER (both rows 4-type; a 5-marker may be anywhere)

Write N = V \ {z}, F = {b : alpha_zb=4}, F* = {a : alpha*_za=4}. The row assumptions and INV give disjoint sets of
size at least three in six neighbours, so |F|=|F*|=3 and N=F disjoint-union F*. Write s_a=alpha_za in {1,2} for a in F*,
s*_b=alpha*_zb in {1,2} for b in F. Each row has at most one such 1.

### C1. Cross-triangle facts

For a in F*, b in F put x=alpha_ab, x*=alpha*_ab. In triangle {z,a,b}, all gcds and gamma are 6, ell_z=r_z has no
factor 3, ell_a and ell_b are odd. Thus for values (s_a,4,x),

  tau_min = lcm(4, x/gcd(x,r_z)),
  tau_max = min(s_a x, 4 min(s_a,x), s_a min(4,x)).

The s_a term divides 4. At x=1, tau_min>=4>tau_max (<=2). At x=3, tau_min=12>tau_max (<=6), because 3 does not divide
r_z. At x=5, tau_min=20 if Bz=0, else 4, but any surviving x=5 forces x*=1 by INV; the same triangle test in the
inverse configuration kills x*=1. This also rules out x*=5. Consequently x,x* in {2,4}, not both 4 by INV.
For s_a=1 the table leaves only x=4; for s*_b=1 only x*=4. For surviving x=2 or 4, tau_min=4 (the x term divides 4),
so F7 at apex z gives X_a subset X_b whenever x s_a<=4, where X_j=U_zU_j after normalising g_z=1. The inverse gives
X*_b subset X*_a whenever x* s*_b<=4. All of this allows a 3-marked t or 5-marked p among a,b: their cofactors are
odd, and only 2-divisibility matters for the two compulsory terms s_a and 4.

### C2. VFC facts

Put Y_j=G\X_j and D_j=U_zg_jU_j. D_j subset Y_j, mu(D_j)=alpha*_zj/6 and mu(Y_j)=1-alpha_zj/6, so tight edges
(alpha+alpha*=6) give D_j=Y_j. D_j and Y_j are unions of right cosets of U_z; S_R={j:R subset D_j} is allowed for
local indices d_j=alpha*_zj r_j. Since all r_j (j!=z) are odd and contain only disjoint 3/5 marker powers, while
alpha*_zj in {1,2,4}, gcd(d_j,d_k)=gcd(alpha*_zj,alpha*_zk). Therefore:
- for a in F* and b!=b' in F, {a,b,b'} is forbidden (all pairwise local gcds <=2, Sun(3));
- alpha*_zj=1 forces every S_R through j to be {j} (Sun(2));
- mu(union D_j)<1 (V4), and these statements hold in the inverse with F,F* and alpha,alpha* exchanged.

### C3. Four exhaustive types (only C1-C2 used)

- Both a0 in F* with s_a0=1 and b0 in F with s*_b0=1 exist: C1 forces alpha_a0b0=alpha*_a0b0=4, contradicting INV.
- Only b0 exists: for any a in F*, C1 gives x*=4, x=2 and X_a subset X_b0. Then D_b0 subset Y_b0 subset Y_a=D_a
  (za tight), contradicting the singleton rule for the nonempty D_b0 of measure 1/6. Only a0 exists is the same
  argument in the inverse configuration (which has exactly the same indices).
- Neither exists: all z-edges are tight. If b in F has an incident cross edge x=2, choose that a in F*: C1 gives
  X_a subset X_b, hence D_b=Y_b subset Y_a=D_a; then D_b cap D_b'=empty for every other b' in F, else a right coset
  in the intersection would carry {a,b,b'}, forbidden by C2. If at most one b had no such cross-2 edge, every pair
  among the three b's would contain one with a cross-2 edge, so their D_b (each measure 1/3) would be pairwise
  disjoint, contrary to V4. Thus at least two b have x=4 to every a. Inversion gives at least two a with x*=4 to
  every b. Their crossing gives (x,x*)=(4,4), contrary to INV.

This proves D23-CENTER for all allowed 5-marker placements and arbitrary exponents. R0 gives arbitrary groups.

## 2. Row dichotomy at z when W=V\{z,t} is unmarked (5 absent/on z/on t)

At apex z the five W labels are w_u alpha_zu and the t-label is 3^A5^Bt w_t alpha_zt. This is exactly the
ROW-1-degree transfer accepted in Z2 erratum G2: five unmarked neighbours, one odd {3,5}-smooth marked neighbour,
g=6, LFC(d). The apex's own index is irrelevant to these labels.

ROW-1(a) gives k5 in {0,5}. If k5=5, INV forces alpha*_zu=1 for every u in W; the five inverse labels w_u are
pairwise coprime and coprime to 3^A5^Bt w_t alpha*_zt, so y=1 everywhere contradicts inverse LFC (value at least 6).
Thus k5=0. If some W-entry is 3, ROW-1(b) gives:

  **3-type:** T={u in W:alpha_zu=3} has size at least 3, alpha_zu in {1,2} for u in W\T, alpha_zt in {1,2}.

If no W-entry is 3, its five entries are {1,2,4}; ROW-1(c) gives a 4-type full row. Exactly the same dichotomy holds
for alpha*: the inverse has the same indices and markers. The 3/4 alternatives are disjoint.

## 3. Proof of D23-MIX (exclude the mixed 3/4 combination)

By inversion it suffices to exclude: alpha-row at z 3-type, alpha*-row 4-type. Let T be the 3-type set from section 2.
An alpha*_zj=4 cannot lie in T (3+4>6), while at least three alpha* entries equal 4. Only 6-|T| positions remain, so
|T|=3 and all remaining positions equal 4. Put P=W\T={p1,p2}. Then:

  alpha_za=3, alpha*_za=q_a in {1,2}  (a in T),
  alpha_zp=s_p in {1,2}, alpha*_zp=4  (p in P),
  alpha_zt in {1,2}, alpha*_zt=4.

### M1. For every a in T, p in P: alpha*_za=2 and alpha*_ap=2, and X*_a subset X*_p

Write x=alpha_ap and x*=alpha*_ap. Here a,p are unmarked. For the alpha-triangle {z,a,p}, entries are (3,s_p,x),
so tau_min=lcm(3,s_p,x/gcd(x,r_z)) is at least 3s_p, while

  tau_max=min(s_p x, s_p min(3,x), 3 min(s_p,x)).

x=1 gives tau_max=s_p<3s_p; x=2 gives 2s_p<3s_p. Hence x in {3,4,5}. (We do not silently forbid 5: it may survive
this single triangle if Bz>0.) In the inverse triangle (q_a,4,x*), the C1 table above uses only q_a in {1,2}, odd
ell_a,ell_p and 3 not dividing r_z, and gives x* in {2,4,5}, with x*=4 when q_a=1. But x*=5 would force x=1, already
excluded; x=5 would force x*=1, excluded. Thus x in {3,4}, x* in {2,4}, and INV forces x*=2. Hence q_a cannot be 1:
q_a=2. Now inverse tau_min=4 and F7 at apex z gives X*_a subset X*_p (4 >= 2*2).

### M2. Inverse VFC disjoint masses contradict M1

Normalise the inverse configuration at z. Write Y*_j=G\X*_j, D*_j for its VFC set; by INV and VFC,

  mu(Y*_a)=1-q_a/6=4/6,   mu(D*_a)=alpha_za/6=3/6,
  mu(D*_p)=alpha_zp/6=s_p/6,   D*_p subset Y*_p subset Y*_a  (M1).

The inverse VFC local indices at z are d*_a=3w_a and d*_p=s_p w_p. Their gcd is 1 for s_p in {1,2}. Thus
D*_a cap D*_p=empty: a nonempty intersection is a union of right cosets and would put the forbidden pair {a,p} in
an allowed S-set. It follows that

  mu(D*_p1 cup D*_p2) <= mu(Y*_a)-mu(D*_a)=1/6.

If either s_p=2, its D*_p alone has measure 2/6, contradiction. If both s_p=1, the two local indices w_p1,w_p2 are
coprime, so their D-sets are disjoint and their union has measure 2/6, again a contradiction. The mixed combination
is impossible. The 4/3 combination is its inverse, and 4/4 was excluded by CENTER. This proves D23-MIX.

## 4. Exact consequence and residual obstruction

For distinct 2-marker z and 3-marker t, **both z-rows 4-type are excluded with the 5-marker anywhere**. When the
5-marker is absent or sits on z or t, **every actual configuration must have both z-rows 3-type**:

  alpha_zt, alpha*_zt in {1,2};
  alpha_zu, alpha*_zu in {1,2,3} for u in W;
  |{u in W:alpha_zu=3}| >= 3 and |{u in W:alpha*_zu=3}| >= 3.

Neither claim excludes these double-3 rows. The abstract family in `K7-D23-obstruction-v1.md` is a precise
obstruction to closing this residue by LFC+INV+T/F7 alone; it is not an actual coset construction.

No claim is made here about: the mixed rows with a third 5-marked vertex; double-3 rows in any placement; or shared
2/3 on z with 5 on y!=z. These remain open families. All proofs above use actual exponents and do not cap them.
