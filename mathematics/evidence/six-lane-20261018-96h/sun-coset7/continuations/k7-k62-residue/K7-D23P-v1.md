## 1. Row trichotomy at z (ROW-2 transferred to the marked apex z)

At z the LFC labels are lambda_u = w_u alpha_zu (u in W'), lambda_t = 3^A w_t alpha_zt, lambda_p = 5^B w_p alpha_zp, all with
g_zj = 6. **ROW-2-deg at z:** four neighbour labels have the form w_u alpha with the w_u pairwise coprime and prime to 30;
one label is 3^A w_t alpha, one is 5^B w_p alpha, with w_t, w_p prime to 30 and to every other w; and LFC (d) holds at z.
These are the only facts the proof of ROW-2 reads (it argues with the six neighbour labels and the bound val < 6; the
apex's own index enters only through 1 - 1/n_z). Hence, exactly as `K7-Z2-erratum-v1.md` G2 transfers ROW-1, ROW-2's
conclusions hold for the alpha-row at z, with k5, k3 the numbers of W'-entries equal to 5, resp. 3:
- (a') if k5 >= 1 then k5 = 4 and alpha_zt = 5;
- (b') if k3 >= 1 then k3 >= 2, no W'-entry is 4 or 5, alpha_zt != 4, and if k3 = 2 then alpha_zp = 3 and the two other
  W'-entries are equal and lie in {1,2};
- (c') if every W'-entry lies in {1,2,4}, the z-row is 4-type.
The three cases are exhaustive. The inverted configuration has the same labelled indices and markers (INV (a), E6), so
the same holds for alpha*.

**Case (a') is impossible.** If (a') holds for alpha, INV (b) gives alpha*_zu = 1 (u in W') and alpha*_zt = 1; put
c' := alpha*_zp. At z in the inverted configuration the labels w_u are coprime to all others, lambda*_t = 3^A w_t and
lambda*_p = 5^B c' w_p, with gcd(lambda*_t, lambda*_p) = 3 if c' = 3 and 1 otherwise. If c' = 3: y = 1 on W' and p,
y_t = 0, val = 4 + 3 = 7; otherwise y = 1 on all six, val = 4 + 1 + c' >= 6. Both contradict LFC (d) for the inverted
configuration. For alpha* the same argument uses LFC (d) of the original (alpha** = alpha).

So each z-row is of type (b') or (c'), and (c') = 4-type.

## 2. Proof of Theorem K7-D23P

By D23-CENTER both rows cannot be of type (c'). By inversion (INV (a), E6) it suffices to exclude: **alpha-row of type
(b'), alpha*-row of type (c').** Let T := T_alpha, |T| in {2,3,4}, and O := W' \ T, so alpha_zo in {1,2} for o in O.

**Positions of the alpha*-fours.** The alpha*-row has at least three entries 4, and alpha*_zj = 4 forces alpha_zj <= 2
(INV). For a in T, alpha_za = 3, so alpha*_za <= 3, and as the alpha*-row lies in {1,2,4}, **q_a := alpha*_za in {1,2}**.
Hence the alpha*-fours lie in O cup {t, p}, at positions where alpha <= 2.
- |T| = 4: O is empty and at most two positions remain. Impossible.
- |T| = 3: O = {o}, and the three fours are exactly at o, t, p (so alpha_zo, alpha_zt, alpha_zp lie in {1,2}). Put Q := {o, p}.
- |T| = 2: (b') gives alpha_zp = 3, so p is not a position; O = {o1, o2}, and the fours are exactly at o1, o2, t. Put
  Q := {o1, o2}.
In both remaining cases Q consists of two vertices b with alpha*_zb = 4, s_b := alpha_zb in {1,2}, and r_b odd and
prime to 3 (b unmarked, or b = p with r_p = 5^B w_p).

**M1 (triangles {z, a, b}, a in T, b in Q).** Here g = gamma = 6, ell_z = r_z = 2^E w_z (no factor 3 or 5), ell_a = w_a,
ell_b = r_b.
- alpha-triangle (alpha_za, alpha_zb, alpha_ab) = (3, s_b, x): tau_min = lcm(3/gcd(3, r_b), s_b/gcd(s_b, w_a),
  x/gcd(x, r_z)) = lcm(3, s_b, x/gcd(x, r_z)) >= 3 s_b, and tau_max = min(x s_b, s_b min(3, x), 3 min(s_b, x)). For x in {1,2}:
  tau_max <= x s_b < 3 s_b. For x = 5: x/gcd(5, r_z) = 5, tau_min >= 15 > 3 min(s_b, 5). Hence **x in {3, 4}**.
- alpha*-triangle (alpha*_za, alpha*_zb, alpha*_ab) = (q_a, 4, x*): tau*_min = lcm(q_a, 4, x*/gcd(x*, r_z)) =
  lcm(4, x*/gcd(x*, r_z)) (q_a divides 4; r_b odd; w_a odd), tau*_max = min(x* q_a, 4 min(q_a, x*), q_a min(4, x*)).
  x* = 1: tau*_max <= q_a <= 2 < 4. x* = 3: tau*_min = 12 > 3 q_a. x* = 5: tau*_min = 20 > 5 q_a. x* = 2: tau*_max = 2 q_a,
  so q_a = 2. x* = 4: tau*_min = 4 <= 4 q_a. Hence x* in {2, 4}, and x* = 2 forces q_a = 2.
- INV: x + x* <= 6 with x >= 3 gives **x* = 2, hence q_a = 2**, alpha*_ab = 2, alpha_ab in {3,4}.
- F7 in the inverted configuration at apex z for the ordered pair (a, b): tau*_min g_za = 4 * 6 >= gamma_z alpha*_ab
  alpha*_za = 6 * 2 * 2, so **X*_a subset X*_b**.

**M2 (inverted VFC at z).** Normalise g_z = 1; then the inverted configuration is normalised at z as well
(g*_z = g_z^-1 = 1, U*_z = U_z). With X*_j := U_zU*_j (measure alpha*_zj/6, L0.3), Y*_j := G \ X*_j and
D*_j := U_z g*_j U*_j: VFC (V1) for the inverted configuration gives D*_j subset Y*_j, INV (b) for it with alpha** = alpha
gives mu(D*_j) = alpha_zj/6, and its (V3) local indices are d*_j = alpha_zj r_j. Fix a in T. Then
mu(Y*_a) = 1 - q_a/6 = 4/6 and mu(D*_a) = 3/6. For b in Q, D*_b subset Y*_b subset Y*_a (M1). The local indices
d*_a = 3 w_a and d*_b = s_b r_b are coprime (3 does not divide s_b r_b; the w's are coprime), so D*_a cap D*_b is empty
(a non-empty intersection is left U_z-invariant, hence contains a right coset R with {a, b} subset S*_R, contrary to
Sun(2) through (V3)). Thus both D*_b (b in Q) lie in Y*_a \ D*_a, of measure 1/6. If some s_b = 2, that D*_b alone has
measure 2/6. If both s_b = 1, their local indices r_b, r_b' are coprime, so the two sets are disjoint and their union has
measure 2/6. Either way 2/6 <= 1/6, a contradiction.

Hence the alpha-row cannot be (b') while the alpha*-row is (c'); by inversion the alpha-row cannot be (c') while the
alpha*-row is (b'); with D23-CENTER and section 1, both rows are of type (b'), which is the statement. The argument
holds in every finite group, and R0 (which preserves alpha and alpha*) lifts it to arbitrary groups. QED
