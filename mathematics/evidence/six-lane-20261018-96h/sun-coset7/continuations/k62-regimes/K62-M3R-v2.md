## 4. Proposition W6 (structure of an R1-instance) [NEW; CANDIDATE_UNVERIFIED]
Let (x; a, b) be an R1-instance, in the translated x-configuration at t, and W := <Phi_a, Phi_b>, m := [G : W]. Then:
(a) Phi_tW = X^t_a = X^t_b, W != G, m divides 6, x_ab / 6 <= 1/m, and [Phi_t : Phi_t cap W] = 2m/3, so m is 3 or 6;
(b) **m = 3 is impossible;** (c) m = 6, x_ab = 1 and Phi_aPhi_b = W; G minus X^t_a is the union of the two distinct W-cosets
h_aW and h_bW; h_dPhi_d meets neither of them; a vertex l not in {a, b} meets h_aW only if a = p and [Phi_l : Phi_l cap W] = 5
(likewise for b).
*Proof.* (a) X := Phi_tPhi_a = Phi_tPhi_b is right-invariant under Phi_a and Phi_b, hence under W; as 1 lies in X, W lies in X,
so Phi_tW lies in Phi_tX = X, and X = Phi_tPhi_a lies in Phi_tW. mu(X) = 4/6 < 1, so W != G. m divides n_a and n_b, hence
g_ab = 6. Phi_aPhi_b lies in W: x_ab / 6 <= 1/m. mu(Phi_tW) = [Phi_t : Phi_t cap W] / m = 4/6 (L0.1), so 3 | m.
Common facts: X is a union of 2m/3 left W-cosets and Y := G minus X of m/3. Since t's coset Phi_t and h_aPhi_a are disjoint,
h_a is not in Phi_tPhi_a = X (L0.2), so the W-coset h_aW lies in Y, and h_aPhi_a lies in h_aW; likewise for b. For any vertex l,
h_lPhi_l meets exactly t_l := [Phi_l : Phi_l cap W] <= m left W-cosets, and its piece in such a coset cW is a left coset
y(Phi_l cap W); translated by c^-1 these pieces are left cosets in W of subgroups of index [W : Phi_l cap W] = n_l t_l / m,
pairwise disjoint for distinct l (Sun(k) inside W applies, k <= 6). Since [G : Phi_d cap W] = n_d t_d is divisible by m and
3 does not divide n_d, 3 | t_d.
(b) If m = 3, Y is the single W-coset h_aW = h_bW, and t_d = 3, so h_dPhi_d meets it. The three pieces of a, b, d there have
indices n_a / 3 = 2 ell_a, 2 ell_b and n_d t_d / 3 = n_d in W, with pairwise gcds 2 (ell_a, ell_b odd, coprime, without 3, and coprime
to n_d / 2 = 2^(e_d - 1) 5^(h_d) w_d since at most one vertex carries a 5): Sun(3) inside W fails.
(c) So m = 6 and x_ab <= 1; Phi_aPhi_b lies in W with measure 1/6 = 1/m, hence equals W. If h_aW = h_bW, then h_a^-1 h_b = uv
with u in Phi_a, v in Phi_b, and h_a u = h_b v^-1 lies in both cosets: impossible. So Y = h_aW disjoint-union h_bW. A vertex l
meeting h_aW has its piece there disjoint from h_aPhi_a (index n_a / 6 = ell_a in W), so Sun(2) inside W needs
gcd(ell_a, n_l t_l / 6) >= 2; ell_a is coprime to 6 and to n_l (l != a), so gcd(ell_a, t_l) > 1, which (t_l <= 6, prime factors of
ell_a >= 5) means a = p and t_l = 5. For l = d, t_d is 3 or 6, so d meets neither h_aW nor h_bW. QED

## 5. Proposition R2N (necessary conditions for an R2-instance) [NEW; CANDIDATE_UNVERIFIED]
Let y be an R2-instance of an M3(1,1) configuration, a := number of entries 2 among y_tu (u in O'), so 4 - a entries are 1
(a >= 2 by E3 Lemma 2). Then:
(i) **y_(t i*) = 2.**
(ii) In the LFC fibre system of the y-configuration at t, let E be the number of fibres met by no coset (E >= 1: Phi_t), N1 the
number of fibres meeting a vertex with y_tu = 1, and f_k (k = 1, 2, 3) the number of the remaining fibres with |S_F| = k. Every
such S_F with three elements is {u, i*, d} with y_tu = 2, no S_F has four elements, and **f_3 = f_1 + n_t / 6 + 2E**; hence
**E <= n_t / 12** and at least n_t / 6 + 2E fibres carry {u, i*, d}.
(iii) **9 | T_y'(V minus {t})**, hence (Lemma C) **tau_y'({d, i*, e}) = 3 and y'_(e i*) >= 3 for some e in O'**.
*Proof.* Labels at t: lambda_u = ell_u y_tu, lambda_(i*) = n_(i*) z / 6 = 2 * 5^(h*) w_* z (z := y_(t i*)), lambda_d = 2^(e_d - 1) 5^(h_d) w_d;
odd parts pairwise coprime except possibly a 5 shared by lambda_(i*) (if 5 | z) and a 5-marked O'-vertex or d. LFC (d) at t:
val(c) := sum_u c_u y_tu + 3 c_d + z c_(i*) < 6 (units of 1/6) for every feasible c.
(i) z = 5 forces h_d >= 1 (Lemma A), so no ell_u has 5. Certificates (feasibility on the positive support, by heredity): entries 1
are isolated (their labels are coprime to all other labels); c1 (z in {1, 3}): 1 on entries 1, 1/2 on entries 2, on d and on i*;
the non-isolated positive labels have pairwise gcd 2 (2-parts 2, 2, >= 8 and 2), so no three form an allowed set (Sun(3));
val = 4 + 3/2 + z/2 >= 6. c2 (z in {4, 5}): 1 on entries 1, 1/2 on entries 2 and on i*, 0 on d; pairwise gcds of the non-isolated
positive labels are 2; val = 4 + z/2 >= 6. Both contradict LFC (d), so z = 2.
(ii) With z = 2: lambda_(i*) = 4 * 5^(h*) w_*. Pairwise gcds: two entries 2: 2; entry 2 with i* or d: 2; i* with d: 4. So an allowed
set with >= 3 elements contains i* and d (otherwise three labels with pairwise gcd 2: Sun(3)); {u, u', i*, d} is not allowed
(its subset {u, u', i*}); so S_F has at most three elements, and three only as {u, i*, d}. Counting fibres and incidences
(LFC (a)-(b): t_u = n_t y_tu / 6, t_(i*) = n_t / 3, t_d = n_t / 2):
n_t = E + N1 + f_1 + f_2 + f_3, N1 = (4 - a) n_t / 6, and f_1 + 2f_2 + 3f_3 = a n_t / 3 + n_t / 3 + n_t / 2.
Subtracting twice the first relation (minus E and N1) gives f_3 - f_1 = n_t / 6 + 2E. As every 3-element fibre is met by i*,
f_3 <= t_(i*) = n_t / 3, so 2E <= n_t / 6.
(iii) The light-fibre argument (Lemma RC of K62-CAP-DT-erratum-v1 CD3, re-proved here): after the translation, let W' be the
intersection of the subgroups of the y'-configuration over j != t (all conjugated by the same element), T' := [G : W'] =
T_y'(V minus {t}). If w u (w in W', u in Phi_t) lay in a fibre met by h_jPhi_j, write w = h_j a h_j^-1 with a in Phi_j (W' lies in
the conjugate of j's y'-subgroup, which is h_jPhi_jh_j^-1); then h_j^-1 would lie in Phi_jPhi_t, i.e. h_j in Phi_tPhi_j, so h_jPhi_j
would meet Phi_t: impossible. So the M := [W' : W' cap Phi_t] fibres inside W'Phi_t are empty, and M = [G : W' cap Phi_t] / T' >=
n_t / gcd(n_t, T') (Lagrange). Hence n_t / Gamma_y'(t) <= M <= E <= n_t / 12, so Gamma_y'(t) >= 12, and by Lemma C (a) 9 | T'. QED
