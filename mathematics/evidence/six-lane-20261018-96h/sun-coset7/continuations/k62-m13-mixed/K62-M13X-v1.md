## 6. Triangles, chain steps and the budget [re-proved; MK-v2 P2-P4, BUD, T16, ONE, TRI arguments, attributed]

**P4 (apex identity).** For distinct a, b, c in O: mu(Phi_b(Phi_a cap Phi_c)) = tau_x({a,b,c}) / (6 x_ac). Hence if x_ba = x_bc = 4

Phi_b(Phi_a cap Phi_c) lies in X^b_a and in X^b_c and has their common measure 4/6. QED
*Instances:* type (2,4,4) with tau = 8 at the apex opposite the 2-pair; type (4,4,4) with tau = 16 at every apex.

**P2 (triangles; DG-3 in its G3 form).** For an O-triple R = {a, b, c} and a family x with sorted values x1 <= x2 <= x3:
T_x(R) = 6 ell_a ell_b ell_c tau_x(R), tau_x(R) is a positive integer divisible by each of its three values, tau_x(R) <= x1 x2, and
delta_x(R) = 1 + v2 tau_x(R).
*Proof.* lcm(n_a, n_b, n_c) = 6 ell_a ell_b ell_c (odd, pairwise coprime ell's). T_x(R) is divisible by T_x({a,b}) = 6 ell_a ell_b x_ab, so

apexes. v2(6 ell_a ell_b ell_c) = 1. QED

**P3 (chain step at e != t).** For a non-empty S in O and e in O minus S with e != t: gcd(n_e, T_x(S)) = 6;
T_x(S cup {e}) = lcm(n_e, T_x(S)) k with k = 6 mu(Phi_eW_S) <= x_ej for every j in S (W_S := intersection over S), with **k = x_ej iff

*Proof.* Let ell°_j denote the prime-to-6 part of ell_j (ell°_j = ell_j for j != t; ell°_t = 5^(h_t) w_t). First, the prime-to-6
part of T_x(S) is the product of the ell°_j (j in S): build S one
vertex at a time with t first if t is in S; the start n_j has prime-to-6 part ell°_j; when e' != t is added to S_0, ell_e' is
prime to 6 and to every ell°_j (j in S_0), so gcd(n_e', T_x(S_0)) = 6 (6 divides every n_j) and the multiplier is
6 mu(Phi_e'W_(S_0)) <= x_e'j <= 4 (L0.1, P1), with no prime factor > 3; as T_x(S) depends only on S, the claim holds. Then for
e != t the same computation gives gcd(n_e, T_x(S)) = 6 and k = 6 mu(Phi_eW_S); Phi_eW_S lies in X^e_j (measure x_ej/6), with equality
of measures iff equality of sets. v2 lcm(n_e, T_x(S)) = v2 T_x(S) (v2 n_e = 1). Finally 2^(v2 k) <= k <= m. QED

**BUD.** For each family x: n_4(x) <= 8 - 2 u(x).

**T16.** For each family x, no O-triple has type (4,4,4) with tau_x = 16. Consequently every O-triple has delta_x(R) <= 4, and
delta_x(R) = 4 iff tau_x(R) = 8, which happens only for types (2,4,4) and (4,4,4).

**ONE.** For each family x, at most one O-triple has type (2,4,4) with tau_x = 8; if one exists, then u(x) >= 2 and n_4(x) <= 4.

**TRI (graph lemma).** A graph on five vertices with maximum degree <= 3, no K4 and no two triangles sharing exactly one vertex
has at most two triangles, and two of its triangles share an edge (a diamond).
*Proof.* Two distinct triangles on five vertices share at least one vertex (3 + 3 > 5), hence exactly two. Let {A, B, C}, {A, B, D}
be triangles and suppose a third T exists. If T contains A and B, then T = {A, B, E} and A has degree 4. Otherwise T shares

{A, B, D}, so Y = D, and {A, B, C, D} spans a K4. QED
