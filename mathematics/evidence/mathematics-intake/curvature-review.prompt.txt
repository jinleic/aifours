THEOREM (fixed nested deterministic design; any fixed dimension p):
Let (x_i) be deterministic vectors in R^p with S_n=sum_{i<=n}x_i x_i^T positive definite for all n>=n0. One fixed rho:R->R is C^2 and obeys 0<m<=rho''(r)<=M<infinity everywhere. Y_i=x_i^T beta0+epsilon_i, iid errors with finite variance sigma^2 (mean need not be zero; atoms allowed). Define beta_hat_n as the unique minimizer of F_n(b)=sum rho(Y_i-x_i^T b). The repeated-design experiment uses x_1,...,x_n a second time with an independent iid n-vector of errors; let beta_tilde_2n minimize the combined criterion. If beta_hat_n -> beta0 in probability, then beta_tilde_2n -> beta0 in probability.
Let theta_n minimize E F_n. Claims:
(1) E||beta_hat_n-theta_n||^2 <= M^2 sigma^2 p/(m^2 lambda_min(S_n));
(2) E||beta_tilde_2n-theta_n||^2 <= M^2 sigma^2 p/(2m^2 lambda_min(S_n));
(3) If errors are nondegenerate, original weak consistency forces lambda_min(S_n)->infinity; thus consistency is equivalent to Gram divergence plus theta_n->beta0 for nondegenerate laws in this loss class.
Degenerate errors are included in the replication implication, not in the equivalence of (3).

PROOF:
Put psi=rho'. Curvature bounds imply psi M-Lipschitz with at most linear growth, rho has quadratic lower bound up to an affine term and at most quadratic growth. Full-rank finite design makes F_n and E F_n finite, coercive, strictly convex with unique minimizers. Finite second moments permit differentiating the finite expected sum. Sample minimizers are continuous measurable functions of the response vector by strict convexity/implicit function theorem.

STEP 1 (Gram necessity; only nondegeneracy needed here): If lambda_min(S_n) does not diverge, S_n^{-1} decreases in Loewner order to a nonzero PSD B, since the design is nested. The first n0 x_i span, so some fixed i<=n0 has c=x_i^T B x_i>0. Thus x_i^T S_n^{-1}x_i>=c for every n>=n0. Fix all responses except y_i. The sample minimizer b(y_i) is continuously differentiable because its Hessian H=sum rho''(y_j-x_j^T b)x_j x_j^T obeys m S_n<=H<=M S_n and is invertible. Differentiating sum x_j psi(y_j-x_j^T b)=0 gives db/dy_i=H^{-1}rho''(y_i-x_i^T b)x_i. Therefore g_n(y_i)=x_i^T b(y_i) has g_n'(y_i)>= (m/M)x_i^T S_n^{-1}x_i >=alpha=(m/M)c>0 uniformly in all responses and n.
Nondegeneracy gives a<b with q_-=P(epsilon<=a)>0 and q_+=P(epsilon>=b)>0. Conditional on all other responses, g_n for these two error tails has ranges separated by at least alpha(b-a). An interval of radius eta=alpha(b-a)/4 around x_i^T beta0 cannot intersect both ranges. Hence P(|x_i^T beta_hat_n-x_i^T beta0|>eta | other responses)>=min(q_-,q_+)>0. Independence leaves the conditional error law unchanged. Averaging contradicts weak consistency, since x_i is fixed. This proves Gram divergence.

STEP 2 (concentration): Set Z_n=sum x_i[psi(Y_i-x_i^T theta_n)-E psi(Y_i-x_i^T theta_n)]. Population first-order condition gives grad F_n(theta_n)=-Z_n. For d=beta_hat_n-theta_n, strong monotonicity and grad F_n(beta_hat_n)=0 imply
m d^T S_n d <= d^T Z_n <= ||S_n^{1/2}d|| ||S_n^{-1/2}Z_n||.
So ||S_n^{1/2}d|| <= (1/m)||S_n^{-1/2}Z_n|| (including d=0).
For any deterministic t and iid copies epsilon,epsilon',
Var(psi(epsilon-t))=(1/2)E[psi(epsilon-t)-psi(epsilon'-t)]^2 <= (M^2/2)E(epsilon-epsilon')^2=M^2 sigma^2.
Independence and sum x_i^T S_n^{-1}x_i=p give E||S_n^{-1/2}Z_n||^2<=M^2 sigma^2 p. Since ||d||^2<=lambda_min(S_n)^(-1)d^T S_n d, (1) follows. Expected pooled criterion is 2 E F_n with same theta_n, and pooled Gram=2S_n, giving (2).

STEP 3 (transfer): For nondegenerate law, consistency implies Gram divergence by Step1. By (1), beta_hat_n-theta_n->0 in probability; since theta_n deterministic, theta_n->beta0. Then (2) implies pooled consistency. Converse in (3) follows directly from (1). If sigma^2=0, errors equal one constant almost surely; repeated criterion is exactly twice original at every n, so minimizers agree and replication holds trivially.
