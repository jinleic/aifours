# Erratum 1 to the SEP comparable-fibre packet — Theorem E is not necessary; a two-level criterion

## 1. Withdrawn phrase

§6 of the main packet ends with: "I do not claim that Theorem E is necessary, beyond the right-regular reflection." The phrase **"beyond the right-regular reflection"** is withdrawn. It suggests that Theorem E is necessary for separation in the right-regular reflection of B_q. That is **false**.

**Counterexample.** Let S = {e, f, m}, where e and f form a right-zero band and m is a zero.

- S is a right regular band in V. It is book weakly E-unitary (all elements are idempotent). Its corners are semilattices, so it is an original-class source.
- τ is universal, C is trivial, and B_q ≅ S. Hence β(e) ≠ β(f), and S is its own right-regular reflection.
- Yet every map H into R¹ = Z ∪ {1} that respects M satisfies H(m) = H(m)·H(e) = H(e) and H(m) = H(m)·H(f) = H(f). So **no R¹-valued host separates e from f.**

**Root of the mistake.** Points of a *presented* band are separated by homomorphisms into members of the variety, not necessarily into a generator of the variety.

Theorem E remains **correct as a sufficient criterion**. Its proof is unaffected.

## 2. Two-level criterion (strictly more flexible)

Adjoin a zero: let R¹₀ = Z ∪ {1, 0}. This is right regular, hence in V.

**Theorem E′.** Let e ≠ f be an R-pair in E_c. Suppose there are closed placement sets Ψ₁ ⊆ N with (1,e), (1,f) ∈ N ∖ Ψ₁. Let ≈ be the equivalence on N ∖ Ψ₁ generated by:

- (u, st) ≈ (u·w(s,t), t) whenever (u·w(s,t), t) ∈ N ∖ Ψ₁;
- (u, st) ≈ (u, s) whenever (u·w(s,t), t) ∈ Ψ₁;

in both cases with (u, st) ∈ N ∖ Ψ₁. If (1,e) ≉ (1,f), then β(e) ≠ β(f).

**The host.** Take B = (R¹₀)^G with pointwise product and the translation action, and set

- b_s(x) = 0 if (x⁻¹, s) ∉ N;
- b_s(x) = 1 if (x⁻¹, s) ∈ Ψ₁;
- b_s(x) = [(x⁻¹, s)] otherwise.

**Proof of the host identity.** Put w = w(s,t) and compare b_st(x) with b_s(x)·b_t(w⁻¹x).

1. *(x⁻¹, st) ∉ N.* Since N is closed under composition, one of the factors (x⁻¹, s) or (x⁻¹w, t) lies outside N. So both sides are 0.
2. *(x⁻¹, st) ∈ N.* Since N is closed under factorization, both factors lie in N. Split on where (x⁻¹w, t) lies.
   - *(x⁻¹w, t) ∈ Ψ₁.* The right side is b_s(x). If (x⁻¹, s) ∈ Ψ₁, composition puts (x⁻¹, st) in Ψ₁, so both sides are 1. Otherwise (x⁻¹, s) and (x⁻¹, st) both lie in N ∖ Ψ₁; the latter does, because a placement in Ψ₁ would put its factor (x⁻¹, s) in Ψ₁. The second generating relation of ≈ makes them equivalent, so both sides are equal classes.
   - *(x⁻¹w, t) ∈ N ∖ Ψ₁.* The right side is the class of (x⁻¹w, t). By factorization closure of Ψ₁, (x⁻¹, st) lies in N ∖ Ψ₁, and the first generating relation makes it ≈ (x⁻¹w, t).

In every case the two sides agree, so B is a host. ∎

**Check on the counterexample.** In {e, f, m}, take N = {e, f}, Ψ₁ = ∅ and m ↦ 0. N is closed, the relation ≈ is trivial on {e, f}, and the host separates e from f.

**Remark (general hosts).** Iterating this idea gives right-regular hosts with several semilattice levels: a chain of closed sets with right-zero labels on each layer. A general V-host may also use local structure that is not right regular. So neither E nor E′ is claimed to be necessary for (R).
