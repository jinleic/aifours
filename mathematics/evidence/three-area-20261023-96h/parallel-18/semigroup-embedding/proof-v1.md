## 1. Exact claims and dependencies

Let V be the band variety of locally regular bands: every eBe satisfies

    xyxzx = xyzx.

In a band this local condition is equivalent to the identity

    e z e x e z e y e z e = e z e x e y e z e.       (LR)

The two product readings P and R are exactly those frozen in selection-v1.md and the existing dossier. Write K_P(V) and K_R(V) for semigroups embeddable into the corresponding band-by-completely-simple products with band in V. No equivalence between the two product classes is needed for the new proofs below.

The new written claims are:

**A — Actual product gluing.** Fix either P or R. Suppose every finite nonempty subset F of a semigroup S is contained in a subsemigroup T_F belonging to K_P(V), respectively K_R(V). Then S itself belongs to the same class. The local embeddings, bands, completely simple factors, and actions need not be compatible. The proof explicitly constructs the global band, completely simple factor, action, and injective map.

**B — Finitely inverse-generated obstruction localization.** If a weakly E-unitary locally r-orthodox regular semigroup fails the chosen embedding reading, then, for any fixed choice of a mutual inverse s^dagger for every s, some subsemigroup generated by a finite set under multiplication and this chosen inverse operation also fails that reading. This subsemigroup is regular, inherits the exact book hypotheses, and is at most countable. It need not be finite. In particular a failure cannot exist only as an incompatibility among all countable regular subsemigroups that individually embed.

**C — Infinite extension of the existing pure-quotient theorem.** Let S be regular, weakly E-unitary and locally orthodox. Assume every finite nonempty F is contained in a regular subsemigroup T_F having a nonempty completely simple ideal. Then S has an idempotent-pure surjective homomorphism onto a completely simple semigroup. S itself need not have any completely simple ideal. Its least completely simple congruence exists and is idempotent pure. In particular this holds for a directed union of finite regular subsemigroups.

**D — Necessary purity in arbitrary cardinality.** Every regular S embeddable into either fixed product has an idempotent-pure completely simple quotient. This is the regular infinite version of the dossier's finite necessary condition, not a sufficient embedding theorem.

These claims share one compactness mechanism and are not four independent discoveries. A and B use classical ultrafilter/choice and explicit Rees coordinates only. C additionally imports the existing, written parent-audited Theorem X, at pinned bytes; it does not newly authenticate its proof or claim that purity implies embedding. D uses the elementary image lemma proved below. The local regular-band identity is preserved, not replaced by a weaker band condition.

## 2. Elementary completely-simple image lemma

**Lemma 2.1.** A nonempty regular subsemigroup T of a completely simple semigroup C is completely simple. No finiteness is needed.

**Proof.** Use Rees coordinates C=M[G;I,Lambda;P]. Its local monoid eCe, for any idempotent e, is the group in the H-class of e. First observe that eTe is a subgroup of this group. Indeed, for x in eTe choose a mutual inverse z of x in T. Then eze belongs to eTe and is a mutual inverse of x, because

    x(eze)x = xzx = x,
    (eze)x(eze) = ezxze = eze.

In the ambient group these equalities imply x(eze)=(eze)x=e, so eTe is closed under group inverses and has identity e.

For arbitrary idempotents e,f in T put v=(efe)^(-1), the inverse in the group eTe, and d=vf. Since ev=ve=v and v(efe)=e,

    vfe = v(efe) = e,
    d^2 = vfvf = vfevf = evf = vf = d.             (2.1)

Thus d is an idempotent in T whose ambient Rees row is that of e and whose ambient column is that of f.

Now take t in T and t' in V_T(t). Set e=tt' and f=t't. The idempotent d constructed above is in the same ambient H-class as t, so dt=t=td. Hence t belongs to dTd, which was just proved to be a subgroup. Thus every t lies in its ambient H-class group inside T.

It remains to show simplicity, rather than merely complete regularity. Take arbitrary u,v in T. Their group identities and the cross-row/column construction (2.1) give idempotents a,b in T where a has row(u),column(v), and b has row(v),column(u). Then w=avb belongs to the same ambient H-class as u. Its group inverse w^# belongs to T by the preceding paragraph. Therefore

    u = u w^# w = (u w^# a) v b.

So u belongs to T v T for every u,v. Consequently T is simple. Every idempotent of T is primitive, since a natural-order comparison between idempotents in T is also one in C, where it forces equality. T is completely simple. The singleton case is included. QED.

**Corollary 2.2.** The image of any homomorphism from a nonempty regular semigroup into a completely simple semigroup is completely simple.

The image is regular: images of mutual inverses remain mutual inverses. This is exactly why no finite-image argument is needed here.

**Lemma 2.3.** Every nonempty regular S has a least completely simple congruence xi.

**Proof.** Let R be the set of all congruences rho for which S/rho is completely simple; it contains the universal congruence. Choose a Rees representation for each quotient. The direct product of these quotients is completely simple: explicitly it is the Rees matrix semigroup with group product of their groups, row-set product of their row sets, column-set product of their column sets, and coordinatewise sandwich matrix. Choice ensures the index products are nonempty. The diagonal homomorphism

    S --> product_(rho in R) S/rho

has kernel xi=intersection R. Its image is regular and completely simple by Corollary2.2, so xi itself belongs to R and is the least member. QED.

## 3. Explicit ultraproduct of the actual P targets

Let D be a nonempty index set and U an ultrafilter on D. For each d take nonempty structures

    B_d in V,
    C_d = M[G_d; I_d,Lambda_d; P_d],
    alpha_d: G_d acts on B_d by automorphisms.

All ultraproduct equivalences mean equality on a U-large set. Put

    B = product_U B_d,
    G = product_U G_d,
    I = product_U I_d,
    Lambda = product_U Lambda_d,
    p_([lambda_d],[i_d]) = [p^d_(lambda_d,i_d)].

B is a band: the associative and idempotent identities hold at every coordinate. It lies in V because (LR) is an identity and hence holds coordinatewise; this is a direct argument, not an inference from finitely many evaluations. G is a group with multiplication, identity and inverse defined coordinatewise. Define the action by

    alpha_([g_d])([b_d]) = [alpha^d_(g_d)(b_d)].     (3.1)

Changes of representatives affect values only outside a finite intersection of U-large equality sets, so all definitions are well defined. The identity, action law and preservation of multiplication hold at every coordinate. The inverse automorphism to alpha_[g_d] is alpha_[g_d^(-1)]. Therefore this is an actual group action by band automorphisms.

Let C=M[G;I,Lambda;P]. It is completely simple by the Rees construction, without any finiteness assumption. The map

    [(b_d,(i_d,g_d,lambda_d))]
       |---> ([b_d],([i_d],[g_d],[lambda_d]))        (3.2)

is a bijection from product_U (B_d odot C_d) onto B odot C. Injectivity uses the intersection of the four coordinate equality sets; surjectivity uses representatives in each of the four nonempty sorts. The actual multiplication is preserved because its first coordinate becomes

    [b_d alpha^d_(g_d p^d_(lambda_d,j_d))(c_d)]
      = [b_d] alpha_([g_d] p_([lambda_d],[j_d]))([c_d]),

and the Rees coordinates become

    ([i_d], [g_d] p_([lambda_d],[j_d]) [h_d], [mu_d]).

These are precisely the fixed P formula, including the sandwich factor in the action. It has not been replaced by the ordinary semidirect product formula. This proves closure of the actual P targets under the required many-sorted ultraproduct construction.

## 4. Explicit ultraproduct of the actual R targets

For the fixed R reading, let C_d act on B_d by endomorphisms with the dossier's action law. Define B,C as above and

    phi_([x_d])([b_d]) = [phi^d_(x_d)(b_d)].        (4.1)

It is a well-defined endomorphism action: phi_(xy)=phi_x phi_y holds coordinatewise. For x=(i,g,lambda) in C, let

    epsilon_x = (i,p_(lambda,i)^(-1),lambda),
    x^# = (i,p_(lambda,i)^(-1) g^(-1)
                      p_(lambda,i)^(-1),lambda).

Then x^# is the group inverse of x in its H-class and xx^#=epsilon_x. The fixed restricted carrier is exactly

    R(B,C) = {(b,x): phi_(epsilon_x)(b)=b}.         (4.2)

For clarity, this does not impose an extra H-class-inverse assumption on the dossier's carrier. For any x' in V_C(x), xx' is an idempotent in the same Rees row as epsilon_x. If e,f are idempotents in the same Rees row then ef=f and fe=e. Their action endomorphisms have the same fixed set: phi_e phi_f=phi_f and phi_f phi_e=phi_e imply this directly. Thus being fixed by phi_(xx') is equivalent to (4.2).

A sequence of valid restricted pairs maps under (3.2) to a pair satisfying (4.2). Conversely, if (b=[b_d],x=[x_d]) satisfies (4.2), the set

    A={d: phi^d_(epsilon_(x_d))(b_d)=b_d}

is U-large. At d outside A replace b_d by any point of the image of the idempotent endomorphism phi^d_(epsilon_(x_d)); for example choose c_d in B_d and use phi^d_(epsilon_(x_d))(c_d). This repairs each bad coordinate to a valid restricted pair without changing [b_d]. Consequently (3.2) restricts to a bijection between the ultraproduct of the restricted carriers and (4.2).

Multiplication on both carriers is the fixed completely-simple specialization

    (b,x)(c,y)=(b phi_x(c),xy).

Equation (4.1) makes the restricted bijection multiplicative. This handles the restricted carrier as well as the action, rather than merely gluing ordinary semidirect products. B still satisfies (LR), as in §3.

## 5. Construction proving A

Let D be the finite nonempty subsets of S. For each F choose the hypothesized subsemigroup T_F, its embedding j_F into an actual target U_F, and a dummy point u_F of U_F. Let U be an ultrafilter containing every tail

    D_H={F: H is a subset of F},

where H is finite nonempty. These tails have the finite-intersection property because D_H intersect D_K=D_(H union K), so the ultrafilter lemma supplies U. It may be principal when S is finite; no free-ultrafilter assumption is needed.

For s in S define

    a_F(s) = j_F(s) if s belongs to T_F,
             u_F    otherwise,
    j(s)=[a_F(s)].                                (5.1)

For s,t, on the U-large tail D_{s,t}, both belong to T_F, and so does st. Thus

    a_F(st)=a_F(s)a_F(t)

on that tail; hence j(st)=j(s)j(t).

If s is not t, on the same tail D_{s,t} the injectivity of j_F gives a_F(s) not equal to a_F(t). Therefore the two ultraproduct classes are distinct. This proves actual injectivity, not merely idempotent separation or a homomorphic-image statement.

Sections3–4 identify the codomain product_U U_F with an actual target of the same fixed P or R type, with a locally regular band, a completely simple factor, and the correct action. Composing that identification with (5.1) proves A. No T_F needs to be finite, and no compatibility between different j_F is used. QED.

**Important scope:** this is not a proof that every finite P1 semigroup embeds. If finite pieces are used, their embeddings must be supplied by a theorem or actual certificates. The existing finite purity theorem cannot be substituted for them.

## 6. Construction proving B and the countability barrier

Fix any choice s^dagger in V_S(s). For a finite nonempty F, let T_F be the set of values of all finite expressions formed from F using multiplication and dagger. It is a subsemigroup closed under dagger, and hence regular. There are only countably many finite such expressions, so T_F is at most countable. Every finite subset of S is contained in its corresponding T_F.

The exact book hypotheses pass from S to any regular subsemigroup T:

- If e=fa=ag with e,f,g idempotent and all elements in T, the same equations hold in S; weak E-unitarity of S gives a^2=a in T. No equality of the intrinsic natural order with its restriction is assumed.
- For e in E(T), the local monoid eTe is regular: if x in eTe and z in V_T(x), then eze is an inverse in eTe by the calculation in Lemma2.1.
- Idempotents of eTe are idempotents of eSe. Their products and the regular-band identity are therefore inherited. Thus eTe is orthodox with a regular idempotent band.

If every T_F embeds for the fixed reading, apply A to obtain an embedding of S. The contrapositive gives B. In particular, it is enough for a positive answer to P1 to handle all finitely inverse-generated members with an arbitrarily chosen inverse operation; handling merely finitely generated underlying semigroups is not asserted to be equivalent.

An unrestricted infinite counterexample, if one exists, therefore has a countable regular counterexample of this very specific kind. A cannot turn a finite search miss into an all-order theorem: the finitely inverse-generated witness may still be infinite.

## 7. Construction proving C; the purity obstruction cannot be hidden at a covered limit

The imported statement X is:

> If T is regular, has a nonempty completely simple ideal, and every local monoid is orthodox, there exists a surjective homomorphism q_T to a completely simple semigroup. Weak E-unitarity makes q_T idempotent pure.

The exact source is the existing `hmz-lean-20261014/xi-purity.md`, SHA-256 `c7696fff180e29e7bfbc1fe9ed2e0070b2afe6a675a86171df45facd12f5e318`; its old written parent audit has SHA-256 `2b28bf192f54f18b0d973f788246276b48c89fdf34fb04fe3b22ad3a6e49e73b`. X is not a formal/kernel import, and it is a baseline result.

Under C's hypotheses, each T_F inherits weak E-unitarity and local orthodoxy by §6. Choose the idempotent-pure q_F:T_F --> C_F supplied by X. Use the same tail ultrafilter and the same dummy-value construction as (5.1), now with q_F instead of j_F. This yields a homomorphism

    q:S --> product_U C_F.

The ultraproduct is completely simple by the Rees-coordinate construction in §3 without its band/action sorts. q is idempotent pure: if s^2 is not s, then on every F containing s, q_F(s)^2 is not q_F(s). The latter follows from purity of q_F and the fact that multiplication inside T_F is the original multiplication. Since those F form a U-large tail, q(s)^2 is not q(s). Conversely a homomorphism always sends idempotents to idempotents.

The image q(S) is regular and hence completely simple by Lemma2.1. Restrict the codomain to that image to obtain the promised surjective map. By Lemma2.3, the least completely simple congruence xi exists; xi is contained in ker q. If s xi e and e is idempotent, then q(s)=q(e) is idempotent, so s is idempotent. Thus xi is idempotent pure. QED.

A directed union of finite regular subsemigroups satisfies the cover hypothesis: directedness puts every finite set in one such subsemigroup, and a nonempty finite regular semigroup has a completely simple minimal ideal, the standard finite-semigroup import already used by X's finite corollary. **This is a proof for genuinely infinite covered S, not a retry of finite-purity counterexample enumeration.**

**Do not inflate the scope.** I have not shown that every locally finite regular semigroup, in the ordinary underlying-semigroup sense, has a cofinal cover by finite regular subsemigroups. Iteratively adding inverses can leave a finite subsemigroup, and ordinary local finiteness does not make that iteration stop without an additional argument. C uses the exact cover hypothesis stated, not that unstated implication. Nor does C give a faithful functor or an embedding.

## 8. Construction proving D

For P, projection to C is idempotent pure whenever the base is a band: for x=(i,g,lambda),

    (b,x)^2=(b alpha_(g p_(lambda,i))(b), x^2).

If x is idempotent then g=p_(lambda,i)^(-1), so the acting group element is 1, and the first coordinate is b^2=b. The converse follows by projection. For R, if x is idempotent then epsilon_x=x, and the restricted-carrier condition gives phi_x(b)=b, so (b,x)^2=(b^2,x)=(b,x). Again the converse follows by projection.

If S is regular and j embeds it into either target, q=projection composed with j is idempotent pure, and q(S) is completely simple by Corollary2.2. This proves D, including the necessity of pure xi by Lemma2.3. Neither local regularity of the target band nor finiteness of S is needed for this necessary direction. QED.

## 9. Exact nonvacuity/rejection control for the infinite regime

The following is an explicit example, not a newly discovered embedding theorem. It shows that C covers source objects having **no completely simple ideal**, even outside the globally orthodox and locally inverse subclasses.

Let E have upper right-zero band U={0,1} and lower left-zero ideal K={2,3,4}. Thus uv=v for u,v in U and kx=k for k in K,x in E. Let u act on K by theta_u, with both maps fixing 3,4 and

    theta_0(2)=4, theta_1(2)=3;
    uk=theta_u(k).

Since theta_u theta_v=theta_v for u,v in U, all mixed associativity equations hold; the other cases follow from the right-zero and left-zero laws. This is the dossier's E5, now justified by these formulas rather than a fresh program. For k in K, kEk={k}. For u in U,

    uEu={u,3,4},

where u is the identity and {3,4} is left zero. This is a regular band: if the outer variable in xyxzx=xyzx is u the identity, both sides are yz; if it is in {3,4}, both sides are that outer element. Thus E is locally regular. It is not regular because

    0*1*0*2*0=4, whereas 0*1*2*0=3.

Let L=(Z,min), B=E cross L, and

    C=M[C2;{0,1},{0,1}; [[1,1],[1,h]]],
    h^2=1, h not equal to 1.

B is locally regular by direct products. Take S=B cross C, the P product with trivial group action. Directly, S is regular; projection to C and the trivial natural order of C show weak E-unitarity; and every local monoid is (bBb) cross a group, whose idempotents form the regular band bBb. Hence S satisfies the exact book hypotheses.

S is not globally orthodox: in C the two idempotents (0,1,0) and (1,h,1) have product (0,h,1), whose square is (0,1,1), not itself. A fixed B coordinate transports this failure to S. It is not locally inverse: at a B coordinate (0,n), the two local idempotents associated with 3 and 4 do not commute.

S has no nonempty simple ideal. If J is a nonempty ideal and contains an element at integer level n, multiplication by an element at level m<n gives an element of J at level m. Then

    J_(<=m)={x in J: integer level(x)<=m}

is a nonempty proper ideal of J, because multiplication takes the minimum of integer levels. Thus J is not simple and in particular not completely simple.

Nevertheless S is the increasing union, N>=1, of the finite regular subsemigroups

    S_N=E cross ([-N,N],min) cross C.

Each has a completely simple ideal K cross {-N} cross C: K is left zero, and its product with the Rees semigroup C is another Rees semigroup, with rows K cross {0,1}, the same columns/group, and the repeated sandwich columns prescribed by C. Claim C therefore applies although S has no completely simple ideal. Here its conclusion can also be read directly from projection to C; and S already has its displayed trivial-action embedding. This is a consistency/nonvacuity control, not a counterexample or a new solution of an uncovered book case.

## 10. Remaining attack boundary and calibration

The first selection route also tried to reduce Billhardt's N1–N3 condition symbolically. For the literal premises, set r=a'e a and w=f r f g; all these and a'a,b'b are idempotent in the orthodox E-unitary setting. The premises become

    (a'a) f g = f g,
    (b'b) w = w.

The desired conclusion is e a f g = e b w. Passing to the local band at e by multiplying on the right by a'e produces idempotents, but local regularity alone has not yet justified the insertion/deletion of a'e a across the outside f,g factors. No proof that Billhardt's obstruction vanishes is claimed. No new solver search was run. This algebraic branch is explicitly unresolved, not silently promoted by the compactness result.

What this manuscript actually eliminates is a concrete infinite-only route:

- A counterexample cannot be manufactured solely by taking a locally embeddable union with incompatible local band/action choices: §§3–5 construct a global target and injection anyway.
- A xi-nonpurity counterexample cannot be a directed union of finite regular members, or more generally have C's cofinal regular completely-simple-ideal cover.
- A genuine counterexample to either embedding reading can be localized to a countable finitely inverse-generated regular source. It must still defeat actual embeddings, not just a chosen presentation.

The full book target remains unresolved. The finite nonorthodox pure-quotient-to-embedding step remains unresolved. The orthodox faithful-functor step for locally regular but globally nonregular bands remains unresolved. Infinite members outside C's cover may still lack a pure completely simple quotient.

The method is a standard ultraproduct/local-theorem mechanism specialized here with all product/action/carrier coordinates exposed. No claim is made that the closure/localization statements are new to semigroup model theory. Significance is **a modest structural barrier and a rigorously delimited infinite extension of an existing reduction**, not a scientific breakthrough, published novelty, or solved-book claim.
