## 2. Objects used throughout, as defined in the base

There are eleven actual row permutations of X, |X|=11, and every ordered distinct pair is LINEARLY adjacent exactly once (110 events). This gives:

- unique starts and ends, and the fixed-point-free endpoint permutation tau;
- f(v), the second symbol of the row starting v, and p(v), the penultimate symbol of the row ending v.

K_v completes the internal-triple matching at v by p(v)->f(v) and v->v. It is a genuine permutation, because an internal triple a,v,a is impossible. Its only possible off-v fixed point is the boundary p(v)=f(v).

`T(a,v)=(v,K_v(a))` follows each row and splices at its end into the row starting there. Its cycles are exactly the tau-cycles stretched by 10.

The split graph has one vertex per off-v K_v cycle (degree = cycle length, label v). It has exactly one edge per unordered pair, 55 edges, and its rotation-system faces are the T-cycles.

**Face tail count (1).** A face carrying r rows lists, as arc tails, the first ten symbols of each row. Each symbol therefore appears r times, minus once if it ends one of the face's rows. The end set equals the start set, because tau-cycles are closed. Hence `nu_x=r-1_(x in C)`, and distinct faces have disjoint start sets.

The full Tuscan-2 iff remains attached throughout: `sum K_v=I+Gamma+GF`, a simple zero-diagonal M, disjoint ten-step layers and Hamilton segments. The n=4 control in the base has GF=I different from M, so GF=M is not a licensed identity.

## 4. Reconstruction of v12

### 4.1 Component identity (Section2)

T-orbits stay inside one split component, because consecutive arcs share a split vertex. So each component H carries whole tau-cycles: r_H rows, start set C_H equal to its end set, and |C_H|=r_H.

Each edge {a,b} of H yields the arcs (a,b) and (b,a), with tails a and b, both on faces of H. So the number of tails labelled x on H's faces equals d_H(x). Summing (1) over the faces of H gives `d_H(x)=r_H-1_(x in C_H)`. Summing over x gives `2|E(H)|=11r_H-r_H`, so `|E(H)|=5r_H`.

If every split degree in H is even, every d_H(x) is even:

- if r_H is even, C_H is empty, contradicting r_H>0;
- if r_H is odd, C_H=X, so r_H=11 and H is the whole graph.

Similarly, with O_H the set of labels of odd d_H:

- r_H even gives C_H=O_H and r_H=|O_H|;
- r_H odd gives C_H=X\O_H and r_H=11-|O_H|.

|O_H| is even by the handshake lemma on the simple projected graph. All of this checks.

### 4.2 Parity tools (Section3)

**Bipartite face-dual.** If the face-dual is bipartite, each edge has its two sides on opposite colour classes. So each class's total perimeter equals |E|, which gives `sum_left r=sum_right r` and r_H even. Dual self-loops block the hypothesis; they are not silently deleted.

**Cycle rank.** With q_H branches of total degree m_H, beta_H leaves and every other vertex of degree 2, the handshake gives `V=E+(beta_H-m_H)/2+q_H`. Hence `b_H=E-V+1=(m_H-beta_H)/2-q_H+1`.

**Face parity.** Contracting a spanning tree preserves faces and leaves a bouquet of b_H loops. Each added loop changes the face count by ±1. So `1<=F_H<=b_H+1` and `F_H≡b_H+1 (mod 2)`; this is Euler's formula for the orientable rotation system defined by the actual K_v cycles.

### 4.3 Support 8: all placements (Section4)

Partitions of 8 into parts >=3 are only 8, 5+3 and 4+4.

**Boundary parity.** At each centre the ten neighbours split into long cycles, 2-cycles and at most one off-v fixed point, which must be the boundary. A fixed point therefore occurs exactly when the long support at that centre is odd. This gives:

- beta=0 for a single 8-cycle, same-centre 3+5, and both 4+4 placements;
- beta=2 for distinct-centre 3+5, with leaves labelled v (3-cycle) and w (5-cycle).

**One 8-cycle.** All degrees are even, so by 4.1 there is a single component. Its core is a bouquet of 4 loops, b=4, so F is 5, 3 or 1. F=5 needs at least 2×5=10 branch corners, but only 8 exist (lemma 5). F3 and F1 remain.

**Same-centre 3+5.**

- The two odd vertices share a component; any other component would be an all-degree-2 cycle, excluded by lemma 1.
- With k connecting threads, 3=k+2L_3 and 5=k+2L_5, so k=1 or 3.
- k=1 forces a loop at the degree-3 branch. For either 3-cycle rotation (h1 h2 h3), with α swapping the loop ends h1 and h2, the face permutation σ∘α fixes one loop end. That is a one-visit face, excluded by lemma 5.
- So k=3: three parallel threads plus one loop at the 5-branch, b=3, F=4 or 2.
- At F=4, all faces have exactly 2 corners, so φ=σα is a fixed-point-free involution. Then ασα=σ^{-1}, so conjugation by α permutes the cycle supports of σ. The unique 3-support must be fixed, but a fixed-point-free involution cannot preserve an odd set. **F4 is impossible; F2 remains.**

**Two 4-cycles, same or distinct centres.**

- Everything is even, so there is one connected component; k=0 threads is excluded by connectivity. Hence k=4, or k=2 with one loop at each branch, b=3, F=4 or 2.
- At F=4, α maps σ's two 4-supports onto supports. Preserving both would mean no connecting thread; swapping them makes all four edges threads.
- In the four-thread core with σ=(h1 h2 h3 h4), the relation ασα=σ^{-1} gives σ(α h_i)=α h_(i-1). So the faces are {h_i, α h_(i-1)}: cyclically adjacent thread pairs, with perimeters `l_i+l_(i-1)=10r`.
- The alternating sum gives r0+r2=r1+r3, so the total row count is even. But this single component carries 11 rows. **F4 is impossible for both placements; F2 remains for both core types.**

### 4.4 Distinct-centre 3+5 (Section5)

Only branch v (degree 3), branch w (degree 5) and the two leaves have odd degree. So d_H(v) is odd exactly when H contains an odd number of {branch v, leaf v}, and similarly for w. Hence O_H is a subset of {v,w}, and it is ∅ or {v,w}.

**Disconnected case.**

- A proper H with O_H=∅ is impossible by 4.1. So each proper component has r_H=2 or 9, and the row count 11 forces exactly 2+9.
- In the 2-row component, d(v)=d(w)=1 and every other label has degree 2. So it contains no branch (degree >1 at that label) and is a leaf-to-leaf path with 10 edges.
- By its degrees it is a Hamilton path, traversed by its face as a row and its exact reverse. The leaf reflections are the actual boundaries p=f.
- The 9-row component contains both branches and no leaves, starts the other nine labels, and has degrees 9, 9 and 8. Its core is reduced exactly as for same-centre 3+5: k=1 is excluded by the degree-3 loop, and F4 by the unique 3-support. Two faces remain.

**Connected case.**

- No reverse pair exists, since one would be an isolated component.
- By the same-label rule, leaf v's first branch is w and leaf w's is v. The two tails cannot meet first, since that would be a separate path component.
- Removing both tails leaves degrees w:4 and v:2. Suppressing v gives a figure-eight at w, so the core is two parallel v–w threads plus one loop at w.
- **Tail bound for leaf v.** The two arms leave w, cannot reach branch v (same label as the leaf) and cannot return to w (repeat).
  - When both arms are the two ends of the w-loop, parametrise the loop as w=y_0,…,y_L=w.
  - The row ending at v traverses the − arm toward w, using the directed arcs y_(L-j)→y_(L-j+1). The row starting at v traverses the + arm, using y_j→y_(j+1).
  - Any positional overlap would make one directed arc occur twice, contradicting exact adjacency coverage (equivalently, the disjoint arc layers). So the arms are position-disjoint, and the small-set lemma gives l_v<=6.
- **Tail bound for leaf w.** Its arms are the two distinct threads toward w, giving l_w<=6.
- Cycle rank (8-2)/2-2+1=2, so F=3 or 1. Both remain open in v12; nothing is mis-assigned.

### 4.5 Component-conditioned identity (Section6)

- Every off-centre arrow a→K_v(a) whose cycle lies in H is either an internal triple of an H-row, and so lies in Γ_H, or the boundary arrow at v. The boundary arrow lies in H exactly when v∈C_H. Hence `Λ_H=Γ_H+B_H`.
- The row sum of Λ_H at a counts the edges {a,v} at copies of a in H, which is d_H(a). Subtracting B_H's margins c_(p,H) and c_(f,H) gives (7).
- Summing over H returns `ΣK_v=I+Γ+GF`, since Σ_H B_H=GF.
- Under Tuscan-2, the Γ_H are 0/1 with disjoint supports and `M=J-I-ΣΓ_H`.
- For the forced path x0=v,…,x10=w, the distance-two events are (x_i,x_(i+2)) and their reverses, i=0..8: 18 arcs. These are the even-position path on 6 vertices and the odd-position path on 5 vertices.
- Its B_H is the two diagonal boundary loops at x1 and x9. All of this checks.
