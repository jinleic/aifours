## 5. NEW/UNREVIEWED: normal-section dichotomy and simple-factor descent

This section is a full new written proof, not an imported review result. It is independent of G4/F7 and needs NO invocation of Sun7 after the initial R8 reduction. It applies directly to any finite P7 configuration, including one cofactor carrying an arbitrary power of7.

### 5.1 Elementary multiple-coset CRT

If finite-index subgroups H_1,...,H_m of a group have pairwise-coprime indices a_i, then

    [G:intersection_i H_i]=product_i a_i,

and EVERY prescribed family of left cosets x_iH_i has a common point.

**Proof.** The map from G/(intersection H_i) into the Cartesian product of G/H_i is injective, so the intersection index is at most the product. It is a multiple of each a_i, hence of their pairwise-coprime product; equality follows. For the arbitrary-coset claim, proceed inductively: the intersection of an already intersecting family is a left coset of the subgroup intersection, of index equal to the product of their a_i. That product is coprime to the next a_i, so the elementary two-coset intersection lemma supplies a point in both. This proves nonemptiness for every finite family, with the original left-coset orientation. QED.

The product equality is justified by COPRIMALITY. We do not claim an arbitrary subgroup-intersection index divides the product of its indices; without normality/subnormality that divisibility can fail.

### 5.2 Normal-fibre bookkeeping and the no-split lemma

Let N◁G and π:G→Q=G/N. For each original subgroup/coset put

    V_i=NU_i/N,
    c_i=[N:N∩U_i],       D_i=[Q:V_i].

The second-isomorphism/index formulas give the EXACT factorization

    n_i=c_i D_i=7r_i.                                    (N1)

If a projected coset π(g_i)V_i meets a projected coset π(g_j)V_j at q, the original cosets meet the normal fibre π^{-1}(q) in nonempty LEFT cosets, after translation into N, of N∩U_i and N∩U_j. Their indices are c_i,c_j. Thus

    gcd(D_i,D_j)=1 and gcd(c_i,c_j)=1
       imply g_iU_i intersects g_jU_j.                   (N2)

Indeed quotient coprimality gives the common q, then fibre coprimality gives a common original point. This is not the false assertion that projecting disjoint subsets always preserves disjointness.

Choose a distinguished vertex e so that7 does NOT divide r_i for every i≠e. Such e exists because the r_i are pairwise coprime; if no r_i contains7, choose any e. Let I be the other SEVEN vertices. Each i∈I has exactly one factor7 in n_i, so in(N1) that factor lies either wholly in c_i or wholly in D_i.

**No-split lemma.** Among I, both placement types cannot occur.

**Proof.** If7|c_i,7∤D_i and7|D_j,7∤c_j, then each pair gcd in(N2) is1. Both c_i,c_j and D_i,D_j divide their corresponding n_i,n_j, whose gcd is exactly7; in each pair one number lacks7. Equation(N2) forces the two original cosets to intersect, contradiction. Thus all seven regular vertices have the same placement type. QED.

## 6. The exact simple-group frontier and unavailable shortcuts
