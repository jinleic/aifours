# Three-complement gate: two-point switches cannot realize the Shor rows

This is a uniform restriction on ENTIRE unknown common complements at EVERY order, not a fixed-modulus enumeration. It does not bound the total allowed trade size and is NOT a full common-complement obstruction.

## 1. Hypotheses

Let A+B=Z_n directly with

    {0,3,8,11} subset A,
    J0={0,1,7,13,14} subset B.

Let D2,D3 be complements of the SAME A, containing

    J2={0,2,4,6} subset D2,
    J3={0,1,2} subset D3.

These are exactly the strengthened full16-word gate in `shor-three-complement-bayonet-gate-v1.md`. Every set, including all unknown additional elements, is retained. All three complements have cardinality n/|A|.

The existing written multiplier lemma gives330||B|, 2||A|, 660|n and n>=1320. These are inherited facts, not new order bounds. In particular60 and660 divide n.

**Theorem.**

    |D2 minus B|=|B minus D2| >= 4,
    |D3 minus B|=|B minus D3| >= 3,
    |D3 minus D2|=|D2 minus D3| >= 3.

Thus the three complements are pairwise distinct. In particular neither required low-row change can be achieved by altering at most two points on each side.

## 2. Forced membership/exclusion, by literal tiling collisions

In any complement D of A, two D values differing by3,5,8 or11 are forbidden, because those are differences of A0. This gives:

- B excludes2,4,6:

      3+7=8+2,  3+1=0+4,  8+6=0+14.

- D2 excludes1,7,13,14:

      0+4=3+1,  3+7=8+2,
      0+13=11+2,  0+14=8+6.

- D3 excludes4,6,7,13:

      0+4=3+1,  3+6=8+1,
      3+7=8+2,  0+13=11+2.

All displayed collisions use distinct included A residues, with the one disputed D value on one side. Hence they are valid modulo every n under the hypotheses, not just integer sample cases.

Consequently B minus D2 contains1,7,13,14, proving the first bound by equal cardinality. Also B minus D3 contains7,13 while D3 minus B contains2; and D2 minus D3 contains4,6 while D3 minus D2 contains1. To improve these latter lower bounds from2 to3 we use the following exact root-of-unity argument.

## 3. Quadrilateral lemma

If complex unit numbers z1,z2,z3,z4 satisfy z1+z2=z3+z4, then either the unordered pairs {z1,z2} and {z3,z4} agree, or BOTH sums are0.

Proof. For a nonzero common sum c, each z lies on both the unit circle and the circle |c-z|=1. These two distinct circles have at most two intersections, the pair already given. Equivalently the two pairs are endpoints of unit-circle chords with the same nonzero midpoint, and such a chord is unique. If c=0, both pairs are antipodal. This includes repeated endpoints.

It follows that, for a root of unity zeta satisfying

    zeta^p + zeta^u = zeta^r + zeta^s,

at least one of

    zeta^(p-r)=1,
    zeta^(p-s)=1,
    zeta^(s-r)=-1

holds. The exponent u may be ANY residue; no bound on it is used.

## 4. A two-point B/D3 trade would force A periodic modulo660

Suppose |D3 minus B|=|B minus D3|<=2. Section2 forces this number to be exactly2, with

    B minus D3={7,13},
    D3 minus B={2,u}

for some entirely unknown u. Convolving the difference by1_A gives0 because BOTH sets complement A:

    1_A * (e2+e_u-e7-e13)=0 in Z[Z_n].

At ANY nth root zeta, finite Fourier evaluation therefore gives

    A(zeta)*(zeta^2+zeta^u-zeta^7-zeta^13)=0.

Whenever A(zeta) is nonzero, the quadrilateral lemma forces

    zeta^5=1 OR zeta^11=1 OR zeta^6=-1.

Thus the order of every such zeta divides5,11 or12, hence divides660. Finite Fourier inversion now gives

    1_A(x+660)=1_A(x) for EVERY x in Z_n.

Indeed every surviving Fourier character has zeta^660=1. This is an EXACT period, not a count relaxation or an asymptotic assertion.

Quotient by the subgroup generated by660. Because A is periodic there, uniqueness in A+B makes reduction injective on B; projecting and counting gives a direct factorization of Z_660 with all forced seeds. Such a FACT pair cannot exist:330||Bbar| and |Abar|>=4 would give660=|Abar||Bbar|>=1320. This is also the special case of the exact periodic-descent theorem.

Therefore no two-point B/D3 trade is possible, proving |D3 minus B|>=3. The proof covered every unknown u and every order n, including arbitrarily large ones.

## 5. A two-point D2/D3 trade would force A periodic modulo60

Similarly, if the common trade size between D2,D3 were<=2, Section2 would force

    D2 minus D3={4,6},
    D3 minus D2={1,u}.

At each nth root where A(zeta) is nonzero,

    zeta^1+zeta^u=zeta^4+zeta^6.

The quadrilateral lemma forces

    zeta^3=1 OR zeta^5=1 OR zeta^2=-1.

These orders all divide60. Hence A is periodic modulo60. Quotienting would yield a FACT pair at60, impossible already because the seed-bootstrap forces the start-side cardinality to be at least330. Thus this trade size is at least3 as claimed.

## 6. What the obstruction leaves

The signed annihilator trades

    E=1_D2-1_B,  F=1_D3-1_B,
    A*E=A*F=0

must therefore have at least4 and3 positive terms, respectively, with equal negative counts, and D2/D3 have trade size at least3. Larger trades can contain arbitrarily many unknown positive and negative locations. The four-unit-circle argument does NOT extend automatically to these longer vanishing sums. In particular it does not prove a fixed period for A in the general case.

A positive gate witness must realize three sufficiently different complements of one A, not just a few local switches. A sufficient negative answer still needs an all-order obstruction covering ALL such larger trades and then the actual raw-code quantifier. Neither a common-complement witness nor that universal obstruction is supplied here.

The proof uses the already-written multiplier/quotient facts, elementary unit-circle geometry, and finite Fourier inversion.
