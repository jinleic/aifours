## 2. The exact small-row ideals through level four

Identify V with traceless3x3 matrices, with v=e3 identified with E31. Put u=E1v=-E32 and w=E2v=E21, where E_j=ad(e_j). Work first in the free Lie algebra L(V), carrying its actual finite-Levi action. The original third-leading relation for row-entry q=-a_3j is, up to sign,

    t_(q,j) = (ad v)^q(E_j v),

at level q+1 and full degree(1,0,q+1) for j=1, or(0,1,q+1) for j=2. It is nonzero: the tensor word v^q(E_jv) has coefficient1 in its associative expansion and v,E_jv are independent.

Every F_i=ad(f_i), i=1,2, kills t_(q,j). Indeed F_i v=0, F_i E_j v=0 for i!=j, and F_j E_j v=v; the remaining term is (ad v)^q v=0. Hence its Levi span Q_(q,j) is the simple module generated by this lowest vector. For j=1 its lowest weight is(1-q,-q-2), its highest weight is(q+2,q-1), and its dimension is

    (q+3) q (2q+3)/2.

For q=1,2,3 these dimensions are10,35,81; j=2 gives the dual modules. The dimension formula is the usual(p+1)(q+1)(p+q+2)/2, with highest-weight coordinates as just specified.

Define

    Q_l(r,s) = [Q_(l-1,1) if r=l-1, else0]
               +[Q_(l-1,2) if s=l-1, else0],  l=2,3,4.

Let T be the graded ideal of L(V) generated by all actual third-leading Serre modules. Positive Lie brackets cannot lower level. Through level four its components are EXACTLY

    T2 = Q2,
    T3 = [V,Q2] + Q3,
    T4 = [V,[V,Q2]] + [V,Q3] + Q4.                 (2.1)

To verify completeness, a homogeneous ideal generated in levels2,3,4 is spanned, up to level4, by generators and their iterated brackets with V. L(V) is generated by V. A bracket[L2(V),Q2] is already in[V,[V,Q2]] by Jacobi. The Levi spans of these spaces introduce no new terms: all factors and the bracket are Levi stable/equivariant. This proves both inclusions in(2.1), not merely a collection of necessary relations. The positive elimination presentation now gives

    S_k = L_k(V)/T_k, k<=4.                        (2.2)

In particular, the source and target quotient ideals change at entries1 and2; an entry3 only introduces Q4. A free-space rank test with these modules omitted is not valid for the general small-row problem.

Finite-Levi complete reducibility also proves the precise lowest-space lifting fact: if P(M)=ker(F1)∩ker(F2) in a finite-dimensional Levi module M and T is a submodule, then

    P(M/T) = P(M)/(P(M)∩T)                         (2.3)

## 3. The unique invariant line of L4(V)

    V⊗V = 1 ⊕ 2V ⊕ M1 ⊕ M2 ⊕ L(2,2).           (3.1)

Here is a short written character calculation, rather than an invocation of a saved program. If χ is the adjoint character, the Witt identity gives

    χ_(L4(V)) = (χ^4 - (ψ2χ)^2)/4,

where ψ2χ(g)=χ(g^2). Taking invariant multiplicities, the first term is dim End_g(V⊗V)=1+4+1+1+1=8, by(3.1). For any representation R, the invariant multiplicity of ψ2χ_R is the trace of the flip on(R⊗R)^g: this follows by averaging tr((ρ(g)⊗ρ(g))flip)=tr(ρ(g)^2) over the compact form SU3.     (8-4)/4=1.                                    (3.2)

Construct this line without a 208-word tensor. Choose invariantly dual bases(m_i) of M1 and(n_i) of M2, and set

## 5. Two independent adjoint images in L3(V)

We need a representation-theoretic fact that rules out cancellation between the two different original-row contributions. The degree-three exact sequence is

    0 → Λ3 V --j--> V⊗Λ2 V --b--> L3(V) →0,

with

    j(x∧y∧z)=x⊗(y∧z)+y⊗(z∧x)+z⊗(x∧y),
    b(x⊗(y∧z))=[x,[y,z]].

The wedge composed with j is3, Jacobi gives bj=0, and dimensions56,224,168 prove exactness; the Witt formula supplies168. This is a finite-dimensional Lie-space identity.

Each V⊗Mi, i=1,2, contains the adjoint once: by tensor-Hom duality its multiplicity is the multiplicity of Mi* in V⊗V, which is1 by(3.1). Their two adjoint copies remain injective and independent after b. To prove this last assertion it suffices to show that the unique adjoint copy in Λ3 V has a NONZERO projection under j to V⊗M0, because no element supported only in V⊗(M1⊕M2) can then lie in j(Λ3 V)'s adjoint part.

Here are the full small written calculations establishing that point. Put

    a=E31, b=E21, c=E32, d=E12, e=E23,
    h=H1=E11-E22, k=H2=E22-E33.

The weight(-1,-1) space in Λ3 V has basis

    A=a∧h∧k, B=a∧d∧b, C=a∧e∧c,
    D=b∧c∧h, E=b∧c∧k.

The matrix commutator gives the following lowering equations for a combination αA+βB+γC+δD+εE:

    F1:  (α+β+δ)a∧b∧h + (2α+ε)a∧b∧k,
    F2:  (-2α+δ)a∧c∧h + (-α+γ+ε)a∧c∧k.

Thus the joint kernel is precisely the single line

    Z=A-3B+3C+2D-2E.                               (5.1)

A lowest vector of weight(-1,-1) generates an adjoint summand, so this proves its multiplicity in Λ3 V is exactly1. Let δ0=(id⊗[-,-]_matrix)j, whose second factor is the projection onto M0 up to a nonzero scalar. The five needed images are

    δ0 A = -h⊗a+k⊗a,
    δ0 B = a⊗h+b⊗c,
    δ0 C = a⊗k-c⊗b,
    δ0 D = -b⊗c-2c⊗b-h⊗a,
    δ0 E = 2b⊗c+c⊗b-k⊗a.

Consequently

    δ0 Z = -3(h⊗a+a⊗h)+3(k⊗a+a⊗k)
            -9(b⊗c+c⊗b) != 0.                    (5.2)

This proves the required nonzero M0 projection, and therefore proves that the adjoint images

    B1 ⊆ [V,M1] ⊆ L3(V),
    B2 ⊆ [V,M2] ⊆ L3(V)

are nonzero and have direct sum B1⊕B2. Their independence is a written consequence of(5.1)–(5.2), not a rank heuristic or an assumption about multiplicity spaces.

## 6. The actual original-row lowering of X

Let W be the level-minus-one finite-Levi module generated by f3. It is the dual adjoint representation. Before imposing the third-leading relations, the actual local bracket gives equivariant maps W⊗Mi→V. Crucially, D itself is NOT a full Levi homomorphism, and we do not treat it as one.

With the book's sign convention[e_i,f_i]=h_i, Dv=-h3. Direct defining-bracket calculation gives

    D[v,u] = 2(r-1)u,
    D[v,w] = 2(s-1)w.                              (6.1)

For example Du=[e1,-h3]=-r e1, h3 acts on u with weight2-r, and

    D[v,u]=[-h3,u]+[v,-r e1]
           =-(2-r)u+r u=2(r-1)u.

Since[f3,e1]=[f3,e2]=0, D commutes with E1,E2. Every Mi is generated from its displayed lowest vector by those raisings. Therefore the ENTIRE map D|M1 is(r-1) times a fixed nonzero map and D|M2 is(s-1) times a fixed nonzero map. Taking Levi translates of f3 gives the same factorization for the equivariant W-actions:

    [W,M1]=(r-1) φ1(W,M1),
    [W,M2]=(s-1) φ2(W,M2),                         (6.2)

where the bilinear maps φi are fixed and nonzero, with values in V. The factorization is a polynomial identity in the original row parameters; it is not deduced from finitely many values.

Apply the derivation rule to X=Σ[m_i,n_i]. Its full W-action has the form

    [z,X] = (r-1) A(z) + (s-1) B(z), z∈W,          (6.3)

where A:W→[V,M2] and B:W→[V,M1] are equivariant maps independent of r,s. Both are NONZERO. For A, the tensor map

    z ↦ Σ φ1(z,m_i)⊗n_i ∈ V⊗M2

is nonzero because the pairing is perfect and φ1 is nonzero; contraction is a faithful identification with Hom(M1,V). As W is simple adjoint, this map occupies the unique adjoint copy of V⊗M2. Section5 proves bracket is injective on that copy, so A is nonzero. The same argument applies to B, with the sign from the switched bracket irrelevant. Moreover A(W) and B(W) are independent by §5. A nonzero equivariant map from simple W is injective, hence A(f3) and B(f3) are nonzero. Formula(6.3) therefore implies

    D X != 0 in L3(V)

whenever(r-1,s-1)!=(0,0). There is no off-diagonal cancellation: the two contributions lie in independent adjoint submodules. This is the point that generic specialization alone cannot establish.
