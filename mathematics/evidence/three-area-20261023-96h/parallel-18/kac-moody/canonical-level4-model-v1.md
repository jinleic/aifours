## 1. A stronger model than character equality

For a complex vector space V define the natural GL(V)-equivariant map

    F: Sym2(V) tensor Λ2(V) --> L4(V),
    F((a b) tensor(c wedge d))
      = [a,[b,[c,d]]] + [b,[a,[c,d]]].              (1.1)

Here a b is the abstract commutative symmetric product. The formula is symmetric in a,b and alternating in c,d, so F is well defined. **F is an isomorphism.** This is a classical free-Lie/Schur-functor fact proved below in writing; no novelty is claimed.

This establishes an explicit natural map, rather than merely equal Levi characters as in free-level4-character-derivation-v1.md. It may be used to write all original-row couplings on a fixed representation model, but it does not automatically conjugate that lowering to a scalar or a Levi homomorphism.

## 2. Complete written proof of invertibility

The PBW/Witt character identity proves that L4(V) and Sym2(V) tensor Λ2(V) have the same GL(V) character. Pieri decomposes the latter into the two multiplicity-one Schur modules

    S_(3,1)(V) direct-sum S_(2,1,1)(V).            (2.1)

Thus it suffices to show F nonzero on both summands. In dimension at least2, the weight a^3 b contains only the(3,1) summand. Its image

    F(a^2 tensor(a wedge b))=2[a,[a,[a,b]]]

is nonzero in the free tensor algebra, proving the first assertion.

For the second use three independent letters a,b,c. The a^2 b c weight space in Sym2(V) tensor Λ2(V) has basis

    a^2 tensor(b wedge c),
    (a b) tensor(a wedge c),
    (a c) tensor(a wedge b).

In the actual free Lie algebra put

    X=[a,[a,[b,c]]],
    Y=[a,[b,[a,c]]],
    Z=[b,[a,[a,c]]].

They are independent: in their associative expansions the respective words aabc,abac,baac occur with coefficient1 only in X,Y,Z, respectively. Jacobi gives the three displayed source images, in this independent basis,

    2X,       Y+Z,       -2X+3Y-Z.                (2.2)

For the last identity, [c,[a,b]]=-[a,[b,c]]+[b,[a,c]], so

    [a,[c,[a,b]]]=-X+Y,
    [c,[a,[a,b]]]=-X+2Y-Z.

Their sum is the third vector in(2.2). The3x3 coefficient determinant is-8, nonzero in characteristic zero. Hence F has rank3 on this weight space. The(3,1) Schur summand has multiplicity2 at weight a^2bc; the(2,1,1) summand has multiplicity1 (equivalently its GL3 realization is determinant tensor the standard representation). Therefore rank3 is impossible if F vanishes on the second summand. F is nonzero there as well.

Since both source and target in(2.1) are semisimple multiplicity-free modules of identical types, Schur's lemma proves F is an isomorphism. Dimensions0/1 give zero spaces, dimension2 has only the first summand, and dimension>=3 is covered by the three-letter calculation and naturality. This is a universal written map proof, not a sampled rank inference: the Schur decomposition reduces it to two irreducible types, and the explicitly exhibited weights detect BOTH types.

## 3. Exact source-ideal expression in this model

For a quadratic relation submodule R⊆Λ2(V), the degree-four ideal generated by it is

    T4=[V,[V,R]].

Split the two outer V factors into symmetric and alternating parts. Jacobi gives the exact sum

    T4 = F(Sym2(V) tensor R) + [Λ2(V),R].         (3.1)

Indeed the symmetric part gives the first term in(3.1), while

    [a,[b,r]]-[b,[a,r]]=[[a,b],r]

gives the second. Conversely both terms belong to the iterated ideal span. No directness of this sum is asserted.

The SECOND term has an exact independent tensor description. The map

    Λ2(Λ2(V)) --> L4(V), p wedge q --> [p,q]

is injective, by degree-two block concatenation in the tensor algebra. Therefore

    [Λ2(V),R] ≅ (Λ2(V) tensor R)/Sym2(R).         (3.2)

The kernel really is precisely Sym2(R): extend a basis of R to one of Λ2(V), so the kernel of u tensor r --> u wedge r consists exactly of symmetric tensors on R. This is not a generic dimension assumption.

For a single row1, R=M1 or M2 has dimension10. The first term in(3.1) has dimension36*10=360 because F is an isomorphism. The second term has dimension28*10-55=225 by(3.2). The unresolved part of determining T4's exact dimension is their INTERSECTION:

    dim T4 = 585 - dim(F(Sym2(V) tensor R)
                       intersect [Λ2(V),R]).    (3.3)

That intersection must be proved, not guessed to vanish from dimensions or generic rank. This isolates a precise finite representation-theoretic syzygy obstruction in the most delicate row1 source.
