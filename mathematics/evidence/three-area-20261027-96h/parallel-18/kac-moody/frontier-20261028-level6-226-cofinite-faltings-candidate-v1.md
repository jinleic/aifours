## 3. Proof

**Named imports.** Gauss' lemma; standard resultant/discriminant identities; Hensel's lemma for complete local rings (Stacks [04GE](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04GE), [04GG](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04GG)(3), [04GM](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04GM)); the completion decomposition `L⊗_K K̂ ≅ ∏_w L_w` for a finite separable extension of a valued field (Serre, *Local Fields*, II §3, Thm. 1); characteristic-zero Riemann–Hurwitz (Stacks [0C1B](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0C1B)); **Faltings' theorem** (Mordell conjecture: a smooth projective geometrically integral curve of genus ≥2 over a number field has finitely many rational points). For integer points only, Siegel's integral-point theorem (genus ≥1) is an alternative final import. Public Stacks pages were read during this work; their bytes were not pinned and no external proof was rechecked.

**Step A — R is irreducible over Q.** By the pinned Rabin certificate, `R(0,y)` mod 61 has full degree 21 (`c≡25`) and is irreducible over F61. If `R=AB` in Q[x,y] with nonunits, the identity `lc_y(A)lc_y(B)=c` in Q[x] forces both y-leading coefficients to be nonzero constants, so both factors have positive y-degree (a y-degree-0 factor would be a constant). Setting x=0 would factor `R(0,y)` over Q, and Gauss reduction mod 61 (leading coefficients prime to 61) would factor it over F61, a contradiction.

**Step B — the discriminant has ≥388 simple roots in Q̄.** Let `Res(x)=Res_y(R,∂_yR) in Z[x]` with formal degrees 21,20; since `n(n-1)/2=210` is even, `Res=c·disc_yR`. Because `deg_x [y^k]R<=21-k`, the resultant has `deg Res<=21·20=420`. For p=32749 (p∤21c), reduction mod p commutes with the universal Sylvester determinant, so `Res mod p` equals the pinned polynomial `D_p`, which has degree exactly 420; hence `deg Res=420` and its leading coefficient ℓ is prime to p. The pinned guard proves `D_p=S·T` with `deg S=388`, `gcd(S,S')=gcd(S,T)=1`, so every root α∈F̄_p of S is a simple root of `D_p`. Put `f=ℓ^{-1}Res`, monic over the complete DVR `W(F_p(α))`. Hensel gives a unique `a` with `f(a)=0`, `ā=α`, and `f'(a)` a unit, so `a` is a simple root; distinct α give distinct a. Roots in Q̄_p of a rational polynomial are images, with multiplicity, of its roots in Q̄. Thus `disc_yR` has at least 388 distinct simple roots in Q̄; in particular `disc_yR≢0`.

**Step C — dichotomy.** Suppose R is reducible over Q̄. Then `R=λ∏_{k=1}^m R_k` with `m>=2` and R_k irreducible over a finite Galois K/Q; each R_k has constant y-leading coefficient and positive y-degree, no two are associate (else `disc_yR≡0`), and Gal(K/Q) permutes them transitively (an orbit product is rational up to scalar and divides the Q-irreducible R). A rational point on `R=0` lies on some `R_k=0`, hence on every conjugate `σ(R_k)=0`, so it lies in `V(R_1)∩V(R_2)`, which is finite because coprime curves meet in finitely many points. So `C(Q)` is finite in this branch.

**Step D — genus in the absolutely irreducible branch.** Let R be irreducible over Q̄, `L=Q̄(x)[y]/(R)`, and `π: C̃→P^1` the x-map from the smooth projective model; `deg π=21`. Fix a simple root x0 of `disc_yR`, `t=x-x0`, `O=Q̄[[t]]`, `K̂=Q̄((t))`, `f=c^{-1}R(x0+t,y)`, monic in O[y] with `v_t(disc f)=1`. Hensel factors `f=∏f_j` with distinct `f_j mod t=(y-β_j)^{m_j}`. Then `disc f=∏disc f_j·∏_{j<k}Res(f_j,f_k)^2`, and each cross resultant is a unit. A monic degree-m factor congruent to `(y-β)^m` has, after translation, all non-leading coefficients `a_i` in `tO`; its discriminant is isobaric of weight `m(m-1)` (weight `a_i`=i), so every monomial has at least `m-1` factors and `v_t(disc)>=m-1`. Since the total valuation is 1 and some `m_j>=2`, exactly one factor has `m_j=2` and `v_t=1`. Writing it `y^2+a1y+a2`, `v(a1^2)>=2` forces `v(a2)=1` and `(y+a1/2)^2=t·unit`; odd valuation makes it irreducible over K̂, a ramified quadratic extension. By `L⊗K̂≅∏K̂[y]/(irreducible factors)` there is a place above x0 with `e=2`. Riemann–Hurwitz in characteristic 0 gives

\[ 2g-2=21(-2)+\sum_P(e_P-1)\ge -42+388=346,\qquad g\ge174. \]

**Step E — finiteness.** In the absolutely irreducible branch, C is geometrically integral over Q; its normalization `C̃_Q` is smooth projective over Q of genus ≥174, and Faltings makes `C̃_Q(Q)` finite. Rational smooth points of C lift to rational points of `C̃_Q`, while `Sing(C)=V(R,R_x,R_y)` is finite because R is irreducible and does not divide `R_y` (`deg_yR_y=20`). Hence `C(Q)` is finite. Steps C–E prove finiteness unconditionally on the branch, so the integer exceptional set `{x,y>=0: R=0}` is finite, and §2 gives the cofinite candidate.
