## 2. Exact original assertion and meanings

For a finite group G of order n, let O(G) denote:

> For EVERY word a1,…,a_(2n−1) in G, there are ORIGINAL indices i1<…<i_n whose product a_i1…a_i_n, in THAT order, is the identity.

The original problem is whether O(G) holds for EVERY finite G, or an exact counterexample. No family, solvability, commutativity, prime or dihedral restriction is inserted.
For n=1 the single group element is the identity and the assertion is immediate.
The results below neither prove nor refute the original assertion.

A packet means an actual set of occurrence indices, read in increasing original order. Distinct packets in a family are disjoint. A packet product ALWAYS uses that internal original order.
Packets B1,…,Bt are **chronologically separated** if every index in B_j precedes every index in B_(j+1).
Disjointness alone does not imply separation.
For a selected family of packets, its chronological union product is read in increasing order of ALL its indices, not packet-concatenation order.

## 3. The valid separated-packet transport implication

## 6. Crossing correction: kernel packet products do not determine the chronological product

For ANY group and ANY four actual letters a,b,c,d in that ORIGINAL order, take packets A=(a,c) and B=(b,d).
Define the packet-concatenation product P=(ac)(bd), and chronological product T=abcd.
With commutator convention `[b,c]=b^-1 c^-1 b c`, direct group multiplication gives

`T = P · d^-1 [b,c] d`.

Indeed multiplying the right side gives
`a c b d d^-1 b^-1 c^-1 b c d = a b c d`.
This is an ALL-variable identity, not an estimate or a selected commutator check.
Therefore when P=1, the chronological product is identity IFF the crossing correction is identity, equivalently iff b and c commute.
The two packet products alone do not pay that condition.
If the quotient G/K is abelian, the correction is an element of K, but membership in K is NOT equality to1. Normality, or abelianness of the quotient, does not cancel it.

A particularly strong specialization is c=a^-1,d=b^-1: BOTH packets individually have product1, while their chronological union product is

`a b a^-1 b^-1`.

This is nonidentity for EVERY choice of noncommuting a,b. No packet-product condition, even individual packet identity, alone forces the crossed union to have identity product.

## 7. Sharp universal transparency criterion for a prescribed packet ordering

**Theorem B.** Fix any finite ordered set of actual occurrence labels and any partition into packets with a prescribed concatenation order. Compare that concatenation permutation with chronological order.
For a FIXED group G, equality of the two products for EVERY assignment of G-elements to those labels holds precisely in either of the following situations:

- the two orders are literally the same; or
- G is abelian.

**Proof.** If the orders agree, associativity suffices. If G is abelian, any permutation preserves the product.
Conversely if the orders differ, there are two labels r<s reversed by the concatenation permutation.
Assign arbitrary x,y∈G to r,s and identities to ALL other labels. The two products are xy and yx.
Universal equality therefore forces xy=yx for EVERY x,y, so G is abelian.
This is a universally quantified variable-isolation proof, not a chosen finite control.

The theorem concerns unconditional product transparency. It does NOT say that every nontrivial interleaving must fail for every actual assignment, or that no additional constrained packet coupling can succeed in a nonabelian group.
Nor does it characterize the separately constrained case where every packet product is1; nested packets can cancel in that case. That distinction is retained for the next admitted step.
