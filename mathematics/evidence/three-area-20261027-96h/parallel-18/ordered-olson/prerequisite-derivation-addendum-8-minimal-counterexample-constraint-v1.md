## 4. Solvable groups: the elementary kernel is an ACTUAL normal subgroup

For completeness the group-theoretic bridge needed to apply the previous result in the solvable case is proved here, rather than assumed from a classification source.

**Lemma B.** A nontrivial minimal normal subgroup A of a finite solvable group is elementary abelian C_p^m for some prime p and m≥1.

**Proof.** A inherits solvability: its successive derived subgroups are contained in those of G.
The commutator subgroup A' is characteristic in A and hence normal in G. If A'=A≠1, the derived series of A could never terminate, contrary to solvability.
Thus A' is proper and normal in G. Minimal normality forces A'=1; A is abelian.
Choose a nonidentity element and a prime p dividing its order. A suitable power has order p.
The set A_p of all elements of p-power order is a nontrivial characteristic subgroup of the ABELIAN A: products/inverses retain p-power order and automorphisms retain order.
Hence A_p=A by minimality.
A finite abelian group generated by p-power elements has p-power order: add its generators one at a time, and each cyclic quotient has p-power order. Thus A is a finite abelian p-group.
The subgroup pA is characteristic. It is proper, because if pA=A then iterating would give p^e A=A for every e, while a sufficiently large p^e annihilates the finite p-group.
Minimality therefore forces pA=0, so A is a finite vector space over F_p and A≅C_p^m. ∎
