Let `N={0,a,b}` with 0 a zero and `{a,b}` a left-zero band. N is a band. If any of four variables is 0, the normal-band identity `xyzw=xzyw` gives 0 on both sides; otherwise both sides are the first variable. Thus N is normal, hence seminormal, and has a faithful identity-quotient presentation as `P(N,1)`. It is regular, orthodox, and locally inverse: its corners are `{0}`, `{0,a}`, `{0,b}`, each a semilattice.

The idempotent pseudosemilattice operation ∧ on N is its multiplication. In particular `0∧a=a∧0=0`, `0∧b=b∧0=0`, whereas `a∧b=a≠b=b∧a`. Therefore ∧-commutation is **not transitive**: `a∼0∼b` but `a≁b`.

For any physical product P(L,D) with L a semilattice, its idempotent ∧-commutation relation is equality of the Rees actor cell, by the explicit formula proved in the predecessor. It is therefore transitive. An idempotent-separating homomorphic image U remains locally inverse and its idempotent pseudosemilattice is isomorphic to that of P(L,D); any regular subsemigroup's idempotent pseudosemilattice embeds as a subalgebra. These steps import Yeh's theorem that homomorphisms of locally inverse semigroups preserve ∧, as named in the predecessor. Thus transitivity must hold in every regular subsemigroup of every such image. It fails in N, so N admits **no** idempotent-separating image presentation using a semilattice-by-CS product, even after allowing an arbitrary ambient image and subsemigroup embedding.

Hence the seminormal-band-by-CS target is strictly broader than the semilattice-band-by-CS target even **inside locally inverse, orthodox, finite semigroups**; replacing seminormal by semilattice in this Q2 target genuinely changes it. This conclusion is not based on the inexact inference that arbitrary nonorthodoxy rules out semilattice-by-CS products (some nonorthodox completely simple semigroups are positive).
