## 1. Lemma (no single RIGHTSN instance can obstruct an RRL source)

Let S be regular with an idempotent-pure homomorphism q to a CS semigroup, and suppose every local idempotent band E(fSf) is right regular (RRL). If e,x,y∈S satisfy `q(exe)=q(e)=q(eye)`, then

`exeyexe = eyexe`.

Proof. Choose e'∈V(e), f=ee'. Put `p=exee'`, `r=eyee'`. Then `q(p)=q(e)q(e')=q(f)` is idempotent, so p is idempotent by purity; likewise r. Since `ee'e=e`, `fp=p=pf` and `fr=r=rf`, so p,r∈E(fSf), which is right regular: `prp=rp`. Expanding with `ee'e=e`, `prp=exeyexe·e'` and `rp=eyexe·e'`. Right-multiplying by e and using that both words end in e gives the claim. □

Why this is the relevant instance. The RIGHTSN variety is defined by `exeyexe=eyexe`. For any physical host, a substitution of source elements for e,x,y is *label-consistent* (every occurrence of a letter sees the same entering weight) exactly when the prefix actors agree; a direct computation shows all such prefix conditions reduce to `t(exe)=t(e)=t(eye)` for the host actor t, and ker t contains the congruence ν' generated by comparable idempotents. So the RIGHTSN analogue of the SN certificate in `frontier-20261029-SN-instance-no-host-certificate-v1.md` (SHA `2f4fecc838179ddcd0ceba469b37afb8fa0319359a3fc0ea323db666ad418a8d`) **never fires** in an RRL source, and consistent words keep their source evaluation under RIGHTSN rewrites, idempotency deletions and the defining relations. What is still missing for canonical injectivity is control of derivations that pass through label-INCONSISTENT words (for example the insertion `E→EE` when E evaluates to a non-idempotent).
