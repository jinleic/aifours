## 1. Theorem

Let B be a band whose local monoids are regular. Let λ (resp. ρ) be the congruence generated by all pairs x,y lying in a common local monoid eBe with `x R y` (resp. `x L y`). Then

1. `λ⊆R` and `ρ⊆L`, hence `λ∩ρ=Δ`;
2. B/λ is LEFTSN, B/ρ is RIGHTSN, and λ, ρ are the LEAST congruences with these properties;
3. B is a subdirect product of B/λ and B/ρ.

Conversely every subdirect product of a LEFTSN and a RIGHTSN band has regular local monoids. So SN bands are exactly the subdirect products of LEFTSN and RIGHTSN bands; for B regular this recovers Kimura's `B↪B/R×B/L`.

## 2. Proof

**Step 1.** In a regular band M, `x R y` implies `xwy=ywy` for all w∈M. Indeed `x=yx` and `y=yxy`, so `xwy=y x w y=y x y w y=ywy` by the regular identity with letters y,x,w. Dually `x L y` implies `xwy=xwx`.

**Step 2.** Let x,y∈eBe with `x R y`, and z∈B. With `w=eze∈eBe`, `xzy=xwy=ywy=yzy` (using x=xe, y=ey and Step1 in eBe). Hence `(xz)(yz)=(xzy)z=(yzy)z=yz` and symmetrically `(yz)(xz)=xz`, so `xz R yz`. As R is a left congruence on every semigroup, `uxv R uyv` for all u,v∈B¹; λ, the equivalence closure of these translates, lies in R. The dual argument gives ρ⊆L. Bands are H-trivial, so λ∩ρ=Δ.

**Step 3.** In the regular band M=eBe, Step2 applied inside M shows R_M is a congruence, and M/R_M is left regular because `xyx R xy` in every band (`(xyx)(xy)=xy`, `(xy)(xyx)=xyx`). The local monoid of B/λ at e's class is the image of M, a quotient of M/R_M because λ contains R_M; so B/λ is LEFTSN. If B/κ is LEFTSN, its local monoids are left regular, hence R-trivial, so κ contains every local R-pair and λ⊆κ. Dually for ρ. Step3 and λ∩ρ=Δ give the subdirect embedding; the converse holds because left and right regular bands are regular and SN is a variety. □

## 3. Consequences for canonical hosts (all actors, any source)

For a semigroup S with a homomorphism r to `D=M[G;I,Λ;P]`, let `B_V` be the canonical V-band generated by `X_(u,s)` with relations `X_(u,st)=X_(u,s)X_(uw(s,t),t)` (`frontier-20261028-finite-source-canonical-decision-v1.md`, SHA `b6f54918e468755366b215d64ac7fe247fcd3d73be6357105c8817ab47123f47`). λ and ρ are characteristic, hence G-invariant, and by universality `B_LEFTSN=B_SN/λ`, `B_RIGHTSN=B_SN/ρ`. Therefore:

- (a) `B_SN↪B_LEFTSN×B_RIGHTSN`: the SN canonical host separates two elements iff a one-sided canonical host does.
- (b) Within one idempotent r-fibre F, `e↦X_(1,e)` is a band homomorphism (trivial weights). If `e R f` in F, their images are R-related; distinct R-related elements are never ρ-related (ρ⊆L). Hence SN separates e,f **iff RIGHTSN separates them**; dually L-pairs iff LEFTSN separates them.
- (c) For e≠f in F, the chain `e ≥ efe R ef L fef ≤ f` lies in F. So the SN canonical host is injective on F iff it separates every comparable pair (exactly decided by the semilattice Horn criterion, `frontier-20261028-canonical-comparable-horn-exact-v1.md`, SHA `a93c69d997b9e575654fab57f317feed4d1afcecef2be5267a70ba10198be53c`), every R-pair (RIGHTSN) and every L-pair (LEFTSN) in F.

Thus the earlier open "matching one-sided R/L" obligations are neither a weakening nor a strengthening of SN fibre separation: they are equivalent to its R/L parts. Proving them for all original sources remains OPEN.
