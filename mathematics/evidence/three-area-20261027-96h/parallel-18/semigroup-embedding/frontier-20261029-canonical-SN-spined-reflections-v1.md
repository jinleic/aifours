## 1. General canonical theorem

Let S be any semigroup with a specified map into a completely simple Rees actor and a group action in its full canonical mixed band presentation. Write `C_SN`, `C_L`, `C_R`, `C_N` for that canonical presentation in the seminormal, left-seminormal, right-seminormal and normal band varieties respectively. Then, as bands WITH their canonical group actions and source coefficient maps,

`C_SN ≅ C_L ×_(C_N) C_R`.                               (1)

All maps to C_N are the natural canonical reflection maps. This is the FULL fibre product, not merely a subband of it generated by source coefficients. No regularity or weak E-unitarity assumption on the underlying S is needed for (1).

The theorem makes it the full fibre product of these two quotient bands over their join quotient.

The local one-sided identity equivalence identifies the two quotients as C_L and C_R: imposing local LRB/RRB identities after the full SN mixed-source quotient is exactly imposing leftSN/rightSN identities on that canonical presentation. Their join is the least locally-inverse quotient of C_SN. A band is locally inverse iff it is a normal band; this is an explicitly named classical input (Kudryavtseva–Lawson, <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.4296>, Proposition1.1(6)). Hence the join quotient is precisely C_N, by the same universal reflection property. This proves (1) as a band.

The group action preserves every intrinsic local defining-pair relation, so descends to all quotients. The map to their fibre product is equivariant, because it is induced by the original quotient maps. Its inverse is automatically equivariant by bijectivity. Source coefficients map to their corresponding reflection pair, so (1) also preserves the full mixed-source presentation. No ignored source/context relation or injectivity of a factor source map is assumed. □

Thus one can compute C_L and C_R from their smaller free one-sided bands, compute C_N from its content/first/last-letter free normal band, and form the compatible pair set.
