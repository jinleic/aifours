## 2. The universal rectangular invariant

Fmax([X_(u,s)])=((u,c_right(s)),(u g_s,c_left(s)))          (MAX)

### 2.1 Well-definedness on every equation and context

For M, let v=u w(s,t). The product of the two MAX values is

((u,c_right(s)),(v g_t,c_left(t))).

Since c_right(st)=c_right(s), c_left(st)=c_left(t) and v g_t=u g_(st), this is Fmax([X_(u,st)]).

Associativity and idempotency hold in Q_rect. Every LOC side begins and ends with the substituted E-word, so both values are its same rectangular value. Thus arbitrary nonempty word substitutions, both directions, every outer context and transitive chains are respected. Incoherent words are included. ### 2.2 Onto, including arbitrary combinations of coordinates

### 2.4 Every rectangular target factors: last coordinate

### 2.5 Factor, uniqueness and its action

A coordinatewise map between rectangular bands is a homomorphism. The preceding formulas give h=K Fmax on every generator and hence on B_q. Uniqueness follows from Fmax being onto.

                =alpha_k h(b)=alpha_k K Fmax(b).

## 4. Exact verdict for same-fibre R- and L-pairs

Let e,f be idempotents with q(e)=q(f)=(i,g,lambda).

### 4.1 R-pair

Suppose ef=f and fe=e. Then c_right(e)=c_right(f), because e ~_right ef=f. The G coordinates in MAX also agree since q(e)=q(f). Therefore

Fmax(beta(e)) != Fmax(beta(f))
   iff c_left(e) != c_left(f).                            (R-RECT)

If those left components differ, the actual equivariant rectangular quotient supplies a separating host over q by the canonical homomorphism/universal construction.

If they agree, **every rectangular-band-valued homomorphism** on B_q identifies beta(e) and beta(f), by Theorem RECT-MAX. In particular no physical host over q with a rectangular coefficient band can separate them: its coefficient map is an equivariant homomorphism from B_q by S3:155–159.

This negative conclusion concerns rectangular bases only. It is not beta(e)=beta(f) in the full B_q and not original nonembedding.

### 4.2 Distinct dual L-pair

Suppose instead ef=e and fe=f. Then c_left(e)=c_left(f), because e=ef ~_left f. Consequently

Fmax(beta(e)) != Fmax(beta(f))
   iff c_right(e) != c_right(f).                          (L-RECT)

The same positive host consequence and negative universal rectangular limitation hold with the sides exchanged. No symmetry assumption on G or the actual source is used.
