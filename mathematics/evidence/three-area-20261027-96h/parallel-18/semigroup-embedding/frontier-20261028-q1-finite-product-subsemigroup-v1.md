# Finite-product/subsemigroup closure for Q1-positive sources

**Status: NEW AUTHOR WRITTEN structural deduction, with a previously checked finite example; unreviewed/unadopted.** The closure proof is direct. The two universal source classes in §2 are explicitly imported published results, not newly proved here. It does not prove all original sources embed.

## 1. Direct closure

Call a regular S **Q1-positive** here when it is weakly E-unitary and locally r-orthodox and has an embedding into an explicit physical product P(B,D) with B a seminormal band and D completely simple. For every finite list `S₁,...,Sₙ` of Q1-positive semigroups, **every regular subsemigroup T of their direct product is Q1-positive**.

Indeed, the embeddings product to give `∏Sᵢ→∏P(Bᵢ,Dᵢ)`. For finite products, coordinate regrouping gives `∏P(Bᵢ,Dᵢ)≅P(∏Bᵢ,∏Dᵢ)`, with coordinatewise action and a product Rees matrix, as written in `frontier-20261028-q2-product-closure-v1.md`, SHA `9b3cbe5360404898de582cfea279e26fb649f8d7ff8725a01a7b0f5f1d72fdc4`. The band product is seminormal and the finite product of completely simple semigroups is completely simple. Restriction embeds T in this physical host.

A natural-order witness in a regular subsemigroup is also a witness in its ambient regular semigroup, and a natural-order witness in a product projects to witnesses in each factor. Thus weak E-unitarity of all Sᵢ implies that of their product and of T. If `e∈E(T)`, then `E(eTe)⊆∏E(eᵢSᵢeᵢ)`; the latter is a product of regular bands, and the subset is closed under multiplication because T is a subsemigroup. Hence it is a regular band. Since T is assumed regular, every eTe is regular; T is locally r-orthodox. This proves the result, with no appeal to a canonical actor, finite cardinality, or quotient-image extension. □

## 2. Two literature-positive factor classes

The public 2019 survey by M. B. Szendrei, §5.1–5.2, <https://arxiv.org/html/1909.05280v1>, states the following imports:

* Every E-unitary orthodox semigroup with **globally regular** idempotent band has an embedding in a band-by-group product. Hartmann's thesis English summary, theorem 2.15, states the stronger **close** embedding, so the new band belongs to the variety generated by the original regular band. Thus it is itself a regular band. See <https://doktori.bibl.u-szeged.hu/id/eprint/1183/3/HM_angtezis.pdf>. An E-unitary orthodox semigroup is weakly E-unitary, and its E being a regular band implies local r-orthodoxy.
* Every weakly E-unitary locally inverse semigroup embeds in a Pastijn product of a **normal** band by a completely simple semigroup (survey theorem 5.5, attributed to Billhardt and Szendrei). The survey's displayed Pastijn product is our physical multiplication. A locally inverse semigroup is locally r-orthodox because the idempotents in each local inverse semigroup form a semilattice.

Every normal band is regular, and every regular band is seminormal. For the latter implication substitute `x=a`, `y=bc`, `z=cd` into the regular band identity `xyxzx=xyzx`, then use `(bc)(cd)=bcd`; it yields `abcacda=abcda`. Hence both published classes are Q1-positive at the explicit physical convention. We import those published theorems as statements, without claiming to have independently reconstructed their full proofs or resolved every convention in the printed book.

Therefore every regular subsemigroup of any finite product whose factors are drawn from these two classes is Q1-positive, subject to independent checking of those named literature imports and the product convention. Further already-verified physical-embedding families may also be used as factors, at their actual review status.

## 3. The product class is strictly beyond the two factor classes

Let A be the three-element identity-adjoined two-element right-zero band, and D be the 8-element nonorthodox completely simple semigroup in `frontier-20261028-straight-locally-inverse-q2-v1.md`, SHA `61fde8c421f151c0a526c559ba49a7f6df845fbf7ed478410e9d477607226699`. A is a globally regular band and is E-unitary (its trivial group quotient is idempotent-pure); D is weakly E-unitary and locally inverse. The weak-E assertion for D can be checked directly: if an idempotent e=(i,P_(λi)⁻¹,λ) is below x=(j,g,μ), then `ex=xe=e` implies μ=λ, j=i, and g=P_(λi)⁻¹, so x=e.

The product A×D is neither orthodox nor locally inverse: nonclosed idempotents come from D, while the local corner `(1,e₀₀)(A×D)(1,e₀₀)=A×(e₀₀De₀₀)` contains the noncommuting idempotents `(a,e₀₀),(b,e₀₀)`. It is regular and Q1-positive, directly as `P(A,D)` with trivial group action. The exact finite checker `frontier-20261028-q2-product-outside-union-control-v1.py`, SHA `b6f40e63737d5dc1193c4af4f8eed916ba08119c287dded6a8de7da721b2b010`, checked all 13,824 products-of-three, 12 idempotents, all element inverses, the explicit nonorthodox pair and noncommuting local idempotents. This is a finite check, not a proof of the general imports.

## 4. Limits

No arbitrary locally r-orthodox weakly E-unitary source has been shown to factor, or regularly embed in a product of factors, from those classes. In particular this does not settle the same-q R/L separation gap or all of Q1. We make no claim of novelty, independent proof of the two literature theorems, formal proof, or cross-model review.
