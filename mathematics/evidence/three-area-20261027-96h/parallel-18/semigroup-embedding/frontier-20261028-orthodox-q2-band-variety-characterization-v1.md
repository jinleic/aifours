# A variety-parametric boundary for a natural group-actor version of Q2

**Status: NEW AUTHOR WRITTEN, source-dependent candidate; UNREVIEWED / UNADOPTED.** This is a deduction from named literature inputs, not an independently reconstructed proof of those inputs. It concerns arbitrary regular S and the *specific* target of embedding S into an idempotent-separating homomorphic image of a band-by-group semidirect product; it is not a claim about arbitrary completely simple actors, the full printed question's intended interpretation, the original embedding question Q1, or novelty.

## 1. Exact candidate

Let V be any variety of bands. For a regular semigroup S, the following are equivalent:

1. S embeds in a semigroup U for which there are B in V, a group G acting by automorphisms on B, and an onto idempotent-separating homomorphism `π:B⋊G→U`.
2. S is orthodox and its band E(S) belongs to V.

The forward implication is proved here. The reverse implication is conditional on the precise construction imported in §3 below (and, in the inverse case, the classical Fact2 embedding). The text does **not** infer the reverse merely from the headline assertion that every orthodox S embeds in an almost factorizable semigroup: control of the band variety is an additional, essential proof-level ingredient.

For `V=SN`, the seminormal band variety `abcda=abcacda`, condition 2 is equivalent to S being **orthodox and locally r-orthodox**, conditional only on the independently unreviewed local-regular-band/SN equality in `frontier-20261028-local-regular-seminormal-equivalence-v1.md` (SHA-256 `bf7af7afe0440fda2774231c99577fa1d36ebf4b12f70628eddc380a162663ee`). There is no weak E-unitarity assumption in this Q2 candidate. Noninverse examples are included: a nontrivial right-zero band already has the identity presentation with trivial G. It does NOT follow that the larger, CS-actor Q2 target has this orthodox boundary.

## 2. Necessity, directly

Put H=B⋊G with `(b,g)(c,h)=(b(g·c),gh)`. Every `(b,g)` has inverse `(g⁻¹·b,g⁻¹)`, and `E(H)=B×{1}`, a band isomorphic to B. Thus H is orthodox.

Idempotents lift through a surjective homomorphism of a regular semigroup: if `u∈E(U)`, choose `x∈H` with `π(x)=u` and choose an inverse z of `x²`. Then `p=xzx` is idempotent because `p²=xz x² z x=xzx`, and `π(p)=u π(z) u=u`, since π(z) is an inverse of `u²=u`. Therefore `π|E(H)` is bijective; it is a band homomorphism because E(H) is a band. So `E(U)≅B∈V`.

If S is a regular subsemigroup of U, its idempotents are exactly `S∩E(U)` and form a subband of E(U), because both S and E(U) are multiplicatively closed. Therefore S is orthodox and `E(S)∈V` by closure of varieties under subalgebras. This also shows that no nonorthodox regular S can be embedded into *any* homomorphic image of a band-by-group product, even without idempotent separation; the V-bound specifically needs separation.

For orthodox S and `e∈E(S)`, the equality `E(eSe)=eE(S)e` holds: one inclusion is immediate and the other uses closure of E(S) as a band. Each local monoid eSe is regular: for `x=exe`, an inverse of x can be obtained inside eSe by sandwiching an inverse in S and taking the standard reflexive inverse. Hence local r-orthodoxy is exactly local regularity of E(S), and the cited separate candidate identifies that condition with SN.

## 3. Named source input and the variety-preservation step

Primary sources:

* M. Hartmann, *Orthodox semigroups and semidirect products*, University of Szeged dissertation (2008), **unpublished** in the repository's bibliographic classification: <https://doktori.bibl.u-szeged.hu/id/eprint/1183/>. The full Hungarian text is <https://doktori.bibl.u-szeged.hu/id/eprint/1183/1/HM_dissz.pdf>, §5, pp. 37–43, especially 5.5, 5.7, 5.8, 5.9. The English thesis summary dated 2007 is <https://doktori.bibl.u-szeged.hu/id/eprint/1183/3/HM_angtezis.pdf>, §5. The public text was read; the long graph/free-cover proof was NOT independently reconstructed here.
* Szendrei's close-cover theorem/construction as quoted in that dissertation, theorem 2.16 and the beginning of §5; the proof-level selection and compatibility with Hartmann's extension are imported, not reproved here.
* Classical printed Fact2: each inverse semigroup embeds into an idempotent-separating image of a semilattice-by-group product. This is a named import, not independently proved here.

The English §5 summary explicitly starts with a special E-unitary cover `q:T→S`, where q is onto and idempotent-separating, and an embedding of T in `B₀⋊G` with `B₀∈var(E(T))`. It states that a suitable idempotent-pure quotient of this product permits the cover congruence to extend as an idempotent-separating congruence. The dissertation §5 identifies this quotient more precisely: if α is the kernel of q and `α̂` is the congruence of `B₀⋊G` generated by its copy, then (5.5) `α̂|T=α`; if `β=α̂|B₀`, then (5.7) β is G-invariant. Write `B₁=B₀/β`; the text's 5.8 says the natural quotient into `B₁⋊G` still embeds T, and the end of §5 says the induced α-congruence on that product is idempotent-separating and induces `S=T/α` as a subsemigroup of its image. These precise source assertions, their hypotheses, and their use together are the load-bearing import to be independently reviewed.

The group-invariant β indeed gives the needed *band quotient*, rather than an arbitrary new band: define `δ` on `B₀⋊G` by `(b,g) δ (c,h)` iff `g=h` and `b β c`. This is a congruence and the quotient is `(B₀/β)⋊G`. Conversely δ is generated by β in the product: if `b β c`, then

`(b,g)=(b,1)(b,g) ≡ (c,1)(b,g)=(cb,g)`

and, using G-invariance,

`(cb,g)=(c,g)(g⁻¹·b,1) ≡ (c,g)(g⁻¹·c,1)=(c,g)`.

Therefore the thesis quotient preserves all identities of B₀: `B₁∈var(E(T))`.

Since q is surjective, the idempotent-lifting argument in §2 applies to regular T. Since q is idempotent-separating and T and S are orthodox, `q|E(T):E(T)→E(S)` is a band isomorphism. Hence `var(E(T))=var(E(S))`, and if `E(S)∈V` then `B₁∈V` by closure under generated varieties. The source's induced idempotent-separating quotient and embedding now give condition 1 for every **noninverse** orthodox S.

The thesis starts its §5 proof by excluding inverse S, citing the known inverse case. If S is inverse and E(S) has at least two elements, it contains a two-element semilattice, which generates the variety of all semilattices. Consequently every semilattice appearing in Fact2 is in `var(E(S))⊆V`. If E(S) has one element, S is a group and use the one-element band with G=S and the identity quotient. This pays the inverse boundary without assuming the noninverse graph argument applies there.

## 4. Source ambiguity and scope ledger

The English summary's standalone theorem 2.16 describes **idempotent-separating** homomorphic images of bifree orthodox semigroups, whereas the Hungarian thesis theorem 2.16 says `idempotenstiszta` (idempotent-pure) and the English §5 discussion also explicitly uses **idempotent-pure** images. This discrepancy is NOT silently resolved: review the original cited Szendrei paper and the special-cover existence/hypothesis, not just either isolated phrasing. The English summary does separately assert the existence of a closely embeddable E-unitary cover and describes the §5 selected cover, but that is a source import rather than an independent verification of compatibility.

Hartmann's separately published article *Embedding into almost factorizable orthodox semigroups*, Acta Sci. Math. 73 (2007), 71–89, has a zbMATH review at <https://zbmath.org/1135.20040> stating results for **regular** bands of idempotents. We did not verify that that paper contains the broader dissertation §5 construction, and do not attribute it to the published article. The source's 2008 thesis proof has not received an independent mathematical or translation review in this run.

In particular, an arbitrary application of the almost-factorizable existence theorem followed by a newly chosen band-by-group representation does NOT establish its band is in `var(E(S))`; it is specifically the cited cover/congruence construction and the quotient β that would provide this. The converse for a **CS-actor** host remains open here. This candidate is not an unqualified solution, formal proof, novelty finding, or independent review.
