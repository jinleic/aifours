## 3. Any faithful actor image has group C₂

T is a finite regular subsemigroup of D₀, hence CS by the Green-inheritance argument spelled out in `frontier-20261028-quotient68-global-host-optimum-v1.md`, SHA `d259e08d89fab9a4c2132e04ffd685cbf7bf226db69015f6756f6da826027dd2`. Its actor map r is onto and pure by faithfulness of the physical host. Equal r-values have identical source idempotence contexts, so actual q factors through an onto `f:T→D₈`; f is pure because q is pure.

An onto pure map between CS semigroups is injective on each local group: in a maximal group, if f(x)=f(y), then xy⁻¹ maps to an idempotent, so purity makes it an idempotent group element and hence the identity. Since D₈ local groups have order2, T local groups have order at most2. Order1 would make T a rectangular band and its image D₈ idempotent, a contradiction. Thus T has group order2. Surjectivity onto D₈ forces at least two rows and two columns; §2 bounds these by two and three. Hence `|T|=2·2·2=8` or `2·2·3=12`.

If |T|=8, f is a surjective map between sets of size8, so an isomorphism and `r=f⁻¹q`.

If |T|=12, suppose `r₁₂(s)=r₁₂(t)`. Their r₁₂ rows and columns agree, hence s,t are in the same finest source row and column classes, so r(s),r(t) are in the same Rees H-cell of T. Also `q(s)=q(t)`, since q factors through r₁₂. Injectivity of f on that H-cell then gives `r(s)=r(t)`. Therefore `h(r₁₂(s))=r(s)` is a well-defined onto homomorphism `D₁₂→T`; both have 12 elements, so h is an isomorphism and `r=h r₁₂`. □
