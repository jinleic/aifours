## 3. Printed weak E-unitarity implies the imported unitary-E hypothesis

Assume only printed weak E-unitarity and that E(S) is a subsemigroup. First, if b³=b², then d=b² is idempotent and d=db=bd, so d≤b using actual idempotent witnesses d,d. Weak E-unitarity gives b∈E.

Therefore `ab∈E iff ba∈E` for arbitrary a,b∈S: if ab is idempotent then `(ba)³=b(ab)²a=b(ab)a=(ba)²`, and the previous paragraph applies. The converse interchanges a,b. This is a full general statement, not an assumed cancellation law.

Now take e∈E and a∈S with ea∈E. Cyclic idempotence gives ae∈E. As E is a band, z=(ae)(ea)=aea is idempotent; moreover `z=(ae)a=a(ea)`. Thus z≤a with the ACTUAL idempotents ae and ea, and weak E-unitarity gives a∈E. Applying the same argument with ae and ea exchanged proves the right-unitary condition. Hence E is left and right unitary in S. Because S is regular and E is closed, S is orthodox, and the 2019 author-source definition in §2 applies exactly. No extra group-image, global natural-order compatibility, finiteness or nonempty identity assumption was used.

## 4. The pure actor is the ACTUAL syntactic quotient

### 4.1 Source-defined syntactic congruence

Define `s τ t` iff, for every x,y∈S¹ (the formal absent-factor convention), `xsy∈E` exactly when `xty∈E`. This is an equivalence. For any a∈S, closure under left and right multiplication follows by incorporating a into the corresponding context; hence τ is a congruence. No adjoined element is falsely treated as an element of S.

By SZ87 there are a regular band B∈var(E), a group G acting by automorphisms on B, and an injective homomorphism

`ι:S → B ⋊ G`,      `ι(s)=(b_s,ρ(s))`.

Its product is `(b,g)(c,h)=(b α_g(c),gh)`. Because B is a band, an element of B⋊G is idempotent exactly when its group coordinate is 1. Injectivity gives

`ρ(s)=1  iff  s∈E(S)`.                                      (PURE)

S is regular, so ρ(S) is not merely a subsemigroup of G: if s' is a mutual inverse of s, then ρ(s')=ρ(s)^(-1), and 1=ρ(ss') is in the image. Therefore H=ρ(S) is a subgroup even if S has no identity, and ρ:S→H is onto and idempotent pure.

### 4.2 Equality `τ = ker ρ`, including absent contexts

If ρ(s)=ρ(t), then for all x,y∈S¹, extend ρ over a formal absent factor by the group identity. The products ρ(xsy),ρ(xty) coincide, and by PURE they are idempotent in S simultaneously. Thus s τ t.

Conversely if s τ t, choose an actual mutual inverse s' of s. The context `x=s'`, `y=absent` shows s't is idempotent because s's is. PURE gives ρ(s')ρ(t)=1, and ρ(s')=ρ(s)^(-1), so ρ(s)=ρ(t).

Thus q:S→S/τ is a pure **group** quotient, not merely an abstract unrelated quotient, and the unique group isomorphism

`j:S/τ → H`,     `j(q(s))=ρ(s)`

is well-defined, onto and injective. This establishes the actual quotient match without assuming a general pure-actor push-forward theorem. As a byproduct, any two idempotent-pure group homomorphisms of the SAME regular S have identical kernels; the proof is the same inverse/context argument. In particular there is no hidden identity-image case or arbitrary contextual cancellation.

## 5. Explicit canonical-band homomorphism and R/L separation

Take q:S→C=S/τ as above, now a group, and write its identity as 1. Let V be the band variety in the actual canonical presentation, consisting of idempotent semigroups whose local corners are regular bands. Form B_q as the free V-band on all `X_(u,s)` with `u∈C,s∈S`, modulo every relation

`X_(u,s) X_(u q(s),t) = X_(u,st)`.                          (M)

This is precisely the all-rows-one/all-columns-one group specialization of the existing Rees-coordinate definition; no numerical quotient or restricted finite alphabet is substituted. Set `β(s)=[X_(1,s)]` and `κ_q(s)=(β(s),q(s))` in the semidirect product B_q⋊C.

Since E satisfies (RB), every B∈var(E) satisfies (RB), and every local subband of B satisfies it. Thus B∈V. Restrict the original G-action to H and transport it along j. Assign on **every** generator

`X_(u,s) ↦ α_(j(u))(b_s)`.

Each M relation is respected:

`α_j(u)(b_s) α_(j(u)ρ(s))(b_t)`
`= α_j(u)(b_s α_ρ(s)(b_t))`
`= α_j(u)(b_(st))`,

where the last equality is the band coordinate of the ACTUAL source homomorphism ι. Every V identity, every substitution and all outer contexts hold in B; therefore this assignment induces an equivariant band homomorphism `h:B_q→B`. In particular `h(β(s))=b_s`.

If κ_q(s)=κ_q(t), then q(s)=q(t) implies ρ(s)=ρ(t), and β(s)=β(t) implies b_s=b_t after applying h. Hence ι(s)=ι(t), and source injectivity gives s=t. This proves the canonical map injective for the whole declared global-band subclass. It immediately proves separation for both R-idempotent and L-idempotent pairs in every actual q fibre; in fact it proves more, namely separation of all source elements by the complete pair `(β,q)`.

The proof does not assert that β alone separates elements in distinct q fibres, that B_q is finite, or that arbitrary R/L host data imply the printed full theorem. It depends on existence of the B⋊G embedding supplied by SZ87, not on a newly constructed explicit global B for every S.
