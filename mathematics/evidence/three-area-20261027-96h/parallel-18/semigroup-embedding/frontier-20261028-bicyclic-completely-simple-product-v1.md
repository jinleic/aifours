`(i,j)(k,ℓ)=(i+(k-j)_+, ℓ+(j-k)_+)`,

Define its actual syntactic congruence τ_S by all two-sided idempotence tests, and q_S:S→S/τ_S. Let θ:Bic→Z be θ(i,j)=i-j, and let τ_C be C's own actual syntactic congruence.

**Claim.**

1. S is a regular, weakly E-unitary, locally inverse (hence locally r-orthodox) original-class source. Its actual pure actor is

   `S/τ_S ≅ Z × (C/τ_C)`,     `q_S(b,c)=(θ(b),q_C(c))`.          (1)

   The quotient is completely simple; the equality is proved below, not assumed from an arbitrary product-of-congruences slogan.

It separates **all** idempotents of S, and `(β,q_S)` is injective on ALL S. In particular it separates every actual same-q R-idempotent pair and every actual same-q L-idempotent pair.

## 2. Original source conditions for all C

Realize Bic as partial translations on N_0: `(i,j)` has domain n≥j and sends n↦n-j+i. Composition gives the displayed product, so associativity is literal partial-map composition, its identity is(0,0), its inverse is `(j,i)`, and its idempotents are the diagonal pairs. Its pure group quotient θ is onto Z and θ=0 iff idempotent.

In Rees coordinates, C has multiplication

`(i,g,λ)(j,h,μ)=(i,g p_(λ,j) h,μ)`.

Every element lies in its H-class group; for `e=(i,p_(λ,i)^(-1),λ)`, the corner eCe is exactly the group with row i and column λ. Hence Bic and C are regular, their product S is regular, and every idempotent corner of S is the direct product of an inverse monoid and a group. It is inverse, hence orthodox with semilattice idempotents and in particular has a regular-band idempotent set. This is the original local-r condition, with no global E(S)-closure assumed.

The natural order on any completely simple semigroup is equality. Indeed if a=f b=b g for idempotents f,g, the two product descriptions force a and b to have the same Rees row and column, so f and b have the same row. In that case the explicit Rees product gives f b=b, hence a=b. This works for arbitrary, not only normalized, P.

The product actor in (1) is idempotent pure (§4) and completely simple: if `C/τ_C=M[H;I',Λ';Q]`, then `Z×(C/τ_C)` is `M[Z×H;I',Λ';(0,Q)]`. A homomorphism preserves the printed natural-order witnesses. Thus if an idempotent e of S satisfies e≤a, then q_S(e)≤q_S(a); the completely simple actor has equality natural order, so q_S(a)=q_S(e) is idempotent and purity forces a idempotent. Therefore S is weakly E-unitary. No use is made of a fictitious S identity when C is nonunital.

## 3. A Rees-context lemma, including BOTH absent-factor cases

Let `c=(i,g,λ)` and `d=(j,h,μ)` in the arbitrary C above. For each `ν∈Λ`, `k∈I`, put

`K_c(ν,k)=p_(ν,i) g p_(λ,k)`, `K_d(ν,k)=p_(ν,j) h p_(μ,k)`.

**Lemma.** c τ_C d iff `K_c(ν,k)=K_d(ν,k)` for every ν,k.

If c τ_C d, for any fixed ν,k choose arbitrary row a and column b and the ACTUAL contexts

`x=(a,1,ν)`, `y=(k,K_c(ν,k)^(-1) p_(b,a)^(-1),b)`.

Then xcy is the idempotent of H_(a,b). Equivalence of idempotence for xdy forces `K_d(ν,k)=K_c(ν,k)` by group cancellation. This uses genuine source elements, not an adjoined common identity.

Conversely, assume all K values coincide. For actual x=(a,u,ν), y=(k,v,b), both products are idempotent precisely when `u K_c(ν,k) v=p_(b,a)^(-1)` and the same equation for K_d; the tests agree.

For x absent and y actual, cy is idempotent iff `g p_(λ,k) v p_(b,i)=1`, equivalently `K_c(b,k) v=1`, so the K identity supplies the same test for d. For y absent and x actual, xc is idempotent iff `u K_c(ν,a)=1`, again the same for d. For BOTH absent, if c is idempotent then `g p_(λ,i)=1`; equality at `(ν,k)=(μ,i)` gives

`p_(μ,i)=p_(μ,j) h p_(μ,i)`,

so `p_(μ,j)h=1` and d is idempotent. Reverse c,d for the converse. This completes all S¹ contexts, including noncommutative groups and nonnormalized sandwich matrices.

In particular, equality of all ACTUAL two-sided context tests already forces c τ_C d: its group-parameter comparison produces every K equality, and the above handles the omitted contexts.

## 4. Exact τ of the infinite product

For regular C, every c admits actual x_C,y_C such that x_C c y_C is idempotent: take a mutual inverse c' and set `x_C=c'`, `y_C=c'c`. Likewise every q_C class is idempotent pure by the regular-source idempotent-lifting argument reconstructed in `frontier-20261028-cryptogroup-band-retraction-v1.md` §2. C/τ_C is completely simple: this follows either from the same fully reconstructed SQ-E lemma there, since C is weakly E-unitary and locally a group as in §2, or from the ordinary theorem that homomorphic images of completely simple semigroups are completely simple.

If θ(b)=θ(b') and c τ_C d, then for any actual or absent product contexts the Bic product tests idempotent simultaneously by θ-purity, and the C tests agree by τ_C. Thus `(b,c) τ_S (b',d)`.

Conversely suppose `(b,c) τ_S (b',d)`. Let b' denote the SECOND Bic element and let b^{-1} be the unique Bic inverse. Choose actual product contexts

`x=(b^{-1}, c')`,  `y=(1_Bic,c'c)`

with c'∈V(c). Then x(b,c)y is idempotent. Its τ-equivalent counterpart is also idempotent; its Bic component `b^{-1} b'` has θ=0, hence θ(b')=θ(b). For any actual x_C,y_C, use contexts `(b^{-1},x_C)` and `(1_Bic,y_C)`. Both Bic factors are now idempotent, so equality of product idempotence tests gives

`x_C c y_C ∈E(C) iff x_C d y_C ∈E(C)`.

Section 3 upgrades those ACTUAL two-sided tests to `c τ_C d`, including absent factors. This proves exactly

`(b,c) τ_S (b',d)  iff  θ(b)=θ(b') and c τ_C d`,

and establishes (1). The argument quantifies over the whole infinite Bic and arbitrary C; it does not infer quotient equality from finitely many contexts or conflate the homomorphic image with an independently chosen actor.

## 5. Uniform explicit canonical-band invariant

Use arbitrary Rees coordinates for C/τ_C, with structure group H. The structure group for the actor `Z×(C/τ_C)` is `Z×H`, and its sandwich entries have first coordinate0. Let

- D be all integer upper tails A_n={m∈Z:m≥n}, with intersection as multiplication;
- R=I×Λ be the rectangular band `(i,λ)(j,μ)=(i,μ)`, using the **source C** row/column sets;
- B=D×R, with the `Z×H` action `α_(u,h)(A_n,(i,λ))=(A_(u+n),(i,λ))`.

D is a semilattice, R is a rectangular band, and their product satisfies every local regular-band identity, so B is in V. Define for a source element `s=((i,j),(a,g,λ))`

`b_s=(A_i,(a,λ))`,  `r_s=(i-j,q_C(a,g,λ))=q_S(s)`.

For t=((k,ℓ),(b,h,μ)), the actor weight's Z coordinate is i-j; the other coordinate acts trivially on B. Therefore

`b_s α_(weight(s,t))(b_t)`
`=(A_i ∩ A_(i-j+k),(a,μ))`
`=(A_(i+(k-j)_+),(a,μ))=b_(st)`.

Thus `j:S→P(B,S/τ_S)`, `j(s)=(b_s,q_S(s))`, is an actual homomorphism into the WHOLE physical product. It is also the source of a canonical-band map: for every group label `(u,h)∈Z×H` and every source s,

`X_((u,h),s) ↦ α_(u,h)(b_s)`.

The displayed all-source equation proves every mixed relation and the V identities hold automatically, hence this is an equivariant homomorphism from the actual presented B_q. In particular hβ(s)=b_s; this is an all-derivation invariant, not a witness only on coherent words.

To prove injectivity, equality b_s=b_t determines the first Bic coordinate i and the C source row and column. Equality of θ then determines the second Bic coordinate j. The C coordinates lie in the same H-class group. Since q_C is pure and their q_C values agree, multiplying by the H-group inverse of one makes their quotient image idempotent; purity makes that product the unique H-group idempotent, so the C elements are equal. Hence j is injective and so is κ_q. On idempotents, the band coordinate alone already determines `(n,n)` and the unique C idempotent in its fixed source row/column, so it separates ALL source idempotents, not merely the same-q pairs.

The Z shift is essential in general: all band homomorphisms out of Bic collapse its distinct idempotents 1 and qp, as proved in the preceding bicyclic candidate. The source-row rectangle and the shifted tail are synchronized with the SAME actual actor; no external actor-coordinate push-forward theorem or action globalization is assumed.
