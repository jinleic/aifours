## 1. Actual scope and the shared-prime correction

    |P| = 15·2^a,   a≥0.                                      (1)

The words "two split odd head primes" in the dispatch cannot be a hypothesis for an actual anchored tiling. Both 3 and 5 must divide |Q| too: for ℓ∈{3,5}, if ℓ∤|Q|, Tijdeman gives P⊕ℓQ; the A-difference ℓ and the ℓ-scaled Y-difference 1 have the same gcd ℓ with n, contradicting Sands. No full-cardinality or tiling obstruction follows from the wording correction.

The classical imports are only Sands' divisor-exclusion theorem, Łaba–Londner [arXiv:2106.14042v3, p. 10, Thm 2.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14042v3), and Tijdeman's coprime dilation theorem, [same paper, p. 3 and proof of Cor. 6.2 on p. 26](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14042v3). ## 2. The valid cross-intersection implication

In any actual anchored tiling satisfying (1), consider x=q−q′ for two Q-elements of opposite parity that agree mod 11. Since 11 divides n and x, the implication

    3 | x  OR  5 | x                                          (2)

is necessary. Indeed, if neither 3 nor 5 divides x, then g=gcd(x,n) is odd, divisible by 11 and coprime to 15. The integer t=g/11 is therefore coprime to |P|. In tP⊕Q, the scaled A-difference t·11=g has the same gcd g with n as x; g<n since n is even. Sands forbids the collision.

For one fixed residue class mod 11, let U and V be the sets of (q mod 3,q mod 5) labels of its even and odd Q-elements. Equation (2) says that **each u∈U and v∈V agree in at least one coordinate**. It says nothing by itself about pairs of labels within the same parity set.

The invalid inference is that all labels in U∪V must have a common 3-coordinate or a common 5-coordinate. The explicit opposite-corners rectangle already falsifies it:

    U = {(0,0),(2,2)},       V = {(0,2),(2,0)}.              (3)

Each cross pair agrees in one coordinate, but both coordinates vary on U∪V. This is an abstract cross-intersection counterexample, not a tiling.

## 4. What exactly fails and what remains

The single-split-prime proof propagated a fixed congruence along a connected graph because each relevant opposite-parity pair had to agree modulo **the same single prime**. Here the derived implication is only a disjunction, "agree mod 3 **or** agree mod 5". Rectangle (3), realized by the anchor-compatible lift above, uses a different prime along alternating edges and has neither a global mod-3 label nor a global mod-5 label. Therefore a connectivity argument that identifies one common congruence class is invalid, even after the explicitly checked scalar-anchor and parity constraints.

A complete obstruction for (1) must use an additional same-tiling restriction not present in this relaxation: for example simultaneous constraints from non-anchor P-differences, full Q cardinality and coverage, a justified multi-coordinate compatibility theorem, or stronger cyclotomic ownership with all import hypotheses checked. The example neither proves such a restriction impossible nor substitutes a chosen small order for the all-order target. The literal |P|=15·2^a branch remains OPEN.
