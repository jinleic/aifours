## 2. An intransitivity lemma from an actual prime cycle

Suppose r is a prime >7 with n/2<r≤n−2 and H<A_n has r-free index. A Sylow r-subgroup is generated by an r-cycle R fixing t=n−r points, and H contains such a cycle. If H is transitive, it is primitive: a nontrivial block system has fewer than r blocks, so R fixes each block and its r moved points lie in one, forcing n≥2r. For t≥3, Jordan therefore makes H contain A_n, a contradiction.

If n is an odd power of a prime p≠7, Bertrand with m=(n−1)/2 gives such an r, and t is even. Only t=2 needs extra treatment. Jones's k=2 theorem would force q=n−1=r+1 to be an even prime power 2^e. If p≠3, neither n=p^a nor r>7 is divisible by 3, but one of 2^e−1 and 2^e+1 is divisible by 3, impossible. If p=3, r=2^e−1 prime forces e itself prime. For e>3, e≡1 or 5 mod 6, so 2^e+1≡3 or 6 mod 9; it cannot equal 3^a≥17. The cases e=2,3 give n=5,9, both below scope. Thus t=2 is also impossible for a proper transitive H. Every r-free proper H is intransitive in these odd-base cases.

If n=2^a≥17, put m=n/2 and apply Bertrand to m−1: it gives m−1<r<2m−2=n−2. Since m is composite, r>m=n/2, and t=n−r≥3, so Jordan directly gives intransitivity. This handles all powers of 2 without assuming a prime-gap threshold.

For n=7^a, the separate choices in Section 4 ensure t≥3, so the same Jordan intransitivity argument applies.

## 3. The common-index prime and the exact P7 consequence

For any of the r choices above, the intransitive H-orbit containing the r moved points has a complement of size u with 1≤u≤t. Its alternating set stabilizer has index C(n,u), which divides [A_n:H]. In characteristic p,
\[(1+X)^{p^a}=1+X^{p^a},\]
so p divides C(n,u) for every 1≤u<n. Hence **every r-free proper-subgroup index is divisible by p**.

In a three-index pattern with pairwise gcd exactly 7, at most one index is divisible by r>7, so two are r-free. If p≠7, those two indices are both divisible by the distinct primes 7 and p, hence have gcd at least 7p>7, contradiction. This proves direct P7 for every prime-power degree n≥17 with p≠7. When p>7, the common p itself exceeds 7, giving the stronger no-three-indices-with-gcd≤7 statement and Sun8 without S7/R8.
