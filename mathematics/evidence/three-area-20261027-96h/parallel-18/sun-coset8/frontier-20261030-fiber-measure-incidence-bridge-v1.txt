B_7(X)<=7 (Sun8(X)) means every eight pairwise-disjoint nonempty actual LEFT
cosets of finite-index subgroups of X have two subgroup indices with gcd>=8;
subgroups and labels may repeat and unit indices are allowed. A BAD FAMILY
in G is a family C_i=g_iU_i, i in a finite label set, of pairwise-disjoint
actual left cosets of finite-index subgroups with ALL pairwise index gcds in
{2,...,7}. The original conjecture says no bad family of size eight exists.
Throughout, G is an arbitrary group, N a normal subgroup, Q=G/N, pi:G->Q.

1. Adopted-scope lemmas, re-proved (self-contained)

(L1) Index product. For U<=G of finite index put V=pi(U)<=Q, K=U cap N.
Then NU is a subgroup, [G:NU]=[Q:V]=:a, [NU:U]=[N:K]=:b via the left-coset
bijection N/K -> NU/U, and n=[G:U]=ab. Consequently
gcd(a_i,a_j)gcd(b_i,b_j) divides gcd(n_i,n_j) for any two labels.

(L2) Coprime simultaneous intersection. For finitely many finite-index
subgroups W_i of ANY group X with pairwise-coprime indices d_i, every
prescribed tuple of actual left W_i-cosets has a common point: the map
X/(intersection W_i) -> product X/W_i on left cosets is injective, the
domain index is at most and divisible by the product of the d_i, hence
equal, and the map is onto. Unit indices and repeated whole-group factors
are included. In particular two actual left cosets of coprime-index
subgroups always meet, so two DISJOINT left cosets have non-coprime
subgroup indices.

(L3) Measure lemma. If x_iH_i, i=1..k, are pairwise-disjoint actual left
cosets of finite-index subgroups of ANY group X, then sum_i 1/[X:H_i]<=1.
Proof: M=intersection_i core_X(H_i) has finite index and M<=H_i; in X/M
the images remain pairwise disjoint (any meeting would lift to a meeting
inside some x_jH_j since M<=core(H_j)), with unchanged indices; finite
counting gives the inequality. This handles repeated subgroups, unit
indices and infinite X without any finiteness assumption on X.

(L4) Conjugated fiber identity (adopted f265 A scope). If A_i=pi(g_i)V_i
and A_j=pi(g_j)V_j meet at q, choose ONE t in G over q and x_l in
C_l cap Nt. Then, for every label l with q in A_l,
  (C_l cap Nt)t^{-1} = (x_lt^{-1})(tK_lt^{-1}),
an actual LEFT coset in N of the conjugated subgroup, of index b_l; and
these cosets are pairwise disjoint for distinct labels because the C_l are.
The single common t and the conjugation are essential; nothing here needs
U_l normal, N finite, or the labels distinct.

(L5) Finite-core reduction (adopted f265 A scope). R=intersection core_G(U_i)
is normal of finite index inside every U_i; in G/R all eight cosets, all
indices n_i, both factor index lists a_i,b_i, and every subset-intersection
question of the images A_i are preserved, and both factor B_7 bounds pass to
the quotients of N and Q. Used only to speak of finite orders when helpful;
every statement below is proved without it except where noted.

2. THEOREM 1 (fiber-measure incidence inequality; all-variable).

Let C_i=g_iU_i, i=1..k, be a bad family in G (pairwise disjoint, all
pairwise index gcds in {2,...,7}). For every q in Q let
S(q)={i : q in A_i}, where A_i=pi(g_i)pi(U_i). Then:

  (T1.1) sum_{i in S(q)} 1/b_i <= 1 for every q in Q;
  (T1.2) if also B_7(N)<=7, then |S(q)|<=7 for every q;
  (T1.3) both hold with one common conjugating t per point, repeated
         subgroups allowed, and b_i=1 permitted.

Proof. Fix q and one t over q. Each i in S(q) contributes the actual
disjoint left N-coset of (L4) with index b_i; apply (L3) in N. This gives
(T1.1) with the simultaneous single-t conjugation exactly as in (L4);
nothing distinguishes repeated subgroups (their cosets are still disjoint)
or unit indices. For (T1.2), if |S(q)|>=8, the slices are >=8 disjoint
N-cosets whose pairwise index gcds divide the original pairwise gcds, hence
are <=7, contradicting B_7(N)<=7. (T1.3) is the conjunction of the two
observation scopes just made. Theorem 1 needs no finiteness of G, N or Q,
no normality of any U_i, and no disjointness of the images A_i. □

(T1.1) is strictly stronger pointwise than the global measure bound
sum_i 1/n_i<=1 obtained from (L3) applied to the C_i themselves; that
global bound is the q-integral of (T1.1), so no information is lost by
stating (T1.1) alone. (T1.2) is independent of (T1.1): eight slices of
equal, sufficiently large indices satisfy (T1.1) with room to spare yet
violate (T1.2).

3. Corollaries (all-variable, same hypotheses as Theorem 1)

(C1) Isolation. If b_i=1 (that is, N<=U_i), then A_i cap A_j is empty for
every other label j of the family. Indeed q in A_i cap A_j would give two
slices at q, the i-slice being the entire fiber; (T1.1) forbids a second
nonempty slice. So a label whose subgroup contains N meets no other image.

(C2) Coprime-index clique. If the labels in J have pairwise-coprime a_i,
then by (L2) all A_i, i in J, share one common point q, and (T1.1) gives
sum 1/b_i<=1 with each pair's kernel gcd in {2,...,7}; e.g. this forces
the b_i to be far from uniformly small, and bounds the size of any
coprime-a subfamily through sum 1/b_i<=1 together with (T1.2).

(C3) Complete pair-type table. For every pair i!=j in a bad family, with
d_A=gcd(a_i,a_j), d_B=gcd(b_i,b_j), g=gcd(n_i,n_j) in {2,...,7}:
  (i) d_A d_B divides g; (d_A,d_B)!=(1,1);
  (ii) if A_i cap A_j is nonempty then d_A in {1,2,3} and d_B>=2;
  (iii) if A_i cap A_j is empty then d_A in {2,...,7} and d_B=g/d_A=1
        whenever d_A in {4,5,6,7};
  (iv) arithmetic alone never suffices: patterns with d_A=d_B=1 and g>=2
       satisfy (i) but are excluded by the actual coset structure, since
       coprime quotient indices force the images to meet (L2) and meeting
       images force kernel gcd >=2 by (L4)+(L2).
The table is derived from (L1),(L2),(L4) only; no incidence assumption on
the images is made beyond what a bad family provides.

4. THEOREM 2 (exact failing premise of the natural closure bridge).

Consider the natural closure bridge for a hypothetical bad eight-family
under BOTH original factor bounds B_7(N)<=7, B_7(Q)<=7, assembled from:
(B1) the pair-type table (C3); (B2) the pointwise fiber capacity (T1.1)
with (T1.2); (B3) the quotient independence bound alpha(Gamma)<=7, where
Gamma is the quotient overlap graph (an independent 8-set would be eight
disjoint images with pairwise a-gcds <=7, contradicting B_7(Q)<=7);
(B4) the global measure bound sum 1/n_i<=1. The bridge fails, and its
exact failing premise is the following missing principle.

MISSING PREMISE (forced overlap). Some statement that lower-bounds the
actual overlap of the quotient images of a bad family -- for instance a
positive lower bound on sum_{i<j}|A_i cap A_j|/|Q| uniform over bad
families, or a lower bound on the maximum point multiplicity
max_q |S(q)|, or any structural restriction forcing Gamma to contain a
large intersecting configuration beyond alpha(Gamma)<=7.

Proof that the premise is missing, i.e. that (B1)-(B4) cannot supply it.
Observe large-prime inflation: given any assignment of indices and
incidence satisfying (B1)-(B4), replace a_i by a_i*p for a fresh prime p>7.
No pairwise gcd d_A, d_B or g changes (p divides no other label's index);
no b_i changes; the allowed incidence patterns and |S(q)| are untouched;
(B3) is a statement about disjointness patterns, unaffected by index
inflation; and (B4) only improves, since 1/n_i decreases. Hence every
constraint in (B1)-(B4) is inflation-invariant or inflation-monotone. On
the other hand the only general lower bound on an edge intersection is
|A_i cap A_j| = |Q|/[Q:V_i cap V_j] >= |Q|/(a_i a_j) (the intersection, if
nonempty, is a single left coset of V_i cap V_j, whose index is at most
a_i a_j), and this bound degenerates exactly under inflation. Therefore
no constraint family all of whose members are inflation-invariant or
inflation-monotone in the sense displayed for (B1)-(B4) can force a
uniform positive MEASURE lower bound on relative overlap: after inflation
every such forced lower bound becomes arbitrarily small while all of
(B1)-(B4) still hold.
Dually, rich-overlap configurations are the ones constrained by (T1.1),
(T1.2): the capacity side of the bridge is complete. The gap is precisely
the sparse-overlap regime, where neither factor bound restricts the images:
B_7(Q)<=7 forbids only eight pairwise-disjoint images, and B_7(N)<=7
constrains only the fibers at points where images actually meet. □

Reading of Theorem 2. Any completion of THIS bridge must add a premise
that either forces structural, scale-free overlap of the images beyond
alpha(Gamma)<=7, or relates index scale to incidence so as to break the
inflation degeneration just displayed; (B1)-(B4) provide neither. This is
a property of the proof obligations, not an assertion that a bad family
exists: Theorem 2 does not construct or exclude any family, and does not
claim the constraint system (B1)-(B4) alone is consistent for eight
labels. It records exactly which premise a completion must add. In the
rich-overlap direction the bridge is closed by Theorem 1; the honest
remaining duty is a forced-overlap principle, or an eight-label
counterexample to the original conjecture, neither of which is produced
here.
