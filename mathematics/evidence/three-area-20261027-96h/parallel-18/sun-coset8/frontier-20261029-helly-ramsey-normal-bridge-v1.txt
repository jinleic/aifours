1. Exact original scope and new result

For any group X and integer T>=2 write B_T(X)<=r for the assertion: every
collection of r+1 pairwise-disjoint nonempty LEFT cosets of finite-index
subgroups of X has a pair of subgroup indices with gcd>T. The subgroups and
indices may repeat. This formulation does NOT claim a finite maximum exists.
Thus the original Sun8(X) assertion is B_7(X)<=7.

Call a group Q left-coset 3-Helly if any three pairwise-intersecting LEFT
cosets of finite-index subgroups of Q have nonempty common intersection.

THEOREM A. Let G be ANY group and N any normal subgroup. If B_7(N)<=2,
B_7(G/N)<=2, and G/N is left-coset 3-Helly, then B_7(G)<=5. In particular
Sun8(G) holds. No covering, splitting, finite-original-group, solvability or
normality of the eight individual subgroups is assumed. The hypothesis B_7<=2
is substantially STRONGER than Sun8; the theorem is a conditional bridge,
not a proof that every group satisfies Sun8.

THEOREM B (all-variable clique-Helly form). Let G be any group, N normal,
T>=2 and positive integers r,s. Suppose B_T(N)<=r, B_T(G/N)<=s, and every
finite pairwise-intersecting family of finite-index left cosets in G/N has
nonempty common intersection. Then B_T(G)<=R(r+1,s+1)-1, where R(a,b) is
the ordinary finite two-colour Ramsey number. Theorem A uses only 3-Helly
rather than this stronger all-size Helly property and uses R(3,3)=6.

Equivalent obstruction form without any Helly hypothesis: if B_7(N)<=2 and
B_7(G/N)<=2 and six pairwise-disjoint original cosets have every index gcd
at most seven, then their six projected quotient cosets contain a triple
which is pairwise-intersecting but has EMPTY total intersection. In particular,
any successful unrestricted extension of this route must eliminate or handle
such non-Helly triangles, not assume them away.

2. Actual left-coset fiber identities

Let pi:G->Q=G/N. For every original pair g_i,U_i, set

  A_i = pi(g_i) pi(U_i),   K_i=U_i cap N,
  a_i=[Q:pi(U_i)],        b_i=[N:K_i].

All indices are finite and [G:U_i]=a_i b_i: first divide by NU_i, then use
the bijection N/(N cap U_i) -> NU_i/U_i. Therefore gcd(a_i,a_j) and
gcd(b_i,b_j) divide gcd([G:U_i],[G:U_j]).

If a quotient point q lies in all A_i for i in a finite label set J, choose
t in G above q and, for each i in J, choose x_i in g_i U_i cap Nt. Then

  (g_i U_i cap Nt)t^{-1} = (x_i t^{-1})(t K_i t^{-1}),

an actual LEFT coset in N of a subgroup of index b_i. The equality follows
because y=x_i u lies in Nt exactly when pi(u)=1. Since the original cosets
are pairwise disjoint, these actual kernel cosets are pairwise disjoint. The
same t is used throughout and all conjugated K_i remain subgroups of N.

If instead the A_i, i in J, are pairwise disjoint, they themselves form
actual pairwise-disjoint LEFT cosets of finite-index subgroups in Q, with
indices a_i. These are exact implications in both infinite and finite G;
no quotient image is asserted disjoint merely because its original was.

3. Proof of the bridge

Assume a family of m pairwise-disjoint original cosets has all original
index gcds <=T. Make a graph on its labels: connect i,j exactly when the
actual quotient cosets A_i,A_j intersect. Under quotient finite-coset Helly,
a clique of size r+1 has a common q; the first fiber identity gives r+1
actual disjoint N-cosets, with all pairwise kernel-index gcds <=T, contrary
to B_T(N)<=r. An independent set of size s+1 gives s+1 actual disjoint
quotient cosets, with quotient-index gcds <=T, contrary to B_T(Q)<=s.
Thus Ramsey's theorem forces m<R(r+1,s+1), proving Theorem B.

For r=s=2 the proof uses only triples. In a graph on six vertices, choose
one vertex; among its five incident edges at least three have the same
colour. If two of those neighbours have that colour between them, they and
the chosen vertex form a monochromatic triangle; otherwise those three
neighbours form a triangle of the other colour. Hence R(3,3)<=6. A triangle
of quotient intersections has a common q by 3-Helly and contradicts
B_7(N)<=2; a triangle of quotient disjointness contradicts B_7(Q)<=2.
Thus no six-label bad family exists and Theorem A follows. Applying this to
any six members of an eight-coset family gives the exact original Sun8
conclusion for this conditional class.

The obstruction form follows from the same six-vertex argument without
Helly: Q's B_7<=2 prohibits an independent triple, so an intersecting
triangle exists; if it had a common point the N fiber argument would violate
B_7(N)<=2. The triangle is therefore necessarily non-Helly.

4. Cyclic quotient and a nonabelian extension family

Every cyclic group Q, finite or infinite, has the all-size finite-coset
Helly property. For Q=Z, a finite-index coset is a congruence x=c_i mod a_i;
for Q=Z/MZ, the same holds with every a_i dividing M. Two congruence cosets
intersect iff c_i=c_j mod gcd(a_i,a_j). Pairwise compatibility implies global
compatibility by generalized CRT: amalgamate inductively with modulus L=lcm of
the earlier a_i and use gcd(L,a_new)=lcm_i gcd(a_i,a_new). In the finite
case the resulting solution modulo L is well-defined modulo M because L|M.

There are genuine nonabelian groups where both B_7 hypotheses equal 2,
where the source-free parent criterion requiring mu>=8 does not apply, and
where the earlier sufficient hypothesis that every two disjoint kernel cosets
have gcd>=8 is false. For arbitrary primes p,q>7 let

  N=D_{2p}=<r,s | r^p=s^2=1, srs=r^{-1}>,
  Q=C_{2q},     G=D_{2p} x C_{2q}.

Use N=D_{2p} x {1}, so G/N=Q and Q is cyclic. In N and Q, subgroup indices
divide 2p and 2q respectively. In either factor, an index in {p,2p} (or
{q,2q}) can occur at most once in a bad family, since two such indices have
gcd at least that large prime. All other proper subgroup indices are 2.
Each factor has a unique index-two subgroup (the Sylow-p rotation subgroup
or the order-q subgroup), so two disjoint index-two cosets partition that
factor and admit no third nonempty coset. Hence B_7(N)<=2 and B_7(Q)<=2;
each is exactly 2 because its two index-two cosets are disjoint with gcd 2.
Theorem A gives B_7(G)<=5 and Sun8(G). Here mu(G)=2, so the cited parent
criterion with mu>=8 does not certify this example. Its kernel has two
disjoint index-two cosets, so a pairwise kernel gcd>=8 condition fails.
On the other hand the subgroup C_p x C_q has index 4 in G: its four cosets
are disjoint and all pairwise index gcds are 4. Consequently B_7(G)>=4;
the theorem does not claim the upper bound 5 is sharp.

The theorem itself is all-variable; this illustrative family is not an
unrestricted G result, a counterexample to Sun8, or a new simple-family
classification. No numerical sample or search is used.

5. Exact limitations and failed shortcuts

(a) Sun8(N) and Sun8(Q) alone are NOT the assumptions of Theorem A: they
say B_7<=7, not B_7<=2. For example, C7 satisfies Sun8 because it has only
seven elements, but its seven singleton cosets have indices all 7, so
B_7(C7)>=7. Taking N=Q=C7 and G=N x Q, the seven original cosets
{(i,y): y in C7}, i in C7, are pairwise disjoint, all have original index 7,
and ALL project onto the same quotient coset Q. Both factors satisfy Sun8.
This exact actual-coset example disproves any step asserting that factor
Sun8 makes disjoint originals project disjointly or bounds an overlap fiber
by two. It supplies seven, not eight, cosets and is NOT a counterexample to
Sun8 or to an eight-coset implication.

(b) 3-Helly is a real additional structural hypothesis, not automatic for
quotients. In Q=F5^2 the three affine lines y=0, x=0, and x+y=1 are
pairwise intersecting but have empty total intersection. They are genuine
left cosets of index-five subgroups. For G=C5 x Q, put them on three distinct
C5 layers; these are three pairwise-disjoint actual original left cosets
projecting to the non-Helly triple. Each original index is 25, so their
pairwise gcd is 25: this illustration does NOT show that a non-Helly triangle
occurs under the gcd<=7 hypothesis, nor that B_7(Q)<=2 in this example.
It refutes only the unconditional projection-Helly shortcut.

(c) For arbitrary N,Q satisfying only Sun8, even a Helly quotient gives at
best graph constraints forbidding an eight-vertex clique or independent set;
those alone do not yield a contradiction (for example an abstract eight-cycle
has neither). No claim is made that this abstract graph is realized by an
actual bad coset family. The exact unpaid step is to use the actual subgroup
indices, fiber coset compatibility, and all-variable extension structure to
rule out or handle the remaining quotient incidence patterns WITHOUT replacing
B_7<=7 by B_7<=2 or assuming quotient 3-Helly. Existing f184/f188 and A7/Fano
packets remain pending; no assembled all-simple or arbitrary-group conclusion
is drawn.
