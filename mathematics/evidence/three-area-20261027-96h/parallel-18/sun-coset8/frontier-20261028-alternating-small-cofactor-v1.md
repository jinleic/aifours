## 2. A prime r with t<n/100

Set x=100(n−4)/101. At n≥500,000, x≥396,738 and log x>2; for example e<3 and x>9. Dusart gives a prime r>x with
\[r\le x\left(1+\frac1{25\log^2x}\right)
 <x\frac{101}{100}=n-4.\]
Set t=n−r. Then t>4 and
\[t<n-x=\frac{n+400}{101}<\frac n{100},\]
because n>40,000. Thus 3≤t<r and r>7. The source supplies a real-x prime interval; no unproved assumption about a particular computed prime is used.

## 3. Actual subgroup and common-prime-power argument

Since n=r+t<2r, an r-free-index subgroup H contains a Sylow r-subgroup generated by an r-cycle with t≥3 fixed points. If H were transitive, any nontrivial block system would have fewer than r blocks, so the r-cycle fixes all blocks and places its r moved points in one block, forcing n≥2r. Thus H would be primitive; Jordan would force H=A_n, contrary to properness. Hence every r-free proper H is intransitive.

Let U be its orbit containing the r moved points and let u=|U^c|∈{1,…,t}. The setwise stabilizer in A_n has index C(n,u), which divides [A_n:H]. The elementary identity u C(n,u)=n C(n−1,u−1) implies n/gcd(n,u) divides C(n,u).

The hypotheses give a≥k: otherwise n=m p^a≤m p^(k−1)≤100. Moreover
\[u\le t<\frac n{100}\le p^{a-k+1},\]
because m p^(k−1)≤100. Thus v_p(u)≤a−k while v_p(n)≥a; consequently p^k>7 divides n/gcd(n,u), every C(n,u), and every r-free proper-subgroup index.

Among three indices with pairwise gcd≤7 at most one is divisible by r>7. The other two share p^k>7, contradiction. Seven gcd-7 indices are therefore impossible. In a family of eight disjoint left cosets all subgroups are proper, so Sun8 also follows under the stated source imports.
