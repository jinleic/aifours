1. Exact all-variable step at the original factor-Sun8 boundary

For T>=2, B_T(X)<=r means every r+1 pairwise-disjoint nonempty actual LEFT
cosets of finite-index subgroups of X have two subgroup indices with gcd>T.
Subgroups/labels may repeat. Sun8(X) is B_7(X)<=7. No finite-original-group,
covering, normality of the original subgroups, chosen-index or solvability
restriction is part of the target.

THEOREM (prime polarization). Let G be ANY group, N any normal subgroup,
Q=G/N, and C_i=g_i U_i a finite pairwise-disjoint family of actual LEFT
cosets of finite-index subgroups with all pairwise original-index gcds <=T.
For each i write

  a_i=[Q:pi(U_i)],  b_i=[N:N cap U_i],  n_i=[G:U_i]=a_i b_i,
  A_i=pi(g_i)pi(U_i).

Let p be ANY prime with T/2<p<=T, and let S_p={i:p divides n_i}. If at least
two labels lie in S_p, then EITHER

  (Q-side) every a_i, i in S_p, is divisible by p; the actual A_i, i in S_p,
           are pairwise disjoint, with every quotient-index pair gcd p; OR
  (N-side) every b_i, i in S_p, is divisible by p; ALL A_i, i in S_p, share
           one actual quotient point, and in that same fiber their translated
           actual N-cosets are pairwise disjoint with every kernel-index
           pair gcd p.

These alternatives are not asserted mutually exclusive for zero/one labels;
for at least two, either one is a valid conclusion. At most ONE n_i with
p|n_i can contain p^2. If B_T(N)<=r and B_T(Q)<=s, then

  |S_p| <= max(r,s).                                          (P)

The finite family and all subgroup indices are arbitrary; G, N and Q may be
infinite and the extension need not split. Neither factor is strengthened
to a bound of 2. This is a necessary restriction, NOT an unrestricted Sun8
proof, an eight-coset witness, a finite classification, or a novelty claim.

ORIGINAL-THRESHOLD CONSEQUENCE. If BOTH N and Q satisfy Sun8 and an actual
bad family of eight existed in G, no prime 5 or 7 could divide all eight
original indices. Equivalently, at least one of the eight indices is not
divisible by 5, and at least one is not divisible by 7; the two exceptional
labels need not be the same. This does NOT rule out an eight-label family
whose common or edge-wise index divisors are other primes <=7.

2. Two elementary actual-coset facts

First, if two finite-index subgroups of any group have coprime indices, any
two of their actual LEFT cosets meet. The intersection subgroup has index at
most the product and divisible by both indices, hence exactly the product;
the injective map to the product of the two left-coset sets is then onto.
The same argument for any finite number of pairwise-coprime indices gives a
common point of ALL their prescribed left cosets, even in a nonabelian group.

Second, the product n_i=a_i b_i follows by [G:NU_i][NU_i:U_i], where NU_i
is a subgroup by normality of N and N/(N cap U_i)->NU_i/U_i is a bijection
of left-coset sets. If A_i and A_j share a quotient point q, choose one lift
t of q and points x_l in C_l cap Nt. The actual translated fibers are

  (C_l cap Nt)t^{-1}=(x_l t^{-1})(t(N cap U_l)t^{-1}), l=i,j.

They are disjoint LEFT N-cosets of subgroup indices b_i,b_j. Consequently,
if gcd(b_i,b_j)=1 then A_i and A_j cannot meet. Conversely, if
  gcd(a_i,a_j)=1
then the quotient coset lemma forces A_i and A_j to meet.

3. Proof of prime polarization

Fix p with T/2<p<=T and two distinct labels i,j in S_p. Since p divides both
n_i,n_j while their gcd is at most T<2p,

  gcd(n_i,n_j)=p.                                             (1)

Thus p^2>T implies at most one original index in S_p is divisible by p^2.
Moreover gcd(a_i,a_j) and gcd(b_i,b_j) each divide p. They cannot both equal
1: coprime quotient indices would force A_i and A_j to meet, but coprime
kernel indices would make their actual disjoint N-fiber cosets intersect.
Their product divides gcd(n_i,n_j)=p, so exactly one equals p and the other
is 1. Therefore every pair of labels in S_p shares a factor p in the a-side
or shares a factor p in the b-side.

For each label i in S_p, its p-support among {a-side,b-side} is a nonempty
subset of a two-element set. The previous paragraph says these support sets
are pairwise intersecting. Any pairwise-intersecting family of nonempty
subsets of a two-element set has a common element: a label supported only on
a and a label supported only on b could not coexist. Hence every label in
S_p has p on the common a-side, or every one has p on the common b-side.
This argument explicitly permits a label that carries p on BOTH sides; (1)
allows at most one such label.

In the common a-side case, (1) makes every quotient-index gcd exactly p and
every kernel-index gcd 1. By Section 2 the actual quotient images A_i are
pairwise disjoint. If B_T(Q)<=s, there can be at most s such labels.

In the common b-side case, every quotient-index pair gcd is 1 and every
kernel-index pair gcd p. By the simultaneous coprime-index lemma the actual
quotient images A_i have a common q. The common-fiber identity gives actual
pairwise-disjoint LEFT N-cosets whose kernel-index gcds equal p<=T. If
B_T(N)<=r, there can be at most r such labels. Therefore (P) follows. The
case |S_p|<=1 trivially obeys it because r,s>=1. No numerical group example
or finite computation is used. □

4. Exact cross-coupling for ALL 28 pairs

The preceding theorem used only divisibility. For future eight-label work,
the exact relation is more informative and prevents silently discarding
crossed quotient/kernel prime powers. For ANY positive integers
n_i=a_i b_i and n_j=a_j b_j, put d_A=gcd(a_i,a_j), d_B=gcd(b_i,b_j), and

  c_ij = gcd(a_i/d_A, b_j/d_B) * gcd(a_j/d_A, b_i/d_B).

Then

  gcd(n_i,n_j) = d_A d_B c_ij.                               (2)

Proof: for a prime l put alpha_i=v_l(a_i), beta_i=v_l(b_i), and similarly
for j. The difference between min(alpha_i+beta_i,alpha_j+beta_j) and
min(alpha_i,alpha_j)+min(beta_i,beta_j) is

  min((alpha_i-alpha_j)_+,(beta_j-beta_i)_+)
 +min((alpha_j-alpha_i)_+,(beta_i-beta_j)_+),

exactly the l-valuation of c_ij. This is an identity, not an assumption that
the separate factor gcds already equal the original gcd.

For every pair in an ORIGINAL bad family with T=7, both d_A=d_B=1 are
impossible by the actual quotient/fiber argument in Section 2. If BOTH
factor gcds exceed 1, then d_A d_B>=4; (2) and gcd(n_i,n_j)<=7 force c_ij=1.
Equivalently, at every prime, the quotient- and kernel-exponent differences
across that pair cannot point in opposite directions. The only numerical
factor-gcd products in this mixed case are 4 and 6. This is a restriction on
each of all 28 actual original pairs; it neither selects a concrete input
nor decides the remaining pair patterns. If exactly one factor gcd is 1,
the cross term may be nontrivial and must remain in the accounting.
