## 2. Simultaneous coset CRT, with nonnormal subgroups and the full converse

**Lemma 2.1.** Let Q be ANY group and A_1,...,A_k finite-index subgroups with pairwise-coprime indices a_i. Then ANY prescribed left cosets q_i A_i have a common point.

**Proof.** The diagonal map

`Q/(intersection_i A_i) → product_i (Q/A_i)`

is injective. Its domain has finite size M≤product_i a_i. Index chains make every a_i divide M; pairwise coprimality makes their product divide M. Hence M equals the product and the map is a bijection. The prescribed tuple of cosets has an actual common representative. This proves surjectivity on actual coset tuples, not only an index identity. It is valid for infinite Q, nonnormal/repeated subgroups, indices1 and k=1; k=0 has the whole Q as intersection. □

Conversely, surjectivity of this SAME diagonal map on every tuple is equivalent to the index equality M=product_i a_i. Pairwise coprimality is a sufficient condition, NOT asserted necessary for that equality. No Chinese-remainder assertion for abstract independent fibre data is used.

## 3. Common-fibre transfer, with the original indices retained

**Lemma 3.1.** Let N normal in G and suppose `Sun8(N)`. For any eight original actual disjoint left cosets g_i U_i, define the actual quotient cosets C_i and indices a_i,b_i as in v1:

`C_i=π(g_i)π(U_i)`, `a_i=[G:NU_i]`, `b_i=[N:N∩U_i]`,
`[G:U_i]=a_i b_i`.

If `intersection_(i=1)^8 C_i` is nonempty, then the ORIGINAL conclusion holds: some pair of G-indices has gcd≥8.

**Proof.** Choose q in the common intersection. By the exact fibre identity, the SAME eight original cosets cut that fibre in eight pairwise-disjoint ACTUAL left cosets of N∩U_i inside N after a common translation. Apply the stated premise `Sun8(N)`: some pair has gcd(b_i,b_j)≥8. Since b_i,b_j divide their respective original G-indices, their gcd divides the original-index gcd. Thus the latter is at least8. □

The actual-object converse is the v1 fibre equivalence: every original intersection would be detected in one actual fibre, and all induced cosets use the original fixed U_i,g_i. Independent local packings do not provide a converse construction of G or U_i. The lemma also does NOT infer `Sun8(N)` from Sun7(N), a proper-subgroup inclusion or a dimension statement.

**Corollary 3.2.** Under `Sun8(N)`, any original configuration with pairwise-coprime quotient-image indices a_i satisfies Sun8. Apply Lemma2.1 to its actual C_i, then Lemma3.1. No condition on whether the original indices are coprime is required.

## 4. A GENERAL quotient class on which extension is proved

Define an explicit quotient condition, denoted `(R7)`:

> For EVERY finite-index subgroup A of Q, every prime divisor of [Q:A] is strictly greater than7.

Index1 satisfies the condition vacuously. No choice of particular numerical subgroup or viability probe is involved.

**Theorem 4.1.** For ANY exact group extension `1→N→G→Q→1`, if `Sun8(N)` and `(R7)` for Q, then `Sun8(G)`.

**Proof.** Suppose an original counterconfiguration in G existed. Its quotient-image indices a_i divide n_i=[G:U_i]. Thus `gcd(a_i,a_j)≤gcd(n_i,n_j)≤7`. By `(R7)`, a nontrivial gcd(a_i,a_j) would have a prime divisor strictly greater than7, impossible. Hence all a_i are pairwise coprime. Corollary3.2 contradicts the counterconfiguration. □

This theorem is general in N, G and Q, including infinite G/Q, and does not assume the extension splits. It uses the quotient's index-spectrum condition, not an arbitrary transfer of Sun8(Q). In particular:

- Any finite Q whose order has no prime divisor at most7 satisfies `(R7)`, by Lagrange's theorem for subgroup indices.
- Finite p-group quotients with p>7 are included, but no p-group structure theorem or selected prime test is needed.

## 6. Exact failed common-intersection implication at small quotient indices

The following is an ALL-variable obstruction to the stronger implication, not a selected numeric group/control experiment and not a Sun8 counterexample.

Suppose Q has ANY proper finite-index subgroup A with `2≤a=[Q:A]≤7`. Choose two DISTINCT actual left A-cosets, and take the other six quotient cosets to be Q itself (the subgroup Q has index1). All eight quotient subgroup-index gcds are at most7, yet their total intersection is empty because the first two are disjoint.

Therefore the implication

`all quotient pair-index gcds≤7 ⇒ all eight quotient cosets share a fibre`

is FALSE on every quotient possessing such an A. No N-fibre packing, full G, U_i or Sun8 counterconfiguration is constructed by this quotient witness. It proves only that the CRT/common-fibre route cannot be extended by dropping its hypotheses.

Consequently Theorem4.1 does not establish general closure from `Sun8(N)` and `Sun8(Q)`: the latter is a disjoint-eight-coset assertion, not the much stronger joint-intersection property required by Lemma3.1. Some projected cosets can intersect pairwise or fail total intersection while original disjointness is realized across distinct fibres. Exactly that mixed compatibility is still unpaid.
