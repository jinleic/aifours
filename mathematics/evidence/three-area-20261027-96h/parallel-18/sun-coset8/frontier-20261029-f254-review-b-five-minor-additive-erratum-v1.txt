1. m1: finite indices, not finite coset/subgroup sets

At candidate LF67, replace 'Each is finite' with:

  'The indices a_i, b_i and n_i are finite, and n_i=a_i b_i.'

The projected left cosets A_i and the kernel-intersection subgroups K_i may be
infinite, including when all indices are finite. The theorem needs only finite

D=(Q,+), G=D x C2 x D, N=D x C2 x {0}, and U=D x {0} x D. The two U-cosets

3. m3: all extensions in THAT example split; the theorem is not split-only

Candidate LF98-100 correctly applies the general theorem to every extension
1 -> D x C7 -> G -> F_p^2 -> 1 for every prime p>7. However, the phrase 'not
just this direct product' must not be read as a claim that this particular
kernel/quotient family contains a nonsplit extension. In fact every such
extension splits; this is a property of that example, NOT an additional
hypothesis or restriction of the general transfer theorem.

Proof of split status. The kernel N=D x C7 is abelian. Hom(D,C7)=0 because D
is divisible, Hom(C7,D)=0 because D is torsion-free, and Aut(D)=Q^*. Hence
Aut(N)=Q^* x Aut(C7), and it has no element of order p>7: the only rational
roots of unity are +/-1 and |Aut(C7)|=6. The conjugation action of F_p^2 on N
is therefore trivial; N is central in G. Multiplication by p is bijective on
N, and N has no p-torsion. Choose lifts x,y of a basis of F_p^2. Their pth
powers lie in N. Multiplying each by a suitable central pth root in N gives
lifts with x^p=y^p=1. Their commutator lies in central N, and its pth power is
[x^p,y]=1; no p-torsion forces [x,y]=1. The adjusted lifts generate a group
mapping isomorphically onto F_p^2, because their images are a basis. This is
a section. The direct product in the frozen candidate remains one valid
example; universal quantification over every extension remains correct.

A genuinely nonsplit extension IS available elsewhere in the general theorem,
for example the reviewer-B construction reconstructed in Section 4 below.
Its existence does not retroactively make the rational/C7 family nonsplit.

4. m4: an actual prime pair and a nonsplit nonabelian application

The candidate LF107-112 gives a conditional family p,q>7 with q|(p-1) but
does not exhibit a prime pair or prove that such pairs exist. One exact
witness is p=23, q=11: both are prime, 11 divides 22, and

  2^11=2048=23*89+1,   2 is not 1 mod 23.

As 11 is prime, multiplication by 2 has exact order 11 on C23. Thus
Q=C23 semidirect_2 C11 is nonabelian of order 253=23*11, coprime to 210;
every finite quotient has order dividing 253 and is 7-rough.

A concrete nonsplit extension with this quotient is

  G=C23 semidirect_2 C121,     N=<y^11> isomorphic to C11,

where y generates C121 and acts on C23 by multiplication by 2. The action is
well-defined because its order 11 divides 121. Reduction of the C121
coordinate modulo 11 defines a homomorphism onto Q with kernel N; y^11
centralizes C23 and C121, so N is central. Every lift of the quotient element
(0,1) has the form (0,1+11k), k mod 11, and its 11th power is (0,11), not
identity in C121. A section would have to send the quotient element of order
11 to a lift of order dividing 11, impossible. Hence this extension does not
split. N=C11 satisfies B_7(N)<=1: two nonempty disjoint cosets, if present,
necessarily have indices 11 and gcd 11>7. The frozen general theorem applies
at r=1. This reconstructs the reviewer-provided example; it does NOT assert
