# A_(r+1): transferring an A_r Sylow-overgroup theorem across degree

**Status: WRITTEN candidate, contingent on the external classification below; not independently reviewed or accepted.** Let r≥11 be a prime, r∉{11,23}, and suppose r is not (q^d−1)/(q−1) for any prime power q and integer d≥2. Then every proper subgroup H<A_(r+1) with r-free index has
\[r+1\mid[A_{r+1}:H].\]
Consequently A_(r+1) has no three proper subgroup indices with every pairwise gcd≤7, hence no direct P7 or Sun8 configuration. The conclusion is an actual all-subgroup statement for each eligible r; it does not assert the restriction holds for every prime or that there are infinitely many eligible primes.

The key point is that this is **not** an application of a claimed unique maximal Sylow-r overgroup in A_(r+1). Instead it transfers a unique-overgroup theorem in the point stabilizer A_r into a common *index divisor* even for transitive overgroups in A_(r+1). In particular r=19 gives A20, a degree not among the four parameter forms in `frontier-20261028-alternating-prime-degree-families-v1.md` SHA `590e8940ca35424a041798661bf8787d2c6477600440082fd5397faad48389a0` for any prime≥11. ## 2. The common-index theorem

Let G=A_(r+1). Since v_r(|G|)=1, a Sylow r-subgroup R is generated by an r-cycle and fixes exactly one point, say x. A proper subgroup H with r-free index has full r-part, hence contains a Sylow r-subgroup. Conjugating H does not change its index, so assume R≤H.

If H is intransitive, its orbit containing the r moved points of R has size r or r+1. The second would be transitive; hence it has size r and x is fixed by H. Therefore H≤G_x=A_r and [G:H] is divisible by r+1.

Now suppose H is transitive. Then H_x=H∩A_r contains R. If H_x=A_r, orbit-stabilizer gives |H|=(r+1)|A_r|=|G|, contradicting H<G. Thus H_x is proper. The imported theorem applied to A_r gives
\[H_x\le AGL_1(r)\cap A_r=:M_0.\]
The affine group has order r(r−1) and contains an odd permutation: multiplication by a primitive element of F_r fixes 0 and acts as a single (r−1)-cycle on the nonzero elements. Hence |M_0|=r(r−1)/2, and Lagrange plus orbit-stabilizer imply
\[|H|=(r+1)|H_x|\mid \frac{(r+1)r(r-1)}2.\]
The latter integer divides |A_r|=r!/2, because r+1 divides (r−2)!: writing m=(r+1)/2, the distinct factors 2 and m both occur in (r−2)! for r≥11, and their product is r+1. Therefore |H| divides |A_r|. It follows that
\[[G:H]=(r+1)\frac{|A_r|}{|H|}\]
is divisible by r+1, as claimed. This also covers all proper nonmaximal H and does not assume any classification of maximal subgroups of A_(r+1).

## 3. Three-index consequence

If three subgroup indices have pairwise gcd≤7, at most one is divisible by r because r>7. The other two are r-free, and Section 2 makes both multiples of r+1≥12, contradiction. This proves the stated direct-P7 and Sun8 exclusions for this family without a Sun7 hypothesis.

## 4. A20 has a genuine transitive competing overgroup

The prior alternating-family candidate proves that r=19 is eligible: for d≥5 a geometric sum with q≥2 exceeds 19; for d=2 the only q is 18, not a prime power; d=3,4 have no integral solution. The natural action of PSL2(19) on P¹(F19) is transitive of degree 20, and its order is 19(19²−1)/2=3420. Translation x↦x+1 fixes infinity and is a 19-cycle. The action lies in A20, and a transitive subgroup cannot lie in the point stabilizer A19. Thus a Sylow 19-subgroup is contained in at least two distinct maximal subgroups of A20: the point stabilizer and some maximal subgroup containing this transitive group. The source's unique A19 overgroup therefore does **not** extend by mere uniqueness to A20; the common-divisor argument is essential for this route.
