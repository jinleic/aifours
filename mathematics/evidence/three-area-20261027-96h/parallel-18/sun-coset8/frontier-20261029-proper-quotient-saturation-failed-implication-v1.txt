1. Scope: a failure certificate, not another extension theorem

The original target is every group G and every eight pairwise-disjoint LEFT
cosets of finite-index subgroups: two actual subgroup indices must have gcd
at least 8. Here we exhibit a universally parameterized ACTUAL seven-coset
family which disproves two tempting inference steps in attacking the normal-
extension gap. Seven is not eight: this is NOT a Sun8 counterexample.

For group X, B_7(X)<=r means every r+1 pairwise-disjoint LEFT cosets of
finite-index subgroups contain two indices with gcd>=8. J_7(X)<=s means any
s+1 proper finite-index subgroups, with repetition allowed and NO coset
hypothesis, contain two indices with gcd>=8.

The false implications are:

(F1) B_7(Q)<=1 forces J_7(Q)<=2.

(F2) If N and Q satisfy Sun8, Q satisfies the stronger B_7(Q)<=1, and a
family of pairwise-disjoint original cosets has pairwise index gcd<=7, then
at most six members can project to proper quotient cosets that share a
common quotient point and whose indices are pairwise coprime.

The witnesses below disprove F1 even with arbitrarily large finite J_7(Q),
and disprove F2 with seven actual cosets. They do not decide whether eight
such original cosets exist in any other group.

2. The complete parameterized construction

Let m be ANY integer >=7 and let q_1,...,q_m be ANY distinct primes >7.
Set

  N=C7,    Q=product_{j=1}^m C_{q_j},    G=N x Q,

with additive coordinates and pi:G->Q the second projection. Q is cyclic
because its factors have pairwise coprime orders, and G is cyclic of the
squarefree order 7*q_1*...*q_m. For each i=1,...,7, let

  V_i={x in Q : x_i=0},  U_i={0} x V_i,
  g_i=(i-1,0_Q),        C_i=g_i+U_i.

All U_i are actual subgroups, and since G is abelian these are genuine LEFT
cosets. We retain all original labels and actual cosets, not just index lists.
For i!=j the first coordinates of C_i and C_j are distinct in C7, hence they
are disjoint. Their actual finite indices are

  [G:U_i]=7q_i,    gcd([G:U_i],[G:U_j])=7.

Their projections are the seven distinct proper subgroups V_i of Q, with
[Q:V_i]=q_i, pairwise coprime quotient indices, and

  0_Q in intersection_{i=1}^7 V_i.

Thus all seven original cosets meet the same quotient fiber N x {0_Q}, at
seven distinct points. The bound six in F2 is false, including when every
quotient image is proper and the seven quotient indices are distinct primes.

3. Both factors' exact packing and the quotient-index gap

N has order 7, so it cannot contain eight pairwise-disjoint nonempty cosets;
its seven singleton cosets have index 7 and all pairwise gcds 7. Therefore
B_7(N)<=7 but B_7(N)<=6 is false: N satisfies Sun8 exactly at this packing
boundary.

For Q, any two disjoint finite-index cosets have non-coprime indices. Indeed,
if indices a,b are coprime, [Q:H cap K] is a multiple of ab and at most ab,
so HK=Q and every pair of their left cosets meets. Every prime divisor of
|Q| is >7, so a nontrivial common index divisor is >7. Consequently
B_7(Q)<=1, stronger than Sun8.

Every proper-subgroup index of Q is >1 and divides q_1*...*q_m. From any
m+1 such indices, two contain a common prime q_j by pigeonhole, and then
have gcd at least q_j>7. On the other hand the m distinct proper subgroups
ker(Q->C_{q_j}) have pairwise-coprime indices q_j. Therefore J_7(Q)<=m but
J_7(Q)<=m-1 is false. The gap in F1 is unbounded even though Q has the
strongest possible two-coset index-gcd exclusion B_7(Q)<=1.

4. These seven actual cosets cannot be extended to an eighth bad coset

This check is included to prevent any mistaken claim of an original-target
counterexample. Suppose an eighth actual left coset xH were disjoint from all
C_i and all seven gcd([G:H],7q_i) were <=7. Disjointness gives gcd>=2 by the
same coprime-index lemma. Since q_i>7, this forces 7|[G:H] and q_i not dividing
[G:H], simultaneously for i=1,...,7.

By Lagrange [G:H] divides the squarefree group order. Since 7 divides this
index, |H| is prime to 7. Its projection into the first coordinate C7 must
therefore be trivial, so H={0} x V for a subgroup V<=Q. Its quotient index
[Q:V] is coprime to every selected q_i. The eighth coset lies in some layer
{k} x (z+V), k in C7. In that same layer, the selected original coset is
C_{k+1}={k} x V_{k+1}. The Q-subgroup indices [Q:V] and q_{k+1} are coprime,
so V_{k+1} meets every coset z+V. Hence the eighth coset meets C_{k+1}, a
contradiction. This works even if m>7 and the eighth index uses additional
large prime factors from the remaining quotient coordinates.

In fact B_7(G)<=7 for this concrete G: every pair of disjoint cosets has
index gcd at least 2, and 7 is the only prime <=7 dividing |G|. Thus a bad
family's pairwise index gcds are all exactly 7. In the cyclic group G, each
coset is a residue class modulo its subgroup index; disjoint classes with
index gcd 7 have distinct residues modulo 7 by generalized CRT. At most
seven residues exist. The seven C_i show B_7(G)<=6 is false. This last
statement concerns only this explicit group family, not all cyclic groups.

5. Remaining original gap and evidence boundary

This exact failure does not infer that B_7(N)<=7 and B_7(Q)<=1 imply or do
not imply Sun8(G) for arbitrary normal extensions. It shows that a proposed
proof cannot simply upgrade the kernel's seven-coset capacity to six, or
replace quotient packing B_7(Q)<=1 with the index-only J_7(Q)<=2, even when
the obstructing quotient images are all proper and pairwise coprime-index.
The unrestricted mixed-fiber/quotient question remains open.
