## 2. An explicit r with 3≤t<r and t<n/7

Put x=6n/7. For n≥500,000, x≥396,738 and log x>1. Dusart gives a prime r>x and
\[r\le x\left(1+\frac1{25\log^2x}\right)
 <\frac{6n}{7}\frac{26}{25}=\frac{156n}{175}<n-3.\]
The final inequality follows from 19n>525, certainly true at the stated threshold. Thus t=n−r is an integer at least 4 and strictly less than n/7. Also r>6n/7>7 and t<r.

## 3. Every r-free proper subgroup index has a common divisor >7

Since n<2r, a Sylow r-subgroup is generated by an r-cycle fixing exactly t≥4 points. Any proper subgroup H<A_n with r-free index contains such a cycle. If H were transitive and imprimitive with k≥2 blocks of equal size, then k≤n/2<r. The r-cycle would act trivially on the blocks and put all r moved points in one block, forcing n≥2r, contradiction. Therefore a transitive H would be primitive, and Jordan would make it contain A_n; this contradicts properness. So H is intransitive.

The orbit containing the r moved points has complement size u with 1≤u≤t. Its alternating set stabilizer has index C(n,u), which divides [A_n:H]. Choose k≥1 minimal with p^k>7; then p^(k−1)≤7. As n=p^a≥500,000, necessarily a≥k. From
\[u\le t<n/7\le n/p^{k-1}=p^{a-k+1}\]
we get v_p(u)≤a−k. The elementary identity u C(n,u)=n C(n−1,u−1) implies n/gcd(n,u) divides C(n,u), so **p^k>7 divides every C(n,u)** with u≤t. Consequently it divides every r-free proper-subgroup index.

In any three proper-subgroup indices with pairwise gcd≤7, at most one is divisible by r>7. The other two are r-free and share p^k>7, contradiction. This proves the stated three-index exclusion conditional on the source theorems. Every subgroup in a family of eight pairwise disjoint cosets is proper, so Sun8 follows at the same exact family scope.
