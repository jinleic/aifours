1. Exact arbitrary-group statement

Let T>=2, G be ANY group, N normal, Q=G/N, and C_i=g_i U_i a FINITE family
of pairwise-disjoint actual LEFT cosets of finite-index subgroups U_i. Suppose
all pairwise gcds of the original indices n_i=[G:U_i] are <=T. Put

  A_i=pi(g_i)pi(U_i),  a_i=[Q:pi(U_i)].

For a finite index set I whose actual quotient images A_i meet pairwise:

(A) If their total intersection is empty, then Q has a finite quotient F
    whose order has a prime divisor p<=floor(T/2). More precisely, with
    V_i=pi(U_i), the explicit quotient

      F=Q / intersection_{i in I} core_Q(V_i)

    is finite, the images of the SAME cosets A_i remain pairwise
    intersecting with empty total intersection, and there are i!=j for
    which 1<gcd(a_i,a_j)<=floor(T/2). In particular a prime dividing that
    gcd divides |F|. No V_i is assumed normal and Q may be infinite.

(B) Equivalently, if every finite quotient of Q has order coprime to
    product_{p<=floor(T/2)} p, EVERY clique of quotient images arising from
    an actual original bad family has a common quotient point. This is a
    RESTRICTED clique-Helly assertion; Q need not be globally 3-Helly.

At the original threshold T=7, an actual low-gcd non-Helly quotient clique
therefore forces an explicitly constructed finite quotient of Q whose ORDER
is divisible by 2 or 3. This does NOT say Q has a quotient group C2 or C3,
or a subgroup of index 2 or 3. It also does not claim such a clique exists,
that every bad family has one, or that unrestricted Sun8 follows.

2. Actual fiber pair bound, with subgroup orientation

Let K_i=U_i cap N and b_i=[N:K_i]. Since N is normal, NU_i is a subgroup,
N/K_i -> NU_i/U_i is a left-coset-set bijection, and

  n_i=[G:U_i]=a_i b_i.

If A_i and A_j meet, choose q in their intersection, t in G above q, and
x_l in C_l cap Nt for l=i,j. Their actual fiber slices translate to disjoint
LEFT cosets in N,

  (C_l cap Nt)t^{-1}=(x_l t^{-1})(t K_l t^{-1}),

with indices b_l. Two disjoint finite-index left cosets cannot have coprime
indices: if their indices were coprime, the intersection subgroup index
would be both at most and divisible by their product, hence equal; the
natural injection into the two coset sets would be onto. Thus

gcd(b_i,b_j)>=2. The product gcd(a_i,a_j)*gcd(b_i,b_j) divides both
n_i and n_j. Since gcd(n_i,n_j)<=T,

  gcd(a_i,a_j)<=floor(T/2) whenever A_i cap A_j is nonempty.    (1)

This applies to actual images only and does not assert images are disjoint,
that their corresponding original subgroups are normal, or that N is finite.

3. Simultaneous coset intersection and the finite witness quotient

For ANY group X and finitely many finite-index subgroups W_i of pairwise-
coprime indices d_i, every prescribed tuple of actual left W_i-cosets has
a common point. Indeed, the natural left-coset-set map

  X/(intersection_i W_i) -> product_i X/W_i

is injective. The domain index is at most product_i d_i and divisible by
each d_i, hence by their product. Equality makes the injection onto. Unit
indices and repeated labels are included, and no subgroup is assumed normal.

Now take a quotient clique I with empty total intersection. If all a_i were
pairwise coprime, the preceding lemma would supply a common point; hence some
pair has gcd(a_i,a_j)>1. Its two quotient images meet, so (1) bounds that
gcd by floor(T/2).

For each i, K_i^Q=core_Q(V_i) is the kernel of Q's action on the finite left
coset set Q/V_i. Hence it is normal and finite-index and lies in V_i. Their
finite intersection K is normal and finite-index, so F=Q/K is finite. Each
A_i is the FULL inverse image of its image coset in F because K<=V_i.
Consequently pairwise intersection and empty total intersection are exactly
preserved in F, and [F:V_i/K]=a_i. Since any subgroup index divides the
finite group order, a common prime p dividing the nonunit quotient gcd also
divides |F| and satisfies p<=floor(T/2). This proves (A); its contrapositive
proves (B). For floor(T/2)<2 the prime product is empty, so (B) holds for
every Q and forbids non-Helly quotient cliques under those original bounds.

4. A nonabelian quotient showing the restricted/global distinction

This section is an explicit mathematical illustration of the difference
between restricted bad-family Helly and global Helly. It is NOT a low-gcd
original-coset witness, a new group-family proof, or evidence of novelty.

Let Q=F_11 semidirect C5, with t in Z/5Z acting on F_11 by multiplication
by 3^t. Its multiplication is (x,t)(y,u)=(x+3^t y,t+u). The action has
order 5 since 3^5=243=11*22+1 and 3!=1; hence Q is nonabelian of order 55.
Every finite quotient of Q has order dividing 55, so all are coprime to 6;
the group itself has order divisible by 5, so the separate rough-210
hypothesis of f259 does not hold for this Q.

Let H_0={(0,t):t in C5} and H_c=(c,0)H_0(-c,0)={(c(1-3^t),t):t in C5}.
These are actual subgroups of order 5 and index 11. Define the actual LEFT
cosets

  A_1=H_0,    A_2=H_1,    A_3=(10,0)H_5.

At t=0, A_1 and A_2 meet at (0,0), while A_3 has first coordinate 10.
At t=1, A_1 and A_3 meet at (0,1), while A_2 has first coordinate 9.
At t=2, A_2 and A_3 meet at (3,2), while A_1 has first coordinate 0.
A_1 cap A_2 can occur only at t=0 because 3 has order 5, so all three have
empty total intersection. Thus Q is NOT globally 3-Helly even though it has
no finite quotient order divisible by 2 or 3. The indices of these three
cosets are all 11, so their pairwise gcd is 11>7; they cannot be the clique
of original low-gcd images appearing in Section 1. The restricted assertion
(B), which applies to ANY extension with quotient Q, is not contradicted.
