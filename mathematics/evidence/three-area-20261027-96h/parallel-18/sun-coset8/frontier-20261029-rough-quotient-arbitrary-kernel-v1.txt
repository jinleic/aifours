1. Exact all-variable theorem

For a group X and integers T>=2, r>=1, write B_T(X)<=r for: every r+1
pairwise-disjoint nonempty LEFT cosets of finite-index subgroups of X has two
actual subgroup indices whose gcd exceeds T. Subgroups may repeat. The
original unrestricted Sun8(X) target is B_7(X)<=7.

Put M_T=product of all primes <=T. A group Q is T-ROUGH-FINITE if the order
of every finite homomorphic quotient of Q is coprime to M_T. Equivalently,
every finite-index subgroup of Q has index coprime to M_T (proved below).
This includes, but is not limited to, finite groups whose orders have no
prime factor <=T and groups with no nontrivial finite quotients.

THEOREM. For ANY group G and ANY normal subgroup N, if Q=G/N is
T-ROUGH-FINITE and B_T(N)<=r, then B_T(G)<=r. In particular, for T=7,

  Sun8(N) AND every finite quotient of G/N has order coprime to 210
       ==> Sun8(G).

The kernel may be infinite, nonabelian and nonsolvable, and the extension
need not split. No original coset is discarded, selected, normalized or
assumed to cover G; its subgroup need not be normal. No finite-original-group
or finite-quotient-degree restriction is imposed. The quotient roughness is
an explicit sufficient premise, not a consequence claimed for arbitrary Q;
the theorem does not by itself resolve unrestricted Sun8.

2. Finite-quotient equivalence

If all finite-index subgroup indices of Q are coprime to M_T, the kernel of
any homomorphism from Q ONTO a finite group F has index |F|, so F has order
coprime to M_T.

Conversely let V<=Q have finite index a. The action on the finite left-coset
set Q/V has finite image and kernel K=core_Q(V)<=V. Thus F=Q/K is a finite
quotient, and a=[F:V/K] divides |F|. If |F| is coprime to M_T, so is a.
No normality of V or finiteness of Q is needed.

3. Pairwise coprime subgroup indices give simultaneous coset intersection

Let H_1,...,H_k be finite-index subgroups of an arbitrary group X, with
pairwise-coprime indices a_i. Their intersection H has finite index at most
product a_i: the map of LEFT-coset sets

   X/H -> product_i X/H_i,       xH |-> (xH_i)_i

is injective. Each a_i divides [X:H], so their pairwise-coprime product
divides [X:H]. Hence [X:H]=product a_i and the injection is also surjective.
Every prescribed tuple of actual left cosets has a common element. This holds
with repeated subgroups and unit indices; no subgroup is required normal.

For k=2 this also implies the usual coprime-index fact: two disjoint left
cosets have non-coprime subgroup indices.

4. Actual common fiber and proof of the theorem

Assume B_T(N)<=r but r+1 original cosets C_i=g_i U_i in G are pairwise
disjoint and all original-index gcds are <=T. Let pi:G->Q, and put

  A_i=pi(g_i)pi(U_i),  K_i=U_i cap N,
  a_i=[Q:pi(U_i)],     b_i=[N:K_i],    n_i=[G:U_i].

Each is finite, and n_i=a_i b_i. Indeed NU_i is a subgroup by normality of
N, [G:NU_i]=a_i, and N/K_i -> NU_i/U_i is a bijection of left-coset sets.
Thus gcd(a_i,a_j) divides gcd(n_i,n_j)<=T. By roughness, a_i and a_j have
no prime divisor <=T; their gcd must therefore be 1. Section 3 implies that
ALL quotient cosets A_i share at least one actual point q, even when Q is
nonabelian or the A_i repeat.

Choose a single t in G above q, and choose x_i in C_i cap Nt for each i.
For each label,

  (C_i cap Nt)t^{-1}=(x_i t^{-1})(t K_i t^{-1}),

an actual LEFT coset in N of a subgroup of index b_i. The equality follows
from x_i U_i cap Nt=x_i(U_i cap N). The common right translation preserves
original disjointness. Moreover gcd(b_i,b_j) divides gcd(n_i,n_j)<=T. Thus
these are r+1 disjoint kernel cosets violating B_T(N)<=r, a contradiction.
This proves B_T(G)<=r, with all original labels and actual cosets retained.

5. Examples and exact boundary

For every prime p>7 the abelian Q=F_p^2 is 7-ROUGH-FINITE, yet is NOT
3-Helly: its affine lines x=0, y=0, x+y=1 are actual cosets of index p,
meet pairwise and have empty triple intersection. Their pairwise quotient
index gcd p>7 shows exactly why they cannot be a quotient clique arising
from a bad original family in the proof. Thus no global quotient Helly
assumption is hidden in the theorem.

Take N=(Q,+) x C7 and G=N x F_p^2. N is infinite and B_7(N)=7: every
finite-index subgroup of N contains the divisible rational factor, and the
seven cosets over C7 are disjoint of index 7. Every finite-index subgroup
of the rational additive group is the whole group because multiplication by
its index is surjective. The theorem therefore proves Sun8(G) for every
p>7; it applies to ANY extension of this N by this Q, not just this direct
product. Here the displayed quotient fails the global 3-Helly premise of
f243. The index-only J_7(Q)=1, so f242 with kernel bound 7 gives only
B_7(G)<=8, not Sun8. In the displayed direct product mu(G)=7, outside the
mu>=8 sufficient hypotheses in f151. These are comparisons with precisely
those displayed sufficient interfaces, NOT a claim that the example was
previously open or that no alternative proof exists.

The quotient need not be abelian. For any primes p,q>7 with q|(p-1), choose
an automorphism of C_p of order q and put Q=C_p semidirect C_q, nonabelian of
order pq. Every quotient of Q has order dividing pq, hence is 7-ROUGH-FINITE.
The theorem applies to ANY extension by this Q, and its kernel may be any
Sun8 group. This is an example of the quotient premise, not a classification
or a uniqueness/novelty claim.

The premise does NOT follow from arbitrary factor Sun8. A quotient having a
finite quotient of order divisible by a prime <=7 lies outside this theorem,
even if that quotient itself satisfies Sun8. In particular the arbitrary
mixed-index normal-extension gap remains: no claim is made about such
quotients, an eight-coset counterexample, unrestricted Sun8, or book/source
priority.
