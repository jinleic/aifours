Q_0=G_0/N_0 such that:

- SIX actual pairwise-disjoint LEFT cosets of finite-index subgroups partition
  G_0 and have all original indices 6, so all pairwise index gcds equal 6<=7;
- three actual quotient images are pairwise intersecting with empty total
  intersection (a low-original-gcd non-Helly triangle);
- B_7(N_0)=6, B_7(Q_0)=4 and B_7(G_0)=6, so each of the kernel, quotient,
  and whole group satisfies the original Sun8 assertion.

Here B_7(X)=k is used in the explicit sense B_7(X)<=k and NOT B_7(X)<=k-1;
B_7(X)<=r means every r+1 disjoint finite-index LEFT cosets have a pair of

ANY group surjecting onto G_0; no finite-original-group or splitting

2. The explicit nonabelian group and six actual cosets

Let S3=<r,s | r^3=s^2=1, srs=r^{-1}>, and write every element uniquely as
r^t s^e, t in Z/3Z and e in F_2. Let

  G_0 = S3 x F_2(x) x F_2(y),
  N_0 = <r> x {0}_x x F_2(y).

N_0 is normal, isomorphic to C3 x C2, and has order 6. The quotient map is

  pi(r^t s^e,x,y)=(e,x) in F_2^2,

so Q_0=F_2^2 and |G_0|=24. Let three actual subgroups of G_0 be

  U_0 = {1} x F_2(x) x F_2(y),
  U_1 = <(s,0,0),(1,0,1)>,
  U_2 = <(s,1,0),(1,0,1)>.

Each has order 4: U_1 and U_2 are generated by two commuting involutions.
Define six actual LEFT cosets by

  C_0 = (1,0,0) U_0,      C_3 = (s,0,0) U_0,
  C_1 = (r,0,0) U_1,      C_4 = (r,1,0) U_1,
  C_2 = (r^2,1,0) U_2,    C_5 = (r^2,0,0) U_2.

Their subgroups are explicitly allowed to repeat, as in the original target.
All indices are 24/4=6. Since right multiplication by s toggles e while
keeping the displayed t in r^t s^e fixed, their actual members are:

  C_0: t=0, e=0, x arbitrary, y arbitrary;
  C_3: t=0, e=1, x arbitrary, y arbitrary;
  C_1: t=1, e arbitrary, x=0, y arbitrary;
  C_4: t=1, e arbitrary, x=1, y arbitrary;
  C_2: t=2, x+e=1, y arbitrary;
  C_5: t=2, x+e=0, y arbitrary.

For each of t=0,1,2 its two rows split that whole t-layer into two disjoint
sets; all six rows are disjoint and their union is the whole G_0. Thus all
15 pairwise original-index gcds are exactly gcd(6,6)=6<=7.

The quotient images are actual subgroup cosets in Q_0, namely

  pi(C_0): e=0;       pi(C_3): e=1;
  pi(C_1): x=0;       pi(C_4): x=1;
  pi(C_2): e+x=1;     pi(C_5): e+x=0.

In particular A_0=pi(C_0), A_1=pi(C_1), A_2=pi(C_2) intersect pairwise at
(0,0), (0,1), (1,0), respectively, and have empty total intersection. All
six quotient subgroup indices are 2. For each original subgroup U_i,
[N_0:N_0 cap U_i]=3: in each case its intersection with N_0 is exactly
{1} x {0}_x x F_2(y). Thus each actual index factors as 6=2*3. These are
exact group/coset identities, not a list of hypothetical abstract indices.

3. Exact packing bounds for this example

N_0 has six elements, and its six singleton cosets have subgroup index 6 and
all pairwise index gcds 6. Thus B_7(N_0)<=6 and B_7(N_0)<=5 is false.
Q_0 has four elements, and its four singleton cosets have index 4 and gcd 4;
thus B_7(Q_0)<=4 and B_7(Q_0)<=3 is false.

For G_0, the six displayed cosets show B_7(G_0)<=5 is false. To prove
B_7(G_0)<=6, consider ANY hypothetical bad family of seven disjoint cosets
(all pairwise index gcds <=7). By Lagrange, each proper subgroup index divides
24, so lies in {2,3,4,6,8,12,24}. The whole group index 1 cannot occur in a
family of at least two disjoint cosets. Among the 'high' indices 8,12,24,
each can occur at most once; 24 cannot occur with either 8 or 12, because
the respective pairwise gcd is >=8. Thus at most two high indices occur,
and if two occur they are 8 and 12. Every other index is at most 6.

For disjoint cosets in a finite group, sum_i 1/[G_0:U_i]<=1. With zero high
indices seven terms are at least 7/6>1; with one high index the six remaining
terms are already >=1 and the high term is positive. With two high indices,
the five other terms plus 1/8+1/12 are at least

  5/6 + 1/8 + 1/12 = 25/24 > 1.

All cases contradict disjointness. Therefore B_7(G_0)<=6, and in the
predicate sense specified above B_7(G_0)=6. In particular the construction
is compatible with Sun8(G_0), not an eight-coset counterexample.

4. Arbitrary-group pullback and infinite example

Let G be ANY group with a surjective homomorphism rho:G->G_0. Put
N=rho^{-1}(N_0), so N is normal and G/N is isomorphic to Q_0. Pull back each
U_i and choose a lift of its representative. The six resulting actual left
cosets are precisely the preimages of the six displayed C_i; their indices
remain 6, they remain pairwise disjoint and partition G, and their quotient
images retain the stated non-Helly triangle. This pullback does not assert
that an arbitrary kernel N or an arbitrary G satisfies Sun8.

For an infinite example take any group D with no proper finite-index subgroup,
for instance D=(Q,+), and use G=G_0 x D, N=N_0 x D. Every finite-index
subgroup W of G contains {1} x D: W cap D has finite index in D by the
injective coset-set map D/(W cap D)->G/W. Thus every finite-index coset in G
is the preimage of a coset in G_0; similarly in N. For D=(Q,+), divisibility
shows that any finite-index subgroup is all of D. Consequently the exact
bounds B_7(N)=6, B_7(Q_0)=4 and B_7(G)=6 persist, and all six cosets are
actual infinite subsets of an infinite original G.
