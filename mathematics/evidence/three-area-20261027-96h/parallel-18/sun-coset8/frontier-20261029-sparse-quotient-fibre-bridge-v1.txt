1. Definitions and complete general bridge

For any group X and integer T>=2, let B_T(X)<=r mean: any r+1 pairwise-
disjoint LEFT cosets of finite-index subgroups of X have two subgroup indices
whose gcd is >T. Their subgroups and indices may repeat. The property is
vacuous if no such family exists. Thus original Sun8(X) is B_7(X)<=7.

Let J_T(X)<=s mean: among ANY s+1 proper finite-index subgroups of X, possibly
repeated and with NO coset/disjointness hypothesis, two indices have gcd >T.
For s=0 this means there are no proper finite-index subgroups. In particular
J_T implies the corresponding B_T statement when s>=1;
repetitions of a subgroup of index <=T are explicitly permitted and would
make every finite J_T bound false.

THEOREM. For ANY group G, any normal subgroup N, and Q=G/N, if B_T(N)<=r and
J_T(Q)<=s then B_T(G)<=r+s. Here T>=2, r>=1 and s>=0. No finiteness,
covering, splitting, solvability, normality of the individual subgroups,
chosen-index or selected-coset hypothesis is placed on G. All cosets are
LEFT cosets of actual subgroups.

IN PARTICULAR, if B_7(N)<=5 and J_7(Q)<=2 then the original Sun8 statement
holds for G, including all eight original cosets and all original indices.
The sufficient conditions on N and Q remain explicit; this is not a proof of
Sun8 for arbitrary G without them.

2. Exact proof using one common quotient fiber

Suppose r+s+1 pairwise-disjoint original cosets C_i=g_i U_i have every pair
of actual indices n_i=[G:U_i] with gcd(n_i,n_j)<=T. Let pi:G->Q and put

  V_i=pi(U_i), A_i=pi(g_i)V_i, K_i=U_i cap N,
  a_i=[Q:V_i], b_i=[N:K_i].

All indices are finite. Since N is normal, NU_i is a subgroup and

  n_i=[G:NU_i][NU_i:U_i]=a_i b_i.

Thus gcd(a_i,a_j)<=gcd(n_i,n_j)<=T and likewise for the b_i. Because the
V_i need not be distinct, the repeated-subgroup quantifier in J_T is
essential: at most s of the V_i can be proper. Hence at least r+1 have
V_i=Q; choose any q in Q and any lift t in G. Every such C_i has a nonempty
intersection with the SAME actual fiber Nt. Choose x_i in C_i cap Nt. Then

  (C_i cap Nt)t^{-1} = (x_i t^{-1})(t K_i t^{-1}),

which is an actual left coset in N of index b_i. Indeed, y=x_i u belongs to
Nt exactly when pi(u)=1. The r+1 resulting cosets are pairwise disjoint and
have all kernel-index gcds <=T, contradicting B_T(N)<=r. This proves the
bridge. No infinite-volume fraction or Haar measure is used.

3. Concrete quotient class, proved from elementary matrices

For EVERY odd prime p>7, the quotient Q=PSL2(F_p) satisfies J_7(Q)<=2.
This subsection is an elementary example of the general bridge, NOT a
finite-family limitation on that bridge and NOT an imported maximal-subgroup
classification.

Count |Q|=p(p^2-1)/2 by choosing the nonzero first column of an SL2 matrix,
then one of p second columns of determinant 1, and quotienting by {+I,-I}.
On P^1(F_p), the stabilizer B of infinity has order p(p-1)/2 and index p+1.
Its upper-unipotent subgroup

  U_infinity = { [1 t; 0 1] : t in F_p }

is its unique Sylow-p subgroup: it is normal in B and has order p. A subgroup
H whose index in Q is not divisible by p contains a Sylow-p subgroup, so after
conjugation assume U_infinity<=H. If H does not fix infinity, choose g in H
with g(infinity) finite and multiply g on the left by a suitable element of
U_infinity so that (ug)(infinity)=0. Then H also contains the conjugate
U_0 of U_infinity. It is the lower-unipotent subgroup (it is the normal
Sylow-p subgroup of the stabilizer of 0). The upper and lower unipotent
matrices generate SL2(F_p) by elementary row operations, so H=Q. Thus every
proper subgroup of p-prime index lies in a point stabilizer, and its index is
a multiple of p+1. Among any three proper subgroup indices, either two are
divisible by p or two are prime to p; in the first case their gcd>=p>7, in
the second it is >=p+1>7. This establishes J_7(Q)<=2, with repetitions
allowed and WITHOUT assuming the relevant cosets disjoint.

Hence ANY group extension 1->N->G->PSL2(F_p)->1 with p>7 and B_7(N)<=5
satisfies the original Sun8(G). The extension need not split, and N and G
may be infinite. For an explicitly infinite example, N=(Q,+) has no proper
finite-index subgroup: if H has finite index d, the order-d quotient Q/H is
killed by d; divisibility gives x=d y in H for every x, so H=N. Thus B_7(N)<=1,
and the direct product (Q,+) x PSL2(F_p) is an infinite original group covered
by the bridge.

EVERY, not only split, group extension with kernel A8 and quotient PSL2(F_p),

4. Stronger separation of the earlier Helly assumption

The prior independently written 3-Helly/Ramsey bridge required B_7(Q)<=2
AND quotient 3-Helly. The latter does not follow from the former, even if
Q has the stronger J_7(Q)<=2. This can be seen for EVERY p>7 above, with no
numerical search.

Identify the p+1 projective points with F_p union {infinity}, and select a
nonsquare z in F_p^*. Let E(x,y)={h in Q:h(x)=y}; each is an actual left
coset of a point stabilizer, of index p+1. Set

  A=E(infinity,infinity), B=E(0,0), C=E(1,z).

A cap B contains the identity. A cap C contains the upper unipotent with
translation z-1. B cap C contains [1 0; z^{-1}-1 1], which fixes 0 and maps
1 to z. But A cap B consists of the projective diagonal matrices
[a 0; 0 a^{-1}], acting by x->a^2 x. None maps 1 to nonsquare z. Therefore
A,B,C are pairwise intersecting and have empty total intersection: Q is not
3-Helly although J_7(Q)<=2 and hence B_7(Q)<=2.

Each of these THREE quotient indices is p+1>7, so the triangle itself is NOT
a projected low-gcd counterconfiguration. It establishes exactly that
B_7(Q)<=2 does not generally imply quotient 3-Helly. It does not decide
whether a non-Helly triple is possible under all the additional low-gcd
constraints of an original Sun8 counterconfiguration. The sparse-quotient
bridge in Sections 1-2 requires NO Helly condition at all, and covers Q here.

5. What is and is not paid

This result pays a genuine all-variable normal-extension composition step.
It retains every actual original coset: surjectivity of selected U_i to Q is
NOT assumed in the theorem but forced for at least r+1 labels by the uniform
index-only quotient hypothesis. The criterion J_7(Q)<=2 is proved for the
stated PSL2 quotients directly, and the kernel is any group satisfying its
named B_7 property. The result is useful even for an infinite original group.

It does NOT establish that an arbitrary quotient obeys any finite J_7 bound:
if Q has one proper subgroup of index at most 7, repeating it gives arbitrarily
many subgroup-index labels with pairwise gcd<=7, so J_7(Q)<=s fails for every
finite s. It does NOT prove B_7(N)<=5 for an arbitrary kernel. Thus it leaves
the unrestricted original Sun8 conjecture open, including the genuinely
mixed normal-extension case where many quotient images are proper and the
strong index-only sparsity premise is absent. Source/book equivalence, global
reduction, prior prime-2/3 stopped routes, novelty and all stronger gates are
not silently filled by this theorem.
