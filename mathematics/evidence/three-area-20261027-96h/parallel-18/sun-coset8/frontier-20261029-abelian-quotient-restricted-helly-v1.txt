1. Original target and exact new bridge

B_7(X)<=r means every r+1 pairwise-disjoint actual LEFT cosets of finite-index
subgroups of X have a pair of subgroup indices with gcd>=8. Subgroups may
repeat. The original Sun8(X) target is B_7(X)<=7. No covering, finiteness,
normality of the eight subgroups, solvability or chosen-index restriction is
part of that target.

THEOREM. Let G be ANY group, N any normal subgroup, Q=G/N abelian, and r,s
positive integers. Suppose Q has NO subgroup of index 3 and NO subgroup of
index 4. If B_7(N)<=r and B_7(Q)<=s, then

  B_7(G) <= R(r+1,s+1)-1.

No global quotient-Helly hypothesis, finite-original-group assumption,
splitting, or normality of the original subgroups is imposed. In particular,
if Q is abelian and both B_7(N)<=2 and B_7(Q)<=2, then B_7(G)<=5, hence
Sun8(G). The two factor-packing conditions remain explicit and are stronger
than factor Sun8; this is NOT an unconditional theorem for arbitrary groups.
The no-index-3/no-index-4 hypotheses are automatic in this particular case.

The gain over the frozen f243 interface is that Q need not be globally
3-Helly or even globally finite-coset Helly: only the quotient images of an
actual original low-gcd disjoint family acquire the clique-Helly property.
The proof below reconstructs the required group/coset steps. No f243 proof
or unchecked candidate is assumed as a premise.

2. Local actual-coset constraint

Let pi:G->Q and let C_i=g_i U_i be pairwise disjoint with all original-index
gcds <=7. Put

  V_i=pi(U_i), A_i=pi(g_i)V_i, K_i=N cap U_i,
  a_i=[Q:V_i], b_i=[N:K_i], n_i=[G:U_i]=a_i b_i.

All indices are finite, even for infinite G. The product identity is the
ordinary left-coset bijection N/K_i -> NU_i/U_i, with NU_i a subgroup because
N is normal. The positive integer

  gcd(a_i,a_j)*gcd(b_i,b_j)

divides both a_i b_i and a_j b_j, hence divides gcd(n_i,n_j).

Suppose A_i meets A_j, at a point q. Choose one lift t of q and points
x_i in C_i cap Nt and x_j in C_j cap Nt. Then the two sets

  (C_l cap Nt)t^{-1}=(x_l t^{-1})(t K_l t^{-1}),   l=i,j,

are disjoint actual LEFT cosets in N of subgroups of indices b_i,b_j. Two
disjoint finite-index cosets cannot have coprime indices: if a,b are coprime,
[H:P cap R] is both a multiple of ab and at most ab, hence PR=H and any two
left cosets intersect. Therefore gcd(b_i,b_j)>=2. Since gcd(n_i,n_j)<=7,

  gcd(a_i,a_j) <= 3                                           (1)

for every edge of the quotient-overlap graph. Importantly, (1) is asserted
only when the actual projected cosets intersect; it does not assert that all
quotient cosets intersect, or that disjoint original cosets project disjointly.

3. Restricted quotient-clique Helly

Fix a finite clique I in the quotient-overlap graph, so the actual A_i,
i in I, meet pairwise. Let H=intersection_{i in I} V_i. It has finite index
in Q and is normal because Q is abelian. Put F=Q/H and identify each A_i
with its image as an actual coset of V_i/H in the finite abelian group F.
Its index there remains a_i. Each intersection in Q is nonempty exactly
when the corresponding intersection in F is nonempty.

The hypotheses on Q also hold for every finite quotient F: the preimage of
an index-d subgroup of F is an index-d subgroup of Q. Therefore 3 does NOT
divide |F| and 4 does NOT divide |F|. To see this from elementary finite
abelian group theory, split F into its primary components. Any nontrivial
finite abelian p-group has a maximal proper subgroup of index p. Thus a
nontrivial 3-primary component would yield an index-3 subgroup; a 2-primary
component of order at least 4 yields an index-4 subgroup by taking two
successive index-2 subgroups. Consequently

  F = F_2 x F_odd,    |F_2| in {1,2},   3 does not divide |F_odd|.

The odd factor may contain ANY other primes, including 5 and 7.

Every subgroup of F splits as its intersection with F_2 times its
intersection with F_odd, since their orders are coprime: for an element of a
subgroup, suitable integer powers obtained by Bezout separately extract the
two components. Thus each actual quotient coset A_i decomposes as an
F_2-coset times an F_odd-coset, and a_i decomposes into the corresponding
indices. Pairwise intersections of the full A_i imply pairwise intersections
of their F_2 components. Because |F_2|<=2, all these F_2 components have a
common point: any singleton components must be the same singleton.

By (1), gcd(a_i,a_j)<=3 for all distinct i,j in I. Since the odd factor has
no factor 3, the indices of the odd components of A_i are PAIRWISE COPRIME.
For any finite collection of subgroups of ANY group with pairwise coprime
finite indices c_i, the intersection index is exactly product c_i: it is at
most the product and divisible by each c_i, hence by their product. The
natural map from the left-coset set of the intersection into the product of
the individual left-coset sets is injective between finite sets of equal
size, hence surjective. Thus ANY prescribed left cosets, including these odd
components, have a common point. Combining the common odd and F_2 points and
lifting from F to Q gives

  intersection_{i in I} A_i is nonempty.                       (2)

This is a clique-Helly statement for THIS original bad family. It is NOT a
claim that every pairwise-intersecting family of arbitrary Q cosets is Helly.

4. Ramsey composition and exact limits

Colour an edge of the label graph when the corresponding A_i meet. A clique
of r+1 has a common quotient fiber by (2), hence yields r+1 disjoint actual
kernel cosets with indices b_i and gcds <=7, contrary to B_7(N)<=r. An
independent set of s+1 gives s+1 pairwise-disjoint actual quotient cosets,
whose index gcds are <=7, contrary to B_7(Q)<=s. The finite Ramsey definition
therefore excludes R(r+1,s+1) original labels, proving the stated bound.
The proof is valid when G, N or Q is infinite.

If B_7(Q)<=2, any subgroup of index d in {3,4,5,6,7} would give three of its
own disjoint left cosets with every index gcd d<=7. Therefore Q has no index
3 or 4 subgroup. Taking r=s=2 gives R(3,3)=6, so B_7(G)<=5; any eight-
coset original family contains six labels and has a pair of indices >=8.

Exact remaining gap: the original factor-Sun8 hypotheses say only B_7<=7,
not B_7<=2. An abelian quotient may have subgroups of index 3 or 4 and need
not satisfy restricted clique-Helly; an arbitrary quotient need not be
abelian. Neither this theorem nor the adopted f243/f242 dispositions establish
Sun8 for arbitrary N,Q,G. No assumption is silently removed.

5. A non-global-Helly example outside the earlier displayed interfaces

Let p be ANY prime >7, let N=C2, Q=C2 x F_p^2 and G=N x Q. The quotient is
abelian. N has two disjoint index-2 cosets, so B_7(N)=2. Every subgroup index
of Q divides 2p^2. In a bad family of at least two cosets, disjointness forces
pairwise index gcd>=2; since p>7, that gcd can only be 2. Thus every index is
even and at most one is divisible by p. All other indices are 2. Q has a
unique index-2 subgroup F_p^2; its two cosets partition Q, so three bad cosets
are impossible, while two exist. Hence B_7(Q)=2.

Q is NOT globally 3-Helly: the three actual quotient cosets C2 x {y=0},
C2 x {x=0}, and C2 x {x+y=1} in C2 x F_p^2 meet pairwise and have empty
triple intersection. Each has index p>7. This does not conflict with (1):
they cannot simultaneously occur as pairwise-overlapping quotient images of
three disjoint original cosets with all original index gcds <=7, since their
quotient index gcd is already p. The displayed no-index-3/no-index-4 condition
holds because every index divides 2p^2. The theorem gives B_7(G)<=5, while
the four cosets of the index-4 subgroup {0} x {0} x F_p^2 give B_7(G)>=4.
No sharpness of the upper bound 5 is claimed.

For this displayed N, Q, and G, the earlier f243 sufficient hypothesis of
global quotient 3-Helly fails; f242's finite J_7 quotient hypothesis fails
because its index-2 subgroup may be repeated; and f151's directly stated
mu(G)>=8 hypotheses fail because G has index-2 subgroups. This is only a
comparison with those exact sufficient interfaces, not a claim that no other
proof applies or that the example is novel.
