## 2. Two- and three-subgroup intersection indices

Because I_ij is a multiple of both m_i and m_j, write

\[I_{ij}=\operatorname{lcm}(m_i,m_j)c_{ij},\qquad1\le c_{ij}<d_{ij}.\tag{1}\]

Thus, if p>7, d_ij≤7 and p divides neither index, then p does not divide I_ij: it divides neither the lcm nor c_ij≤6. Therefore H_i∩H_j contains a Sylow p-subgroup of G.

For three vertices, set J=H_i∩H_j. The disjointness of g_iH_i and g_kH_k gives H_iH_k≠G, and JH_k⊆H_iH_k, so JH_k≠G. Hence

\[I_{ijk}=\operatorname{lcm}(I_{ij},m_k)c',\qquad 1\le c'<\gcd(I_{ij},m_k).\tag{2}\]

Let

\[B_{ij\mid k}=(d_{ij}-1)\operatorname{lcm}(d_{ik},d_{jk}).\]

The exact prime-valuation identity

\[\gcd(\operatorname{lcm}(m_i,m_j),m_k)=\operatorname{lcm}(d_{ik},d_{jk})\]

and (1) yield gcd(I_ij,m_k)≤B_ij|k. If p>7 divides none of the three indices, p does not divide I_ij. If also p≥B_ij|k, then c'<gcd(I_ij,m_k)≤p, so p does not divide I_ijk. Therefore the **triple intersection** contains a Sylow p-subgroup of G. The common Sylow depends on the chosen triple; no single Sylow for all eight vertices is asserted.

## 4. From common Sylows to maximal overgroups

If μ(G)≥8 and d_ij≤7, the subgroup generated by H_i and H_j has index dividing both indices and therefore dividing d_ij; it cannot be proper, so ⟨H_i,H_j⟩=G. If two proper subgroup vertices share a Sylow p-subgroup P, choose maximal overgroups M_i⊇H_i and M_j⊇H_j. They both contain P and must be distinct, since their equality would contradict pair generation. Similarly, three vertices sharing P need three distinct maximal overgroups of P.

In an eight-coset Sun8 counterconfiguration all pairwise d_ij≤7, and any p>7 divides at most one index. Thus at least seven indices are p-free.
