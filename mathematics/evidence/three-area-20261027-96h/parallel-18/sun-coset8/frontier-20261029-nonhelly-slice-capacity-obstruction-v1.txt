1. General slice-capacity lemma in ANY group

For any group X, integer T>=2 and s>=1, write B_T(X)<=s if every s+1
pairwise-disjoint actual LEFT cosets of finite-index subgroups of X contain
a pair of indices with gcd>T. Subgroups and indices may repeat.

LEMMA. Let Q be ANY group with B_T(Q)<=s. Let V_1,V_2,V_3 be finite-index
subgroups and A_i=x_i V_i actual left cosets. Suppose A_1 intersects both
A_2 and A_3, but A_1 cap A_2 cap A_3 is empty. Define

  d=[Q:V_1], h_j=[V_1:V_1 cap V_j] for j=2,3.

If d>=s, then

  d * gcd(h_2,h_3) > T.                                      (1)

This requires neither normality nor any assumption on A_2 cap A_3. The
indices are finite and all cosets/subgroups are the actual ones.

Proof. Choose w_j in A_1 cap A_j. Since w_j belongs to both left cosets,
D_j=A_1 cap A_j=w_j(V_1 cap V_j), an actual left coset. D_2 and D_3 are
disjoint by the empty triple intersection. Their indices are

  [Q:V_1 cap V_j]=d h_j.

The d-1 other left cosets of V_1 are disjoint from A_1 and from each other.
Together with D_2,D_3 they give d+1 actual pairwise-disjoint Q-cosets. Their
index gcds are d (between two complement cosets or one complement and one
D_j) and d*gcd(h_2,h_3) (between D_2 and D_3). If the latter were <=T,
all pairwise gcds would be <=T, and d+1>=s+1 would contradict B_T(Q)<=s.
Hence (1). This proof uses a partition into all d finite-index cosets even
when Q itself is infinite. □

If V_1 is normal in Q, then V_1 V_j is a subgroup and

  [Q:V_j]=[Q:V_1 V_j] [V_1:V_1 cap V_j],

so h_j divides a_j=[Q:V_j]. In that case (1) also forces

  d * gcd(a_2,a_3) > T,                                     (2)

but the normality assumption must not be dropped for the divisibility step.
The slice lemma itself never needs it.

2. Constraints inherited from actual original fibers

Let G be ANY group, N normal, Q=G/N, and C_i=g_i U_i pairwise-disjoint
actual left cosets of finite-index subgroups. Put

  A_i=pi(g_i)pi(U_i), a_i=[Q:pi(U_i)],
  b_i=[N:N cap U_i], n_i=[G:U_i]=a_i b_i.

Suppose gcd(n_i,n_j)<=T for every original pair. For every pair whose actual
quotient images A_i,A_j intersect, choose a common q and lift t. Their
intersections with Nt, followed by the same right translation by t^{-1},
are disjoint actual LEFT N-cosets of indices b_i,b_j. A disjoint pair of
finite-index cosets has non-coprime indices, so gcd(b_i,b_j)>=2.

The positive product gcd(a_i,a_j)*gcd(b_i,b_j) divides both original indices
n_i,n_j. Therefore every intersecting quotient pair satisfies

  gcd(a_i,a_j) <= floor(T/2).                                (3)

No assertion is made about the gcd of quotient indices for DISJOINT images
beyond its inherited upper bound T. The fiber cosets require conjugation:
(C_i cap Nt)t^{-1}=(x_i t^{-1})(t(U_i cap N)t^{-1}), with x_i in C_i cap Nt.

Any finite family of cosets of subgroups with pairwise-coprime finite indices
has a common point, even in a nonabelian group: the index of the intersection
is at most the product and divisible by every index, hence equals the product;
the natural injective map of left-coset sets into their product is then
surjective. Consequently, if three projected actual cosets are pairwise
intersecting but have empty total intersection, at least one pair of their
quotient indices has gcd>1. In a low-original-gcd family all three pairs
also satisfy (3).

3. A sharper necessary obstruction under the f243 factor bounds

PROPOSITION. For ANY G,N normal and ANY T>=2, assume B_T(Q)<=2. Suppose
three pairwise-disjoint actual G-cosets have all original-index gcds <=T,
and their three actual quotient images form a non-Helly triangle: every pair
meets but their triple intersection is empty. Then

  a_i >= T+1 for all three labels;                         (4)
  gcd(a_i,a_j) <= floor(T/2) for all pairs;                (5)
  gcd(a_i,a_j) > 1 for at least one pair.                  (6)

Moreover each vertex i satisfies the exact inequality (1), with s=2, d=a_i
and h-values computed from the ACTUAL projected subgroups V_i. Statement (1)
is meaningful in arbitrary Q and does not assume V_i normal.

Proof. Equation (5) is (3); (6) follows from the simultaneous-intersection
argument in Section 2. No a_i can be 1: then A_i=Q and the other pair's
nonempty intersection would be a common point. If 3<=a_i<=T, choose three of
the distinct left cosets of V_i. They have equal indices a_i and gcd<=T,
contradicting B_T(Q)<=2. If a_i=2, V_i is normal, and its two h-values
relative to the other projected subgroups divide those other quotient
indices. By (5), 2*gcd(h_j,h_k)<=2*floor(T/2)<=T. The normal-free slice
lemma (1), with d=s=2, gives the opposite strict inequality, a contradiction.
Therefore every a_i>=T+1. This proves all the statements. □

If also B_T(N)<=2 and SIX actual original cosets have every original-index
gcd <=T, their quotient overlap graph has no independent triangle by the
quotient factor bound. On six vertices Ramsey's elementary R(3,3)<=6
argument supplies an overlap triangle. If it had a common quotient point,
the common-fiber N-cosets would violate B_T(N)<=2. Hence it is non-Helly,
and the proposition applies. In particular, for T=7 any such six-label
configuration must contain a projected non-Helly triangle in which ALL
three quotient indices are at least 8, each pair has quotient-index gcd at
most 3, and at least one quotient-index gcd is nontrivial.

This is a necessary condition under the STRICT factor bounds B_T<=2. It
does not establish that a six-label configuration exists or cannot exist;
it does not say anything comparable follows solely from both factor Sun8
bounds B_7<=7. No claim of all-group Sun8 or an eight-coset counterexample
is made.
