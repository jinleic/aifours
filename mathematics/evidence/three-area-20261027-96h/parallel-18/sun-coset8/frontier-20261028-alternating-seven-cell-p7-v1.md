# Seven-cell exclusion of alternating direct P7 through 33 positions

**Status: WRITTEN candidate, conditional on the cited public A_r classification; not independently reviewed, formally verified, accepted or priority-cleared.** Let r≥419 be a prime which is not a prime-power repunit (q^d−1)/(q−1), d≥2. For any 1≤t≤33 satisfying
\[r+1>7t!,\]
the actual simple group A_(r+t) has no seven proper subgroups whose indices all have pairwise gcd exactly 7. For each fixed t there are infinitely many eligible r. ## 2. No factorial-terminal index in a seven-pattern

Set n=r+t. Since t≤33<r, the Sylow r-subgroups of A_n have order r and are generated by r-cycles. A proper subgroup of A_N, N≥5, has index at least N: otherwise its core-free action would embed A_N into S_(N−1), whose order is smaller.

First, if p is a prime with r<p≤r+t, **7p is not a subgroup index in A_n**. Such an index is r-free, so conjugate the subgroup to contain an r-cycle R. Descend through point stabilizers fixed by R whenever it is transitive; this preserves the index and properness. If the descent reaches A_r, the cited source makes the index divisible by (r−2)!, which exceeds 7(r+33)≥7p. Otherwise, at the first intransitive stage A_(r+j), the orbit containing the r moved points has complement size u∈{1,…,j}, j≤33, and C(r+j,u) divides 7p. For u≥2, u<(r+j)/2 and
\[\binom{r+j}{u}\ge\binom{r+1}{2}>7(r+33)\ge7p.\]
For u=1, B=r+j divides 7p and satisfies 7<B<7p, so B=p. The subgroup fixes the remaining point and has index 7 in A_(p−1), impossible because the latter's proper-subgroup indices are at least p−1>7.

Next, at most ten primes of |A_n| lie in [r−1,r+t]. Apart from r, candidates are the E=floor(t/2)≤16 odd numbers r+2,r+4,…,r+2E. At least floor(E/3) are composite multiples of 3. For E≤13 this leaves at most 1+E−floor(E/3)≤10 primes including r. For E=14,15,16, there are at least two **additional** composite multiples of 5: according to r mod 5, offsets 4,14,24; 8,18,28; 2,12,22; or 6,16,26 are all present, and the three offsets in each line have distinct residues mod 3. Therefore at most 1+E−floor(E/3)−2≤10 possible primes remain.

Suppose F=(r−2)! divides one of seven indices with pairwise gcd 7. Every other index is 7 times a number >1, and these six cofactors are pairwise coprime with all prime factors at least r−1; F contains every smaller prime and 7². The six nonempty prime supports use at most ten primes. At least two supports are singletons, otherwise they use at least 1+5·2=11 primes. Every such large prime is >n/2, so appears only once in |A_n|. At most one singleton is r, hence some other index is 7p for a prime p∈(r,r+t], impossible by the previous paragraph. Thus **no P7 index is divisible by F**.

## 3. Exact alternating-block quotient

Let H<A_(r+j), j≤t, contain an r-cycle R and be intransitive. Let U be its orbit containing the r moved points, m=|U|=r+j−u, and let V be the complement of size u≥1. Write
\[B=(S_U\times S_V)\cap A_{r+j},\quad K=H\cap(A_U\times A_V),\quad P=\pi_U(K).\]
Then R≤K and P contains R. If u=1, B=A_U, K=H, and
\[[A_{r+j}:H]=\binom{r+j}{u}[A_U:P].\]
For u≥2 set h=[H:K]∈{1,2} and L=K∩(1×A_V). Since K projects onto P,
\[c=\frac2h\frac{|A_V|}{|L|}\quad\text{is an integer dividing }u!,\]
and the exact formula is
\[[A_{r+j}:H]=\binom{r+j}{u}[A_U:P]c.\tag{1}\]
Indeed [B:A_U×A_V]=2, and |A_V|/|L| divides |A_V|=u!/2. This proves the quotient bound whether or not the small-block action is full or split.

P is transitive on U: it is normal of index at most 2 in the transitive projection of H. If it had two equal orbits, each would have size |U|/2<r because |U|≤r+t<2r; this is impossible since P contains an r-cycle. If P<A_U, continue with this actual proper subgroup, using transitive point-stabilizer descents as necessary; these preserve index. If P=A_U, terminate. If properness persists all the way to A_r, the source theorem forces F=(r−2)! into the original index, which Section 2 excludes in a P7 pattern. Thus every r-free P7 index has an **exact** finite product representation
\[N=\prod_i \binom{r+j_i}{u_i}\,c_i,\qquad c_i\mid u_i!,\tag{2}\]
where each intransitive step consumes the consecutive cell window {j_i−u_i+1,…,j_i}, and later windows are disjoint and lie at lower positions. The factors c_i may be any permitted integers; they are not assumed to be 1.

## 4. Every r-free P7 label must consume a seven-cell

Call h∈{1,…,t} a **seven-cell** if 7|(r+h). Suppose a chain in (2) uses no seven-cell. Every one of its consecutive windows then has length u_i≤6, since any seven consecutive positions contain a seven-cell. Each binomial numerator is a product of 7-free integers, its denominator u_i! is 7-free, and c_i|u_i! is 7-free. Hence N is 7-free, contradicting the fact that every index in a seven-pattern with pairwise gcd 7 is divisible by 7.

Therefore every r-free label consumes at least one seven-cell. Windows of **different** labels cannot share any cell h: each binomial containing r+h is divisible by
\[\frac{r+h}{\gcd(r+h,t!)}>7,\]
because u_i! divides t! and r+1>7t!. Type-1/point windows are covered by the same argument. This would give a gcd above 7. Thus the labels consume **distinct** seven-cells.

At most one of the seven indices is divisible by r>7, so at least six are r-free. But among t≤33 consecutive positions there are at most ceil(33/7)=5 seven-cells. Six r-free labels cannot consume six distinct seven-cells. This contradiction proves the fixed-r result.
