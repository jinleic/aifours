## 2. The close prime and actual intransitivity

Put x=6n/7. Since n≥500,000, x≥396,738 and log x>1. Dusart gives a prime r>x and
\[r\le x\left(1+\frac1{25\log^2x}\right)
 <\frac{156}{175}n<n-3.\]
The final inequality follows from 19n>525. Thus t=n−r satisfies t≥4 and 7t<n; in particular t<r and r>7.

Any proper H<A_n of r-free index contains a Sylow r-subgroup, generated by an r-cycle fixing t≥4 points. If H were transitive, a nontrivial block system would have fewer than r blocks; the r-cycle fixes them all and places its r moved points in one block, implying n≥2r, contradiction. Hence H would be primitive, and Jordan would force H=A_n. Therefore every r-free proper H is intransitive. Its orbit containing the r moved points has complement size u with 1≤u≤t, and the corresponding alternating set stabilizer has index C(n,u), which divides [A_n:H].

## 3. Exact binomial collision lemma

For integers 1≤i<j≤t with n>7t, put A=C(n,i), B=C(n,j) and g=gcd(A,B). The elementary identity
\[\binom ni\binom{n-i}{j-i}=\binom nj\binom ji\]
implies A/g divides C(j,i), because A/g and B/g are coprime. But
\[\frac{\binom ni}{\binom ji}
 =\prod_{h=0}^{i-1}\frac{n-h}{j-h}
 \ge\frac nj>7.\]
If g≤7, then A/g≥A/7>C(j,i), impossible for a positive divisor of C(j,i). Thus gcd(C(n,i),C(n,j))>7. For i=j, C(n,i)≥n>7 because 1≤i≤t<n/7<n/2. Therefore **every two**, even repeated, binomial factors from u∈{1,…,t} have gcd above 7.

Take any three proper subgroups of A_n. At most one index is divisible by r>7. The other two are r-free, hence divisible by two factors C(n,u), C(n,v) with u,v≤t; those factors have gcd>7, and so do the two indices. This proves the three-index statement. Seven pairwise-gcd-7 indices are impossible. Eight disjoint cosets cannot use the whole group as a subgroup, so the same three-index statement gives Sun8 at this scope without a Sun7 import.
