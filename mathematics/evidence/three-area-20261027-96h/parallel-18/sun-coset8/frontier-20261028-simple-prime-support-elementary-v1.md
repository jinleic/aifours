# An elementary direct-P7 criterion for large simple groups

**Status: WRITTEN, UNREVIEWED.** Let S be a finite nonabelian simple group whose order does not divide 2520; in particular this holds if |S|>2520. If 7 does not divide |S|, or if |S|/7 has at most seven distinct prime divisors, then S admits no eight pairwise disjoint left cosets whose subgroup indices have every pairwise gcd exactly 7. The proof uses no Jones, Jordan, CFSG or subgroup classification. It applies to actual simple groups satisfying the displayed order condition, not just to an integer relaxation, and makes no claim that the class is infinite, novel, independently reviewed or sufficient for every simple group.

## 1. Excluding an index-seven subgroup

If H<S has index 7, the coset action embeds S into S7: the core of H is a proper normal subgroup and is trivial by simplicity. The sign homomorphism from S to C2 is trivial because S is nonabelian simple. Therefore the image lies in A7. Lagrange's theorem gives |S| dividing |A7|=2520, contrary to the hypothesis. Thus S has no index-7 subgroup.

## 2. The prime-support count

If 7 does not divide |S|, no subgroup index is divisible by 7. Otherwise a direct-P7 configuration has indices m_i=7a_i for 1≤i≤8, with pairwise-coprime positive a_i. Since index 7 does not occur, every a_i>1. Each index divides |S| by Lagrange, so every a_i divides |S|/7. Each nonunit a_i must have a prime divisor different from those of all other a_j. Consequently |S|/7 has at least eight distinct prime divisors, contradicting the stated bound of seven. This remains valid when 7² divides |S|: the prime 7 is then counted among the possible distinct divisors of |S|/7. No inference is made that arbitrary arithmetic cofactors are realized as subgroups.
