# An explicit degree-only alternating subgroup criterion

**Status: WRITTEN candidate, conditional on the named Dusart and Jordan theorems; not independently reviewed, formally verified or accepted.** For n≥500,000 define, using only integer comparisons,
\[L(n)=\max\{\ell\ge1: 2\cdot11^\ell<4^\ell(n-4)\},\quad
 T(n)=4+\left\lceil\frac{n-4}{25L(n)^2}\right\rceil,\]
\[Q(n)=\frac n{\gcd(n,\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,T(n)))}.\]
If Q(n)>7, then A_n has no three proper subgroup indices whose pairwise gcds are all at most 7. Consequently it excludes direct P7 and satisfies Sun8 for actual disjoint cosets at this exact source-conditional scope. The criterion depends only on the degree, not on supplying a prime r. Degrees with Q(n)≤7 remain open to this argument; no all-degree/global, novelty or published-priority claim is made.

## 2. An exact integer bound for the unknown prime gap

Put y=n−4. The bound e<11/4 follows directly from the exponential series: the first four terms sum to 8/3 and the remaining tail is at most (1/24)/(1−1/5)=5/96, giving e<87/32<11/4. Hence the definition of L implies log(y/2)>L. For n≥500,000, L≥12: explicitly 11^12=3,138,428,376,721 <249,998·4^12=4,194,270,445,568≤(y/2)4^12.

Set ε=1/(25 log²(y/2)) and x=y(1−ε). Since L≥12, ε<1/3600, so x>3599y/3600>396,738 at the stated n threshold and x>y/2. The latter makes log x>log(y/2), hence 1/(25 log²x)<ε. Dusart supplies a prime r>x satisfying
\[r\le x\left(1+\frac1{25\log^2 x}\right)
 <y(1-\epsilon)(1+\epsilon)<y=n-4.\]
Consequently t=n−r is an integer at least 5 and
\[t<n-x=4+y\epsilon<4+\frac y{25L^2}\le T(n).\]
Also T(n)≤5+n/3600<n/2 for n≥500,000, so t<r and r>7. The inequalities are strict where asserted; T is a conservative integer bound, not an empirically measured gap.

## 3. Actual subgroup indices

An r-free proper subgroup H<A_n contains a Sylow r-subgroup generated by an r-cycle, since n<2r. If H were transitive, it would be primitive: any nontrivial block system has fewer than r blocks, so the r-cycle fixes all blocks and puts its r moved points in one, forcing n≥2r. Jordan's theorem applies because t≥5, forcing H=A_n, contradiction. Hence H is intransitive.

The orbit containing the r moved points has complement size 1≤u≤t<T. Its alternating set stabilizer has index C(n,u), which divides [A_n:H]. Since u divides lcm(1,…,T), the elementary identity u C(n,u)=n C(n−1,u−1) gives Q(n)|C(n,u). Thus every r-free proper-subgroup index is divisible by Q(n)>7. Among three indices with all pairwise gcd≤7, at most one is divisible by r>7, leaving two r-free indices sharing Q(n), contradiction. The direct-P7 and actual-coset Sun8 consequences follow as usual because any coset of the whole group meets all others.

The huge lcm need not be materialized to evaluate the criterion: if n=∏p^a, then
\[Q(n)=\prod_{p^a\parallel n}p^{\max(0,a-\lfloor\log_p T(n)\rfloor)},\]
where the exponent is computed by integer powers. This is an exact degree condition, not an assertion that Q(n)>7 for every n.
