# Alternating degrees at least 14 from Nagura and Jordan

**Status: WRITTEN-checked-in-kernel, source-conditional, UNREVIEWED.** For every integer n≥14, among any four proper subgroups of A_n some pair of their indices has gcd at least 9. In particular A_n satisfies the eight-coset Sun8 conclusion, and also the corresponding nine-coset index conclusion, at this source-conditional scope. This is a statement about actual subgroups, not an integer relaxation. It says nothing about degrees n≤13, all simple groups, novelty or adoption. The stronger numerical threshold 9 is part of this candidate, not an assertion about a previous review.

## 2. A prime cycle in every required degree

For n≥50 let m=floor(n/2)≥25. Nagura gives m<r<6m/5 for a prime r. Since r≥m+1, r>n/2 whether n is even or odd. Moreover

\[t=n-r\ge 2m-r>\tfrac45m\ge20,\]

so t≥3 and t<r; also r>8. Both endpoints are strict where needed. No prime gap or asymptotic hypothesis beyond the named Nagura statement is used.

## 3. Every r-free proper subgroup is intransitive

Fix n≥14 and a prime r as above, and write t=n−r. Since n<2r, a Sylow r-subgroup of A_n has order r and is generated by one r-cycle. If a proper subgroup H has r-free index, H contains an r-cycle by Sylow's theorem. If H were transitive and imprimitive, a nontrivial block system would have at most n/2<r blocks, so this r-cycle would fix each block; its r moved points would lie in one block, whose size is at most n/2<r, a contradiction. Thus a transitive H would be primitive, and the imported Jordan theorem would force H to contain A_n because the r-cycle fixes t≥3 points. This contradicts H proper. Hence H is intransitive.

The H-orbit containing the r moved points has complement size 1≤u≤t<n/2. The stabilizer of that orbit has index binomial(n,u) in A_n, and therefore binomial(n,u) divides [A_n:H]. The alternating group is transitive on u-sets: a symmetric-group set stabilizer contains an odd transposition, so the alternating and symmetric orbits agree.

## 4. The binomial collision at threshold 8

For integers 3≤i<j<n/2 put A=binomial(n,i), B=binomial(n,j) and g=gcd(A,B). The exact identity

\[A\binom{n-i}{j-i}=B\binom ji\]

implies A/g divides binomial(j,i). Thus

\[g\ge \frac{A}{\binom ji}=\prod_{h=0}^{i-1}\frac{n-h}{j-h}\ge\left(\frac nj\right)^i>2^i\ge8.\]

When i=j≥3 the shared binomial factor itself exceeds 8. Hence a collection of indices with all pairwise gcd≤8 can contain **at most one** r-free index whose u is at least 3.

For u=1 and u=2 the factors are n and n(n−1)/2, whose gcd is n for odd n and n/2 for even n. This exceeds 8 for every n≥15 except n=16, and is 7 for n=14. In the ordinary n≥15, n≠16 case, there can therefore be at most one index of either type u=1 or u=2; together with the previous paragraph, there are at most two r-free indices.

The two exceptional degrees use the prime r=11 in the table. At n=14 the factors are 14,91,364. The factor 364 shares gcds 14 and 91 with the first two, so at most two r-free indices occur. At n=16 the factors are 16,120,560,1820,4368. Although gcd(16,120)=8, the gcds of 120 with the last three factors are 40,20,24 respectively. Two u≥3 labels cannot coexist by the displayed inequality, and no u≥3 label can coexist with both u=1 and u=2; hence again at most two r-free indices. These displayed small binomial values and gcds were checked with exact integer arithmetic in the author's eval kernel.

Finally, two r-divisible indices have gcd at least r>8, so at most one index can be r-divisible. Thus at most three proper-subgroup indices can have every pairwise gcd≤8, proving the four-index assertion.
