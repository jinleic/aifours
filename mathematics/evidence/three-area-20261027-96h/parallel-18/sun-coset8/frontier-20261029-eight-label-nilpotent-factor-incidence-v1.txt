1. Original target and exact statement

For a group X, B_7(X)<=7 means every eight pairwise-disjoint nonempty actual
LEFT cosets of finite-index subgroups have two subgroup indices with gcd>=8.
Subgroups may repeat. The original Sun8 target asks this for EVERY group,
without finite-original-group, covering, subgroup-normality, solvability,
chosen-index or chosen-coset restrictions.

THEOREM A (all groups, exact finite reduction). Let G be ANY group, N normal,
Q=G/N, with B_7(N)<=7 and B_7(Q)<=7. Suppose eight pairwise-disjoint actual
left cosets C_i=g_i U_i have all 28 original-index gcds <=7. There is an
explicit finite quotient bar G=G/K, with normal bar N and quotient bar Q, that
preserves ALL eight original cosets and indices, BOTH factor index lists,
EVERY subset-intersection question for the eight actual quotient images, and
the factor bounds B_7(bar N),B_7(bar Q)<=7. In each actual bar Q-fiber the
corresponding bar N-slices are pairwise-disjoint actual left cosets with the
unchanged kernel indices.

THEOREM B (exact small-prime incidence for nilpotent finite-image factors).
Under Theorem A, additionally suppose EVERY finite quotient of N and EVERY
finite quotient of Q is nilpotent. The finite reduction then has

  bar N = N_small x N_large,   bar Q = Q_small x Q_large,

where the small factors contain exactly the primes <=7, and the large
factors exactly the primes >7. There are eight actual left cosets A_i^small
in Q_small, with subgroup indices a_i^small, satisfying BOTH:

(i) For EVERY subset J of the eight labels, the original quotient images have
    a common point iff the A_i^small, i in J, have a common point. Thus the
    entire quotient intersection hypergraph, including non-Helly failures,
    is preserved; it is not replaced by an abstract graph.

(ii) For EVERY q_small in Q_small, let S(q_small) be the labels whose
     A_i^small contain it. There exists a SINGLE actual bar Q-fiber above
     q_small such that all labels in S(q_small) occur in it. Translating the
     actual bar N-fiber slices and projecting to N_small gives pairwise-
     disjoint actual LEFT N_small cosets, one for each label in S(q_small),
     with subgroup indices b_i^small. Consequently |S(q_small)|<=7.

For all labels, m_i=a_i^small b_i^small is the <=7-prime part of the original
index n_i; m_i>1 and gcd(m_i,m_j)=gcd(n_i,n_j)<=7 for i!=j. Moreover the
finite small factors N_small and Q_small inherit the ORIGINAL factor Sun8
bounds B_7<=7. At least one pair of the A_i^small intersects.

This theorem proves a necessary eight-label incidence reduction, NOT that the
reduced incidence system is unrealizable, NOT a new eight-coset witness in
an independent extension of N_small by Q_small, and NOT Sun8(G). The
nilpotent finite-image property is a stated extra hypothesis on the factors;
it is not silently imposed on arbitrary N or Q and does not assert G or bar G
nilpotent. Under only factor Sun8 it does not automatically hold.

2. Finite reduction preserving actual original and quotient data

Let K=intersection_i core_G(U_i), where core_G(U_i) is the kernel of G's
finite action on its left U_i-cosets. K is normal and finite-index, and
K<=U_i for every i. Set bar G=G/K, bar U_i=U_i/K and bar N=NK/K. Each C_i
is the full inverse image of its left coset bar C_i in bar G; therefore
bar C_i are pairwise disjoint and [bar G:bar U_i]=[G:U_i]=n_i.

For pi:G->Q, put V_i=pi(U_i), A_i=pi(g_i)V_i. The natural quotient map
Q->bar Q=bar G/bar N has kernel pi(K), contained in EVERY V_i. Thus each A_i
is the full inverse image of its bar Q-coset bar A_i. Its quotient index
and all finite subset intersections are exactly preserved in bar Q.

Write K_i=U_i cap N. The map N->bar N is onto with kernel N cap K contained
in K_i. The image of K_i is exactly bar U_i cap bar N: if n in N has image
in bar U_i, then nK=uK for some u in U_i, so n in U_i since K<=U_i. Hence

  [bar N:bar U_i cap bar N] = [N:N cap U_i] = b_i,
  [bar Q:pi(bar U_i)] = [Q:V_i] = a_i,
  n_i=a_i b_i.

bar N is a quotient of N, and bar Q is a quotient of Q. Any B_7<=7 property
passes to a quotient by pulling its actual cosets back to the original group:
the preimages are disjoint and have unchanged indices. Therefore both finite
factor B_7 bounds survive.

For any bar q in bar Q, let S(bar q)={i:bar q in bar A_i}. Choose a common
t in bar G over bar q and x_i in bar C_i cap bar N t for every i in S. The
actual slices translate to disjoint LEFT bar N-cosets

  (bar C_i cap bar N t)t^{-1}
     = (x_i t^{-1}) (t(bar U_i cap bar N)t^{-1}),

with subgroup indices b_i. Their pairwise index gcds divide the original
ones, hence are <=7. This is an actual same-fiber statement, with the
conjugation retained; it is not an independent assignment of kernel cosets.

3. Nilpotent decomposition and high-prime uniqueness

Assume the additional hypothesis of Theorem B. As finite quotients of N and
Q, bar N and bar Q are finite nilpotent groups. Each is the direct product
of its Sylow p-subgroups. Each subgroup of such a finite product splits as
the product of its intersections with the p-factors. Proof: for an element
h=(h_p)_p in a subgroup and any prime p, choose an integer e equal to 1
modulo |P_p| and 0 modulo every |P_q|, q!=p. The power h^e extracts the
p-component and remains in the subgroup. This works for nonabelian Sylow
factors because different direct-product factors commute. Actual left
cosets likewise split into products of their component left cosets.

For any p>7, at most one original index n_i is divisible by p: otherwise
that pair's original gcd would be at least p>7. Since a_i and b_i divide
n_i, at most one label has a proper projected subgroup on Q_p, and at most
one label has a proper kernel-fiber subgroup on N_p. These assertions are
for all eight actual labels, with repetitions allowed; no selected prime or
index pattern is tested.

For bar Q, let A_{i,p} be the actual left-coset component of bar A_i and
A_i^small=product_{p<=7} A_{i,p}. For every high p and every subset J of
labels, all but at most one A_{i,p}, i in J, equal the full Q_p. The high
p-component intersection is therefore nonempty. Combining all components
proves the full-hypergraph equivalence in Theorem B(i). The empty J convention
poses no difficulty; for a singleton every actual coset is nonempty.

4. Every small-quotient point has a realizable actual kernel fiber

Fix any q_small in Q_small and put S=S(q_small). At each high prime p, among
labels in S there is at most one proper A_{i,p}; choose q_p in that actual
coset if present, and choose an arbitrary point of Q_p otherwise. The tuple
bar q=(q_small,(q_p)_{p>7}) belongs to EVERY bar A_i with i in S. No single
high-prime restriction is invented or discarded.

Choose one lift t of this bar q and take the actual translated bar N-fiber
cosets from Section 2. Since N is a direct product of p-factors, each such
coset D_i splits as product_p D_{i,p}. The Sylow factors of bar N are
characteristic in bar N and bar N is normal in bar G, so conjugation by t
preserves each factor; the component subgroup index is exactly the p-part
b_{i,p} of b_i.

For any distinct i,j in S, every p>7 has at most one proper component
subgroup among D_{i,p},D_{j,p}; hence those two high-p cosets intersect.
If the small-component projections D_i^small,D_j^small intersected too,
choosing a point independently in each component would give a point of
D_i cap D_j, contradicting original disjointness. Thus ALL D_i^small,
i in S, are pairwise disjoint actual left cosets in N_small. Their subgroup
indices are b_i^small=product_{p<=7} b_{i,p}.

If |S|>=8, then these eight disjoint small-factor cosets have all pairwise
index gcds <=7, because each b_i^small divides n_i. N_small is a quotient of
bar N and hence of N, so it inherits B_7<=7: contradiction. Thus |S|<=7 for
EVERY q_small. This uses the original factor bound 7, not the stronger
bound 2.

5. Full-index gcd retention and outstanding coupling

Since n_i=a_i b_i and factorization into primes is multiplicative,

  m_i = a_i^small b_i^small = product_{p<=7} p^{v_p(n_i)}.

For i!=j, every prime divisor of gcd(n_i,n_j)<=7 is <=7, and its exponent
is already present in the m-values. Consequently

  gcd(m_i,m_j)=gcd(n_i,n_j) in {2,3,4,5,6,7}.

The lower bound 2 uses actual original disjointness: two finite-index left
cosets of coprime indices necessarily meet. In particular m_i>1 for each of
the eight labels. No selected integer pattern is introduced or enumerated.

Q_small is a quotient of bar Q, and N_small a quotient of bar N, so both
inherit their respective ORIGINAL B_7<=7 bounds. If all eight A_i^small
were pairwise disjoint, they would violate the Q_small bound, because each
pair of their subgroup-index gcds divides gcd(m_i,m_j)<=7. Hence some pair
of A_i^small intersects. Together with |S(q_small)|<=7 and hypergraph
preservation, these are exact necessary constraints on all eight labels.

What is NOT proved: The actual small kernel cosets at different q_small are
linked through the SAME original subgroups, representatives, extension and
conjugating lifts t. Their values cannot be independently chosen merely
because each local family is feasible. Nor is it shown that N_small and
Q_small fit into a separately constructed quotient extension preserving all
eight original cosets. A local incidence assignment satisfying the displayed
conditions is not asserted realizable; no low-prime finite enumeration,
solver, selected viability test, eight-coset witness, unrestricted Sun8
solution or genuine improvement to the f259 theorem is claimed. Arbitrary
factors with nonnilpotent finite images remain completely outside Theorem B.
