# A source-free small-index classification and its Sun8 consequence

**Status: WRITTEN, UNREVIEWED.** Every finite nonabelian simple group with a proper subgroup of index at most 7 is isomorphic to A5, A6, A7 or GL3(2). The proof below uses elementary alternating-group simplicity, Sylow theory, permutation actions and the Fano plane, not Jones, CFSG, a subgroup database or an exhaustive enumeration. Combined with the separately unreviewed written Sun8 proofs for those four groups and the consolidated prime-support lemma, it gives a source-free **conditional written candidate**: every finite nonabelian simple group with at most two prime divisors above 7 satisfies Sun8. It does not settle larger prime-support classes, arbitrary-group Sun8, novelty or formal/independent review.

## 2. Faithful low-degree actions and degrees at most six

An index-m subgroup of a nonabelian simple S gives a faithful transitive action on m cosets: its core is a proper normal subgroup. Its image lies in A_m, since the sign homomorphism to C2 is trivial. For m≤4 this is impossible because A_m is solvable. Thus m∈{5,6,7}; if m≤6 then S embeds in A6 and |S| divides 360.

If 5 did not divide |S|, then |S| would divide 72=2³·3². A nontrivial p-group is not nonabelian simple, so 3 divides |S|. Its number of Sylow-3 subgroups divides 8 and is 1 mod 3, hence is 1 or 4. One would be normal; four would give a faithful conjugation action S→S4, whose image lies in solvable A4. Both are impossible. Hence 5 divides |S|. The Sylow-5 count divides |S|/5, itself a divisor of 72, and is 1 mod 5; it is 6 or 36, not 1. Therefore |S| is a multiple of 30 and, as a divisor of 360, belongs to {30,60,90,120,180,360}. Orders 90,120,180 would give subgroups of A6 of indices 4,3,2, impossible since A6 is simple of order 360 and cannot inject into S4,S3,S2. At order 30, six Sylow-5 groups supply 24 elements of order 5; the Sylow-3 count is 1 or 10, and 10 would supply 20 further elements, impossible. Thus only orders 60 and 360 remain; order 360 gives A6.

For completeness, a nonabelian simple group S of order 60 is A5. Sylow counts give six 5-subgroups and ten 3-subgroups: four 3-subgroups would give a faithful action into S4, and one would be normal. These contribute 24 elements of order 5 and 20 of order 3. Its Sylow-2 count is 5 or 15: counts 1 or 3 are respectively normal or give a faithful action into S3. Count 5 gives a faithful action on five Sylow-2 subgroups, with image A5 by order.

If the count were 15 and the Sylow-2 groups were cyclic C4, their 30 distinct generators of order 4 would exceed the at most 15 nonidentity elements remaining after the 3- and 5-elements. Thus they are V4. For an involution x, its proper centralizer contains a V4 and has order 4,12 or 20. Centralizer order 20 gives a conjugacy class of size 3 and an impossible faithful action into S3. Centralizer order 12 gives a faithful action on a class of size 5, hence S≅A5. If all involution centralizers had order 4, every involution would belong to exactly one Sylow-2 V4, giving 15·3=45 distinct involutions, more than the at most 15 remaining elements. Therefore S≅A5 in every case.

## 3. Possible orders in degree seven

Now suppose m=7 and regard S as a transitive subgroup of A7. Choose a Sylow-7 subgroup P generated by a 7-cycle. Its normalizer in S7 has order 7·6=42; multiplication by −1 on F7 fixes one point and swaps three pairs, so it is odd and the normalizer in A7 has order 21. Thus N_S(P) has order 7 or 21.

The Sylow-7 count divides 360 and is 1 mod 7, so it belongs to {1,8,15,36,120}; the value 1 is impossible for a nonabelian simple group. Combining with the two normalizer orders and the condition |S| dividing 2520 gives possible orders {56,105,168,252,315,840,2520}. The following elementary exclusions leave only 168 and 2520:

- Order 840 would give a subgroup of A7 of index 3, impossible by simplicity of A7. Order 2520 gives S=A7.
- Order 56 has eight Sylow-7 subgroups and hence 48 elements of order 7. The remaining eight elements form every Sylow-2 subgroup of order 8, so that subgroup is unique and normal.
- Order 105 has fifteen Sylow-7 subgroups and hence 90 elements of order 7. Its Sylow-5 count divides 21 and is 1 mod 5, so it is 1 or 21; the latter would contribute 84 further nonidentity elements. Thus a Sylow-5 subgroup is normal.
- Order 315 would give a point stabilizer of order 45 inside A6. Its Sylow-5 subgroup is unique (the count divides 9 and is 1 mod 5), but the normalizer of a C5 in A6 has order 10: its S6 normalizer has order 20 and contains an odd permutation. This is impossible.
- Order 252 has 36 Sylow-7 subgroups and hence 216 elements of order 7. Its point stabilizer H has order 36 and no element of order 7, so it consists of all 36 non-7-order elements. Thus H is unique and normal.

The displayed Sylow-7 count is a finite divisor calculation; the author's eval kernel independently listed the relevant divisors of 360, but the proof uses only the explicit list.

## 4. The order-168 case gives the Fano plane

Assume |S|=168 and let H be a point stabilizer, of order 24, acting faithfully on the other six points. H has no orbit of size 1 or 3: it would embed in an A5 point stabilizer of order 60 or in a 3-set stabilizer in A6 of order 18, neither divisible by 24. If H is intransitive, its orbit partition is therefore 2+4 or 2+2+2. In the latter case its faithful action embeds it into (S2)^3 of order 8, impossible.

For an orbit of size 2 in the 2+4 case, the corresponding orbital directed graph on the seven points has outdegree 2. The reversed orbital also has outdegree 2 and must be the same orbital, since H has only one orbit of size 2. It is therefore a vertex-transitive simple undirected 2-regular graph on seven vertices, hence a 7-cycle. Its automorphism group has order 14, too small to contain S. Therefore H is transitive on the other six points and S is 2-transitive.

The Sylow-2 count of H is 1 or 3. It cannot be 1: a normal subgroup of order 8 in transitive H would have equal orbit sizes on six points, each a power of 2, so three orbits of size 2. It would embed in (S2)^3∩A6, which has order 4. Thus the count is 3. Conjugation on the three Sylow-2 subgroups gives a transitive image C3 or S3; image C3 would have an order-8 kernel containing every Sylow-2 subgroup, again making one normal. Hence the image is S3 and its kernel K has order 4.

The normal subgroup K has equal-size orbits on the six points, with orbit size dividing 4 and 6; faithfulness excludes singleton orbits. Its three two-point orbits form a matching preserved by H. The stabilizer of a matching in A6 has order 2³·3!/2=24, so it is precisely H. This matching is canonical: K is the kernel of H's action on its three Sylow-2 subgroups.

For distinct points p,q, let f_p(q) be the partner of q in the canonical matching of the six points other than p. The pointwise stabilizer J of p and q has order 168/(7·6)=4. In the even matching stabilizer it fixes the partner x=f_p(q) and acts transitively on the other four points, so x is its unique third fixed point. The same J is the pointwise stabilizer of q,p, hence f_q(p)=x. Also J fixes p,x, so it equals the pointwise stabilizer of p,x, giving f_x(p)=q. The matching is equivariant under S, so the triples {p,q,f_p(q)} form an S-invariant Steiner triple system on seven points.

Such a system is the Fano plane: label the three triples through p as {p,1,2}, {p,3,4}, {p,5,6}; after possibly swapping 5 and 6, the triple containing 1 and 3 is {1,3,5}. Pair uniqueness forces the remaining triples {1,4,6}, {2,3,6}, {2,4,5}. These are exactly the zero-sum triples of the seven nonzero vectors of F2³ after mapping p,1,3 to a basis. Any permutation preserving the triples preserves addition of nonzero vectors and therefore is linear; the full automorphism group is GL3(2), of order (8−1)(8−2)(8−4)=168. Since S acts faithfully and preserves the system, S≅GL3(2).
