pairwise-disjoint nonempty actual LEFT cosets of finite-index subgroups of X
have two subgroup indices whose gcd exceeds T. Subgroups and labels may
repeat. The literal original target is B_7(G)<=7 for EVERY group G: for eight
actual disjoint left cosets some pair of actual subgroup indices has gcd>=8.
There is no covering, finiteness-of-G, subgroup-normality, solvability,
chosen-index, valuation or selected-coset restriction.

1. Shared actual-coset, index and finite-core lemmas

Let G be ANY group, N normal, Q=G/N and pi:G->Q. Let a finite family of
actual left cosets C_i=g_i U_i be pairwise disjoint, each U_i of finite index.
Put

  V_i=pi(U_i), A_i=pi(g_i)V_i, K_i=U_i cap N,
  a_i=[Q:V_i], b_i=[N:K_i], n_i=[G:U_i]=a_i b_i.        (1.1)

Only the INDICES are asserted finite; the sets A_i and groups K_i may be
infinite. Normality of N makes NU_i a subgroup. The left-coset-set bijection
N/K_i -> NU_i/U_i gives the product formula. In particular both factor gcds
and their product divide the original gcd:

  gcd(a_i,a_j) gcd(b_i,b_j) | gcd(n_i,n_j).               (1.2)

Proof of the product divisibility: the first gcd divides both a values and
the second both b values, so their product divides both n values.

If actual quotient images A_i,A_j meet at q, choose ONE t in G over q and
x_l in C_l cap Nt, l=i,j. The common right translation gives actual LEFT
N-cosets of subgroup indices b_l:

  (C_l cap Nt)t^{-1}=(x_l t^{-1})(t K_l t^{-1}).          (1.3)

These cosets are disjoint because the original C_l are disjoint. The
conjugation by the SAME t is essential for arbitrary nonnormal U_l.

For finitely many subgroups H_i of ANY group X with pairwise-coprime finite
indices d_i, any prescribed actual LEFT H_i-cosets have a common point. To
see this, the left-coset-set map

  X/(intersection_i H_i) -> product_i X/H_i

is injective. The finite domain size is at most product_i d_i and divisible
by each d_i, hence by their product. Equality makes the map onto. This covers
unit indices and repeated labels. In particular, disjoint finite-index left
cosets must have non-coprime indices.

Now suppose n_i,n_j have gcd <=T. If A_i meets A_j, (1.3) and the two-coset
lemma give gcd(b_i,b_j)>=2. By (1.2),

  gcd(a_i,a_j) <= floor(T/2) whenever A_i cap A_j is nonempty.  (1.4)

For a bad family at T=7 every pair of original indices has gcd in
{2,3,4,5,6,7}: the lower bound is forced by actual disjointness, not assumed.
If gcd(a_i,a_j)=1 then the projected pair meets; if gcd(b_i,b_j)=1 then it
is disjoint. Therefore both factor gcds cannot simultaneously be 1.

For an exact finite reduction, let R=intersection_i core_G(U_i). R is normal
and finite-index, R<=U_i, and bar G=G/R is finite. Put bar N=NR/R and
bar Q=bar G/bar N. Every original C_i is the full inverse image of bar C_i,
so disjointness and all n_i survive. The quotient map Q->bar Q has kernel
pi(R)<=V_i for every i; consequently each A_i is the FULL inverse image of
bar A_i, preserving every finite subset-intersection question and a_i.
The image of N cap U_i is exactly bar N cap bar U_i: if nR=uR for n in N,
u in U_i, then n belongs to U_i because R<=U_i. Hence b_i also survives.
bar N and bar Q are quotients of N and Q respectively. B_T bounds pass to
quotients by pulling actual left cosets back with unchanged indices.

2. Exact ALL-PAIR quotient/kernel gcd coupling

For any positive a_i,b_i,a_j,b_j set d_A=gcd(a_i,a_j), d_B=gcd(b_i,b_j) and

  c_ij=gcd(a_i/d_A,b_j/d_B) * gcd(a_j/d_A,b_i/d_B).

Then the following exact identity holds, not just a divisibility:

  gcd(a_i b_i,a_j b_j)=d_A d_B c_ij.                       (2.1)

For a prime l, the surplus valuation after subtracting min(v_l(a_i),v_l(a_j))
and min(v_l(b_i),v_l(b_j)) is the sum of

  min((v_l(a_i)-v_l(a_j))_+,(v_l(b_j)-v_l(b_i))_+)
  min((v_l(a_j)-v_l(a_i))_+,(v_l(b_i)-v_l(b_j))_+),

which is exactly v_l(c_ij). This proves (2.1) prime by prime.

For each of the 28 pairs in an actual original bad eight-family, d_A=d_B=1
is impossible by Section 1. If BOTH d_A,d_B>1, then d_A d_B>=4 and (2.1)
with gcd(n_i,n_j)<=7 forces c_ij=1; the only possible products d_A d_B are
4 and 6. Equivalently, for every prime, the a- and b-valuation differences
across such a mixed pair cannot point in opposite directions. If exactly one
factor gcd is 1, c_ij may be nontrivial and MUST be retained. No implication
about whether a mixed pair's quotient images meet is inserted.

3. Actual quotient non-Helly localization and the supported rough theorem

Let T>=2 and let I be a finite set of labels whose actual quotient cosets
A_i are pairwise intersecting. If their total intersection is empty, then
some quotient-index pair is noncoprime by the simultaneous-coset lemma, and
(1.4) gives

  1 < gcd(a_i,a_j) <= floor(T/2) for some i!=j in I.       (3.1)

The quotient F=Q/(intersection_{i in I} core_Q(V_i)) is finite. Every A_i is
the full preimage of its actual image coset in F, so all pair intersections
and the empty total intersection survive. Each a_i divides |F|; hence (3.1)
produces a prime divisor p<=floor(T/2) of the ORDER of this explicit finite
quotient. At T=7 this says p is 2 or 3. It does NOT say Q has a quotient
C2 or C3, a subgroup of that index, or that a non-Helly clique must occur.
Conversely, if every finite quotient of Q has order coprime to the product
of primes <=floor(T/2), every quotient clique arising from an ACTUAL bad
original family has a common point. This restricted statement does not make
Q globally Helly or produce an eight-clique.

The parent-supported f259 rough-quotient transfer is the distinct statement:
for M_T=product of primes <=T, if every finite quotient order of Q is
coprime to M_T and B_T(N)<=r, then B_T(G)<=r. Its proof is short with the
above lemmas: core_Q(V_i) makes each a_i divide a finite quotient order, so
all a_i are M_T-rough. Pairwise gcd(a_i,a_j)<=T then forces them pairwise
coprime. All A_i share a quotient point, and (1.3) contradicts the kernel
B_T bound. f259's T=7 premise is coprimality to 210. The non-Helly
localization only needs coprimality to 6 but yields only clique incidence,
NOT the same packing transfer. In particular factor bounds <=7 still allow
non-complete eight-label overlap patterns.

4. Larger-small-prime polarization for arbitrary normal extensions

Let p be a prime with 7/2<p<=7, so p is 5 or 7, and let

  S_p={i:p divides n_i}.

For distinct i,j in S_p, gcd(n_i,n_j)=p because it is a positive multiple
of p at most 7. Thus p^2 cannot divide two of these indices. By (1.2) and
Section 1, exactly one of gcd(a_i,a_j),gcd(b_i,b_j) is p and the other is 1.
Each i in S_p carries p on its a-side, b-side or both. These nonempty
subsets of the two sides are pairwise intersecting, hence all share a common
side; at most one label carries p on both sides.

If the common side is a, every pair of A_i, i in S_p, is disjoint because
the corresponding b-indices are coprime; their quotient-index gcds equal p.
If the common side is b, the a-indices are pairwise coprime, so ALL A_i,
i in S_p, have one common quotient point; the corresponding translated actual
N-fiber cosets are pairwise disjoint and their kernel-index gcds equal p.
Thus if B_7(N)<=r and B_7(Q)<=s, necessarily

  |S_p| <= max(r,s).                                        (4.1)

This is an all-variable necessary result, not a proof that a bad family
exists. The p=7 case overlaps the general topic of prior parent-supported
normal-section/P7 work; no historical priority or inherited proof verdict is
claimed here.

5. A sharper EIGHT-label restriction using ONLY the original factor bounds

Assume exactly eight actual original cosets have all pairwise index gcds <=7,
and assume the ORIGINAL factor hypotheses B_7(N)<=7 and B_7(Q)<=7. Then

  |S_5| <= 2  and  |S_7| <= 2.                             (5.1)

Proof. For p=5 or 7, (4.1) rules out |S_p|=8, so choose a label j outside
S_p. For every i in S_p, original disjointness forces gcd(n_i,n_j)>=2, while
p does not divide n_j. Choose a prime q_i dividing that gcd. It lies in
{2,3,5,7} excluding p. The q_i are distinct: otherwise q_i=q_k!=p divides
both n_i,n_k as well as p, so gcd(n_i,n_k)>=p*q_i>=10>7. Thus |S_p|<=3.

If |S_p|=3, there are five labels outside S_p. For EACH such outside label,
the three distinct q_i exhaust all primes in {2,3,5,7}\{p}; its original
index is divisible by their product, which is 42 for p=5 or 30 for p=7.
Two outside indices would therefore have gcd >=30>7, contradiction. Hence
|S_p|<=2. This proof has no assumption on quotient normality, solubility,
finite G, distinct original subgroups, selected indices or factor B_7<=2.
□

Consequences of (5.1): at least FOUR of the eight original indices are
coprime to 35. Every pair among those four has gcd in {2,3,4,6}, giving at
least six original label pairs constrained to that set. If in addition both
|S_5|=|S_7|=2, each index outside S_5 union S_7 is divisible by 6: apply the
three-prime injection above to its two gcds with the 5-labels; since this
outside index is not divisible by 7, it must carry both 2 and 3. There are at
least four such labels, and all their pairwise original-index gcds are then
EXACTLY 6. None of these statements rules out a bad eight-family.

6. Explicit specialization: nilpotent finite-image FACTORS only

Return to arbitrary N normal in G and a bad eight-family, with both ORIGINAL
factor bounds B_7(N),B_7(Q)<=7. First apply the finite-core reduction of
Section 1, giving finite bar G, bar N, bar Q and unchanged actual data.
Additionally assume EVERY finite quotient of N and EVERY finite quotient of
Q is nilpotent. This is an extra hypothesis on the FACTORS, not a consequence
of Sun8 and not an assertion that G or bar G is nilpotent.

Then bar N and bar Q are finite nilpotent, so each splits as the direct
product of its Sylow factors. Call the product of factors for p<=7 the small
factor, and the product for p>7 the large factor. Every subgroup and actual
left coset splits over these coprime factors: for an element h=(h_p), use an
integer power equal to 1 modulo the order of a chosen p-factor and 0 modulo
all other factor orders to extract its p-component inside the subgroup. This
works also for nonabelian Sylow factors because distinct direct factors
commute.

For p>7, at most one of the eight original indices is divisible by p, since
a pair sharing it would have original gcd>7. As a_i,b_i divide n_i, at most
one quotient subgroup and at most one kernel-fiber subgroup are proper on
that high-p factor. For any label subset J, every high-p quotient component
has a nonempty intersection (all but at most one coset are the full factor).
Therefore the quotient images A_i have a common point for J if and only if
their actual small-factor quotient cosets do. The ENTIRE intersection
hypergraph, not only pairwise edges, is retained.

For each actual q_small in Q_small, let S be the labels whose small quotient
cosets contain q_small. For every high p choose one point in the unique
proper high-p quotient coset among S, if present, and otherwise any point.
These independent coordinates yield one actual full quotient point at which
ALL labels in S occur. Choose one lift t, take the SAME actual disjoint
kernel-fiber cosets (1.3), and project them to N_small. Since a high-p kernel
component has at most one proper restriction among the eight labels, every
pair of high-p kernel cosets intersects. If two small projections also met,
the full actual kernel cosets would meet, contradiction. Thus the small
projections are actual pairwise-disjoint left cosets in N_small. The p-Sylow
factors of bar N are characteristic and bar N is normal in bar G, so
conjugation by t preserves each p-factor and its subgroup index.

Let a_i^small,b_i^small denote the actual small-factor indices and put
m_i=a_i^small b_i^small. Then m_i is exactly the <=7-prime part of n_i,

  gcd(m_i,m_j)=gcd(n_i,n_j) in {2,3,4,5,6,7},   m_i>1.   (6.1)

The small factors are quotient groups of bar N,bar Q and hence inherit the
ORIGINAL B_7<=7 bounds. Consequently no q_small has all eight labels active,
and the eight small quotient cosets cannot all be pairwise disjoint. No
factor B_7<=2 is used anywhere in this section.

This is a necessary incidence representation, not a realization theorem:
the small kernel slices for DIFFERENT q_small are still coupled through the
same actual U_i, representatives, extension and conjugating lifts. It is not
shown that arbitrary local assignments glue, or that there exists a separate
small-group extension whose eight original cosets reproduce these data.
Arbitrary nonnilpotent finite images are not covered by this specialization.
