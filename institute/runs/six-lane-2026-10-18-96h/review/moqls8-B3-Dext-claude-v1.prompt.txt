98: 
99: Let J_i := {j : λ_{i,j} != 0} for i ∈ {2,3,4,5}. Then:
100: - 1 <= |J_i| <= 2;
101: - J_i ⊆ {1,...,5} for i ∈ {2,3}, and J_i ⊆ {1,2,3,6,7} for i ∈ {4,5}.
102: 
103: **(K1)** If J_i = {j*}, then Q_1(i,j*) is a unit multiple of q_1^{5−i} (B rows) or of q_1^{9−i} (C rows). This is exact reuse at that cell.
104: 
105: **(K2)** Suppose |J_i| = 2; take the B case i ∈ {2,3} (the C case reads {4,5} for {6,7} and e_4, e_5 for e_6, e_7). Then each square s >= 2 has in row i exactly one cell where Q_s(i,j) is a unit multiple of e_6, exactly one where it is a unit multiple of e_7, and no other cell with nonzero V_B-weight. These cells are as follows:
106: - each of the three columns j ∈ {1..5} \ J_i carries one e_6-cell and one e_7-cell, of two different squares;
107: - (i,6) is an e_7-cell;
108: - (i,7) is an e_6-cell.
109: 
110: *Proof (B case).*
111: - **Demands.** By (W) and (AX), each square s >= 2 has V_B-weight exactly 2 in the interior of row i. Indeed q_s^i ⊥ V_B because supp q_s^i ∩ supp q_1^i = ∅ and supp q_1^i = {6,7}. So squares 2-5 need total V_B-weight 8 in row i.
112: - **Capacity per cell.** For j ∈ J_i, λ_{i,j} q_1^{5−i} has both coordinates 6 and 7 nonzero, so 6, 7 ∈ S(Q_1(i,j)). By (F2), no square s >= 2 has V_B-weight at (i,j). For j ∈ {1..5} \ J_i, by (F2) at most two squares s >= 2 meet {6,7}, each with V_B-weight at most 1. If one of them uses both coordinates, it is alone. So the total is at most 2. For j ∈ {6,7}, coordinate j is excluded by (F1), so at most one square uses the single coordinate 13−j, with weight at most 1.
113: - **Count.** 8 <= 2(5 − |J_i|) + 2, so |J_i| <= 2. Since sum_j |λ_{i,j}|^2 = 1, |J_i| >= 1.
114: - **K1.** If J_i = {j*}, then ||P_B Q_1(i,j*)|| = 1, so the unit vector Q_1(i,j*) lies in V_B and equals λ q_1^{5−i} with |λ| = 1.
115: - **K2.** If |J_i| = 2, every inequality is tight. At each j ∈ {1..5} \ J_i, two squares each have V_B-weight 1 on a single coordinate, so their vectors are unit multiples of e_6 and e_7. Each of (i,6), (i,7) hosts one square whose vector is a unit multiple of e_7, resp. e_6. A square cannot have two e_6-cells in an orthonormal row, so each square has at most weight 2 from such cells. Weight 2 is needed, so each square has exactly one e_6-cell and one e_7-cell.
116: QED

144: **Theorem KA (rows).** Let i ∈ {1,7} (B3), or i = 6 when every q_s^6, s >= 2, is ⊥ e_1 (true in D). Put K_i := {j : P_A Q_1(i,j) != 0} and σ_i := sum_{j ∈ K_i} |S(P_A Q_1(i,j))|. Then:
145: - |K_i| >= 2 and σ_i <= 6;
146: - for i ∈ {1,7}, every such component has support of size at least 2, hence |K_i| <= 3;
147: - if σ_i = 6 (saturation), every A-coordinate slot (j,d), d ∈ A \ {j}, not used by square 1 carries a square s >= 2 whose vector at (i,j) is a unit multiple of e_d.
148: 
149: *Proof.*
150: - **Demands.** q_s^i ⊥ V_A for s >= 2 (by (AX) for i ∈ {1,7}, by hypothesis for i = 6). So by (W) each square s >= 2 has V_A-weight 3 in the interior of row i, a total of 12. Square 1 has total 2 by Lemma B(c), with each cell carrying at most 1, so |K_i| >= 2.
151: - **Slots.** Row i has 3·2 + 4·3 = 18 A-coordinate slots (j,d), d ∈ A \ {j}; coordinate j is excluded by (F1). By (F2) each slot is used by at most one square. A square s >= 2 has V_A-weight at most 1 at a cell, and at most the number of slots it uses there. So the total weight of squares 2-5 is at most 18 − σ_i, giving σ_i <= 6.
152: - **Support bound.** For i ∈ {1,7}, P_i contains no basis vector (Lemma B(c)), so each component's support has size at least 2.
153: - **Saturation.** Equality forces every remaining slot to carry weight exactly 1 from a single square using only that coordinate.
154: QED

165: ## 6. Limits and the exact remaining obligation
166: 
167: **Remark L1 (reuse cannot complete any column of square 1).** Assume H1-H4 for full squares. For every j >= 1, at least one of Q_1(1,j), ..., Q_1(7,j) is not a unit multiple of any q_1^m.
168: 
169: *Proof.* Column j is an ONB containing Q_1(0,j) = w_{1,j} e_j, so Q_1(1,j), ..., Q_1(7,j) are an ONB of e_j^⊥. Suppose each were a unit multiple of some q_1^{m_i}. Orthonormality makes the m_i distinct. Let m* be the missing index; then the span of the seven vectors is (q_1^{m*})^⊥, which equals e_j^⊥ only if q_1^{m*} is a unit multiple of e_j. But supp(q_1^0) = {0}, and by H3-H4 supp(q_1^m) has at least two elements for m >= 1. So no q_1^m is a unit multiple of e_j for j >= 1. QED
170: 
171: So Theorem R1 does not extend to full columns by reuse alone. Remark L1 proves nothing about extendability by other vectors.
172: 
173: **Exact scope of the consequence.** On every member of D, the direct restricted equations on R (with H2-H4) are jointly consistent. Therefore no argument whose premises are only these direct equations can exclude genuine P1-B3. In particular, the full-row two-frame conditions plus row ONBs give no universal contradiction. As in the parent's scope erratum to the sparsity Theorem D, this concerns only the direct-equation relaxation on R. Stronger necessary conditions on the R-variables, derived from cells outside R (rows 2-7 at columns >= 1), are not addressed. They could exclude some or all members of D. No full row-1-plus-anything extension, full configuration or extendability is claimed. Whether every admissible B3 support class (outside D) admits a row 1 is not decided; that question is not needed to refute a universal row-1 contradiction.
174: 
175: **Remaining obligation for genuine B3 exclusion.** It must use at least one direct equation involving a cell (i,j) with i >= 2 and j >= 1, or a non-direct consequence of such equations. By Remark L1, completing any interior column of square 1 needs vectors outside its column-0 basis.
