## 2. Theorem A: sparse unitary supports

In this section U is a k x k unitary matrix (orthonormal rows and columns). Its support graph G(U) is the bipartite graph on the k rows and k columns with an edge {r,c} iff U[r,c] != 0.

**Lemma A1 (components are unitary blocks).** Let R and C be the row and column sets of a connected component of G(U). Then U[R,C'] = 0 and U[R',C] = 0 (primes denote complements), |R| = |C|, and U[R,C] is unitary. Hence there are permutation matrices P, P' such that PUP' is block diagonal with unitary blocks whose support graphs are connected, and nnz(U) is the sum over the blocks.

*Proof.* If r is in R and U[r,c] != 0, then c is adjacent to r, so c is in C; thus U[R,C'] = 0, and symmetrically U[R',C] = 0. The rows indexed by R are orthonormal vectors supported in C, so |R| <= |C|. The columns indexed by C are orthonormal vectors supported in R, so |C| <= |R|. Thus U[R,C] is square with orthonormal rows, hence unitary. Every row and column of U is nonzero, so each component contains a row and a column. QED

The size of a component is |R| = |C|.

**Lemma A2 (a singleton line is an isolated entry).** If row r of U has exactly one nonzero entry, at column c, then column c has no other nonzero entry, so {r,c} is a component of size 1. The same holds with rows and columns exchanged. Hence in a component of size at least 2 every row and every column has at least two nonzero entries.

*Proof.* For r' != r, orthogonality of rows r and r' reads 0 = sum_d conj(U[r,d]) U[r',d] = conj(U[r,c]) U[r',c], and U[r,c] != 0. The column statement is the same computation with columns. QED

**Lemma A3 (two-entry rows).** Let r != r' be rows of a component of size k >= 3, each with exactly two nonzero entries. Then their supports are disjoint.

*Proof.* If the supports meet in exactly one column, the inner product of the two rows is a single nonzero product, contradicting orthogonality. Otherwise both supports equal some {a,b}. The 2x2 matrix U[{r,r'},{a,b}] then has orthonormal rows, so it is unitary and |U[r,a]|^2 + |U[r',a]|^2 = 1. Column a of U has norm 1, so U[x,a] = 0 for every row x outside {r,r'}; likewise for column b. So no edge leaves {r,r',a,b}, which is then a whole component of size 2, contradicting k >= 3. QED

Lemma A3 uses the norms of actual unitary columns, not only the zero/nonzero pattern. Theorem A is a statement about genuine unitary matrices; nothing in this note is claimed for Boolean patterns satisfying only support axioms.

**Lemma A4 (minimum size of a component).** A component of size 1 has 1 nonzero entry and one of size 2 has exactly 4. A component of size k >= 3 has at least 3k - floor(k/2) nonzero entries. A component of size 3 has 8 or 9 nonzero entries, and 8 means exactly one zero entry.

*Proof.* Size 2: by A2 both rows have two nonzero entries among the two columns. Size k >= 3: by A2 every row has at least two nonzero entries. By A3 the rows with exactly two have pairwise disjoint supports inside the k columns of the component, so there are t <= floor(k/2) of them, and nnz >= 2t + 3(k-t) = 3k - t. For k = 3 this is at least 8 of the 9 cells. QED

The resulting lower bounds f(k) for a component of size k are f(1)=1, f(2)=4, f(3)=8, f(4)>=10, f(5)>=13, f(6)>=15, f(7)>=18.

**Theorem A.** Let U be a 7x7 unitary matrix with nnz(U) <= 12. Then its component sizes and block shapes are exactly one of:

| type | component sizes | non-singleton blocks | nnz |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | 1,1,1,1,1,1,1 | none (a phase times a permutation matrix) | 7 |
| D1 | 2,1,1,1,1,1 | one 2x2 block without zero entries | 9 |
| D2 | 2,2,1,1,1 | two 2x2 blocks without zero entries | 11 |
| T | 3,1,1,1,1 | one 3x3 block with exactly one zero entry | 12 |

*Proof.* By A1, nnz(U) is the sum of the block sizes in entries, so nnz(U) >= sum_j f(k_j) over the component sizes k_1 + ... + k_m = 7. All fifteen partitions of 7, with that lower bound:

|---|---|---|
| 7 | 18 | no |
| 6+1 | 16 | no |
| 5+2 | 17 | no |
| 5+1+1 | 15 | no |
| 4+3 | 18 | no |

Size-2 blocks have exactly 4 entries and size-1 blocks exactly 1, so the surviving partitions give exactly the four rows of the theorem; in the partition 3+1+1+1+1 the size-3 block must have exactly 8 entries, that is, one zero (A4). QED

*Realizability.* Let H = (1/sqrt2)[[1,1],[1,-1]] and

    M = [[0, a, a], [a, 1/2, -1/2], [a, -1/2, 1/2]],  a = 1/sqrt2.

The rows of M are orthonormal: (0,a,a).(a,1/2,-1/2) = a/2 - a/2 = 0; (0,a,a).(a,-1/2,1/2) = 0; (a,1/2,-1/2).(a,-1/2,1/2) = 1/2 - 1/4 - 1/4 = 0; every squared norm is 1. Hence every listed shape, with any placement of its blocks and of the zero of the 3x3 block, is the support of a unitary matrix.

## 4. Theorem B: forced column-0 structure

Assume H1-H4 throughout this section; s ranges over {2,3,4,5}.

**B1 (sparsity and type count).** nnz(X_s) <= 12 for each s. So each X_s has one of the types P, D1, D2, T of Theorem A, and the multiset of types of X_2,...,X_5 is one of {P,P,P,P}, {D1,P,P,P}, {D1,D1,P,P}, {D2,P,P,P}, {T,P,P,P}. In particular at least two X_s are permutation matrices.

*Proof.* By B0(ii), sum_{t=1..5} nnz(X_t) <= 49. Also nnz(X_1) = 16, and nnz(X_t) >= 7 for every t, since every row of a unitary matrix is nonzero. Hence nnz(X_s) <= 49 - 16 - 3*7 = 12, and sum_{s=2..5} (nnz(X_s) - 7) <= 5. The excesses of P, D1, D2 and T are 0, 2, 4 and 5. Any two non-P types have excess sum at least 4, with equality only for D1+D1, and three non-P types have excess sum at least 6. So the multisets of four excesses with sum at most 5 are exactly the five listed. QED

**B2 (forced isolated entries).** For each s there are tau_s in {4,5,6,7} and rho_s, rho'_s in {2,3,4,5} such that:

1. (1, s+2), (7, tau_s), (rho_s, 2) and (rho'_s, 3) are isolated entries of X_s, alone in their row and in their column of X_s. Equivalently, Q_s(1,0), Q_s(7,0), Q_s(rho_s,0) and Q_s(rho'_s,0) are unit multiples of |s+2>, |tau_s>, |2> and |3>.
2. s -> tau_s, s -> rho_s and s -> rho'_s are bijections onto {4,5,6,7}, {2,3,4,5} and {2,3,4,5}.
3. tau_s != s+2, so the map s+2 -> tau_s is a derangement of {4,5,6,7}; and rho_s != rho'_s.

Consequently no X_s has type D2, and the type multiset is one of {P,P,P,P}, {D1,P,P,P}, {D1,D1,P,P}, {T,P,P,P}.

*Proof.* Row 1: by H3, row 1 of X_s is {s+2}; by A2, applied to W_s (unitary by B0), column s+2 of X_s is {1}. Row 7: row 7 of F is {4,5,6,7}, four cells. Each of the four X_s has a nonzero entry in row 7, and they are disjoint, so each has exactly one, at some column tau_s, and the tau_s are distinct. By A2, column tau_s of X_s is {7}. Since column s+2 of X_s contains only row 1, tau_s != s+2. Columns 2 and 3: column 2 of F is the row set {2,3,4,5}, four cells, and each X_s has a nonzero entry in column 2. The same argument gives exactly one entry, at a row rho_s, with the rho_s distinct, and by A2 row rho_s of X_s is {2}. Likewise column 3 gives rho'_s, with row rho'_s of X_s equal to {3}. Row rho_s of X_s is {2}, which does not contain 3, so rho'_s != rho_s. The four entries lie in the distinct rows 1, 7, rho_s, rho'_s and the distinct columns s+2, tau_s, 2, 3, and each is a component of size 1. Type D2 has only three components of size 1, so it is excluded, and B1 gives the stated multisets. QED

**B3 (the residual block).** Put

    E_s := {2,3,4,5} \ {rho_s, rho'_s},   D_s := {4,5,6,7} \ {s+2, tau_s},
    R_s := {6} u E_s,                      C_s := {1} u D_s,

three rows and three columns. Then W_s[R_s,C_s] is a 3x3 unitary matrix. X_s is the disjoint union of the four entries of B2 and the residual support Y_s := X_s intersected with R_s x C_s, and Y_s is contained in Z. The residual size n_s := |Y_s| is 3, 5 or 8 according as X_s has type P, D1 or T.

*Proof.* The rows and columns of W_s outside R_s and C_s are exactly those of the four size-1 components of B2. By A1 every other component lies inside R_s u C_s and is a unitary block, so W_s[R_s,C_s] is block diagonal with unitary blocks, hence unitary, and W_s vanishes on R_s x C_s' and on R_s' x C_s. Since R_s is contained in {2,...,6} and C_s in {1,4,5,6,7}, Y_s is contained in F intersected with R_s x C_s, which lies in Z. Finally nnz(X_s) = 4 + n_s, with nnz = 7, 9, 12 for P, D1, T. QED

Call E_s (resp. D_s) *pure L* if it is {2,3} (resp. {4,5}), *pure H* if it is {4,5} (resp. {6,7}), and *mixed* otherwise.

**B4 (local cases; exhaustive).** For each s exactly one of the following holds.

- **(a)** E_s and D_s are pure of opposite levels. This case is impossible.
- **(b)** E_s and D_s are pure of the same level. Then all of R_s x C_s lies in Z, and Y_s is any 3x3 unitary support with at most 8 cells: a permutation (n_s = 3), a full 2x2 block plus an isolated entry (n_s = 5), or a 3x3 block with exactly one zero (n_s = 8).
- **(c)** E_s is pure of level l and D_s is mixed. Let z be the column of D_s of level l and w the other. Then (6,w) is an isolated entry of Y_s, and Y_s restricted to E_s x {1,z} is the support of a 2x2 unitary: a 2x2 permutation (n_s = 3) or full (n_s = 5).
- **(d)** E_s is mixed and D_s is pure of level l. Let a be the row of E_s not of level l and b the row of level l. Then (a,1) is an isolated entry of Y_s, and Y_s restricted to {b,6} x D_s is a 2x2 permutation (n_s = 3) or full (n_s = 5).
- **(e)** Both are mixed: E_s = {a_L, a_H} and D_s = {d_L, d_H}, subscripts giving levels. Then Y_s is one of exactly five sets:
  - the permutations {(a_L,1),(a_H,d_H),(6,d_L)}, {(a_L,d_L),(a_H,1),(6,d_H)} and {(a_L,d_L),(a_H,d_H),(6,1)};

In particular type T occurs only in case (b), and in cases (c)-(e) n_s is 3 or 5.

*Proof.* The nine combinations of E-type and D-type fall into (a)-(e): (L,L) and (H,H) give (b); (L,H) and (H,L) give (a); (pure, mixed) gives (c); (mixed, pure) gives (d); (mixed, mixed) gives (e). In every case the allowed cells are Z intersected with R_s x C_s, as described by levels in Section 3. Y_s is the support of the 3x3 unitary W_s[R_s,C_s], so its shape is in the 3x3 list after Theorem A, with n_s <= 8 by B3.
(a) Both rows of E_s have only column 1 among their allowed columns, so their nonzero vectors W_s[r,C_s] are multiples of the same basis vector, contradicting orthonormality.
(b) All nine cells are allowed, and the shapes are the 3x3 list with at most 8 cells.
(c) The two rows of E_s are orthonormal vectors supported in the two columns {1,z}, so the 2x2 block W_s[E_s,{1,z}] is unitary. Its columns then have norm 1 inside E_s, so row 6 has zero entries in columns 1 and z. Row 6 is therefore the singleton {w}, isolated by A2. A 2x2 unitary support is a permutation or full, by A2.
(d) Row a has only column 1 among its allowed columns, so it is the singleton {1}, isolated by A2. The remaining rows {b,6} and columns D_s carry a 2x2 unitary, and all four of its cells are allowed.
(e) The allowed cells are (a_L,1), (a_L,d_L), (a_H,1), (a_H,d_H), (6,1), (6,d_L) and (6,d_H): seven cells, since (a_L,d_H) and (a_H,d_L) are forbidden. A 3x3 block with at most one zero needs 8 allowed cells, so Y_s is a permutation or a full 2x2 block plus an isolated entry. Permutations: a_L goes to 1 or d_L, and a_H to 1 or d_H. If a_L goes to 1, then a_H goes to d_H and 6 to d_L. If a_L goes to d_L, then either a_H goes to 1 and 6 to d_H, or a_H goes to d_H and 6 to 1. A full 2x2 block needs two rows with two common allowed columns. Rows a_L and a_H have only column 1 in common. Rows a_L and 6 have {1,d_L}, leaving (a_H,d_H), which is allowed. Rows a_H and 6 have {1,d_H}, leaving (a_L,d_L), which is allowed. QED

**B5 (incidence and balance).** Each row i in {2,3,4,5} lies in exactly two of E_2,...,E_5, and each column j in {4,5,6,7} lies in exactly two of D_2,...,D_5. *Proof.* Row i is excluded from E_s iff i is rho_s or rho'_s. ## 5. Theorem C: the exact column-0 residual
