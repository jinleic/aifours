91: **Theorem M.** In no genuine configuration satisfying H1, H2 and the stated part of H4 are all six cells (r,c) ∈ {1,7} × {1,2,3} mixed.
92: 
93: *Proof.* Suppose all six are mixed.

95: - **Matched pairs.** Call (c,c'), c != c', a matched pair of s if d_s(1,c) = d_s(7,c'). Then |G_s((1,c),(7,c'))| = 1. So:
96:   - by H1 against square 1, G_1((1,c),(7,c')) = 0, hence δ(c) = δ(c') by Lemma 3;
97:   - by H1 against any other square t >= 2, square t does not also have (c,c') matched.
98: 
99:   Thus each ordered pair (c,c') is matched by at most one square. Distinct common elements of T^1_s ∩ T^7_s give distinct matched pairs of s, because a coordinate occurs in only one column of each triple.
100: - **Case analysis on δ.**
101:   - **(a) δ(1), δ(2), δ(3) pairwise distinct.** No pair can be matched. But T^1_s ∪ T^7_s ⊆ {0,4,5,6,7} gives |T^1_s ∩ T^7_s| >= 3 + 3 − 5 = 1. Contradiction.
102:   - **(b) Exactly two equal, say δ(c_1) = δ(c_2) != δ(c_3).** Only (c_1,c_2) and (c_2,c_1) can be matched, so at most 2 squares have a matched pair. All 4 need one by (a)'s count. Contradiction.
103:   - **(c) δ(1) = δ(2) = δ(3) = δ.** Now T^1_s, T^7_s ⊆ {0,4,5,6,7} \ {δ}, which has 4 elements. So |T^1_s ∩ T^7_s| >= 2, and each square needs at least 2 matched pairs, at least 8 in total. Only 6 ordered pairs (c,c') with c != c' exist, each matched at most once. Contradiction.
104: 
105: All cases are impossible. QED
