54: ## 3. Theorem R1(7): the class decomposition [NEW]
55: 
56: **Theorem R1(7).** In a CE, a ∈ {0, 1, 2}, and exactly one of the following holds.
57: - **(I)** a ≤ 1, |C| ≥ 4, and either D ⊆ A_2 ∖ A_3 ("type 2") or D ⊆ A_3 ∖ A_2 ("type 3"). (D = ∅ is allowed.)
58: - **(II)** a = 2: A_5 = {x, y} with x ∈ A_2 ∖ A_3 and y ∈ A_3 ∖ A_2, and C = V ∖ {x, y}.
59: 
60: *Proof.*
61: 1. **a ≥ 3 forces a = 7, which is impossible.** By P1(ii), at most two vertices of A_5 lie in A_2 ∪ A_3. If a ≥ 3, some x ∈ A_5 lies in neither. For every j ≠ x, P_xj ⊆ {5} and P_xj ≠ ∅ (P1(i)), so j ∈ A_5. Thus a = 7 and every pair has P = {5}, gcd 5. Any six of the seven cosets then contradict Sun(6) (k6 verdict efe1d849…). So a ≤ 2.
62: 2. **a = 2.** Let A_5 = {x, y}. There is j ∉ A_5, and P_xj ≠ ∅ with 5 ∉ P_xj, so x ∈ A_2 ∪ A_3; likewise y. By P1(ii) they cannot both lie in A_2, nor both in A_3. So after naming, x ∈ A_2 ∖ A_3 and y ∈ A_3 ∖ A_2. For every j ∉ {x,y}: P_xj ≠ ∅ and P_xj ⊆ {2} give j ∈ A_2; P_yj ⊆ {3} gives j ∈ A_3. So V ∖ {x,y} ⊆ C, and x, y ∉ C. This is (II).
63: 3. **a ≤ 1.** No pair has P = {5}. By Sun(5) (k5 verdict 2cf02436…), every 5-subset contains a pair with gcd ≥ 5, i.e. (P1) with gcd 6, i.e. (P1(iii)) a pair inside C. Hence every 5-subset of V meets C in at least two vertices, so |V ∖ C| ≤ 3, i.e. |C| ≥ 4.
64:    - A vertex d ∈ D lies in A_2 ∪ A_3: otherwise P_dj ⊆ {5} would be empty for every j ≠ d (as a ≤ 1), contradicting P1(i). Since d ∉ C, d lies in exactly one of A_2 ∖ A_3, A_3 ∖ A_2.
65:    - If d ∈ A_2 ∖ A_3 and d′ ∈ A_3 ∖ A_2, then P_dd′ = ∅, contradicting P1(i). So D lies entirely in one of them. This is (I). ∎
