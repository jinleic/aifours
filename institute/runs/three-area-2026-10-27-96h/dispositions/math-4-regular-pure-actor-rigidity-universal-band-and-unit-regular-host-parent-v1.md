## Whole regular-source actor rigidity and source separation

Let \(S\) be any nonempty regular semigroup and \(q:S\twoheadrightarrow G\) a prescribed idempotent-pure group homomorphism, so \(q^{-1}(1)=E(S)\). Its idempotents form a band. Let \(\sigma\) be the congruence generated by identifying all idempotents. Every source element has a regular inverse, whose two products with it are idempotent; hence \(S/\sigma\) is a group and \(\sigma\) is the least group congruence. Purity makes the induced \(S/\sigma\to G\) an isomorphism. Thus **every** pure onto group quotient has the same kernel \(\sigma\).

If an actual embedding \(j:S\hookrightarrow B\rtimes H\) exists for any band \(B\) and group \(H\), its induced actor map \(r\) is pure, and its image \(K=r(S)\) is a subgroup. The unique \(G\cong K\) carrying \(q(s)\) to \(r(s)\) transports the restricted \(K\)-action on the same \(B\), giving an actual embedding \(F(s)=(b_s,q(s))\) over **exactly the prescribed \(q\)**. This does not extend an embedding of the entire larger \(H\)-host, or construct a homomorphism \(H\to G\); the earlier \(C_2\subset C_4\) whole-host obstruction remains valid.

Moreover \((q(s),[s]_{\mathcal R},[s]_{\mathcal L})\) separates every element of \(S\). The proof uses a chosen inverse only to show \(a=s't\) is idempotent and both \(ae=a\) and \(ae=e=s's\), with \(ss't=t\). It needs no inverse uniqueness or commuting idempotents. This is set-theoretic source separation, **not** a constructed band multiplication or automatic host.

## An exact all-host criterion, not automatic injectivity

For any nonempty semigroup and group-valued \(q\), let \(C_q\) be the free semigroup on letters \((g,s)\in G\times S\), quotiented by **all** nonempty word-square relations \(ww=w\) and all cocycle relations
\[
(g,st)=(g,s)(gq(s),t).
\]
It is a band; translating all group indices gives a genuine \(G\)-action. With \(c_s=[(1,s)]\), \(\eta_q(s)=(c_s,q(s))\) is an actual homomorphism into \(C_q\rtimes G\). For every band \(B\) with \(G\)-action and every \(H(s)=(b_s,q(s))\), evaluating \((g,s)\mapsto g\cdot b_s\) yields the unique equivariant band homomorphism \(C_q\to B\) taking \(c_s\mapsto b_s\).

Therefore some band host embeds \(S\) over the prescribed \(q\) **iff**
\[
q(s)=q(t)\text{ and }(1,s)\equiv_q(1,t)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad s=t.
\]
A finite defining-relation derivation equating distinct same-\(q\) generators would obstruct every band host; for regular sources actor rigidity would then exclude every group actor. No such collision, automatic injectivity or decision/complexity bound is supplied here. Bounded relation evaluations in small known hosts do not decide this infinite presentation.

## Complete unit-regular noninverse monoid class

For a monoid \(M\) with pure onto \(q:M\to G\), the following are equivalent: \(q(U(M))=G\), \(M=E(M)U(M)\), and \(M\) is unit-regular. The unit restriction of \(q\) is then an isomorphism; let \(u_g\) be its inverse. Conjugation \(\alpha_g(e)=u_geu_g^{-1}\) is an action on the actual, possibly noncommutative band \(E(M)\), and
\[
F(s)=(su_{q(s)}^{-1},q(s)):\ M\overset\cong\longrightarrow E(M)\rtimes_\alpha G
\]
has inverse \((e,g)\mapsto eu_g\) and literal actor projection \(q\).

The author’s actual six-element band-by-\(C_2\) example has a left-zero two-element ideal, noncommuting idempotents, genuinely nonunique inverses and a nontrivial conjugation swapping them; it is not isomorphic to a band–group direct product. A separate five-element regular noninverse **proper submonoid** already embeds into a band-by-\(C_2\) host although its source units do not surject to the actor. Thus unit surjectivity is sufficient for the full isomorphism, not necessary for an arbitrary proper source embedding.
