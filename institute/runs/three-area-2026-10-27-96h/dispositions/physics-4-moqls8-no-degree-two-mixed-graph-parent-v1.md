# Four-MOQLS(8): a nonclassical member needs a mixed line of degree at least three

**Disposition: same-harness written support at the registered definitions.** Let Q be a nonclassical member of an actual ordinary four-MOQLS(8) (four order-8 QLS with unit-vector cells, every row and column an ONB, every pair giving an ONB of same-cell tensor products). Fix any actual row of Q as the coordinate basis and call a cell mixed when its ray is not one of that row's rays. Then some row or column of Q contains at least three mixed cells; equivalently Q's mixed incidence graph is never 2-regular. In particular it is never a single even cycle, nor a single eight-cycle. For general n and family size t, a single mixed cycle `C_{2k}` forces `t≤min(k−1,n−k)≤⌊(n−1)/2⌋`. These are necessary structural restrictions only: no four-family is constructed or excluded, no M(8) bound, A10/A11 closure, novelty or formal proof is asserted, and mixed graphs with a degree-≥3 line are not addressed.

## Reconstruction

A line with exactly two mixed cells has six light cells on distinct axes, so its two mixed rays form an ONB of the complementary two-axis plane and each uses both axes. Around a 2-regular component this propagates one support `{a,b}` and the rays alternate `v, v^⊥`, so each bipartition part is collinear and the parts are perpendicular. For any mate R, collinear Q-rays force orthogonal R-rays (pair-product ONB). If axis a occurs in no other component, every one of the other `n−k` rows carries exactly one light `e_a` cell; their R-rays are orthonormal and orthogonal to every R-ray on the component, so the component's R-rays span exactly k dimensions and both parts are ONBs of that span. The k×k transition unitary between them vanishes at the two cycle neighbours of each cell, and distinct mates have disjoint transition supports, giving `t−1≤k−2`. The `n−k` light-`e_a` R-rays form an ONB of the orthogonal complement, which also contains the first-row `e_b`; each mate therefore has nonzero `e_b`-overlap at one of the other `n−k−1` such cells, and two mates cannot share that cell, giving `t−1≤n−k−1`. At n=8 the minimum is at most 3.

With all mixed line degrees two, the mixed graph is a disjoint union of cycles with at most 7 mixed rows, hence at most three components. If some support axis is exclusive to a component, the bound above applies. If all supports coincide, grouping by projective ray basis makes the groups' R-spans orthogonal with exact dimension counts; two or more groups force at least 10 mixed rows, and one group reduces to the same two pigeonholes on the common span. Otherwise three components must form a support triangle, two components sharing one axis have orthogonal R-spans with exact dimensions, and `Σk_i≤7` forces `k_i≤3`, so `t≤2`. Parent checked by hand the plane/ONB lemma, the collinearity transfer, the `n−k` light-cell count including rows occupied by other components, the two pigeonholes and the component-count arithmetic; a [parent spot check](../review-waves/f133-moqls8-four-family-no-degree2-mixed-graph/parent-support-pigeonhole-spot-check-v1.json), `4e7324ff8b96fb451c6d86e66f09ab9d89790e6982159555069ab4aac3081e19`, confirms the band-avoiding pattern counts 0,1,2,13,80,579 for k=2..7 and the exact maximum k−2 of disjoint supports.
