## Parent reconstruction of the universal proof

1. Roughness is equivalent to every finite-index subgroup of Q having index coprime to M_T. An onto finite quotient gives the necessary normal subgroup; conversely, the finite left-coset action has kernel core_Q(V)⊆V, so [Q:V] divides the order of its finite image. V need not be normal and Q need not be finite.
2. For finitely many H_i≤X with pairwise-coprime finite indices a_i, the injection X/(∩H_i)→∏X/H_i bounds [X:∩H_i] above by ∏a_i. Each a_i divides the intersection index, hence their product divides it. Equality makes the map onto: **every prescribed tuple of left cosets has a common point**, without normality. Unit indices and repeated labels cause no exception.
3. In a hypothetical bad r+1-coset family C_i=g_iU_i, write a_i=[Q:π(U_i)], b_i=[N:U_i∩N], n_i=[G:U_i]=a_i b_i. Only the indices must be finite, not the cosets or subgroups. Pairwise gcd(a_i,a_j) divides a number at most T; roughness rules out every nontrivial such gcd. Thus all projected cosets share an actual q.
4. Choose one t over q and x_i∈C_i∩Nt. With K_i=U_i∩N, the common right translation gives (C_i∩Nt)t⁻¹=(x_it⁻¹)(tK_it⁻¹), disjoint actual LEFT cosets in N of indices b_i. Their pairwise gcds divide the original gcds and remain at most T, contradicting B_T(N)≤r. No quotient or kernel index factors of an individual n_i were assumed coprime.
