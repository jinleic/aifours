## Reconstruction

For each nonzero frequency a, balancedness gives `k(a)=N_a−128`, where `N_a` counts support points with `a·x=1`. Its parity is `a·T`; hence k is even exactly on K. Lucas' bit identity makes `q` quadratic there: its value is the degree-two elementary symmetric function of the support bits `a·x`. The next bit is degree at most four, so the indicator of `Z₈={a∈K:k(a)≡0 mod 8}` is `(1+e₂)(1+e₄)` of degree at most six. Also `0∈Z₈⊆{q=0}`.

For every five-dimensional L⊂K, quotient Parseval on a mixed six-flat makes `Σ_A k² mod 32` constant over its even L-cosets A. The residue calculation gives

`Σ_A k² ≡16[ wt(q|A)/4 + |A∩Z₈| ] (mod 32)`.

The five-variable quadratic weights make the quotient integral. Its parity as a function on K/L is affine: it is zero when the restricted polar rank is at most two, and for rank four with radical generated by r it is `1+q(r)+B(a,r)`. Thus the fifth derivative of `1_Z₈` is affine and its degree is at most six; when rank B≤4 that quotient is constant, so the degree is at most five. The standard minimum-weight bound gives `|Z₈|≥4`, or at least eight when rank B≤4.

If `|Z₈|=4`, a derivative along a difference of two of its points has degree at most five and support strictly smaller than eight, so is zero. All such differences are periods; because zero is in Z₈, it is a linear 2-plane. If a global radical vector lay outside it, choose a five-space L containing that vector and meeting the plane only at zero. The quadratic coset parity would be constant, while the plane occupies four distinct L-cosets out of eight, giving parities `(1,1,1,1,0,0,0,0)`, a contradiction. Hence `rad(B)⊆Z₈` and rank B≥6.

For rank eight, extend the totally singular zero plane `⟨e₁,e₂⟩` to a symplectic basis. Let `D=⟨f₁,f₂,e₃⟩` and `L=D^⊥`. Then L has dimension five, restricted polar rank two, and meets the zero plane only at zero. Every quadratic L-coset weight is divisible by eight, forcing constant Z₈ parity across the eight cosets, again contradicting the four-one/four-zero pattern. Rank eight is excluded, leaving rank six and `Z₈=rad(B)`.

Under the peak bound, `|k|≤7` makes divisibility by eight equivalent to k=0. A rank-six quadratic vanishing on its two-dimensional radical has 112 or 144 zeros; removing the four Walsh zeros leaves respectively 108 or 140 coefficients with `k=±4`, i.e. `|W_f|=16`. Without the peak hypothesis, the argument applies to a four-point divisibility set Z₈, not automatically to the actual Walsh-zero set.
