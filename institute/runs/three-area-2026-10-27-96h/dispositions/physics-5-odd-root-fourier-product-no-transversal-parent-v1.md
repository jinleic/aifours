# QLS10 Fourier products of odd-root Butson matrices have no orthogonal transversal

**Disposition: same-harness written support, conditional on one published import.** For every odd positive integer q and every `K∈BH(10,q)`, the Fourier-product array `ψ_(r,c)(t)=ζ₁₀^{rt}K_(t,c)/√10` has no set of ten pairwise orthogonal cells with distinct rows and distinct columns, under arbitrary unit row/column gauges of K and the physical symmetries. Hence this restricted family contains no 1-resolution. The import is Lam–Leung, *On vanishing sums of roots of unity* ([arXiv math/9511209v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9511209v1)), Corollary 3.4 and Theorem 4.8, used only through: every zero sum of at most ten odd-q roots is a sum of rotated regular odd-prime polygons. ## Reconstruction

Pair the transversal cells at physical rows r and r+5. Their K columns form five disjoint pairs. Because `ζ₁₀^{5t}=(−1)^t`, cell orthogonality gives `S_E−S_O=0`, while K-column orthogonality gives `S_E+S_O=0`; so both five-term half-sums vanish. A five-term zero sum of odd-q roots is one regular pentagon (5 is the only odd-prime partition of 5), so if `5∤q` no transversal exists at once. Otherwise, in `G=μ_q/μ₅`, each matched pair shares one G-value on E and one on O. A ten-term zero sum is either `5+5` (one G-value ten times or two values five times each) or `3+7` (exactly nine distinct G-values: cosets of subgroups of coprime orders meet in at most one point). The G-valued quotient of two orthogonal rows within E is constant on matched pairs, so all multiplicities are even and at most five values occur; neither profile fits unless it is constant. Dephasing makes E identically 1 and O a single pattern `(δ_c)`. Columns with distinct δ have both half-sums zero, so the E-row blocks `A_i` have pairwise orthogonal column spaces with `Σ A_iA_i†=10I₅`; then `5m_i=10·rank(A_i)` makes every group even, at most five groups occur, and an E-row versus O-row quotient must again be constant. Thus K is gauge-equivalent to a `BH(10,5)` matrix, and the universal `BH(10,5)` finite base excludes the transversal.
