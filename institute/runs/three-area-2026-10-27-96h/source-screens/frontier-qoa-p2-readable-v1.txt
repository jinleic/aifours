4. Generalized orthogonality for QLSs and QLCs
In 2018, Goyeneche et al. put forward the notion of quantum orthogonal array [18], which allows to obtain a $k$ -uniform state from a QOA. Moreover, they point out the close relation between QOAs and mutually orthogonal quantum Latin squares (or cubes). In this section, we elaborate on the notions in [18] of orthogonality among quantum Latin squares (cubes) whose arrangements may be entangled. Furthermore, we show a one-to-one relationship between them and QOAs of strength $2,3$ with minimal support, such that a family of $k$ -uniform states for $k=2,3$ can be derived by combining the results of the previous sections. The definition of QOA here is the same as that of IQOA given in Ref. [13].
Let $(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}=\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}$ , be the $N$ -fold tensor product of $\mathbb{C}^{d}$ .
The unit vectors belonging to $(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}$ represents pure quantum states of $N$ parties having $d$ internal levels each.
Definition 4.1.
A quantum orthogonal array $QOA(r,N,d,k)$ is an arrangement consisting of $r$ rows composed by $N$ -partite pure quantum states $|\varphi_{i}\rangle\in(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}$
such that,
(7)
$\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=0}^{r-1}Tr_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{N-k}}(|\varphi_{i}\rangle\langle\varphi_{j}|)$
$\displaystyle=\frac{r}{d^{k}}\mathbb{I}_{d^{k}}.$
for every subset $\{l_{1},\ldots,l_{N-k}\}$ of $N-k$ parties.
Now we give a notion of generalized orthogonality for QLSs and QLCs which allows to establish their equivalence to QOAs of strength 2, 3 with minimal support, i.e. $r=d^{k}$ .
Definition 4.2.
Let $t\geq 2$ . A set of $d^{2}$ $t$ -partite pure quantum states $|\psi_{i,j}\rangle\in(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes t}$ arranged as
$\begin{array}[]{ccc}|\psi_{0,0}\rangle&\cdots&|\psi_{0,d-1}\rangle\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
|\psi_{d-1,0}\rangle&\cdots&|\psi_{d-1,d-1}\rangle\\
\end{array}$
forms a set of generalized mutually orthogonal quantum Latin squares of dimension $d$ , denoted by $t$ -GMOQLS $(d)$ , if the following properties hold:
1. the $d^{2}$ states $|\psi_{i,j}\rangle$ are orthogonal, i.e.
(8)
$\langle\psi_{i,j}|\psi_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\rangle=\delta_{ii^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}.$
2.
(9)
$\sum^{d-1}_{i=0}Tr_{l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{t-1}}|\psi_{i,j}\rangle\langle\psi_{i,j^{\prime}}|=\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\mathbb{I}_{d},$
(10)
$\sum^{d-1}_{j=0}Tr_{l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{t-1}}|\psi_{i,j}\rangle\langle\psi_{i^{\prime},j}|=\delta_{ii^{\prime}}\mathbb{I}_{d},$
for every subset $\{l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{t-1}\}$ of $t-1$ parties.
3.
(11)
$\sum^{d-1}_{i,j=0}Tr_{l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{t-2}}|\psi_{i,j}\rangle\langle\psi_{i,j}|=\mathbb{I}_{d^{2}},$
for every subset $\{l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{t-2}\}$ of $t-2$ parties.
Remark 4.3.
If a $t$ -GMOQLS $(d)$ is composed of fully separable states, i.e. $|\psi^{A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{t}}_{i,j}\rangle\\
=|\psi^{A_{1}}_{i,j}\rangle\otimes|\psi^{A_{2}}_{i,j}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\psi^{A_{t}}_{i,j}\rangle$ for every $i,j\in[d]$ , then the $t$ -GMOQLS $(d)$ is just a $t$ -MOQLS $(d)$ . In fact, Property (3) implies Property (1); Property (2) is equivalent with arrangement $\{|\psi^{A_{s}}_{i,j}\rangle\}$ being a QLS for every $s,1\leq s\leq t$ , according to Definition 2.5; and Property (2) and (3) are equivalent with arrangements $\{|\psi^{A_{1}}_{i,j}\rangle\},\{|\psi^{A_{2}}_{i,j}\rangle\},\dots,\{|\psi^{A_{t}}_{i,j}\rangle\}$ being a set of $t$ -MOQLS( $d$ ) according to Definition 2.8.
Proposition 4.4.
A QOA $(d^{2},t+2,d,2)$ generates a $t$ -GMOQLS $(d)$ , and vice versa.
${\bf Proof:\ }$ Suppose that $|\Phi\rangle$ is the sum of the $d^{2}$ states in the QOA $(d^{2},t+2,d,2)$ . Since $|\Phi\rangle$ can produce a 2-uniform state, we choose the first two subsystems, namely, $i$ , $j$ , then
(12)
$|\Phi\rangle=\sum^{d-1}_{i,j=0}|ij\rangle\otimes|\Phi_{i,j}\rangle.$
Actually, $\{|\Phi_{i,j}\rangle\}$ is a set of arrangements of $t$ -GMOQLS $(d)$ , where $i,j\in[d]$ are the indexes of the rows and columns of the $t$ -GMOQLS.
To show this, we take an arbitrary subset $S\subseteq\{1,2,\dots,t+2\}$ with $|S|=2$ . Consider the following three cases: (1) $|S\cap\{1,2\}|=2$ ; (2) $|S\cap\{1,2\}|=1$ ; (3) $|S\cap\{1,2\}|=0$ .
1.
When $|S\cap\{1,2\}|=2$ , we have
$\displaystyle\rho_{S}$
$\displaystyle=Tr_{3,4,\dots,t+2}\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\in[d]}|ij\rangle\otimes|\Phi_{i,j}\rangle\langle i^{\prime}j^{\prime}|\otimes\langle\Phi_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}|$
$\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\in[d]}|ij\rangle\langle i^{\prime}j^{\prime}|\langle\Phi_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}|\Phi_{i,j}\rangle.$
Thus, $\rho_{S}=\sum\limits_{i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\in[d]}|ij\rangle\langle i^{\prime}j^{\prime}|\langle\Phi_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}|\Phi_{i,j}\rangle=\mathbb{I}_{d^{2}}$ if and only if Eq. (8) holds.
2.
When $|S\cap\{1,2\}|=1$ . Suppose $S\cap\{1,2\}=\{1\}$ , and $\{l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{t-1}\}\cap\{1,2\}=\emptyset$ , then we have
$\displaystyle\rho_{S}$
$\displaystyle=Tr_{2,l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{t-1}}\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\in[d]}|ij\rangle\otimes|\Phi_{i,j}\rangle\langle i^{\prime}j^{\prime}|\otimes\langle\Phi_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}|$
$\displaystyle=\sum_{i,i^{\prime}\in[d]}|i\rangle\langle i^{\prime}|\otimes\sum_{j\in[d]}Tr_{l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{t-1}}|\Phi_{i,j}\rangle\langle\Phi_{i^{\prime},j}|.$
So, $\rho_{S}=\sum\limits_{i,i^{\prime}\in[d]}|i\rangle\langle i^{\prime}|\otimes\sum\limits_{j\in[d]}Tr_{l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{t-1}}|\Phi_{i,j}\rangle\langle\Phi_{i^{\prime},j}|=\mathbb{I}_{d^{2}}$ if and only if Eq. (10) holds.
One the other hand, suppose $S\cap\{1,2\}=\{2\}$ , then $\rho_{S}=\sum\limits_{j,j^{\prime}\in[d]}|j\rangle\langle j^{\prime}|\otimes\sum\limits_{i\in[d]}Tr_{l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{t-1}}|\Phi_{i,j}\rangle\langle\Phi_{i,j^{\prime}}|=\mathbb{I}_{d^{2}}$ if and only if Eq. (9) holds.
3.
When $|S\cap\{1,2\}|=0$ , we have
$\displaystyle\rho_{S}$
$\displaystyle=Tr_{1,2,l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{t-2}}\sum_{i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\in[d]}|ij\rangle\otimes|\Phi_{i,j}\rangle\langle i^{\prime}j^{\prime}|\otimes\langle\Phi_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}|$
$\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in[d]}Tr_{l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{t-2}}|\Phi_{i,j}\rangle\langle\Phi_{i,j}|.$
Thus, $\rho_{S}=\mathbb{I}_{d^{2}}$ if and only if Eq. (11) holds. ∎
