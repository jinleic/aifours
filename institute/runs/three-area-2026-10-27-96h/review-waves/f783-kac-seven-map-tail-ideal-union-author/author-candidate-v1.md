## 3. Actual lowest kernel and whole-Cartan ideal

For an actual homogeneous `v in S_(a,b,4)` with `F_1v=F_2v=0`,
\[
 v\in R\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F_3v=0. \tag{1}
\]
The forward implication uses ideal stability and the imported **entire** `R cap S_3=0`. For the converse assume all three lowerings annihilate `v`. Let `I_+` be the linear span of `v` and every iterated positive-simple adjoint applied to `v`. It is stable under every `e_i` and under the entire Cartan. Jacobi,
\[
 [f_i,[e_j,w]]=-\delta_{ij}[h_i,w]+[e_j,[f_i,w]],
\]
proves stability under every `f_i` by induction on the number of positive adjoints. Thus `I_+` is an ideal of the full actual quotient, not only of the Levi. Every homogeneous root in it has `alpha_3` coefficient at least four. Since simple roots are independent in the full realization, none is the zero weight, so `I_+` has zero intersection with the **whole** Cartan. Whole-Cartan injection makes its inverse image in `G` Cartan-disjoint as well. Every Cartan-disjoint ideal lies in a maximal one by the union-of-chains argument; uniqueness then puts this inverse image in `tau`. This proves (1) without assuming `J=tau`.

The full-Cartan qualification is not merely a check against the span of the `h_i`. In the present numerical domain `det A=3(2-r-s)`, which is nonzero because `r+s>=3`; nevertheless the argument above is formulated for the entire Cartan in any allowed realization, including any allowed nonminimal Cartan directions. It does not rely on an unstated minimal-realization identification.

A nonzero finite-dimensional Levi submodule of `R cap S_4` has a nonzero lowest vector. Combining (1), the complete table, the two whole-isotypic exclusions and the singlet import, grade-four radical vanishing is therefore equivalent to injectivity of **all six entire** non-singlet `F_3` maps. The rank-13 map from the complete `(2,2,4)` root component to grade three has a ten-dimensional root-space kernel, but its intersection with the seven-dimensional lowest space is the relevant issue; the root kernel by itself proves no radical.

For arbitrary allowed `r,s`, one can also encode the exact simultaneous ideal calculation without choosing an overlap. Let `B` contain all length-four right-nested tensor brackets at `(a,b)`, let `I_0,I_1,I_2` be the complete joint seed-ideal columns in the degree-four source and the two `F_1,F_2` targets, and let `I_3` be the complete degree-three ideal at `(a,b)`. With
\[
 M=\begin{bmatrix}F_1B\\F_2B\\F_3B\end{bmatrix},\quad
 T=\operatorname{diag}(I_1,I_2,I_3),
\]
the radical-lowest multiplicity is
\[
 k_{a,b}=\operatorname{rank}B-\operatorname{rank}I_0
 -\operatorname{rank}[M\ T]+\operatorname{rank}I_1+\operatorname{rank}I_2+\operatorname{rank}I_3. \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the source quotient has the first two terms, and the image of the three maps modulo all three target relations has dimension `rank[M T]-rank T`. Ideal stability ensures the map is well defined. Formula (2) is an unevaluated exact criterion, not an executed rank calculation.

## 5. Union, divisor and factorial consumer

For type `i` let `m_i=(4,4,7,6,6,8)` in table order, and let all maximal minors of its integral pencil be considered. Under H4, at least one minor `Delta_i(t)` satisfies `a_i=Delta_i(0)!=0`. It has degree at most `m_i`; therefore its distinct roots number at most `m_i`. If the actual map loses rank, **every** maximal minor vanishes, in particular `Delta_i(t)`. By §3 the actual grade-four radical is nonzero exactly at the union of those six rank-drop loci (on the allowed integer domain). Thus
\[
 |\{s\ge4:R\cap S_4\ne0\}|\le\sum_i m_i
 =4+4+7+6+6+8=35.
\]
This is a bound on the union of parameter values, not a claim that the six failure sets are disjoint or that any failure exists. The singlet contributes no exception by its separate entire-isotypic import. Without that import, the seven-map bound is 36.

With H5 choose independently a maximal minor `Gamma_i(t)` with `b_i=Gamma_i(1)!=0`. They need not be the same row set. If an integer `t>=2` is a genuine bad point for type `i`, both polynomials vanish there, and the integer remainder identity gives
\[
 t\mid a_i,\qquad t-1\mid b_i,\qquad t(t-1)\mid a_i b_i. \tag{3}
\]
The last assertion uses `gcd(t,t-1)=1`. Hence every possible bad point lies in the explicit finite set
\[
 \bigcup_i\{4+d: d\ge2,\ d\mid |a_i|,\ d-1\mid |b_i|\}. \tag{4}
\]
This is a necessary condition only; each surviving candidate still requires the **whole** map, not just the two selected minors, to be checked. The individual `a_i,b_i`, their divisors and any witness minors have not been computed here. Equivalently every bad `t` for type `i` satisfies `t<=|a_i|`, `t<=1+|b_i|`, and `t(t-1)<=|a_i b_i|`.

There is a further exact count refinement, conditional on those same uncomputed integers. If `d_1,...,d_k>=2` are distinct bad integers for one type, the monic factors `(t-d_j)` divide each integral polynomial successively in `Z[t]`; hence `prod d_j` divides `a_i` and `prod(d_j-1)` divides `b_i`. Consequently
\[
 (k+1)!\le |a_i|,\qquad k!\le |b_i|,\qquad k\le m_i. \tag{5}
\]
Summing, for each type, the largest `k` satisfying (5) gives a potentially smaller rigorous union bound. No smaller numerical count is asserted without the actual coefficients. In particular, if an `a_i` is `+1` or `-1`, that type has no integer bad `t>=2` at all. A complete future finite certificate can supply the `a_i,b_i` and, for every integer in (4), an explicit nonzero maximal minor for the corresponding actual type; H4/H5 cover `t=0,1`.

## 6. A smaller explicit uniform tail bound

This is a coefficient-majorant proof, not an executable result. For the six non-singlet positions, both horizontal coordinates are at most three. The free source root dimensions from the displayed Witt coefficient array are at most `65`; consequently the chosen source quotient basis `B` has at most 65 columns. The first seed has horizontal degree `(1,0)`. At the two lower target positions its displacements from the seed are `(a-2,b)` and `(a-1,b-1)`. PBW in `sl_3` shows that a fixed displacement `(u,v)` is spanned by the ordered monomials `E_1^(u-j) E_12^j E_2^(v-j)` with `0<=j<=min(u,v)`, and is zero for a negative displacement. Thus the first relation block has at most two independent columns and the second at most three. In particular `D` has at most `65+2+3=70` columns and rank at most 70.

For each target relation, total horizontal degree is at most five, so a raising word from the seed has length at most four. A length-four bracket has tensor `l1` norm at most eight. S1 records integral elementary letter-action entries of absolute value at most two. Even without assuming sparse columns, the `l1` operator norm of each elementary letter action is at most `8*2=16`, and its induced derivation on four tensor factors is at most 64. Hence every relation column in `D` has norm at most `8*64^4=2^27=H`; the `F_1B,F_2B` columns are also bounded by `H`. Every integer entry of `D` is therefore bounded by `H`.

Choose an invertible rank-sized pivot minor. Cramer's/cofactor construction gives a basis of `ker D` over the rationals whose coordinates are integers of absolute value at most `K=70! H^70`: every relevant determinant has order at most 70 and each entry is at most `H`. A basis of the integer kernel lattice is not required. Injectivity of the projection to `B` makes their projected representatives independent, with at most 65 bracket coefficients each of size at most `K`.

For the constant and `t` coefficients separately, the actual `D_u` in §4 have letter `l1` operator norms at most 2048: the commutator bound is `||[E_1,E_2]||_1<=2*16^2=512`, and the largest constant factor is four. On a length-two bracket the corresponding `F_3` coefficient norm is at most 4096; the length-three recursion gives at most `2*2048*2+2*4096=16384`; and length four gives at most `3*2048*4+2*16384=57344`. Thus each entry of either coefficient matrix of `M_i` is at most `65*57344*K<2^22 K` in absolute value.

Every `m_i<=8`, so the nonzero integer `a_i=Delta_i(0)` obeys
\[
 |a_i|\le m_i!\,(2^{22}K)^{m_i}\le 8!\,(2^{22}K)^8=C.

If an integer `t>=1` is a zero of `Delta_i`, then `t` divides `a_i`, so `t<=C`. Therefore no six-map failure occurs for `t>=C+1`, i.e. `s>=C+5`. By the entire type/ideal consumer in §3 this is a uniform whole-grade-four radical-zero tail at the stated imports. This bound uses H4, not H5. It is not a proof for `5<=s<=C+4`.
