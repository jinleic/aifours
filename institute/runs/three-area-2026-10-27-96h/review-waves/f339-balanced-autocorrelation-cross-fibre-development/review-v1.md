### Proof

#### The direction space and the difference multiplicities

For an arbitrary \(a\in A_2\), the three nonzero elements of \(A_2+a\) are linearly independent. A dependence involving one or two is impossible because they are distinct and nonzero. A dependence involving all three would say \(X(A_2)=0\), contrary to \(x\ne0\). Hence \(\dim L=3\), so \(|L|=8\).

The six unordered pair differences of \(A_2\) are distinct. Indeed, equality between differences from disjoint pairs would give \(X(A_2)=0\), while equality between pairs sharing an atom would identify their other atoms.

None of those six differences equals \(x\): otherwise the remaining two atoms would have XOR zero and would coincide. Therefore the six differences are exactly

\[
L\setminus\{0,x\}.
\]

It follows that the translation correlation of \(A_2\) is

\[
\gamma(d):=|A_2\cap(A_2+d)|
=
\begin{cases}
4,&d=0,\\
0,&d=x,\\
2,&d\in L\setminus\{0,x\}.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]

#### Occupancy of an arbitrary \(L\)-coset

Let \(C\) be any affine \(L\)-coset and put

\[
B=A_3\cap C,\qquad m=|B|.
\]

There are eight translations \(t\) for which \(A_2+t\) lies in \(C\). Over this complete translation class, define

\[
n_t=|(A_2+t)\cap B|.
\]

Since \(A_2+t\subset C\), this is also \(|(A_2+t)\cap A_3|\), so the adopted all-shift bound gives \(n_t\le2\).

Each atom of \(B\) belongs to exactly four of those eight translates. Consequently,

\[
\sum_t n_t=4m\le16,
\]

and \(m\le4\).

If \(m=4\), equality forces \(n_t=2\) for every translation in the class, whence

\[
\sum_t n_t^2=32.
\tag{4}
\]

Let

\[
\ell=|B\cap(B+x)|.
\]

Count the same sum by ordered pairs of atoms of \(B\). Equal atoms contribute \(4m\). Distinct atoms have difference in \(L\); by (3), each ordered pair contributes two unless its difference is \(x\), when it contributes zero. There are exactly \(\ell\) ordered pairs with difference \(x\). Thus

\[
\sum_t n_t^2
=4m+2\bigl(m(m-1)-\ell\bigr)
=2m^2+2m-2\ell.
\tag{5}
\]

For \(m=4\), equations (4)–(5) imply

\[
32=40-2\ell,\qquad \ell=4.
\]

Therefore \(B+x=B\). Because \(x\ne0\), translation by \(x\) partitions \(B\) into two pairs. Each pair has XOR \(x\), so \(X(B)=x+x=0\).

#### Excluding two-coset concentration

If the eight atoms of \(A_3\) occupied at most two \(L\)-cosets, the bound of four atoms per coset would force exactly two occupied cosets, with four atoms in each. Each intersection has XOR zero by the preceding argument. Hence \(X(A_3)=0\), contradicting \(X(A_3)=x\ne0\).

This proves all four assertions. ∎
