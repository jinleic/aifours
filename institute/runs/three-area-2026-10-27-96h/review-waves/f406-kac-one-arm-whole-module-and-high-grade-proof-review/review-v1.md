## 4. Whole actual module: reconstruction

\[

\[

Its kernel is an ideal of a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra. It cannot be the whole algebra, because full-Cartan injection keeps \(h_1,h_3\) nonzero. Therefore it is injective.

\[

h_1v=-v,\qquad h_3v=-sv.

Every positive Lie word with exactly one occurrence of \(e_2\) is a sum of iterated adjoints of words in \(e_1,e_3\) acting on \(v\). This follows inductively from Jacobi: at each bracket, the branch not containing \(e_2\) lies in the \(b=0\) algebra. Consequently,

Thus this is the **whole actual \(b=1\) module**, not one weight space or a selected submodule.

### 4.2 Local finiteness and finite dimensionality

For each simple node, its adjoint raising and lowering derivations are locally nilpotent on \(S\).

On generators this follows from the relevant Serre relation, the Chevalley commutators, and the Cartan action. For a fixed Lie word, repeated application of a derivation distributes over its finitely many generator occurrences; once the exponent exceeds the sum of the individual bounds, every distributed term vanishes.

The resulting finite Weyl conjugacies for nodes \(1,3\) show that every positive-root operator of \(\mathfrak g_{13}\) is locally nilpotent.

Choose a PBW ordering \(x_1,\ldots,x_N\) of its positive-root vectors. To span

\[

\{x_1^{m_1}\cdots x_N^{m_N}v\},

first bound the exponent on the rightmost operator using its local nilpotence on \(v\). This leaves finitely many vectors. Bound the next exponent on that finite collection, and continue. The PBW spanning set therefore reduces to a finite set.

Hence \(M\) is finite-dimensional. This establishes the actual finite module needed for compact averaging; it does not import semisimplicity of the ambient nonsymmetrizable algebra.
