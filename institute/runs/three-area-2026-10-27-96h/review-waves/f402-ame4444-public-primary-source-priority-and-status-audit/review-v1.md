The comparison target is precisely:

Let \(\psi\in(\mathbb C^6)^{\otimes4}\) be normalized and let \(U=6\psi\) have unitary flattenings across all three balanced cuts. Assume:

1. exactly 48 coefficients are nonzero;
2. exactly 24 satisfy \(|U_w|=1\);

The candidate asserts these hypotheses are contradictory, without restricting residual phases to real signs or requiring equal residual moduli.

The claimed distinguishing step is not merely small-block unitarity. Exact two-extension residual fibers then force a rectangle with a forbidden fourth corner.

### 3.2 Lemma 1: genuine overlap, different statement

[§III, Lemma 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.16673v1#Thmlemma1) assumes that each reshaping’s support is a partition into full \(1\times1\) and \(2\times2\) blocks. Writing
\[
\Phi_x=\sqrt{s_x/72}\,e^{i\varphi_x},
\]
it gives:

- \(s_x=2\) on singleton blocks;
- opposite squared moduli equal in each \(2\times2\) block;
- the two squared moduli in a row sum to 2;
- the alternating phase sum is \(\pi\pmod{2\pi}\).

The general statement allows complex phases. Its subsequent \(\mathbb F_2\) criterion addresses **real** solutions on such a support.

**Disposition: SUPPORTED prior machinery.** The complex modulus/phase separation and block criterion should be credited, not described as newly established by the candidate.

The lemma does **not** state a support-48 exclusion, specify 24 maximal entries, introduce residual alphabets of size four, or derive the common-\(P\) matching normal form. Applying its criterion after additional support-combinatorial work is not the same as finding the exact obstruction already stated there.

### 3.3 §VI: support bounds and open status, as dated source claims

[§VI](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.16673v1#S6) states that:

- a coefficient of modulus \(1/6\) in \(\psi\) exhausts its row and column in every reshaping;
- the corresponding classical words form a length-four, distance-three code over six symbols;
- such a code has at most 34 words, citing Parker;
- if \(t\) is the number of classical officers and \(s\) the support, \(s\ge72-t\ge38\);
- conversely, \(s<72\) implies at least \(72-s\) classical officers.

It concludes:

> “Whether the gap between 38 and 60 can be closed is open.”

**Disposition: SUPPORTED as Pandya v1’s stated bound/status.** This is not an independent reconstruction of Parker’s theorem or an exhaustive present-day open-status certificate.

For the supplied \(s=48,t=24\) assumptions, this published counting inequality is saturated. It does not itself contradict that case, and it contains no residual-\(4444\) condition.
