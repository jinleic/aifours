**Status: SUPPORTED. References: S1:49–80.**

\[
P=\{w:|U_w|=1\}
\]
and exactly 24 residual entries
\[
E=\{w:0<|U_w|<1\}.
\]

In any balanced flattening, a modulus-one entry exhausts its row norm and column norm. Consequently:

1. The 24 entries of \(P\) occupy distinct rows and distinct columns.
2. No other nonzero entry uses any of those rows or columns.
3. Deleting those rows and columns leaves a \(12\times12\) unitary matrix containing precisely the 24 entries of \(E\).

Every remaining row and column has at least two nonzero entries: a singleton in a unit-norm row or column would have modulus one and would already belong to \(P\). There are 12 rows and 24 nonzeros, so every remaining row has exactly two nonzeros; the same counting applies to columns.

This argument applies separately to all three cuts
\[
12\mid34,\qquad13\mid24,\qquad14\mid23.

Their row and column coordinate pairs cover all six unordered party pairs.

For every pair \(ij\), therefore,
\[
R_{ij}:=\pi_{ij}(E)

has exactly 12 values, each with exactly two extensions in \(E\). The projection of the **same** set \(P\) is injective and satisfies
\[
\pi_{ij}(P)=[6]^2\setminus R_{ij}.

These are consequences of actual tensor unitarity, not assumptions imposed on six unrelated pair graphs.
