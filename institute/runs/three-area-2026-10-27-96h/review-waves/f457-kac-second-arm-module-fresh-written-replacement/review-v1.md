## 3. Reconstruction of the complete positive degree-two source

Let
\[
\mathfrak a=\mathfrak n_{13}^{+}.
\]

First impose the \(b=0\) Serre relations. They give the positive algebra on nodes \(1,3\). Before imposing relations involving two copies of \(e_2\), the elimination construction has the form
\[
\mathfrak a\ltimes \mathcal L\bigl(U(\mathfrak a)v\bigr).
\]

This follows directly from the universal property. Given a homomorphism from \(\mathfrak a\) into another Lie algebra and an image for \(v\), every vector \(uv\), \(u\in U(\mathfrak a)\), must map to the corresponding iterated adjoint action on that image. That module map extends uniquely to its ordinary free Lie algebra. Conversely, this semidirect construction satisfies precisely those generator and action requirements.

The \(b=1\) Serres are
\[
E_1^2v,\qquad E_3^{s+1}v.

Let \(K\) be their **complete** \(U(\mathfrak a)\)-span inside \(U(\mathfrak a)v\). Imposing these relations and all their Lie consequences replaces
\[
\mathcal L\bigl(U(\mathfrak a)v\bigr)
\quad\text{by}\quad
\mathcal L\bigl(U(\mathfrak a)v/K\bigr).
\]
Indeed, quotienting a free Lie algebra by the Lie ideal generated by a subspace of its generators gives the free Lie algebra on the quotient generator space.

By the imported first-arm positive presentation,
\[
U(\mathfrak a)v/K=M.
\]
Thus, after **all** \(b=1\) closures have been imposed, the formal positive source is
\[
\mathfrak a\ltimes\mathcal L(M),

and its \(b=2\) part is exactly
\[
\mathcal L_2(M)=\Lambda^2M.

### 4.3 Irreducibility, multiplicities, and disjointness

The module \(\Lambda^2M\) is finite-dimensional and semisimple over \(\mathfrak g_{13}\).

A joint simple-lowest vector of fixed weight can have components in several copies of one irreducible representation. This does **not** make its cyclic module the whole isotypic component. Writing that component as \(L\otimes C\), its joint lowest space is \(\ell_{\mathrm{low}}\otimes C\). A single nonzero vector has the form \(\ell_{\mathrm{low}}\otimes c\), and generates precisely \(L\otimes\mathbb Cc\), one irreducible copy.

Therefore
\[
Q_1:=U(\mathfrak g_{13})q_1
\cong L_{\mathrm{low}}(0,2s+r),
\]
\[
Q_3:=U(\mathfrak g_{13})q_3
\cong L_{\mathrm{low}}(3,2s-2).
\]

For either vector, PBW and its annihilation by \(\mathfrak n_{13}^{-}\) give
\[
U(\mathfrak g_{13})q_i=U(\mathfrak n_{13}^{+})q_i.
\]
These are exactly the positive closures required by the degree-two ideal, not larger replacement relation spaces.

Their lowest labels differ. Thus the two simple modules are nonisomorphic and have zero intersection. Consequently,

\[
