## 2. The complete free-Lie character and its 13 multiplicities

Let \(x=e_3\) and \(a=E_1x\). The actual grade-one source \(V\) is the eight-dimensional adjoint \(\mathfrak{sl}_3\)-module, with horizontal character
\[
 H(X,Y)=(1+X)(1+Y)(1+XY).
\]
This is a character of genuine free-Lie letters, not multiplication of \(3\times3\) matrices. Write \(H_j=H(X^j,Y^j)\). Ordinary PBW/Witt gives
\[
 C(X,Y)=\operatorname{ch}\operatorname{Lie}_4(V)
 =\tfrac14(H^4-H_2^2).
\]
For a derivation, compare degree four in the formal logarithm of \(T(V)=U(\operatorname{Lie}(V))\): the degree-two and degree-one terms combine to \(H_2^2/4\). This is a character identity, with no implication about the rank of any Chevalley map.

The \(H_1,H_2\)-weight of a grade-four horizontal component \((a,b)\) is \((2a-b-4,2b-a-4)\). Thus its lowest labels are
\[
 (p,q)=(4-2a+b,\ 4+a-2b).
\]
A lowest position must have \(a,b\ge0\), \(2a-b\le4\), and \(2b-a\le4\). These inequalities imply \(0\le a,b\le4\); direct enumeration yields exactly the 13 rows below, not a selection of examples.

For a reproducible coefficient calculation let \(c_{ab}=[X^aY^b]C\), with out-of-range indices zero, and \(D=(1-X)(1-Y)(1-XY)\). Set binomial coefficients outside their natural range to zero. Explicitly,
\[
 c_{ab}=\tfrac14\left[\sum_{k\ge0}{4\choose k}{4\choose a-k}{4\choose b-k}
 -\mathbf 1_{2\mid a,\,2\mid b}\sum_{k\ge0}{2\choose k}{2\choose a/2-k}{2\choose b/2-k}\right].
\]

\]
For example, the \((2,2)\) numerator is \(36+64+6-6=100\). The coefficient of \(X^aY^b\) in \(DC\) is
\[
 m_{ab}=c_{ab}-c_{a-1,b}-c_{a,b-1}+c_{a-2,b-1}

## 3. Whole grade-four relation and the type-absence boundary

For \(r=3,s\ge4\), the first seed has third grade four and, up to its irrelevant sign, is
\[
 v=(\operatorname{ad}x)^3a.
\]
It is nonzero already in the free Lie algebra: its tensor expansion contains \(x^3a-3x^2ax+3xax^2-ax^3\). The actual Levi action has \(F_1x=F_2x=F_2a=0\) and \(F_1a=x\); hence both \(F_1v\) and \(F_2v\) vanish. Its lowest labels are \((2,5)\). Complete reducibility shows that the cyclic module generated by this *one* lowest vector is one irreducible diagonal copy \(L_{\rm low}(2,5)\), even if the ambient module had equivalent summands.

The other seed has grade \(s+1\ge5\), and bracketing a grade-four seed with a positive-grade letter strictly raises grade. The imported **whole** Serre-ideal bridge therefore identifies the complete actual grade-four relation space with exactly this cyclic module; there are no omitted negative-Serre consequences or untested overlaps in this regime. The Weyl dimension is
\[
 \dim L_{\rm low}(2,5)=\frac{(2+1)(5+1)(2+5+2)}2=81.
\]
Its relative character from the lowest position is
\[
 P_{2,5}(X,Y)=\frac{1-X^3-Y^6+X^9Y^6+X^3Y^9-X^9Y^9}
 {(1-X)(1-Y)(1-XY)}.
\]
At relative \(XY^2\), no nonconstant numerator term contributes and the denominator gives two solutions, corresponding to \(X\cdot Y^2\) and \((XY)\cdot Y\). Thus the **whole relation weight** at absolute \((2,2,4)\) is two-dimensional. The total module dimension is independently consistent with \((8^4-8^2)/4-81=927\), and the irreducible dimensions in the last table column sum to 927.

## 4. Actual full-sign \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) action and surjectivity

The Chevalley brackets give \([E_3,F_3]=H_3\), \([H_3,E_3]=2E_3\), and \([H_3,F_3]=-2F_3\) on the **actual** quotient \(S\). Both \(E_3\) and \(F_3\) are locally nilpotent there, not merely on the eight-letter model:

* for \(i=1,2\), the positive Serres give \(E_3^{1+r}e_1=0\) and \(E_3^{1+s}e_2=0\), while \(E_3f_i=0\);
* the negative Serres give \(F_3^{1+r}f_1=0\) and \(F_3^{1+s}f_2=0\), while \(F_3e_i=0\);
* on \(e_3,f_3\) and on **every** vector of the full Cartan, the Chevalley brackets give short nilpotent strings. In particular extra Cartan directions require no inversion or quotienting.

Each element of \(S\) is a finite linear combination of finite Lie words in these generators. Since \(E_3,F_3\) are derivations, the multinomial Leibniz expansion gives a finite nilpotence bound on each such word. This proves local nilpotence on the whole quotient, using both signs of Serre relations.

Fix a root-homogeneous \(v\) of \(H_3\)-weight \(m\). Its generated \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\)-module is finite-dimensional: ordinary \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) PBW expresses it as the span of \(F_3^j H_3^\ell E_3^i v\); only finitely many \(i\) occur, each \(E_3^iv\) is an \(H_3\)-eigenvector, and for each such \(i\) local nilpotence bounds \(j\). In any finite-dimensional irreducible of highest weight \(L\ge0\), a present weight \(m<0\) satisfies \(m<L\), so its weight-\(m\) vector has a weight-\(m+2\) predecessor with nonzero \(F_3\)-image. Complete reducibility then gives surjectivity on the weight-\(m\) subspace of the generated module.

The full realization separates root degrees. Both \(E_3\) and \(F_3\) preserve \((a,b)\) and change \(n\) by \(+1\) and \(-1\), respectively; the \(H_3\)-weight is
\[
 m=2n-ra-sb.
\]
Consequently, for every allowed \(r,s\), every \(a,b\ge0\), and every \(n\ge1\) with \(2n-ra-sb<0\), the **actual** map
\[
 F_3:S_{a,b,n+1}\longrightarrow S_{a,b,n}
\]
is onto. The predecessor constructed above lies in precisely the indicated root component because its horizontal degrees are unchanged and its \(H_3\)-weight determines the change in third grade. This is a theorem about actual Chevalley operators; it is not a character-as-rank inference or a claim about arbitrary zero/nonnegative weights.

## 5. Exact \((2,2)\) root dimensions and kernel; radical limit

For every \(r=3,s\ge4\), no seed reaches grade three, so
\[
 \operatorname{ch}S_3=\tfrac13(H^3-H_3).
\]
Here the second \(H_3\) in this display denotes \(H(X^3,Y^3)\), **not** the operator \(\operatorname{ad}h_3\). At \(X^2Y^2\) its coefficient is zero, and the \(H^3\) coefficient is \(9+27+3=39\); therefore \(\dim S_{2,2,3}=13\). In grade four, §2 gives free dimension 25 and §3 proves that the entire relation at this weight has dimension 2, hence \(\dim S_{2,2,4}=23\).

For target \((a,b,n)=(2,2,3)\), the actual \(H_3\)-weight is \(6-3\cdot2-s\cdot2=-2s<0\). The independently proved surjectivity therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(F_3:S_{2,2,4}\to S_{2,2,3})=13,
 \qquad \dim\ker F_3=23-13=10.
\]
The **complete** lowest-vector space in \(S_{2,2,4}\) has dimension 7: lowest label \((2,2)\) has free multiplicity 7, and the only removed irreducible has different lowest label \((2,5)\). If \(K\) is the root kernel and \(L\) that lowest space, then
\[
 \dim\bigl((K+L)/L\bigr)=\dim K-\dim(K\cap L)\ge10-7=3.
\]
This does not assume that \(L\subseteq K\). It shows that the root kernel cannot consist only of lowest vectors.

Since \(\mathcal R\) is an ideal and the S4/S5 import says \(\mathcal R\cap S_3=0\), necessarily \(\mathcal R\cap S_{2,2,4}\subseteq K\). The reverse inclusion is **not** proved: \(F_3v=0\) alone is not the required full ideal/radical condition.
