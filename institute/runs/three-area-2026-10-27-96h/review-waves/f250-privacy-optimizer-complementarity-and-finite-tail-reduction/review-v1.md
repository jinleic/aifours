## Independent reconstruction

Write `c(x)=|x|_2^2`, `Phi_V(E)=integral_E(c-V) dx`, and `D={|x|_1<=1}`. All set inclusions stated exactly below use the closed D.

### 1. Existence and the everywhere representative

The normalized product density `(epsilon^2/4) exp(-epsilon |x|_1)` satisfies the shift inequality by the triangle inequality and has second moment `4/epsilon^2`. A minimizing sequence therefore has a uniform finite second-moment bound. Markov's inequality gives tightness; a weakly convergent subsequence has a probability limit. For each fixed h in D, the inequality against nonnegative bounded continuous functions passes to the limit, and regularity/uniqueness of finite Borel measures gives it for every Borel event. Since `min(c,R)` is bounded continuous, the supremum over R proves lower semicontinuity of the cost. Thus the limit is a **feasible attained minimizer**. It has V>0: the only zero-cost probability is delta_0, and for any nonzero h in D the event {0} violates privacy. This does not assert uniqueness.

Integrating `b mu(A) <= mu(A+h)` over D and applying Tonelli gives `2b mu(A) <= integral |D intersect (x-A)| dmu(x) <= |A|`. Hence mu has a bounded Lebesgue density. Define its Borel representative from the limsup of rational-radius ball averages. The same measure inequality for each pair of shifted balls gives `f(x+h)>=b f(x)` for **every** x and h in closed D, not just for a countable family of shifts. Differentiation gives the original density a.e.; the pointwise bound is `f<=1/(2b)`. Some point has positive f, and finitely many D-steps join it to any other point, so f is positive everywhere. Let `M=sup f`, finite and positive.

### 2. Phase decomposition, with optimality derived

For `a in (bM,M)`, set `E_i={f>ab^i}`, `n_a=min{i:x in E_i}` and `u_a=b^n_a`. The pointwise density inequality implies `E_i+D subset E_(i+1)` exactly, hence `n_a(x+h)<=n_a(x)+1` and `u_a(x+h)>=b u_a(x)`. Since f is strictly positive, every n_a is finite. If `f(x)>a`, a sufficiently small rational-radius ball has average greater than a; thus E_0 has positive area. Set `f(x)=M b^kappa` and `a=M b^sigma`, 0<sigma<1. The integer threshold wraps `sigma+n_a` once through a unit interval above kappa, up to endpoints. Therefore `integral_(bM)^M u_a(x) da=(1-b) f(x)`.

Tonelli gives `integral Z_a da=1-b` and `integral N_a da=(1-b) J(mu)`, with `Z_a=integral u_a` and `N_a=integral c u_a`. For Lebesgue-a.e. a, both are finite and Z_a>0. The normalized phase `P_a=u_a/Z_a` is a full-D private probability, and `w(a)=Z_a/(1-b)` is a probability density over a, giving the exact measure mixture `mu=integral w(a)P_a da`. If mu is optimal, each component has cost at least V and its weighted average is V; consequently **almost every** component is optimal. For such a component, the layer-cake identity yields

`Z=(1-b) sum_i b^i |E_i|`, `N=(1-b) sum_i b^i integral_(E_i)c`, and `(1-b) sum_i b^i Phi_V(E_i)=0`.

Both positive series converge, so the signed series is absolutely convergent. The representation and attainment are derived here; they are not an imported optimizer-structure theorem.

### 3. Four directions and canonical exact levels

Use the m1 convention. For each of the two axis and two diagonal directions separately, an e-section of D is a centered interval, possibly a singleton. If `A+D subset B`, the section of B at transverse position p+q contains the Minkowski sum of the corresponding A and D sections. For a nonempty finite-length A section, an inner-regular compact approximation plus the interval added to an extreme point gives `length(A_section+D_section)>=length(A_section)+length(D_section)`; infinite lengths follow by compact approximation. Thus the centered section of `S_e B` contains the sum of the centered section of `S_e A` and the original D section, including endpoints. This proves the exact inclusion. Empty/zero A fibers have been discarded by m1, so they cannot create spurious points. Symmetrized levels retain positive initial area, cover the plane by successive D-expansions, and define a feasible phase.

At each finite-length section, the centered interval uniquely minimizes the integral of the squared along-section coordinate up to null sets; the transverse coordinate contributes an unchanged constant times section length. Each separate symmetrization preserves all areas and can only lower the moment. Since the phase is already globally optimal, the total nonnegative decrease is zero; every level is equal a.e. to its symmetrization in each of the four directions. No claim that one symmetrization preserves the previous three is used.

The double axis symmetrization is a coordinate down-set: after centering horizontal fibers, the vertical section length is nonincreasing in absolute horizontal coordinate. Its boundary is null (a monotone boundary has at most countably many jumps and otherwise at most one boundary height per fiber). Therefore E_i agrees a.e. with its essential support K_i. Contraction along any of the four fiber directions by a factor alpha in (0,1], followed by reflection, preserves a centered-section representative and positive area locally; taking supports and then alpha to zero gives **closed centered interval sections on every line**. The four reflections give D4 symmetry. Positive area and the axis sections put 0 in the interior of K_0. For x in K_i, reflect it into the first quadrant, put s=|x|_1, and use the anti-diagonal section to reach `(s/2,s/2)`; axis sections then contain the square of area s^2. Because `|K_i|=|E_i|<infinity`, all K_i are bounded. They are closed by definition, hence compact.

Finally, if x is in K_i and h is in D, every positive-area neighborhood of x translates into a positive-area neighborhood of x+h inside E_(i+1). Thus `K_i+D subset K_(i+1)` literally, not just a.e. Countable a.e. replacement leaves the phase measure and cost unchanged. This establishes the compact canonical representative for almost every optimal phase of every optimal law.

### 4. One-level variations and null/boundary treatment

For i>=1 put `F_i=K_(i-1)+D`. It is compact. Replace only K_i by `F_i union (K_i intersect closed_B_sqrt(V))`. It remains between F_i and K_i, and its D-dilation is contained in K_(i+1), so all inclusions and the finite positive phase normalization remain valid. The new unnormalized `N-VZ` would then be negative, contradicting V. Thus `K_i\(F_i union closed_B)` is null. Because the bracket is closed and K_i is its own essential support, any point of K_i outside it would have a ball disjoint from the bracket but containing positive area of K_i, a contradiction. This proves the **exact** upper inclusion in A1. For i=0, intersect with the closed ball instead; V>0 and 0 in the interior ensure positive remaining area, and the same argument yields `K_0 subset closed_B` exactly.

Conversely, for i>=0 and 0<r<sqrt(V), add `(K_(i+1) erosion D) intersect closed_B_r` to K_i. ### 5. Equality-case cutoff

Inductively `K_(i-1)+D` contains `K_0+iD`, hence contains iD because 0 is in K_0. For `|x|_2<=sqrt(V)`, Cauchy–Schwarz gives `|x|_1<=sqrt(2V)`. The exact A1 sandwich therefore reduces to `K_i=K_(i-1)+D` exactly. Equivalently an a.e. equality would also upgrade because a nonempty compact A+D is its own essential support, and so is K_i.

Since `V<=U=4/epsilon^2`, `ceil(sqrt(2V))<=ceil(sqrt(2U))=ceil(2 sqrt(2)/epsilon)=N`. V>0 makes N>=1. Thus for each optimal phase, `K_(N-1)+jD` is its exact tail, j>=1. Neither the early prefix nor the tail core is asserted to be common across distinct phases.

- Optimizer structure: A1, A2, (8), A3, and B6 are **necessary for the constructed representatives of almost every optimal phase**, with the specified true V or candidate lambda. They are not sufficient on their own. The universal cutoff is sufficient for the exact eventual tail of such phases, not a claim of an initially common core.
- Actual-V insufficiency, using only the adopted f245 scope: for b>=2/3, the square `C=[-1/2,1/2]^2` and `S_i=C+iD` have `V>1/2`, hence C lies strictly in the cost ball. Thus the actual-V local conditions and immediate common tail hold, but the adopted f245 phase P_K has lower cost than P_S.
