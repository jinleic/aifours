## 1. Results and exact relation to the target

Let \(S\) be a nonempty regular semigroup and let
\[
q:S\twoheadrightarrow G
\]
be the prescribed idempotent-pure homomorphism onto a group. The normalized physical host is
\[
T(C,G)=C\rtimes G,\qquad
(c,g)(d,h)=\bigl(c(g\cdot d),gh\bigr),
\]
where \(C\) is a nonempty band and \(G\) acts by band automorphisms.

This return establishes four results.

1. **Whole-regular-class actor rigidity.** Every onto idempotent-pure group quotient of \(S\) has the same kernel congruence: the least group congruence. Consequently, an embedding of \(S\) into a band semidirect product with **any group actor** can be transported to the **exact prescribed \(q\)**. No homomorphism from an entire larger supplied actor to \(G\) is needed.

2. **Whole-regular-class source separation.** The coordinates
   \[
   \bigl(q(s),[s]_{\mathcal R},[s]_{\mathcal L}\bigr)
   \]
   separate the elements of \(S\). This uses neither a unique inverse nor commuting idempotents. It is a separation statement, not by itself a band-coordinate multiplication theorem.

3. **An explicit universal band-host criterion.** A word presentation constructs a band \(C_q\), an actual \(G\)-action, and a homomorphism
   \[
   \eta_q:S\longrightarrow C_q\rtimes G
   \]
   over exactly \(q\). Embeddability is equivalent to a precisely stated generator-separation condition in that presentation. Any finite derivation violating this condition is a certificate against **every** band host, not merely a failed preselected host.

4. **A complete noninverse class with an explicit physical host.** If \(S=M\) is a unit-regular monoid, then
   \[
   M\cong E(M)\rtimes G
   \]
   over exactly \(q\), with the action and isomorphism constructed directly from \(M,q\). No commutativity or additional band identity is imposed on \(E(M)\). A six-element noninverse example below has a genuinely nontrivial action and is not isomorphic to a direct product of a band and a group.

These are not a self-contained counterexample to general regular-source embeddability, nor a proof that every regular source has a host. The universal criterion is not asserted to be automatically injective.

## 2. Definitions and the physical projection

Write
\[
E(S)=\{e\in S:e^2=e\}.
\]
Idempotent purity of a group-valued \(q\) means
\[
q^{-1}(1)=E(S).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Thus \(E(S)\) is a band: if \(e,f\in E(S)\), then \(q(ef)=1\), so \(ef\) is idempotent. Since \(S\) is regular, \(S\) is therefore orthodox.

An inverse of \(s\) means any \(s'\in S\) satisfying
\[
ss's=s,\qquad s'ss'=s'.
\]
Its group image necessarily satisfies
\[
q(s')=q(s)^{-1}.
\tag{2.2}
\]
No uniqueness of \(s'\) is assumed.

For any band \(C\) and any group action as above,
\[
(c,g)^2=(c,g)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g=1.
\]
Indeed, the forward implication follows from \(g^2=g\) in the group; for the reverse implication, \((c,1)^2=(c^2,1)=(c,1)\). Hence
\[
\pi_G^{-1}(1)=E(C\rtimes G).
\tag{2.3}
\]

It follows that every embedding into this physical host induces an idempotent-pure group-valued actor map. This necessity does not require regularity or surjectivity of the source map.

## 3. Every pure onto group quotient is the least group quotient

### Theorem 3.1 — Intrinsic actor congruence

Let \(S\) be a nonempty regular semigroup. Let \(\sigma\) be the congruence generated by all pairs
\[
(e,f),\qquad e,f\in E(S).
\]
Then \(S/\sigma\) is a group and \(\sigma\) is the least group congruence.

If \(q:S\twoheadrightarrow G\) is idempotent-pure, then
\[
\ker q=\sigma.
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof

All idempotents have the same \(\sigma\)-class; denote this class by \(\varepsilon\).

For \(s\in S\), choose an inverse \(s'\). Both \(ss'\) and \(s's\) are idempotent, so
\[
[s][s']=\varepsilon=[s'][s].
\]
Moreover,
\[
\varepsilon[s]=[ss'][s]=[s],
\qquad
[s]\varepsilon=[s][s's]=[s].
\]
Thus \(\varepsilon\) is a two-sided identity on \(S/\sigma\), and every \([s]\) has inverse \([s']\). Therefore \(S/\sigma\) is a group.

Every homomorphism from \(S\) into a group identifies all idempotents, so its kernel contains \(\sigma\). This proves minimality.

Now take the prescribed onto pure \(q\). It factors through a surjective group homomorphism
\[
\bar q:S/\sigma\twoheadrightarrow G.
\]
If \(\bar q([s])=1\), then \(q(s)=1\). Purity gives \(s\in E(S)\), hence \([s]=\varepsilon\). Thus \(\bar q\) has trivial kernel and is an isomorphism. Consequently \(\ker q=\sigma\). ∎

### Consequence

If
\[
q_1:S\twoheadrightarrow G_1,\qquad
q_2:S\twoheadrightarrow G_2
\]
are both idempotent-pure, there is a unique group isomorphism
\[
\theta:G_1\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}G_2
\]
such that
\[
\theta q_1=q_2.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus two pure onto group actors cannot distinguish different pairs of source elements.

## 4. Transporting any group-actor source embedding to the exact prescribed actor

### Theorem 4.1 — Group-actor independence

Under the hypotheses of Theorem 3.1, the following are equivalent:

1. There exist a band \(C\), a \(G\)-action and an embedding
   \[
   F:S\hookrightarrow C\rtimes G,\qquad \pi_GF=q.
   \]
2. There exist a band \(B\), a group \(H\), an \(H\)-action and an embedding
   \[
   j:S\hookrightarrow B\rtimes H.
   \]

The implication \(2\Rightarrow1\) is constructive and preserves the existing band coordinates of \(j\).

### Proof

Only \(2\Rightarrow1\) needs proof. Write
\[
j(s)=(b_s,r(s)).
\]

The actor map \(r:S\to H\) is idempotent-pure by (2.3) and injectivity of \(j\).

Its image
\[
K=r(S)
\]
is a subgroup of \(H\). It is closed under multiplication; it contains \(1\), since \(r(ss')=1\); and \(r(s')=r(s)^{-1}\) supplies the inverse of each image element.

View \(r\) as a pure onto map \(S\twoheadrightarrow K\). Theorem 3.1 gives
\[
\ker r=\ker q.
\]
Hence there is a group isomorphism
\[
\theta:G\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}K,
\qquad
\theta(q(s))=r(s).
\]

Restrict the original \(H\)-action on \(B\) to \(K\) and transport it along \(\theta\):
\[
g\star b=\theta(g)\cdot_H b.
\tag{4.1}
\]
This is an action of \(G\) by band automorphisms.

Define
\[
F(s)=(b_s,q(s)).
\]
At the actual consuming multiplication,
\[
\begin{aligned}
F(s)F(t)
&=\bigl(b_s(q(s)\star b_t),q(s)q(t)\bigr)\\
&=\bigl(b_s(r(s)\cdot_H b_t),q(st)\bigr)\\
&=(b_{st},q(st))\\
&=F(st).
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(F(s)=F(t)\), then \(b_s=b_t\) and \(q(s)=q(t)\), so \(r(s)=r(t)\). Thus \(j(s)=j(t)\), and injectivity of \(j\) yields \(s=t\). Finally, \(\pi_GF=q\) literally. ∎

### Important boundary

This theorem **does not** extend a homomorphism from \(K\) to all of \(H\). It discards the unused actor elements \(H\setminus K\), then changes the action through \(G\cong K\).

It is therefore consistent with frozen S2’s \(C_2\leq C_4\) whole-host extension obstruction. It also makes no assertion about replacing a genuinely multirow/multicolumn completely simple actor by a group actor.

## 5. Source separation without unique inverses

### Theorem 5.1 — Green-coordinate separation

Let \(S,q\) satisfy the regular-source hypotheses. If
\[
s\mathrel{\mathcal R}t,\qquad
s\mathrel{\mathcal L}t,\qquad
q(s)=q(t),
\]
then \(s=t\).

Equivalently,
\[
s\longmapsto
\bigl(q(s),[s]_{\mathcal R},[s]_{\mathcal L}\bigr)
\tag{5.1}
\]
is injective.

### Proof

Choose an inverse \(s'\) of \(s\), and put
\[
e=s's,\qquad a=s't.
\]
Since \(q(s')=q(s)^{-1}\) and \(q(t)=q(s)\), purity gives
\[
a^2=a.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The relation \(s\mathrel{\mathcal R}t\) supplies \(u,v\in S^1\) such that
\[
t=su,\qquad s=tv.
\]
The relation \(s\mathrel{\mathcal L}t\) supplies \(w\in S^1\) with \(t=ws\).

We have
\[
av=s'tv=s's=e,
\]
and, because \(se=s\),
\[
te=wse=ws=t,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad ae=a.
\]
On the other hand, (5.2) and \(e=av\) give
\[
ae=a(av)=a^2v=av=e.
\]
Thus \(a=e\). Finally,
\[
t=ss't=sa=se=s,
\]
where \(ss't=t\) follows from \(t=su\). ∎

This settles a set-theoretic separation issue for the whole regular class. It does **not** show that the \(\mathcal R\)- and \(\mathcal L\)-coordinates admit the required global \(G\)-action or band cocycle multiplication.

## 6. An explicit universal band host and whole-class obstruction criterion

The construction in this section applies to any nonempty semigroup \(S\) and any homomorphism \(q:S\to G\); regularity and purity are not needed to define it.

### 6.1 Band presentation

Let
\[
A=G\times S,
\]
and let \(A^+\) be the free semigroup of nonempty words over \(A\).

Define \(\equiv_q\) to be the least semigroup congruence containing:

\[
ww\equiv_q w
\quad\text{for every nonempty word }w\in A^+,
\tag{6.1}
\]
and
\[
(g,st)\equiv_q(g,s)(gq(s),t)
\quad
(g\in G,\ s,t\in S).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Set
\[
C_q=A^+/{\equiv_q}.
\]

Relation (6.1) is imposed for **all words**, not only alphabet letters. Consequently every element of \(C_q\) is idempotent, so \(C_q\) is a nonempty band.

For \(h\in G\), left translation of alphabet indices,
\[
L_h(g,s)=(hg,s),
\]
extends to a free-semigroup automorphism. It preserves both relation families. In particular, it sends (6.2) to
\[
(hg,st)\equiv_q(hg,s)(hgq(s),t).
\]
Thus it induces a band automorphism of \(C_q\), and
\[
L_hL_k=L_{hk}.
\]
This is the required \(G\)-action.

Put
\[
c_s=[(1,s)].
\]
Relation (6.2) gives
\[
c_{st}=c_s\bigl(q(s)\cdot c_t\bigr).
\tag{6.3}
\]
Hence
\[
\eta_q(s)=(c_s,q(s))
\tag{6.4}
\]
is an actual homomorphism \(S\to C_q\rtimes G\) over exactly \(q\).

### 6.2 Universal property

Suppose \(B\) is any band with a \(G\)-action, and
\[
H:S\longrightarrow B\rtimes G,\qquad
H(s)=(b_s,q(s))
\]
is any homomorphism over \(q\), not necessarily injective.

There is a unique \(G\)-equivariant band homomorphism
\[
\Phi:C_q\longrightarrow B
\]
such that
\[
\Phi(c_s)=b_s.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Indeed, evaluate each alphabet letter by
\[
(g,s)\longmapsto g\cdot b_s.
\]
Every word-square relation is respected because \(B\) is a band. The cocycle relation is respected because
\[
\begin{aligned}
g\cdot b_{st}
&=g\cdot\bigl(b_s(q(s)\cdot b_t)\bigr)\\
&=(g\cdot b_s)\bigl(gq(s)\cdot b_t\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
The evaluation therefore descends to \(C_q\). Equivariance is built into the alphabet evaluation. Uniqueness follows because \(C_q\) is generated by the elements \(g\cdot c_s\).

### Theorem 6.1 — Exact source-embedding criterion

There exists an embedding
\[
S\hookrightarrow B\rtimes G
\]
over the prescribed \(q\), for some band \(B\) and action, if and only if
\[
q(s)=q(t)\ \text{and}\ (1,s)\equiv_q(1,t)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad s=t.
\tag{6.6}
\]

When (6.6) holds, \(\eta_q\) is the desired embedding.

### Proof

Condition (6.6) is exactly injectivity of (6.4), proving sufficiency.

Conversely, let \(H\) be an embedding over \(q\). If the antecedent of (6.6) holds, the universal property gives \(b_s=b_t\). Together with \(q(s)=q(t)\), this yields \(H(s)=H(t)\), so \(s=t\). ∎

### What would constitute a genuine all-host counterexample?

A source \(S\), a pure onto \(q\), distinct \(s,t\) with \(q(s)=q(t)\), and a finite derivation
\[
(1,s)\equiv_q(1,t)
\]
using (6.1)–(6.2), in arbitrary word contexts, would refute embeddability into **every** band host over \(q\).

Such finite chains characterize the generated congruence: their equivalence relation is itself a congruence containing the defining pairs and is contained in every such congruence.

For regular \(S\), Theorem 4.1 would then also exclude embeddings into band semidirect products with any other group actor.

No such collision is claimed in this return. This section supplies an explicit whole-class criterion and obstruction-certificate format, not an unconditional injectivity proof, a completed counterexample, an efficiency bound or an identification with an unspecified printed-source “canonical host.”

## 7. Complete physical-host theorem for unit-regular monoids

A monoid \(M\) is **unit-regular** if, for every \(s\in M\), some unit \(v\in U(M)\) satisfies
\[
svs=s.
\tag{7.1}
\]

### Theorem 7.1 — Pure unit-regular sources are full band semidirect products

Let \(M\) be a monoid and let \(q:M\twoheadrightarrow G\) be idempotent-pure. The following are equivalent:

1. \(q(U(M))=G\).
2. \(M=E(M)U(M)\).
3. \(M\) is unit-regular.

Under these conditions, \(q|_{U(M)}\) is a group isomorphism. Write its inverse as
\[
g\longmapsto u_g.
\]
Then
\[
\alpha_g(e)=u_geu_g^{-1}
\tag{7.2}
\]
defines a \(G\)-action by automorphisms on the actual band \(E(M)\), and
\[
F:M\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}E(M)\rtimes_\alpha G,
\qquad
F(s)=\bigl(su_{q(s)}^{-1},q(s)\bigr)
\tag{7.3}
\]
is an isomorphism whose actor projection is exactly \(q\).

### Proof

First, \(q|_{U(M)}\) is injective. A unit in its kernel is idempotent by purity, and an idempotent unit is \(1_M\).

Assume condition 1. The restriction is then a group isomorphism, so
\[
u_gu_h=u_{gh},\qquad u_1=1_M.
\]
For \(s\in M\), put \(g=q(s)\). Then
\[
q(su_g^{-1})=1,
\]
so \(e_s=su_g^{-1}\) is idempotent. Therefore
\[
s=e_su_g,
\]
proving condition 2.

Condition 2 implies condition 1 because idempotents map to \(1\), while \(q(M)=G\).

If \(s=eu\) with \(e\) idempotent and \(u\) a unit, then
\[
su^{-1}s=eu\,u^{-1}eu=e^2u=s.
\]
Thus condition 2 implies condition 3.

Conversely, if \(svs=s\) with \(v\) a unit, then \(e=sv\) satisfies
\[
e^2=svsv=sv=e,
\]
and \(s=ev^{-1}\). This proves condition 3 implies condition 2.

Conjugation by \(u_g\) preserves multiplication and idempotence and has inverse conjugation by \(u_g^{-1}\). Formula (7.2) is therefore an action on \(E(M)\) by band automorphisms.

For \(q(s)=g,q(t)=h\), write \(s=e_su_g,t=e_tu_h\). Then
\[
st=e_su_ge_tu_h
   =e_s\alpha_g(e_t)u_{gh}.
\]
Consequently, at the physical host multiplication,
\[
F(s)F(t)
=\bigl(e_s\alpha_g(e_t),gh\bigr)
=F(st).
\]
The explicit inverse is
\[
(e,g)\longmapsto eu_g.
\]
Indeed, \(q(eu_g)=g\) and \((eu_g)u_g^{-1}=e\). Thus \(F\) is bijective and preserves the exact prescribed actor. ∎

### Scope

This is a whole-class result, not an example-dependent construction. The band is the source’s own \(E(M)\), which may be noncommutative and is not required to satisfy a further band identity.

The condition \(q(U(M))=G\) characterizes this full semidirect-product realization. Its failure is **not** a general obstruction to embedding \(M\) as a proper subsemigroup of a larger host.

## 8. A six-element noninverse source with genuinely nontrivial action

This is an explicit written example, not executable output.

Let
\[
C=\{\mathbf1,a,b\},
\]
where \(\mathbf1\) is an identity and \(\{a,b\}\) is a left-zero band:
\[
aa=ab=a,\qquad ba=bb=b.
\]
Let \(G=C_2=\{0,1\}\) act by fixing \(\mathbf1\) and interchanging \(a,b\).

The interchange preserves the band multiplication, so it is an automorphism of order two. Thus \(C\rtimes C_2\) is associative.

Name its six elements
\[
I=(\mathbf1,0),\quad t=(\mathbf1,1),\quad
a=(a,0),\quad b=(b,0),\quad
x=(a,1),\quad y=(b,1).
\]
Its complete multiplication table is:

| \(\cdot\) | \(I\) | \(t\) | \(a\) | \(b\) | \(x\) | \(y\) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| \(I\) | \(I\) | \(t\) | \(a\) | \(b\) | \(x\) | \(y\) |
| \(t\) | \(t\) | \(I\) | \(y\) | \(x\) | \(b\) | \(a\) |
| \(a\) | \(a\) | \(x\) | \(a\) | \(a\) | \(x\) | \(x\) |
| \(b\) | \(b\) | \(y\) | \(b\) | \(b\) | \(y\) | \(y\) |
| \(x\) | \(x\) | \(a\) | \(x\) | \(x\) | \(a\) | \(a\) |
| \(y\) | \(y\) | \(b\) | \(y\) | \(y\) | \(b\) | \(b\) |

The prescribed quotient is
\[
q(I)=q(a)=q(b)=0,\qquad q(t)=q(x)=q(y)=1.
\]

- The idempotents are exactly \(I,a,b\), so \(q\) is pure and onto.
- Regularity follows from inverse choices \(I,t,a,b,y,x\), respectively. In particular, \(xyx=x\) and \(yxy=y\).
- The source is not inverse because \(ab=a\ne b=ba\).
- Inverses are genuinely nonunique: \(x^3=x\), so both \(x\) and \(y\) are inverses of \(x\).
- The group action is nontrivial:
  \[
  tat^{-1}=tat=b\ne a.
  \]

This monoid is not isomorphic to a direct product of any band and any group. In a monoid direct product \(B\times H\), the band \(B\) has an identity and its only unit is that identity. Hence every unit \((1_B,h)\) commutes with every idempotent \((e,1_H)\). The displayed conjugation contradicts that isomorphism-invariant property.

The example therefore goes beyond a trivial band-times-group illustration.

### Boundary control: unit surjectivity is not necessary for proper source embeddings

Take the same band \(C\), now with **trivial** \(C_2\)-action, and the submonoid
\[
N=\{(\mathbf1,0),(a,0),(b,0),(a,1),(b,1)\}
\subseteq C\times C_2.
\]
Projection to \(C_2\) is pure and onto. The submonoid is regular and noninverse, but its only unit is \((\mathbf1,0)\), since the other four elements form a proper ideal.

Thus \(q(U(N))\ne C_2\), although the displayed inclusion is already an embedding over \(q\). This is only a class-boundary control, not additional frontier progress.

## 9. The literal one-row Rees convention, including a nonidentity sandwich entry

Suppose the prescribed group actor is presented as
\[
D=M[G;\{i_0\},\{\lambda_0\};p]
\]
with arbitrary \(p\in G\). Define
\[
\rho(i_0,h,\lambda_0)=hp.
\]
This is the group isomorphism compatible with the physical product.

Apply the preceding constructions to
\[
\bar q=\rho q.
\]
If their band coordinate is \(c_s\), return
\[
F_D(s)=(c_s,q(s)),
\]
not a replacement actor value.

Writing \(q(s)=(i_0,h_s,\lambda_0)\), the consuming twist is
\[
h_sp=\bar q(s),
\]
so
\[
F_D(s)F_D(t)
=\bigl(c_s(\bar q(s)\cdot c_t),q(st)\bigr)
=F_D(st).
\]
No commutation of \(p\) with another group element is used. The actor remains exactly the prescribed \(q\).

No multirow/multicolumn conclusion is inferred from this normalization.
