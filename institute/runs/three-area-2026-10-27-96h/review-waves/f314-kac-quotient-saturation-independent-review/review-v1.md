\Phi_n:

## 4. Equivariance, actual operators and quotient descent — SUPPORTED

**Source:** S1 LF44–83; S2 LF7–17; S3 LF141–218,224–258.

The operator is the actual adjoint-action contraction

\[
A=\Omega_n=\mu_n\mathcal C_n,
\]

where

\[
\mathcal C_n(x)=
\sum_{i,j}(G^{-1})_{ij}u_i\otimes W_jx,
\qquad
\mu_n(v\otimes y)=[v,y].
\]

### Equivariance

The adopted pairing argument uses the universally quantified identity

\[
\operatorname{tr}([g,U]V^T)
+\operatorname{tr}\!\left(U[-g^T,V]^T\right)=0.
\]

Together with \(J(v)=-\theta(v)\) and

\[
[g,J(v)]=J([\theta(g),v]),
\]

this makes the pairing between \(V\) and its negative theta-image invariant. Its nondegeneracy makes

\[
t=\sum_{i,j}(G^{-1})_{ij}u_i\otimes w_j
\]

an invariant coevaluation tensor.

Applying the actual adjoint action to its second factor proves \(\mathcal C_n\) equivariant: the terms involving \([g,u_i]\) and \([g,w_j]\) cancel by invariance of \(t\), leaving \(\mathcal C_n([g,x])\). Jacobi separately gives equivariance of \(\mu_n\). Consequently \(A\) commutes with the finite Levi action.

This establishes lowest-space stability, rather than inferring it from horizontal-degree preservation.

### Descent

The imported whole ideal is stable under all original lowerings. Thus each lowering word satisfies

\[
W_j(I_n)\subseteq I_{n-1}.
\]

The positive ideal property gives

\[
[V,I_{n-1}]\subseteq I_n.

Hence

\[
\mathcal C_n(I_n)\subseteq V\otimes I_{n-1},
\qquad
\mu_n(V\otimes I_{n-1})\subseteq I_n.

Both maps therefore descend:

\[
\overline{\mathcal C}_n:S_n\to V\otimes S_{n-1},
\qquad
\overline\mu_n:V\otimes S_{n-1}\to S_n,

and

\[
\boxed{\overline A=\overline\mu_n\,\overline{\mathcal C}_n.}

## 5. Finite polynomial zero-primary projector — SUPPORTED

**Source:** S1 LF119–201.

Let \(E=E_0(A)\). For nonzero finite-dimensional \(L\), write

\[
\chi_A(t)=t^a h(t),\qquad h(0)\ne0.
\]

When \(a>0\), coprimality supplies a polynomial \(p\) with

\[
p\equiv1\pmod{t^a},
\qquad
p\equiv0\pmod h.
\]

Set \(e=p(A)\). The congruences and Cayley–Hamilton give \(e^2=e\), and

\[
\operatorname{im}e=\ker A^a=E,
\qquad
\operatorname{im}(1-e)=\ker h(A)=E_\times.
\]

Thus

\[
L=E\oplus E_\times.
\]

The restriction of \(A\) to \(E\) is nilpotent. Since \(t\) and \(h\) are coprime, a Bézout identity supplies an inverse polynomial for \(A\) on \(E_\times\). This proves invertibility there without diagonalization.

If \(a=0\), take \(e=0\); the entire space is the invertible part. The zero-dimensional case is interpreted using the unique zero-space maps. These are structural boundary cases, not selected examples.

Since \(I\) is \(A\)-stable, polynomials in \(A\) preserve \(I\). Consequently,

\[
e(I)\subseteq I\cap E.
\]

Conversely, \(e\) is the identity on \(E\), so every element of \(I\cap E\) belongs to \(e(I)\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{e(I)=I\cap E.}
\]

The same projectors split \(I\), giving

\[
S\cong
E/(I\cap E)\ \oplus\
E_\times/(I\cap E_\times).
\]

The first summand has nilpotent induced action. On the second, \(A\) remains invertible: its restriction to the finite-dimensional invariant subspace \(I\cap E_\times\) is injective and hence surjective, so its inverse also preserves that subspace.

It follows canonically that

\[
\boxed{
E_0(\overline A)
\cong E/(I\cap E)
=E/\operatorname{im}(e\Phi_n).
}
\]

This identifies the induced nilpotent operator as well as the vector-space obstruction. No zero-semisimplicity or absence of Jordan nilpotence is assumed.

The projector is defined pointwise at each allowed parameter/realization. No single parameter-polynomial projector across spectral strata is established or needed.

## 6. All six criteria and the first exact sequence — SUPPORTED

**Source:** S1 LF133–226.

Put

\[
P=\ker(F_1,F_2\mid L),\qquad
N=I\cap P,\qquad
Q=P/N,\qquad T=A|_P.
\]

Complete reducibility supplies a Levi-stable complement of \(I\) in \(L\), and therefore the canonical quotient map induces

\[

\]

The action induced by \(T\) is precisely the restriction of \(\overline A\) to this quotient-lowest space.

The candidate’s six conditions are equivalent:

1. \(\overline T\) is injective on \(Q\).
2. \(\overline A\) is an automorphism of \(S\).
3. \(E\subseteq I\).
4. \(\operatorname{im}(e\Phi_n)=E\).
5. \(A^{-1}(I)=I\), with \(A^{-1}\) meaning preimage.
6. \(\ker T\subseteq N\) and \(N\cap T(P)=T(N)\).

Here are the load-bearing implications.

- **1 ⇔ 2:** \(\ker\overline A\) is a finite Levi submodule. If nonzero, lowest-vector theory supplies a nonzero vector in it killed by \(F_1,F_2\), contradicting condition 1. The reverse implication is restriction. Finite-dimensional injectivity is equivalent to automorphism.
- **2 ⇔ 3:** the decomposition in Section 5 identifies the complete zero-primary quotient with \(E/(I\cap E)\); it vanishes exactly when \(E\subseteq I\).
- **3 ⇔ 4:** use \(\operatorname{im}\Phi_n=I\) and \(e(I)=I\cap E\).
- **2 ⇔ 5:** the kernel of the induced quotient map is \(A^{-1}(I)/I\).
- **1 ⇔ 6:** use the following exact sequence.

There is a canonical sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow
\frac{\ker T}{\ker T\cap N}
\longrightarrow
\ker\overline T
\xrightarrow{\delta}
\frac{N\cap T(P)}{T(N)}
\longrightarrow0,
\]

where

\[
\delta(x+N)=Tx+T(N).
\]

It is well-defined because \(T(N)\subseteq N\). Every element of \(N\cap T(P)\) is \(Tx\) for some \(x\in P\), and then \(x+N\in\ker\overline T\), proving surjectivity. Finally,

\[
\delta(x+N)=0
\]

exactly when \(Tx=Ty\) for some \(y\in N\), equivalently when \(x+N\) is represented by \(x-y\in\ker T\). This proves exactness and condition 6.

In particular, merely placing the free kernel in \(N\) eliminates only the left term. It does not eliminate the saturation term on the right. Generalized zero-primary containment is the correct criterion when nilpotent chains have not been excluded.
