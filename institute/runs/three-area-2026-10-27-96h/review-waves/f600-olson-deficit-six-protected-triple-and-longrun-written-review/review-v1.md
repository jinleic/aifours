## 3. Protected-triple selector and actual product routing

**Status: SUPPORTED — S1 LF 59–175.**

### 3.1 Local products, including prefix and suffix rotations

With

\[
\rho_u=(u,0),\qquad \sigma_a=(a,1),\qquad
(x,e)(y,b)=(x+(-1)^e y,e+b),
\]

two chronological reflections have product \(\rho_{a-b}\).

For a chronological triple \((a,b,c)\), selecting two reflections gives

\[
A_0=\{a-b,a-c,b-c\}.
\]

If one selected rotation lies in the first interior gap, the three actual selected subwords give

\[
A_u^{(1)}
=\{a-u-b,\ a-u-c,\ u+b-c\}.
\]

The third expression includes the case in which the rotation becomes a **prefix** after the first reflection is omitted.

For a rotation in the second interior gap,

\[
A_u^{(2)}
=\{a-b+u,\ a-u-c,\ b-u-c\}.
\]

The first expression includes the case in which the rotation becomes a **suffix** after the third reflection is omitted. Thus no uniform interior sign is assumed.

The first gap is safe when \(b\ne c\), since its first two expressions differ. The second is safe when \(a\ne b\), since its last two expressions differ. Consequently:

- A nonconstant triple with unequal adjacent labels has two safe gaps.
- A nonconstant triple with one equal adjacent pair has one safe and one potentially unsafe gap.
- A constant triple always has a nonempty set of attainable exponents.

The potentially unsafe gap need not be the equal-label gap. The author's actual formulas, rather than an identification of those two gaps, are used correctly.

### 3.2 Availability, including constant and protected blocks

Omitting one reflection \(z\) and cutting after it leaves \(3p-3\) reflections partitioned into \(p-1\) disjoint chronological triples.

Let \(B\) count potentially unsafe interior gaps of **nonconstant** triples, and protect \(h\) triples by selecting no rotation in their interiors. Excluding these positions removes at most

\[
B+2h
\]

rotation occurrences. This is an upper bound even if exclusions overlap.

Every interior gap counted here is an original singleton rotation gap. The two gaps originally adjacent to \(z\) remain separate positions at the two ends of the cut word; omission does not merge them into an interior gap or erase their rotations.

If at least three eligible rotations remain, two can be selected with at most one interior to each triple:

- An eligible boundary rotation can be paired with any other eligible occurrence.
- If none is a boundary rotation, three eligible occurrences cannot all be interior to one triple, because that triple has only two original interior gaps.

This remains valid when eligible rotations lie inside constant triples. Such triples were deliberately omitted from \(B\): they need only retain a nonempty exponent set, not a two-element set.

Protection of an AP block costs two interior gaps just as protection of a rich block does. The later uses of \(h=2\) account for that cost.

### 3.3 Consuming-side dispatch into the actual groupword

After fixing the two rotations, each triple contributes a selected subword containing exactly two reflections and either zero or one selected interior rotation. It therefore has even reflection parity and is rotation-valued.

Boundary rotations consequently contribute with positive sign between these rotation-valued blocks. Writing their fixed total as \(c\), the cut product is

\[
\rho_{c+\sum_i d_i},\qquad d_i\in A_i.
\]

This is the required consuming-side calculation: selected interior rotations are routed through their signed local formulas, while selected boundary rotations are routed through the fixed offset. Neither class is dropped.

If the block sumset is the whole field, choosing \(\sum_i d_i=-c\) produces identity. The choices are independent because the reflection triples are disjoint and the selected rotations have already been fixed. The number of actual selected occurrences is

\[
2(p-1)+2=2p.
\]

Grouping exponent sets later in the proof is legitimate addition in the rotation subgroup **after** the chronological products have been evaluated. It does not reorder actual reflections.

Finally, returning the same occurrences to the original linear cut cyclically rotates their groupword. For \(UV=1\),

\[
VU=U^{-1}(UV)U=1.
\]

Thus the construction proves identity in the original occurrence order, not merely in an auxiliary cyclic ordering.

## 4. Sumset and exceptional-growth lemmas

### 4.1 Finite-field sumset bound — SUPPORTED

**S1 LF 181–218.**

The supplied Lagrange-coefficient argument establishes

\[
|A+B|\ge\min(p,|A|+|B|-1)
\]

for nonempty subsets of \(\mathbb F_p\). When \(|A|+|B|-1\le p\), the relevant polynomial degree is less than \(p\), so its displayed binomial coefficient is nonzero. The successive Lagrange coefficient extraction applies at that total degree. The larger-cardinality case follows by taking nonempty subsets whose cardinalities sum to \(p+1\).

Iteration gives

\[
\left|\sum_i A_i\right|
\ge
\min\left(p,1+\sum_i(|A_i|-1)\right).
\]

Accordingly, among \(p-1\) blocks:

- All nonconstant sets of size at least two suffice.
- One protected rich set compensates one constant block.
- Two protected rich sets compensate two constant blocks.

These conclusions require only nonemptiness for the constant-block sets, as the selector provides.

### 4.2 Two AP directions — SUPPORTED

**S1 LF 220–233.**

For

\[
D_d=\{-d,-2d\},
\]

the four sums in \(D_d+D_e\) are distinct when \(d,e\ne0\) and \(d\ne\pm e\). A collision would force one of \(d=0\), \(e=0\), \(d=e\), or \(d=-e\). Therefore

\[
|D_d+D_e|=4.
\]

### 4.3 Rich-plus-AP growth — SUPPORTED

**S1 LF 235–259.**

For

\[
T_{d,e}=\{-d,-e,-d-e\},\qquad d,e\ne0,\ d\ne e,
\]

the cardinality of \(T_{d,e}+D_d\) equals that of

\[
\{d,e,d+e\}+\{0,d\}.
\]

The three-element set \(A=\{d,e,d+e\}\) is proper in the cycle generated by \(d\). Each cyclic component contributes one new point to \(A\cup(A+d)\). Thus cardinality below five is equivalent to \(A\) having one component: a three-element interval in that cyclic order.

The pair \(e,e+d\) is already consecutive. The third element \(d\) must therefore extend that pair at one end:

\[
d=e-d
\quad\text{or}\quad
d=e+2d.
\]

These give respectively \(e=2d\) and \(e=-d\). Conversely, each relation gives a three-element interval. The prime restriction \(p\ge7\) excludes the small-cycle identifications in this argument. Hence

\[
|T_{d,e}+D_d|<5
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
e\in\{2d,-d\}.
\]

Interchanging the two directions gives the stated counterpart for \(D_e\). No exceptional prime in the theorem's domain is lost.

\[

## 7. Single seven-run

**Status: SUPPORTED — S1 LF 362–458.**

Here

\[
N=3p-9.

- \(P_3\): one constant block, one possible nonconstant unsafe gap, whole-\(X\) starts \(0\bmod3\), and endpoint triple \((a,x_1,x_2)\).

A rich block in \(P_2\) or \(P_3\) compensates its one constant block. Two rich blocks in \(P_4\) compensate its two constant blocks. The respective protection costs leave at least five and four eligible rotations.

\[
(3p-8)d=-8d=0,
\]

impossible for an odd prime \(p\ge7\).

### The remaining one-rich-block case

There is exactly one rich \(X\)-block in \(P_4\). With \(r\) preceding and \(s\) following \(X\)-blocks,

\[
r+s=p-4.
\]

Every consecutive triple except that rich block is nonrich. Therefore equality of consecutive increments propagates up to its middle vertex from the left and from there to the right. The two nonzero increments are \(d,e\), with \(d\ne e\), and the segment lengths are exactly

\[
L=3r+2,\qquad M=3s+2.
\]

The endpoint condition is

\[
Ld+Me=0.
\]

If \(e=-d\), this gives \(3(r-s)d=0\). Since \(p\ge7\) and \(|r-s|<p\), it forces \(r=s\), contradicting the odd integer \(r+s=p-4\). Thus \(d\ne\pm e\).

If \(r,s>0\), both AP directions are represented by blocks. Failure of both rich-plus-AP pairs to reach size five would require

\[
e/d\in\{2,-1\},
\qquad
d/e\in\{2,-1\}.
\]

Apart from the excluded possibility \(e=-d\), the simultaneous relations would force characteristic three. Therefore at least one pair has size at least five.

If \(r=0\), the endpoint condition gives

\[
2d+(3p-10)e=0,\qquad d=5e.
\]

There is an available \(e\)-direction AP block because \(s=p-4>0\). Its pair-growth failure would require \(d/e=2\) or \(-1\), neither compatible with \(d/e=5\) in any prime characteristic \(p\ge7\). The case \(s=0\) is symmetric.

Protect the rich block and such an AP block. Since \(B=0\),

\[
R-4\ge p-3\ge4.
\]

The protected pair has sumset size at least five. There are \(p-5\) other nonconstant blocks and two constant blocks, giving

\[
1+4+(p-5)=p.
\]

The resulting full-field sumset supplies the actual two-rotation selector. This covers every remaining seven-run case, including both endpoint cases \(r=0\) and \(s=0\).

### No-rich case

The three phases supply all consecutive unequal-adjacency triples of both chains

\[
a_5,\ X,\ b_1
\quad\text{and}\quad
b_3,\ Z,\ a_1,

\[

2. the constant triple \((a_2,a_3,a_4)\);

\[
1+3+(p-4)=p.

The actual selector therefore yields exactly \(2p\) original occurrences with identity product. This completes the \(5+3\) type without assuming the two long-run labels are different.
