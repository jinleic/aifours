## 2. A self-contained four-qubit obstruction

There is no normalized pure four-qubit state whose three balanced flattenings, multiplied by 2, are unitary. Indeed, suppose there were one. Its two-qubit marginals are `I_4/4`, and its one-qubit marginals are `I_2/2`. Trace out one qubit and expand the remaining three-qubit density operator in Pauli products:

`rho = (I + M)/8`,

where `M` is a real linear combination of Pauli strings of weight exactly 3. There are no weight-1 or weight-2 terms because the corresponding marginals are maximally mixed. The omitted qubit has eigenvalues `1/2,1/2`, so the Schmidt spectrum gives `rho^2=rho/2`. Consequently

`M^2 = 3 I + 2 M`.

The weight-3 part of `M^2` is zero. For two weight-3 Pauli strings, a weight-3 product requires different nonidentity Pauli matrices at all three sites; then the strings anticommute, so their contribution to the anticommutator vanishes. Equal strings give the identity, and all other products have lower weight. Taking weight-3 components in the displayed identity gives `M=0`, contradicting the same identity. This also handles arbitrary complex amplitudes.

We will repeatedly use the following *isolated-block criterion*. If four two-symbol sets support a subarray whose four pair values on every physical pair are occupied by its residual entries, and every residual pair value on those sets is completely exhausted within the subarray, then all three 4-by-4 flattenings of that same subarray are unitary blocks of the corresponding flattenings of `U`. Maximal entries cannot occur in these rows or columns because the residual entries already occupy their pair values. Dividing the subarray by 2 yields the impossible four-qubit state just proved.

## 3. Marginals, alphabet sizes, and local slices

For a symbol `x` of party `i`, write `s_i(x)` for the number of E words with that symbol. For any partner party `j`, write `d_{ij}(x)` for the number of partner symbols appearing with it in E. The number of P words with `i=x` is `6-d_{ij}(x)`, independent of `j`; hence all three `d_{ij}(x)` have the same value, denoted `d_i(x)`. Put

`h_i(x)=s_i(x)-2 d_i(x)`.

Equivalently, for any partner j, this is the sum over occupied pairs `(x,y)` of their multiplicity minus 2. It is nonnegative and independent of j, and

`sum_x d_i(x)=8`, `sum_x h_i(x)=20-2*8=4`.                                      (3.1)

Let `q_i` be the number of symbols with `s_i(x)>0`. No such symbol has `d_i=1`: each of the other three coordinates would then be fixed, leaving at most one E word, whereas an occupied pair has at least two extensions. Thus `d_i>=2` on the residual alphabet and `q_i<=4`.

If some `q_i=2`, the eight occupied pair values force every other `q_j=4` and each `(i,j)` residual pair set is the complete 2-by-4 rectangle. Therefore P words whose i-symbol lies in the two-symbol residual alphabet have their other three coordinates in the two-symbol *complements* of the other residual alphabets. There are exactly four such P words, two at each i-symbol. They form a binary length-4 code of size 4 and minimum distance at least 3, since P is injective on every pair. This is impossible: the sum of six pairwise Hamming distances would be at least 18, while each binary coordinate contributes at most 4, for a total at most 16. Thus `q_i` is 3 or 4.

For `w in P`, let `k(w)` count coordinates lying in the corresponding E alphabets. Counting P pair values inside residual alphabet boxes gives

`C:=sum_P k(w) = sum_i (6 q_i-8)`,
`M:=sum_P binom(k(w),2) = sum_{i<j}(q_i q_j-8)`.                              (3.2)

Since `binom(k,2)>=k-1` for 0<=k<=4, `M>=C-28`. Four q=3 parties give `(C,M)=(40,6)`, and three q=3 parties give `(46,15)`; both violate this inequality. Thus, up to permutation,

`(q_i) = (4,4,4,4), (3,4,4,4), or (3,3,4,4)`.                              (3.3)

In particular at least two parties have q=4. At a q=4 party all four residual symbols have d=2. If two parties have q=3, their residual pair set has eight cells in a 3-by-3 box, so it is the complete box with one missing cell. The unique degree-2 symbol at either end of that missing cell is called its **star**. The missing pair is `(star_A,star_B)`.

We need three properties of a general residual symbol with `d=2`. It sees exactly two symbols in each of the other three parties, so its E slice is a subset of a 2-by-2-by-2 cube of size `4+h`. First, `h` cannot equal 1. With five vertices, each coordinate has 2 and 3 vertices on its two faces. The corresponding two unitary lines have at least one common column and cannot have exactly one, so each coordinate direction supplies at least two induced cube edges. But a five-vertex induced subgraph of the cube has at most five edges: deleting its three complementary vertices removes at least `9-2=7` of the cube's 12 edges. Second, `h` cannot equal 3: a seven-vertex cube slice has a 3/4 split in every direction, but a residual pair system is D or Q and cannot contain both a 3 and a 4. Thus `h` is in `{0,2,4}`.

## 4. Excluding a d=2, h=4 symbol

Suppose party i has a symbol a with d=2 and h=4. Its E slice is the full binary cube on the other parties. Pick a *different* q=4 party j, possible by (3.3), and call the remaining parties k,l. Each pair system `i` with one of j,k,l is Q: its two pair values at i=a each have multiplicity 4. By the imported mixed-component statement, for each pair among j,k,l every one of the four binary values has multiplicity 3. The full a-slice already supplies two of those three extensions.

For either binary symbol of j, the pair `(i=a,j)` is one of the two 4s. Hence its excess at j is 2. Since q_j=4 gives d_j=2, that j-symbol appears in `2*2+2=6` E words, four with i=a and exactly two outside that slice. The multiplicity-3 values of `(j,k)` and `(j,l)` force these two outside words to use both binary k symbols and both binary l symbols. Across the two j values there are exactly four outside words. Their `(k,l)` pairs are distinct because each binary `(k,l)` value has only one extension outside i=a. Consequently the four words form the parity (or opposite-parity) 4-word code on the binary j,k,l coordinates: each pair of these three coordinates appears once.

For each fixed j-symbol, its two outside words have the same i-symbol `b_j`, because its d_j=2 partner symbols in party i consist of a and only one other. If `b_0 != b_1`, fix either k-symbol. The residual row `(i=b_0,k)` overlaps the row `(i=a,k)` in exactly one `(j,l)` column: the latter has all four binary `(j,l)` columns, and among the four outside words only one at that k has i=b_0. This is forbidden. Thus `b_0=b_1=b`.

The full a-cube and the four parity words at i=b form a 2-by-2-by-2-by-2 subarray. In each `i`-containing pair system the a-values have multiplicity 4 and the b-values multiplicity 2; in each complementary pair system the four binary values have multiplicity 3. These are all their actual extensions, so the isolated-block criterion contradicts Section 2. Therefore no d=2 symbol has h=4. In particular every q=4 party has exactly two symbols with h=2 and two with h=0; call these its **high** and **low** symbols.

For a high symbol of a q=4 party, its six E words occupy six vertices of its binary cube, missing two distinct vertices. In a D pair system the two face multiplicities are 3 and 3, so the missing vertices differ in that partner coordinate; in a Q system they are 4 and 2, so the missing vertices agree there. Hence a q=4 party cannot be incident with three Q systems: the two missing vertices would agree in all three coordinates. If it has exactly one D neighbor, its high-symbol slice is a Cartesian product of the two values at that neighbor and three corners of a 2-by-2 box in the other two coordinates.                                               (4.1)

## 5. High-set tools

For two q=4 parties joined by D, the residual pair support is exactly `H x H` together with `L x L`: all four high-high cells have multiplicity 3 and all four low-low cells have multiplicity 2. Indeed a high symbol has d=2 and h=2, so its two incident D cells are both 3s; their opposite endpoints must be high and exhaust the two high symbols on that side. The low part then follows from degree 2. Thus every E word has the same high/low bit at the two parties. A Q pair between q=4 parties has two 4-cells forming a perfect matching of their two high-symbol sets.

We also use this sector lemma. Suppose each of the four parties has two specified h=2 symbols, and there are exactly 12 E words on their 2-by-2-by-2-by-2 box, with each specified symbol appearing in six of those words. For every physical pair all four box values occur, since six words at a specified symbol cannot fit on only one pair value of multiplicity at most 4. In a D system the four cells are 3s: each specified endpoint has h=2 and hence its two 3-neighbors are the two specified symbols on the other side. In a Q system the two 4-cells match the specified endpoints and the remaining two occupied cells are 2s. In either case the total multiplicity of the four box cells is exactly 12. Thus the 12 words exhaust all their pair values. The isolated-block criterion gives a contradiction.                                           (5.1)

### Three synchronized q=4 parties and a q=3 party

Assume the high/low bits of three q=4 parties agree on every E word. Their common high sector has 12 words and their common low sector 8. Let A be a q=3 fourth party. If A has a D edge to one of the three, then for each A-symbol x, `h_A(x)<=2` and precisely `3 h_A(x)` high-sector words use x. If A also has a Q edge, all its h values are even. If instead all three A edges are D, a symbol with h=1 would contribute three high-sector words but have only one high neighbor in each of the three binary parties; all three coordinates would be fixed, allowing only one word. Thus in either case all h_A values are 0 or 2. Exactly two symbols have h=2, and their six high-sector words each give the forbidden sector (5.1).

It remains in this lemma to consider all three A edges Q. Their h_A values are even, so their sum 4 is distributed either as two 2s or one 4. A symbol with h=2 has one incident 4-cell at each high q=4 alphabet and hence has either 4 or 6 words in the high sector. Four words would have all three high coordinates fixed, impossible. Six words use a binary cube with 4/2 face counts in all three directions, so its two missing vertices agree in all coordinates, impossible. Hence there is one h=4 symbol a. Its two 4-cells to each q=4 party give the full 8-word binary cube at A=a. All three pairs between q=4 parties are D, so each high-high pair value has three extensions: two at a and exactly one among the remaining four high-sector words. These four form the parity code on the three binary alphabets. At any fixed high q=4 symbol the two remaining words have the same A-symbol, since that symbol has d=2, one A-neighbor being a. The parity-code incidence graph is connected, so all four have the same A-symbol b. The full a-cube plus the b-parity slice exhaust the 4/2 and 3 pair values in every cut, giving the forbidden isolated block. Therefore **three synchronized q=4 parties are impossible when the fourth has q=3**.      (5.2)

## 6. Exclusion of (4,4,4,4)

Consider the graph of D edges on the four parties. It is connected. If a disconnected component has two vertices, all four crossing edges would be Q, including disjoint complementary Q edges. If a component is a singleton, that vertex has three Q edges, contrary to (4.1). Thus D edges synchronize all four high/low bits. The high sector has 12 words, with six at each high symbol, contradicting (5.1).

## 7. Exclusion of (3,4,4,4)

Let A be the q=3 party and let the other three be q=4. If their induced D graph is connected, (5.2) applies. If it has exactly one D edge, let C be the remaining q=4 party. C has Q edges to the endpoints of that D edge and therefore its edge to A is D, by (4.1). For each high symbol of C, (4.1) says its slice is a product of two A values and three corners in the other two q=4 alphabets. Those two parties have synchronized high/low bits. A three-edge `K_2,2` minus one corner is connected, so all four of its endpoint symbols have the same bit; the bit must be high because each Q high-4 cell joins high endpoints. Thus every word with C high has both other q=4 symbols high. Each high set has 12 words, so all three high/low bits synchronize and (5.2) applies.

It remains to exclude a Q triangle on the three q=4 parties. Their three edges to A are D, since complementary Q systems are forbidden. For each Q edge, its two 4-cells match the two high symbols. Label the high symbols at the three parties C,D,E so that the C-D and C-E matchings preserve labels. The D-E matching either preserves labels or swaps them.

*Swapped matching.* For a high C-symbol c, let d and e be its matched high D- and E-symbols. Its three-corner slice contains `(c,d,e)`. Since d and e are not matched to one another, e is d's secondary E-neighbor and d is e's secondary D-neighbor. The primary corners at d and e then force c's secondary E- and D-neighbors to be the other high symbols. This holds for each high c, so C-D and C-E supports contain all high-high pairs. Degree 2 forces the complementary low-low pairs and synchronizes the three high/low bits. Apply (5.2).

*Preserving matching.* A triple of high symbols with nonidentical binary labels cannot occur: at the minority-label party, both other coordinates are secondary, and the secondary-secondary corner is the omitted corner in (4.1). Consequently the secondary neighbor of a high symbol at either other q=4 party must be low. For each label h, the primary high-high-high triple and the three triples with exactly one low symbol form four distinct q=4 triples. The 4-cell at each high-high pair forces the two high parties to share the same secondary low neighbor at the third party. Their high slices are products with two A-symbols; because the primary triple is common to all three high slices, these two A-symbols are the same set `A_h` throughout all four triples. Every one of the four triples has exactly those two A-extensions. The two labels therefore account for 16 E words and exhaust every word with any q=4 high symbol.

If `A_0 != A_1`, their two 2-subsets of the 3-symbol A alphabet intersect in one symbol. Its h-value is 2, and the other two A-symbols have h=1. A has a degree-2 residual symbol, which cannot have h=1, so it would have to be the intersection symbol. But that symbol occurs with both high symbols and at least one low symbol at every q=4 party, giving degree at least 3, contradiction.

If `A_0=A_1`, both of its symbols have degree at least 3, and (3.1) forces A's degrees to be 3,3,2. At each q=4 party the low neighbors associated with labels 0 and 1 must therefore coincide: either of the two A-symbols already sees both high symbols and has only one further neighbor. For any Q edge between two q=4 parties, both high symbols on either end now connect to the same low symbol on the other end. Those two low symbols have degree 2 and are saturated by high neighbors; the remaining low symbols have no high neighbors and can meet only each other, so their degree is at most 1. Contradiction. This completes (3,4,4,4).

## 8. The (3,3,4,4) case when the q=4 pair is D

Call the q=3 parties A,B and the q=4 parties C,D. Suppose `CD` is D. The C,D high bits synchronize; write `E_H` for the 12 high-high words. If a q=3 party X has at least one D edge to C or D, then every symbol x has `h_X(x)<=2`, and exactly `3 h_X(x)` words of E_H use x. If both X-C and X-D are D and `h_X(x)=1`, those three words have fixed C,D symbols and therefore use all three symbols of the other q=3 party, once each.

First suppose each of A,B has at least one D edge to C,D. If either also has a Q edge, its h-values are even; it has exactly two h=2 symbols and all E_H uses them. An h=1 symbol at the other party, if its two edges are D, would require all three symbols of the first party in E_H, impossible. If no cross edge is Q, suppose A has an h=1 symbol. Its three high words use all B symbols, including B's star. The star has d=2 and h=0 or 2. In the first case it has no high words. In the second, its two D-neighbors at both C,D are high, so all its E words are high; its pair with the chosen A-symbol occurs only once, contradicting the minimum residual pair multiplicity 2. Thus both parties have h-values 0 or 2. Each has two h=2 symbols, with six high words per symbol, and (5.1) applies.

The remaining cross-edge possibility, up to A/B interchange, is that both A-C and A-D are Q. Complementarity forces B-C and B-D to be D. The h_A values are even. If they are `(2,2,0)`, a high A-symbol contributes either 4 or 6 words to E_H: a 4-cell at a high C-symbol, and possibly one additional 2-cell at the other high C-symbol. Four is impossible, because its high 4-cells at C and D would fix both C,D while only three B-symbols are available. Therefore the two high A-symbols contribute all 12 high words; an h_B=1 symbol would again need all three A-symbols, so h_B is also 0 or 2 and (5.1) applies.

If h_A has a single 4 at a, the two high C-pair values at a are 4-cells, so exactly eight E_H words have A=a. Since B's two cross edges are D, its h-values are at most 2. If any h_B is 1, the distribution is `(2,1,1)`: each of the two h=1 symbols has three E_H words at fixed C,D and hence one at every A-symbol. They supply only two of the eight words at A=a; the remaining six share `(A=a,B=b)` for the h=2 symbol b, impossible because a pair multiplicity is at most 4. Thus B has two h=2 symbols. All eight words at A=a fill the binary cube on B-high,C-high,D-high. The resulting 12-word binary subarray exhausts all its 4/2 and 3 pair values and violates the isolated-block criterion. Thus `CD=D` is impossible.

## 9. The (3,3,4,4) case when the q=4 pair is Q

Now `CD=Q`, so `AB=D`. Let `a_*`,`b_*` be the q=3 stars. For this alphabet profile (3.2) gives `C=52`, `M=25`. If `n_k` counts P words with k coordinates in residual alphabets, then

`n_0+n_3+3 n_4 = M-C+28 = 1`.                                               (9.1)

With no k=4 word, some one of these four P words must have k=3. The same count at b_* shows that the unique k=3 word contains both stars. Rename C,D if necessary so this P word has a residual-alphabet C-symbol `c_*` and a nonresidual D-symbol. Let `S_A,S_B` be the two C-neighbor sets of a_*,b_* in E. Neither contains c_*, since that P word occupies both corresponding pair values. Each set has size 2 inside a four-symbol C alphabet, so they intersect.

We need a local observation, equally valid with C and D interchanged. A *high* symbol of a q=4 party cannot be adjacent to both stars while `CD=Q`. Indeed, its six E words use exactly two A symbols and two B symbols, one of each being the corresponding star. Since the star-star pair is missing, only three A-B pairs are allowed, and there are two choices in the fourth coordinate. All six possible words must occur. Its star-A and star-B pair values each then have multiplicity 2; at the high q=4 symbol the other pair value in each system has multiplicity 4. Both edges to A and B would be Q, giving this q=4 party three Q edges including CD, contrary to (4.1).                        (9.2)

Choose `c_0 in S_A intersect S_B`. It is low. Its four words include at least two with a_* and at least two with b_*; the sets are disjoint because `(a_*,b_*)` is missing. Its two A neighbors are `a_*` and some a, and its two B neighbors are `b_*` and some b. Thus its four words are exactly the two pairs `(a_*,b)` and `(a,b_*)`, each with both values in a two-symbol D set T. Both stars have d=2, so T is precisely each star's entire D-neighbor set. By (9.2), both members of T are low D symbols. At a low D symbol every incident pair has multiplicity 2, so each star has exactly four E words and h=0.

Let `c_A` and `c_B` be the stars' respective C neighbors other than c_0. Since `AB=D` and both stars have h=0, each occupied AB pair at either star has multiplicity 2. The two words at c_0 already saturate one such pair, so the two a_*-words at c_A use its other B neighbor and both D values in T. Similarly the two b_*-words at c_B use its other A neighbor and both D values in T. If `c_A != c_B`, each member of T has three distinct C neighbors `c_0,c_A,c_B`, contradicting d=2 at the q=4 party D. Hence `c_A=c_B=c_1`, which is low by (9.2).

The four cells `{c_0,c_1} x T` are all low-low CD cells, each already saturated by the two star words at that pair. They occupy both low C and both low D symbols and have multiplicity 2. Since every q=4 symbol has pair degree 2, the remaining CD support is the complete high-high 2-by-2 box. Thus the C,D high bits synchronize and the eight low words are exactly the star words. Because `AB=D` and both stars have h=0, all four multiplicity-3 AB values are the two nonstar A symbols crossed with the two nonstar B symbols. Their 12 E words all lie in the high-high CD sector. Every nonstar A and B symbol has h=2 and contributes six words there; the four q=4 high symbols also contribute six each. This is exactly the forbidden sector (5.1). Thus `CD=Q` is impossible as well.
