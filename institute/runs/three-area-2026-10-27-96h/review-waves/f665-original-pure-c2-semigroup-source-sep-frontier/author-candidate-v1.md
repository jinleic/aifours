\[

## Two identities already holding in the actual source \(S_0\)

First,
\[
aa'b=ab'b. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, both have grade one and, respectively, band coordinates
\[
 a_0\,\tau(a_1)\,b_0=a_0a_0b_0=a_0b_0,
 \qquad
 a_0\,\tau(b_1)\,b_0=a_0b_0b_0=a_0b_0.
\]
Thus (1) is equality of actual elements of \(S_0\), before quotienting.

Second,
\[
(eaf)a'(eaf)=eaf. \tag{2}
\]
For a completely explicit band calculation, every band satisfies
\[
xyzyxyz=xyzyx(yzyz)=(xyzy)^2z=(xyzy)z=x(yzyz)=xyz. \tag{3}
\]
The first and last equalities use \((yz)^2=yz\), and the middle equality uses \((xyzy)^2=xyzy\). Put \(x=e_0\), \(y=a_0\), \(z=f_1\), and \(w=xyz\). The element \(eaf\) is \((w,1)\); hence
\[
(eaf)a'(eaf)
 =(w\,\tau(a_1)\,w,1)=(w a_0 w,1)=(w,1)=eaf
\]
by (3). Both sides of (2) are literal words in the seven generators and have the same grade. The band calculation is solely a verification of their equality in the already-defined ambient semidirect product; it does not apply a defining source relation in the ambient band.

## Source-composable derivation of \(V=U\)

Write the literal source word
\[
W=fa'eafg\in S_0.
\]
R4 is precisely \(b'bW=W\). All equalities below are equalities in \(S\); every intermediate expression is a word in the source generators:
\[
\begin{aligned}
V
 &=ebW\\
 &=eaa'bW &&\text{by R1, in the right context }bW,\\
 &=ea b'bW &&\text{by the prequotient identity (1),}\\
 &=eaW &&\text{by R4, in the left context }ea,\\
 &=eafa'eafg\\
 &=(eaf)a'(eaf)g\\
 &=(eaf)g &&\text{by the prequotient identity (2),}\\
 &=eafg=U.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves equality in the actual least semigroup quotient. No inference from a nonseparating finite image is involved. R2 and R3 need not be imposed for this derivation.

## Stronger uniform formulation

The same derivation holds for **every** band \(B\) with an involutive automorphism \(\tau\), not just the free band. In \(B\rtimes C_2\), take \(a=(A,1),a'=(\tau A,1),b=(B_0,1),b'=(\tau B_0,1),e=(E,0),f=(F,0),g=(G,0)\). Form the actual subsemigroup generated by these seven elements and any semigroup quotient in which R1 and R4 hold. The two prequotient identities still hold: (1) has common coordinate \(AB_0\); (2) follows from (3) with \(x=E,y=A,z=\tau F\). Hence \(V=U\) in that quotient. This is a theorem about source-first semigroup quotients, not a replacement of the original source.
