## 3. Exact quotient-contraction theorem

Let
\[
E=E_0(A)
\]
be the **complete generalized zero-primary space** of \(A\). Equivalently,
\[
E=\ker A^m
\quad\text{for any integer }m\ge\max(1,\dim L).
\]

Let \(e=e_0(A)\) be its polynomial spectral projector. This is defined intrinsically at the fixed parameters; no uniform polynomial dependence on \(r,s\) is asserted.

### Theorem

The following six conditions are equivalent:

1. The contraction induced by \(T\) on \(Q=P/N\) is injective.
2. \(\overline A\) is an automorphism of the **entire quotient level** \(S_n\).
3. \(E\subseteq I\).
4. \(\operatorname{im}(e\Phi_n)=E\).
5. \(A^{-1}(I)=I\), where \(A^{-1}(I)\) denotes a **preimage**, not an assumed inverse operator.
6. Both
   \[
   \ker T\subseteq N
   \quad\text{and}\quad
   N\cap T(P)=T(N)
   \]
   hold.

Moreover,
\[
E_0(\overline A)
\cong
\mathcal O_n:=
E/\operatorname{im}(e\Phi_n),
\]
with its induced nilpotent operator.

Thus \(\mathcal O_n\) is an exact **entire-level quotient-contraction obstruction**, not a collection of selected-block statements.

### Proof: polynomial decomposition, without a Jordan shortcut

If \(L=0\), take \(E=0\) and \(e=0\); all assertions are immediate.

Otherwise write the characteristic polynomial as
\[
\chi_A(t)=t^a h(t),\qquad h(0)\ne0.
\]
If \(a=0\), take \(e=0\). If \(a>0\), coprimality provides a polynomial \(p\) such that
\[
p\equiv1\pmod{t^a},
\qquad
p\equiv0\pmod h;
\]
take \(e=p(A)\).

Cayley–Hamilton gives the direct decomposition
\[
L=E\oplus E_\times,
\]
where \(E=\ker A^a\), \(e\) is identity on \(E\) and zero on \(E_\times\), \(A\) is nilpotent on \(E\), and \(A\) is invertible on \(E_\times\). This also proves the stabilized-kernel description above.

No diagonalizability or zero-semisimplicity is assumed.

Because \(I\) is \(A\)-stable, every polynomial in \(A\) preserves \(I\). Hence
\[
e(I)=I\cap E,
\]
and \(I\) decomposes into its intersections with \(E\) and \(E_\times\). Passing to the quotient gives
\[
E_0(\overline A)\cong E/(I\cap E).
\]
Substituting \(\operatorname{im}\Phi_n=I\) proves the asserted obstruction formula.

If \(\overline A\) is injective and \(x\in E\), then \(A^k x=0\) for some \(k\), so injectivity of \(\overline A^k\) forces \(x+I=0\). Therefore condition 2 implies condition 3.

Conversely, if \(E\subseteq I\), then \(S\) is a quotient of \(E_\times\). The restriction of \(A\) to \(I\cap E_\times\) is an injective endomorphism of a finite-dimensional invariant space, hence surjective. Thus \(A\) and its inverse descend to that quotient. Condition 3 therefore implies condition 2.

Since
\[
\operatorname{im}(e\Phi_n)=e(I)=I\cap E,
\]
conditions 3 and 4 are equivalent. Condition 5 is exactly injectivity of \(\overline A\), which in finite dimension is equivalent to condition 2.

For conditions 1 and 2, \(\ker\overline A\) is a finite-Levi submodule. If nonzero, it has a nonzero vector killed by \(F_1,F_2\). The imported identification
\[
Q=\operatorname{lowest}(S)
\]
makes this a nonzero kernel vector of the induced contraction on \(Q\). Conversely a lowest kernel vector is already a kernel vector in \(S\). Thus conditions 1 and 2 are equivalent. No irreducible census is needed.

Finally, there is a canonical exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow
\frac{\ker T}{\ker T\cap N}
\longrightarrow
\ker\overline T
\longrightarrow
\frac{N\cap T(P)}{T(N)}
\longrightarrow0.
\]
The last map is
\[
x+N\longmapsto Tx+T(N).
\]

It is well-defined. It is onto because every element of \(N\cap T(P)\) has a preimage \(x\in P\) whose class lies in \(\ker\overline T\). Its kernel consists exactly of classes satisfying \(Tx=Ty\) for some \(y\in N\); these are precisely the image of \(\ker T\).

This proves exactness and the equivalence of conditions 1 and 6. ∎

### What saturation contributes

The theorem does **not** replace quotient injectivity by mere containment
\[
\ker T\subseteq N.
\]
That containment removes only the left term of the exact sequence. The right term
\[
\frac{N\cap T(P)}{T(N)}
\]
is the remaining one-step ideal-saturation defect. It detects a nonideal class \(x+N\) whose image \(Tx\) lies in \(N\), but cannot be obtained as \(Ty\) with \(y\in N\).

Equivalently, the **whole generalized zero-primary space** must lie in the ideal. A nilpotent chain can meet the invariant ideal before its entire chain lies there; removing its terminal free kernel does not by itself remove the induced quotient kernel. No such chain is claimed to exist in this family. Rather, no zero-semisimplicity theorem has been supplied, so omitting this obstruction would be unjustified.

If zero were separately proved semisimple, \(E\) would reduce to \(\ker A\). That additional premise is neither imported nor proved here.

The criterion is also **not**
\[
I=\ker A.
\]
Serre descendants need not all lie in the zero-primary space. The exact identity used is \(e(I)=I\cap E\), and the criterion asks whether this intersection exhausts \(E\).
