### 2.1 Direction dimension and all six unordered differences

Fix an arbitrary \(a\in A_2\). The translated set \(A_2+a\) consists of zero and three distinct nonzero vectors.

Those three vectors are linearly independent:

- A one-vector dependence would make one zero.
- A two-vector dependence would identify two of them.
- A dependence involving all three would make their sum zero, which is exactly \(X(A_2)=0\), contrary to \(x\ne0\).

They generate \(L\), because every difference of two atoms is a sum of their differences from \(a\). Therefore
\[
\dim L=3,\qquad |L|=8.
\]

There are six unordered pairs of distinct atoms of \(A_2\). Their differences are distinct:

- If two pairs share an atom, equality of differences identifies the other atoms.
- If two pairs are disjoint, equality of differences makes the XOR of all four atoms zero.

Neither is possible. Also, none of the six differences equals \(x\). Otherwise the two remaining atoms would sum to zero and hence coincide.

Thus all six differences are exactly
\[
L\setminus\{0,x\}.
\]

It follows that
\[
\gamma(d):=|A_2\cap(A_2+d)|
=
\begin{cases}
4,&d=0,\\
0,&d=x,\\
2,&d\in L\setminus\{0,x\},\\
0,&d\notin L.
\end{cases}
\]
For a nonzero difference represented by one unordered pair, the intersection has two elements, one for each endpoint. This accounts for the factor \(2\).
