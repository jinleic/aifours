## Two independently valid consequences, one ambiguous descriptor

Write `Y_A` for the original-vertex subset indexed by mask `A`; `S=(A_bit,B_bit)` for the full auxiliary state; and `F=l_b(S)` for one of the three nonzero functionals. The frozen written entry `05-0005-prospective-review-entry.json` defines coordinates `h_1(A)=H(Y_A,S)` and `h_b(A)=H(Y_A,F)`; `15-frontier-20261029-all-affine-syndrome-plane-entropy-v1.md`, section 2, states that each functional is a function of `S` and that data-processing consequences follow from these deterministic relations. These are candidate mathematical premises, not an adopted theorem.

1. **Entropy monotonicity under a function.** `(Y_A,F)` is a deterministic function of `(Y_A,S)`, so `H(Y_A,F) <= H(Y_A,S)`. Its exact `le` coefficient vector is `h_b(A)-h_1(A)`. Equivalently, `H(S|Y_A,F) >= 0`; conditioning on the full `S` gives at least as much information as conditioning on `F`.
2. **Mutual-information data processing.** Since `Y_A - S - F` is a Markov chain, `I(Y_A;F) <= I(Y_A;S)`. Its different exact `le` vector is `h_b(empty)-h_b(A)-h_1(empty)+h_1(A)`.

Both follow independently from the written functional definition. The descriptor `['data-processing', A, b]` does not itself specify which of these two consequences its author intended, and no frozen written descriptor convention observed so far disambiguates them. The bounded R382 inspection displayed the first vector; choosing a semantic rule by matching supplied `image_vectors` would be circular. Therefore an archive row using that ambiguous descriptor must settle `UNKNOWN` until a separately frozen source gives an explicit name-to-formula mapping; both derived forms may be provided as separately named internal functions and tested on self-authored fixtures, but neither is silently selected by the observed vector.
