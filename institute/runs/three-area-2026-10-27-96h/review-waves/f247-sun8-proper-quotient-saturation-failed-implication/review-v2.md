## 3. Independent reconstruction of the construction and quotient claims

Put `P=∏_{j=1}^m q_j`. The element `(1,…,1)` has order `P` in `Q`, and `(1,1,…,1)` has order `7P` in `G`; these are the respective group orders. Thus both groups are cyclic, and `7P` is squarefree. For `1≤i≤7`, the coordinate kernel `V_i={x∈Q:x_i=0}` has order `P/q_i` and index `q_i`. Hence `U_i={0}×V_i` is a subgroup of `G` of order `P/q_i`, and `[G:U_i]=7q_i`. Its left coset is exactly `C_i={i−1}×V_i`. The first coordinates are the seven distinct residues of `C_7`; consequently the seven sets are pairwise disjoint. Since the selected `q_i` are distinct primes different from 7, `gcd(7q_i,7q_j)=7` for `i≠j`.

The projection `π:G→Q` sends each `C_i` onto `V_i`, a proper subgroup of index `q_i`; these indices are distinct and pairwise coprime. Their common intersection consists of the quotient points whose first seven coordinates vanish, and includes `0_Q`. For any such point `v`, `C_i∩(N×{v})={(i−1,v)}`. As `i` runs from 1 to 7 these are exactly all seven points in that fibre; in particular the fibre over `0_Q` is saturated. No conclusion about an eighth original coset follows from saturation alone.

The coprime-index lemma used below is proved as follows. For finite `X` and subgroups `H,K` of indices `a,b`, `[X:H∩K]` is divisible by each of `a,b`. Moreover `|HK|=|H||K|/|H∩K|≤|X|`, so `[X:H∩K]≤ab`. If `gcd(a,b)=1`, it follows that `[X:H∩K]=ab`, so `HK=X`. For any `x,y∈X`, `xH∩yK` is nonempty exactly when `x^{-1}y∈HK`; hence every two such left cosets intersect.

For `N=C_7`, eight nonempty disjoint subsets cannot exist, while its seven singleton cosets have index 7 and pairwise gcd 7. Thus `B_7(N)≤7` and `B_7(N)≤6` is false. For `Q`, every subgroup index divides `P`. A pair of disjoint cosets cannot have coprime indices by the lemma; every nontrivial common divisor of their indices contains some `q_j>7`. Therefore every two disjoint quotient cosets have index gcd at least 8, i.e. `B_7(Q)≤1`. A singleton coset exists, so `B_7(Q)≤0` is false and the exact value is 1. In particular both factors satisfy the eight-coset Sun8 property.

For `J_7(Q)`, each proper subgroup index is a nonempty product of the distinct `q_j`. Given any `m+1` proper-subgroup indices, choose one prime factor from each; two chosen factors coincide, so the corresponding indices have gcd at least that prime and hence at least 8. This remains true with repetitions. Conversely the `m` coordinate kernels have indices `q_1,…,q_m`, which are pairwise coprime, so the required pair does not exist among those `m` subgroups. Hence `J_7(Q)=m`. A repeated proper index is never a bad pair here because it is itself greater than 7; this does not incorrectly prohibit repeated cofactor 1 in other arguments.

Thus F1 fails already for every displayed `m≥7`: `B_7(Q)=1` but `J_7(Q)=m>2`. The gap is `m−1` and is unbounded: for any prescribed `m`, there are `m` distinct primes greater than 7 (e.g. Euclid's argument applied after including 2, 3, 5, 7 and any already chosen primes). F2 also fails: `N` and `Q` satisfy Sun8, `Q` has `B_7(Q)≤1`, all seven original cosets are pairwise disjoint with index gcd 7, and their seven proper, pairwise-coprime-index images share `0_Q`, exceeding the proposed maximum of six.

## 4. Independent exclusion of an eighth, and separate Sun8 proof

Suppose a left coset `xH` is disjoint from every displayed `C_i` and `d=[G:H]` has `gcd(d,7q_i)≤7` for each `i≤7`. By the coprime-index lemma each gcd is at least 2. The only possible divisor of `7q_i` in `[2,7]` is 7, so `7|d` and no `q_i`, `i≤7`, divides `d`. Since `d|7P` and `7P` is squarefree, `|H|=7P/d` is not divisible by 7. The image of `H` under projection to `C_7` has order dividing both `|H|` and 7, so it is trivial. Therefore `H={0}×V` for some `V≤Q`, and `d=7[Q:V]`. The coset lies in a single layer `{k}×(z+V)`. Since `q_{k+1}` does not divide `d`, `[Q:V]` and `[Q:V_{k+1}]=q_{k+1}` are coprime. The lemma gives `(z+V)∩V_{k+1}≠∅`, contradicting disjointness from `C_{k+1}`. Other prime factors among `q_8,…,q_m` cause no exception.

Nonextendibility of one seven-coset configuration alone does **not** establish Sun8 for the whole group. Here is the independent stronger argument. Consider any bad pairwise-disjoint family of at least two left cosets of `G`, with all pairwise index gcds at most 7. Every index divides the squarefree order `7P`. Disjointness excludes gcd 1, and 7 is the only prime divisor of `7P` at most 7; hence every pairwise gcd is exactly 7. In particular each index is divisible by 7. As above, each underlying subgroup has trivial projection to `N`, so each coset lies in one of the seven `N`-layers. If two cosets lay in the same layer, their quotient subgroup indices would be coprime, because their original indices have gcd exactly 7; the coprime-index lemma would force the quotient cosets, and therefore the original cosets, to meet. All layers must therefore be distinct, and the family has at most seven members. The constructed seven attain the bound, proving `B_7(G)=7` and Sun8 for precisely this explicit group family.

This also reconstructs the target's cyclic-residue proof. Identify cyclic `G` with `Z/(7P)`. Every subgroup of index `d` is the set of multiples of `d`, and each coset is one residue class modulo `d`. Bézout's identity shows that classes modulo `d,e` intersect exactly when their residues agree modulo `gcd(d,e)`. In a bad disjoint family each gcd is 7, so every pair must have different residues modulo 7. There are only seven such residues. In particular seven repeated index-7 cosets, one per layer, are allowed; the argument never treats repeated cofactor 1 as impossible.
