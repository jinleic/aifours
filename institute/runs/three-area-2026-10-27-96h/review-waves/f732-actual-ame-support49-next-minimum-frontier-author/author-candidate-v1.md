## A. Unitary-support facts, independently derived for the needed excesses

In every cut, U is a 36×36 unitary. Any modulus-one entry saturates its row and column; in all six physical pair systems P, the set of these t entries, is pair-injective, and no E=non-P nonzero can share a P pair value. Delete the t saturated rows and columns in a cut. The resulting n=36−t unitary has N−t nonzeros and no singleton row or column, because a singleton unitary line would itself be in P. Each bipartite support component is square: a rectangular connected block of a unitary has both orthonormal rows and columns. Define its excess e_c=edges−2·order. Hence e=N+t−72=Σe_c≥0.

Distinct rows or columns cannot overlap in exactly one nonzero. In a connected component of order m>2, a degree-two row's two columns have identical row-neighborhoods: every other row must meet both or neither. Both columns have degree at least three, or the two rows/two columns form an isolated unitary block. Two different degree-two rows cannot share one column, and if they shared both, their restrictions would be an orthonormal basis of those two columns and isolate a 2×2 block. Thus their twin column pairs are disjoint. The transpose statement also holds. If the component excess is e_c, there are at least m−e_c low rows and at most e_c high columns, so m≤e_c+floor(e_c/2). A zero-excess component is consequently a full 2×2 block; a component of excess one is impossible. Excess two is necessarily a 3×3 block with one missing entry.

For excess three, m≤4. If m=4, there is exactly one low row: two low rows would require four high columns but there are only three excess units. All other rows and columns then have degree three, and the low row's twin columns, each of degree three, omit one of the high rows. That row has degree three but only the other two columns available, a contradiction. Therefore the only excess-three component is a full 3×3 block.

For excess four, m≤6; there is no connected order-five or order-six component. For m=5, there are one or two low rows. With one, the other four rows all have degree three. Its twin columns have the same degree, either three or four: five would already use six column-excess units. At least one high row avoids these two columns and therefore uses all three other columns; a high row meeting both twins has exactly one further column, creating a forbidden singleton overlap. With two low rows, their four twin columns all have degree three and the fifth column has degree two. Each twin pair meets two of the three high rows; the low column also meets two of those three. Any two 2-subsets of a 3-set intersect, and column orthogonality forbids intersection size one, so all three subsets coincide, leaving the third high row isolated. For m=6 there are exactly two low rows and four high rows, and the column degrees are four threes and two twos. The two low columns have disjoint two-high-row neighborhoods, partitioning the four high rows. The two twin high-column pairs each meet one whole such neighborhood, since an intersection of one with a low column is forbidden. They cannot choose the same neighborhood without isolating the other one; choosing opposite neighborhoods splits the component into two 3×3 components. Thus m=6 is not connected.

For excess five, m≤7. We shall need only that m=6 is impossible. There are one or two low rows, and by transposition at most two low columns. With one low row, the other five rows all have degree three. Since at least four columns are high, its twin columns cannot both have degree four or more: that would consume at least six column-excess units. They therefore have degree three and meet the low row and two high rows. Each of these two high rows has just one further column. Any of the other three high rows meeting that further column would overlap it once. That further column must nevertheless have degree at least two, so the same column is used by both of the two high rows, and the three rows and three columns split off, contradicting connectedness. With two low rows, the high row degrees are 3,3,3,4. The transpose of the preceding one-low-row argument rules out exactly one low column, so there are two low columns and the four high column degrees are also 3,3,3,4. The low-row twin pairs partition those four high columns into equal-degree pairs, impossible with one degree-four column.

## B. Correct support-49 count frontier

For N=49, e=t−23 and n=36−t. Thus t≥23. At t=23, e=0 but n=13 is odd, impossible for 2×2 blocks. At t=24, e=1 is impossible. At t=26, e=3 requires one full 3×3 block and 2×2 blocks, but n=10 is even. At t=27, e=4 is either two excess-two 3×3 components or one excess-four component. The former has even total order after adding 2×2 blocks; the latter has even component order because the only odd candidate order five is excluded above, while orders three and below cannot carry excess four. Thus n=9 is impossible. These four exact-count contradictions do not import no-OLS or a global t bound.

The nontrivial remaining low count t=25 has n=11 and e=2: each cut has ONE 3×3 unitary support with one missing entry plus four full 2×2 blocks. Each physical pair system has 11 E pair values: two of multiplicity three and nine of multiplicity two. In every cut the two multiplicity-three rows meet exactly the SAME three columns: the two multiplicity-three columns and the unique degree-two column of the 3×3 component. The same statement holds with rows and columns exchanged. The next sections exclude the entire t=25 branch.

## C. Same-tensor incidence and an alphabet reduction for t=25

Fix a party i and an E-used symbol x. Let d_i(x) be the number of its E partner values in any other party, and s_i(x) its number of E occurrences. This degree is independent of the other party: the number of P words at x equals six minus the E-partner count, since every pair value has a unitary line and is either one P entry or at least two E entries. Write s_i(x)=2d_i(x)+h_i(x). In any pair ij, h_i(x) counts multiplicity-three pair values incident to x. Therefore each h_i is independent of j, sum_x d_i(x)=11, and sum_x h_i(x)=2. Every used symbol has d_i(x)≥2.

A degree-two symbol cannot have exactly five E occurrences. Its E slice lies in a 2×2×2 cube. For each of the three directions, the two actual unitary rows have respectively two and three entries in four available columns; their overlap is nonempty and cannot have size one, so at least two cube edges run in that direction. But five vertices of the 3-cube induce at most five edges: the three omitted vertices remove nine incident edges with at most two internal edges restored. Six direction-edges are impossible. In particular h=1 forces d≥3.

Each party's h weights are either one symbol of weight two, called concentrated, or two symbols of weight one, called diffuse. At most one party is concentrated: otherwise its two distinct multiplicity-three pair values would both have the same endpoints. In fact none is concentrated. If A has concentrated symbol a, the other parties B,C,D are diffuse. The two high AB values are (a,b0),(a,b1), and the two high CD values form a matching (c0,d0),(c1,d1). Their 3×3 component contains all four words (a,b_p,c_q,d_q), p,q∈{0,1}. In the AC|BD cut, (a,c0),(a,c1) are the two high rows and must have the same three BD neighbors. But the displayed words already give them disjoint two-element subsets {(b0,d0),(b1,d0)} and {(b0,d1),(b1,d1)}, four distinct neighbors. Contradiction.

Thus every party has two diffuse high symbols, and for each pair ij its two multiplicity-three values form a perfect matching between those two symbols. The five-cube lemma makes both high-symbol degrees at least three. Since sum d_i=11 and all other degrees are at least two, every E-alphabet has at most four symbols. It has at least three: initially q_i≤5 from 2q_i≤11, so q_i=2 would require a complementary alphabet of size at least six to contain 11 pair values. Two size-three alphabets cannot coexist because 3·3<11. The only alternatives are 3444 and 4444. For each P word let k be the number of its symbols in the four E alphabets, and n_k count these 25 words. If Q=Σq_i, pair saturation and P pair-injectivity give

    Σ k n_k = 6Q−44,
    Σ binom(k,2)n_k = Σ_{i<j}q_i q_j−66.

For 3444 these are 46 and 18, so n_0+n_3+3n_4=25−46+18=−3, impossible. Consequently q_i=4 for every party. Its high degrees are either (3,3), with low degrees (2,3), or (3,4), with low degrees (2,2). In particular every party has a high symbol of degree three.

## D. Matching consistency and the even-box obstruction

Label the two high symbols in each party by bits, with the high ij pair values defined by x_i⊕x_j=c_ij. For a cut AB|CD, relabel within its pairs so the high AB and CD values are (a0,b0),(a1,b1) and (c0,d0),(c1,d1). The high-high part of the 3×3 block contains all four corresponding words. The high AC matching picks two of them, one for each high AC row. If their BD values were low, they would be two DISTINCT low BD neighbors of the two high AC rows, contradicting the fact that those rows share their UNIQUE low column. Therefore the BD matching agrees with the AC matching. Repeating for the other pairing gives

    c_AB⊕c_CD = c_AC⊕c_BD = c_AD⊕c_BC.

After switching local bit labels, arrange c_AB=c_AC=c_AD=0. The equalities force c_BC=c_BD=c_CD=p for p∈{0,1}. This is a complete signed-matching dichotomy, not an assumption that every triangle is balanced.

We first establish an **eight-box lemma**. Suppose the same U contains the eight even-parity words on the two high symbols of each party. On each physical pair all four high-symbol values already have two E extensions, so its two low pair values are saturated. The unique third extension of each of its two high pair values comes from its exceptional 3×3 component. The two high rows share one low complementary pair value L. If L contained a high symbol in either complementary party, one of the two high-row words would use a low high-symbol pair in a cross projection, already saturated by the eight box words. Thus L contains no high symbol. Consequently the 12 remaining high-pair extensions are distinct words, each with exactly two high coordinates, one for each pair and high value. Only four E words remain, and they have at most one high coordinate.

For each party j and each other party r, denote the j-coordinate of the shared nonhigh pair L_jr by ℓ_{j|r}. Choose a different party i and a degree-three high symbol of i. It already has both high symbols of j as neighbors, by the even box, so it has exactly one nonhigh j-neighbor. The two extension words using that i-symbol with each of the other two possible high partners force ℓ_{j|r}=ℓ_{j|s}, where {i,j,r,s} are the four parties. Varying i proves all three ℓ_{j|r} equal to one ℓ_j. Among the 12 extension words, ℓ_j occurs six times, once for each sign of each high pair among the other three parties. A nonhigh symbol has h=0, so s_j(ℓ_j)=2d_j(ℓ_j)≥6. At q_j=4, the two high degrees are at least three and the other low degree at least two; the degree sum 11 forces d_j(ℓ_j)=3 and both high degrees to be three. Their seven E occurrences each (four in the box and three in the extension words) and ℓ_j's six occurrences are exhausted. Each of the four remaining words can therefore use only the other, single low symbol in every party: at most ONE distinct word exists. Contradiction.

If p=0, all high matchings are globally equal after the above label switches. The four high-high entries in each of the three cuts force the two constant words and the six weight-two words: exactly the even-parity eight-box, excluded by the lemma. It remains to treat p=1, rather than assume it away.

## E. The frustrated-matching case and the complete t=25 exclusion

For p=1, complement the bit labels in B,C,D and call the resulting bits v. A pair value is high exactly when its two v-bits differ. Every two-zero/two-one assignment gives a bipartition of the four parties with a disjoint matching of high pair values. The high-high 2×2 block in that cut forces the corresponding actual E word. Thus all six balanced binary words occur. Each high pair value occurs in exactly two of these six words and has one further E extension; each equal-bit pair value is low, occurs once in the six, and has exactly one further extension.

No four-high word with both bit values can occur outside those six. If it had weight one or three, choose a high pair in it. Its complementary pair is a fully-high equal-bit value, which already occurs in a balanced word with a LOW row in the first pair. If that complementary value were the shared low column of the high rows' 3×3 component, it would additionally have both high rows as extensions, contradicting multiplicity two. An all-zero word forces the all-one word: in any cut the low 00 row contains columns 11 (from a balanced word) and 00 (from all-zero); the low 11 row already contains column 00, and one-overlap prohibition forces column 11. The converse is symmetric. If they occur, the six balanced words plus these two constants are the even eight-box, already impossible. Hence the only four-high words are the six balanced ones.

Call a three-high word **mixed** if its high bits are not all equal; its fourth coordinate is nonhigh. One mixed word forces a three-word bundle. For example, (i0,j0,k1,l=λ) in the ik|jl cut lies at a high row and a low column (j0,λ). The other high row shares that low column, forcing (i1,j0,k0,λ); the jk|il cut similarly forces (i0,j1,k0,λ). These three words use BOTH high values on each of the three pairs among i,j,k, and each was the unique extension beyond the six balanced words. No other mixed three-high word can exist: the corresponding three-party subset meets {i,j,k} in a pair, and its forced bundle would reuse a saturated high value on that pair. Thus there is either no bundle or exactly one bundle, consisting of exactly these three words.

**No bundle.** Every shared low column of an exceptional block has no high coordinate: one high coordinate would produce a mixed three-high word, and two would produce a forbidden mixed four-high word. Hence the 12 unique high-pair extensions are distinct two-high words. The same degree-three-neighbor argument as in the eight-box lemma makes their nonhigh pair values L_jr share one symbol ℓ_j per party; six of those words contain ℓ_j. Therefore d_j(ℓ_j)=3 and both high degrees are three. Each high symbol appears three times among the six balanced words and three times in these extensions, hence exactly once in the other E words, since s=2·3+1=7. For a fixed high symbol iε, each of its three equal-bit low pair values (iε,jε) already occurs once in the balanced words and needs exactly one more extension. None of the two-high opposite-bit extension words supplies it. The one remaining E word containing iε would have to supply all three, hence be the forbidden constant four-high word. Contradiction.

**One bundle.** Let its three high parties be A,B,C, with majority bit zero; D is the nonhigh party. The argument is symmetric if the majority is one. The bundle saturates every high AB, AC and BC value. Every one of the six high values on DA, DB and DC has one remaining extension beyond the balanced words. These six extensions must be distinct two-high words, since any mixed three-high word involving D would force a second bundle and mixed four-high words are impossible. Consequently each of the two high symbols of D occurs three times in the balanced words and three times in these extensions.

For D0, the three equal-bit low values (D0,A0),(D0,B0),(D0,C0) each need one further extension. They cannot be supplied by the bundle or the opposite-bit two-high words. No one E word can supply two of them: it would also contain, for example, A0 and B0, whose low pair already has its two extensions, one balanced word and one bundle word. Therefore D0 has at least three further occurrences; s_D(D0)≥9 and d_D(D0)≥4. For D1, the analogous three equal-bit values also need further extensions. A word can supply at most two, since the all-one word is impossible. Thus D1 has at least two further occurrences; s_D(D1)≥8, but s_D(D1)=2d_D(D1)+1 is odd, so it is at least nine and d_D(D1)≥4. The two other D symbols have degree at least two, contradicting the sum 11. This exhausts both bundle cases and excludes every actual complex N=49,t=25 tensor.

## F. Remaining t=28: necessary all-cut and same-tensor structure

At t=28, n=8, E has 21 words, and total excess is five. Each pair system has eight E values. Define d_i,s_i,h_i as before, now with h_i=s_i−2d_i, so Σd_i=8, Σh_i=5 and every used symbol has d_i≥2. Each q_i≤4. If q_i=2, all other q are four and both of its degrees are four. Its two residual symbols occur in two P words each; all four such P words use only the two outside symbols in each other party. P pair-injectivity makes these four binary length-four words have pairwise distance at least three, but the six pairwise distances total at least 18 whereas four binary columns contribute at most 16. Thus q_i≥3.

For t=28 the P incidence moment identity is

    n_0+n_3+3n_4 = 12−6Σq_i+Σ_{i<j}q_iq_j.

It is −6 for 3333 and −3 for 3334. Hence exactly the as-yet-unexcluded profiles 3344, 3444 and 4444 remain. In 3344 each q3 degree is at most its q3 partner's alphabet size three, so its degrees are 2,3,3. For a q3 party in 3444 the two possible degree multisets are 2,2,4 and 2,3,3; no stronger degree assertion is used.

Every symbol in a q4 party has d=2. Its E slice is a binary 3-cube, so 0≤h≤4; h=1 is excluded by the five-cube argument in section C. With four symbols and total h=5, the only multiset is 3,2,0,0. Thus the h3 symbol has seven actual E words: all but one vertex of its binary 3-cube. In each of the three pair systems involving that party, its two pair values have multiplicities four and three. The slice's two rows in each of its three 2×4 flattenings are actual rows of U, with norm one and zero inner product; dividing the seven-entry slice by sqrt(2) gives a normalized complex three-qubit state with each one-qubit marginal I/2. This does not determine the other slices, phases, or global U.

The excess-five components are either an excess-two 3×3-minus-one plus an excess-three full 3×3 and a 2×2 block, or one excess-five component plus 2×2 blocks. The first option has all pair multiplicities at most three. The second requires the excess-five component to have even order ≤7, and section A rules out order six; it must have order four, with 13 entries, plus two 2×2 blocks. Every cut has on at least one side a pair involving a q4 party, whose h3 slice forces a multiplicity-four line. Therefore the 3+3+2 option is impossible in every cut. In a 4×4, 13-entry support the only possible row-degree multisets are (4,3,3,3) and (4,4,3,2). They agree between rows and columns: if one side has a degree-two line and the other has degrees (4,3,3,3), the twin degree-three columns omit a high row that then has at most two available columns, a contradiction; use the transpose for the reverse. Adding the two 2×2 blocks yields exactly the two degree patterns stated at the start. These are necessary support patterns; their actual phase realizability and simultaneous compatibility are NOT proved.
