## Reconstruction and line-level disposition: C1

Use `D_(2p)=<r,s | r^p=s^2=1, srs=r^(−1)>`, writing each reflection as `r^y s`. Product exponents are calculated in the actual selected order; unselected reflections do not change the parity. The condition in C1:7–13 is an **integer** Q difference, not merely a zero modulo p.

1. **C1:17–23, arbitrary one-pair alternative.** Between a Q-zero pair the numbers of raw `+a` and `−a` rotations are equal. Switching their signs therefore preserves the overall signed multiplicities `2p−1` and `p−3`. Selecting `2p−2` of them with q signed negatives, `0<=q<=p−3`, gives `(2p−2−2q)a=−2(1+q)a`. These are exactly p−2 different field elements, excluding 0 and 2a. The selected pair contributes `y_i−y_j`, so any Q-zero pair with `y_j−y_i` outside `{0,2a}` admits an actual identity selector. All required signed rotations correspond to distinct actual occurrences.
2. **C1:27–34, separated-pair branches.** On choosing two or three chronological Q-zero pairs, the reversed-sign intervals are disjoint and each exchanges equal numbers of positive/negative rotations. Thus all rotations contribute `T_0=2a`; if r selected pair differences equal 2a, adding their reflection endpoints gives exponent `2(1−r)a`. In the mixed four-reflection case omit p signed positives (sum 0). In the all-zero six-reflection case omit p+2 signed positives (sum 2a), possible even at p=3. In the all-2a p>=5 four-reflection case omit p−1 signed positives and one signed negative (sum −2a); `p−3>=1` supplies that negative. Each omission leaves exactly 2p total actual positions. For p=3 and all differences 2a, all six reflection endpoints alone have exponent `−6a=0`; no unavailable negative rotation is used. These independently reconstructed counts validate C1:31–36, including the p=3 edge case.
3. C1:40 correctly makes the three-pair result conditional. Its separate assertion that another all-prime six-reflection-minimal family exists is a named historical import and was **not** verified here; it is not needed for the proof above.

## Reconstruction and line-level disposition: C2

1. **C2:15–38, matching and support.** The p−3 outside adjacent-reflection edges and either edge of the turn give p−2 disjoint two-choice blocks. Keeping one endpoint of each and all singleton reflections yields `3p−2−(p−2)=2p` original reflection occurrences. A block's sign is fixed because it supplies exactly one occurrence; intervening original rotations are skipped. Each independent endpoint switch translates the alternating exponent by a nonzero element of F_p. Starting from a singleton, every proper nonempty support strictly grows upon union with such a translate (otherwise invariance under its nonzero generator would make it all of F_p). Hence the support size is at least p−1. Complementing the endpoint choices maps x to `2μ−x`; if zero is missing, the support must be `F_p\{0}` and its unique missing value must be fixed, so `μ=0` since p is odd.
2. **C2:40–50, local flip.** With ε the first turn-block sign, the doubled turn midpoint for M1 is `ε(y_i+y_(i+1)−2y_(i+2))`, and for M2 is `ε(2y_i−y_(i+1)−y_(i+2))`. Unchanged outside blocks have the same signs because the triple occupies two blocks in either matching. Thus `2(μ1−μ2)=ε(d_i−d_(i+1)) != 0`; both families cannot miss zero. This proves an actual reflection-only length-2p selector, not a virtual raw-gap packet.
3. **C2:56–62, automatic matching and constant-step case.** Deleting the turn triple leaves paths with `L+R=3p−5`. Adjacent greedy pairs give at least `(3p−7)/2` edges; deleting at most z zero edges leaves at least p−3 when `z<=(p−1)/2`. With no equal consecutive exponents and no nonzero turn, all linear differences are the same nonzero d; the first 2p reflections have alternating sum `−p d=0`.
4. **C2:68–78, cyclic cut/restoration.** If `z_cyc=0`, the preceding no-equality result applies. Otherwise cutting at an equal edge removes exactly one zero; when `1<=z_cyc<=(p+1)/2`, at most `(p−1)/2` zero edges remain. A nonzero turn survives because neither of its edges is the cut. The selected reflection list after sorting by original indices is a cyclic rotation of the cut-order selected list. If the latter product is `UV=1`, the former is `VU=1`; the restoration therefore respects Def:8,12. The displayed alternatives in C2:74–78 are necessary conditions on REF failure, not a converse or an existence claim.

REF failure means no original-order reflection-only identity selection of exactly 2p. Do not replace any caller failure or raw matching constraint with this review's conditions.

Let G mean that the original linear reflection order contains a nonzero unequal turn with p−3 disjoint adjacent nonzero-difference reflection pairs outside its triple; let `z_lin` and `z_cyc` count equal adjacencies in the linear and cyclic orders respectively. Every actual residual word must, in addition, satisfy all three explicit necessary conditions:

1. **not G**;
2. `z_lin>=1`;
3. `z_cyc >= (p+3)/2`, **or** `1<=z_cyc<=(p+1)/2` and there is no cyclic nonzero unequal turn (equivalently each maximal run of nonzero cyclic differences has a constant slope).

The exact remaining obligation is to prove, for **every actual word satisfying the retained imported residual and these necessary filters**, an identity selection of exactly 2p distinct original positions—using a suitable maximum-matching packet family or a separately proved actual selector—or supply an actual counterexample. These conditions are not sufficient for REF failure, do not establish that the intersection is nonempty, and do not settle the other callers.
