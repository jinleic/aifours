## 4. Independent count audit of the later no-48 interface

The following elementary argument checks the *count dispatch*, not the imported profile or entire-branch theorems. In a unitary support, two different rows cannot share exactly one nonzero column, nor can two columns share exactly one row: the relevant inner product would consist of one nonzero term. Every connected component of the row–column support graph is square: its isolated submatrix has both row and column identity Grams, so it has the same number of rows and columns.

* At excess zero every row and column has degree two. A row supported on two columns must have the same support as the other row meeting either column, by the no-single-overlap rule; these two rows and columns form an isolated full 2×2 block. Repeating shows that every component is a full 2×2 block and the residual order is even.
* At excess one exactly one row has degree three and all other rows have degree two. Every one of its three columns has a further row; each such row must meet the degree-three row in two columns and hence is supported on one of the three pairs. Covering all three columns requires two distinct pairs, which share exactly one column, contradicting row orthogonality. Thus excess one is impossible.
* At excess two, component excesses are nonnegative; the previous point excludes excess-one components. All but one component therefore have excess zero. In the remaining connected component, either one row has degree four or two rows have degree three. The degree-four case is impossible: for three columns in that row, column orthogonality forces further degree-two rows supported on the first–second and first–third pairs; those two rows overlap in exactly one column. Hence there are exactly two degree-three rows, with supports \(S,T\) of size three. If \(S,T\) are disjoint, every degree-two row meeting \(S\) is supported inside \(S\), isolating it from the second degree-three row, contrary to connectedness. One common column is forbidden by row orthogonality. If they have exactly two common columns, a further row meeting the unique column of \(S\) must also meet \(S\) in a common column, making its overlap with the other degree-three row exactly one; but that column needs a further row. Therefore \(S=T\). Every other row meeting \(S\) is supported within \(S\), so connectedness and the square-component fact make this a 3×3 component with two degree-three rows and one degree-two row: its support is full except for one entry. Together with the zero-excess blocks, the residual order is odd.
