The positive Serre relations make \(E_i\) nilpotent on every \(e_j\). On the other generators:

- \(E_i f_j=0\) for \(j\ne i\);
- the \(f_i,h_i,e_i\) adjoint string terminates;
- \(E_i h=-\alpha_i(h)e_i\), so the string terminates for **every** Cartan element, including additional realization directions.

The negative relations give the corresponding assertions for \(F_i\).

For a derivation \(D\),
\[
D^N[x,y]
 =\sum_{k=0}^{N}\binom Nk[D^kx,D^{N-k}y].

Therefore local nilpotence on generators implies local nilpotence on each finite Lie word, and hence on every element of \(S\).

This argument uses both signs of the full Serre relations. It neither asserts free-source injectivity nor identifies \(J\) with \(\tau\).

\tag{15}
