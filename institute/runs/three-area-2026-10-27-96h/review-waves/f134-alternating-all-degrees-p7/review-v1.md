## 3. The small-degree claim: exact falsification and exact residual truth

**3.1 The error.** Line 52: "they are 7a_i with pairwise-coprime a_i; at most one a_i is 1". Pairwise coprimality of the a_i never bounds the number of a_i equal to 1 (gcd(1,1)=1; gcd(7,7)=7). The sentence silently assumes seven *distinct* index values. For n=7, A_6=Stab(p) has index 7 and there are seven such subgroups. My independent controls (`reviewer-perm-controls-v1.py`, tuples and an independent multiplication-table lattice enumerator, not the author's code): `A7-stab` — A_7 generated as 2520 even permutations, seven distinct point stabilizers of order 360; `A7-cosets` — the seven left cosets {g : g(7)=x} are pairwise disjoint, each of size 360, and partition A_7; `A7-index7` — the full conjugacy-class lattice (3786 subgroups total, equal to the memory-quoted published count; 59 and 501 for A_5, A_6 likewise) has exactly 7 subgroups of index 7; `A7-multiset` — the largest multiset of proper subgroups with every pairwise index gcd exactly 7 has size **9** (seven index-7 stabilizers, one index-35 subgroup, one index-42 subgroup; gcd(35,42)=7). Therefore the line-3 assertion is false at n=7 for seven subgroups, for seven distinct subgroups, for seven pairwise disjoint cosets, and even for eight or nine subgroup indices. This matches the author's two corrections. It gives **no** eight-disjoint-coset Sun8 counterexample: seven cosets of index 7 already exhaust A_7 (density 1), and e.g. the index multiset (7×6, 21, 35) has density 14/15<1 but is not excluded by index arithmetic alone — coset geometry would be needed (not in the candidate; not assessed here as a theorem).

**3.2 What the table does prove (reviewer refinement, not in the frozen candidate).** Control C11 computes, by exact maximum-clique search over the divisors of |A_n|, the largest set of *distinct* divisors with pairwise gcd exactly 7: sizes 0,0,4,4,4,4,5,5,6 for n=5,…,13 — reproducing every entry of the table exactly (e.g. n=7: {7,21,35,56}; n=13: {7,21,35,77,91,3584}). So as a bound on distinct index values the table is correct; its failure is only the distinctness assumption. A repeated value v in a gcd-exactly-7 family needs gcd(v,v)=v=7, i.e. a subgroup of index 7. Such a subgroup exists only for n=7: for n=5,6, 7∤|A_n| (C12c); for n≥8 every proper subgroup of A_n has index ≥n (A_n simple for n≥5, so the coset action embeds A_n into S_k, forcing n!/2≤k!, impossible for k<n). Hence the seven-index exclusion with pairwise gcd exactly 7 is **true unconditionally (elementary) for n∈{5,6,8,9,10,11,12,13} and false exactly at n=7** among n≤13. Control C11 also shows why Sections 3–5 are required beyond 13: for n=14,15,16 the elementary bound still gives 6 under index≥n, and at n=17 it reaches 7.
