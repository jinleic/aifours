## 2. Exact conditional theorem (reconstructed) and import boundary

**Notation.**
- l = sl3, with e1=E12, e2=E23, f1=E21, f2=E32, h1=E11−E22, h2=E22−E33.
- n_+^l = span{e1, e2, [e1,e2]}.
- M = traceless 3×3 matrices, with l acting by ad.
- L(M) ⊂ T(M) is the Lie subalgebra generated by M, and L_n(M) = L(M) ∩ M^(⊗n).
- E_i, F_i, H_i are the derivation extensions of the l-action.
- beta(u_1,…,u_k; z) = ad(u_1)⋯ad(u_k) z.
- The form is <X,Y> = tr(X^*Y) on M, with the product form on M^(⊗n), restricted to L_n(M).

**Premises.**
- **A1** [imported, not reviewed; actual-source/free-Lie identification]: S_n ≅ L_n(M)/I_n as l-modules.
  - I = I^(r) + I^(s), where I^(j) is the ideal of L(M) generated by Q_j.
  - Q_r ⊂ L_{r+1}(M) and Q_s ⊂ L_{s+1}(M).
- **A2** [imported, not reviewed; seed lowestness]: Q_j = U(n_+^l) q_j, where q_j ≠ 0 is homogeneous and F_1 q_j = F_2 q_j = 0.
- **A3** [classical]: L(M) ⊂ T(M) is the free Lie algebra. l acts on it by derivations, and A1 says this is the actual Levi action.
- **A4** [verified exactly here]:
  - (ad e_i)^dagger = ad f_i on M.
  - The ad h_i are self-adjoint.
  - Both properties extend to M^(⊗n).

**Conclusions.** Fix n and a root degree alpha = (a,b,n). Let P = {x ∈ L_n(M)_alpha : F_1x = F_2x = 0}, and let Pi_P be the orthogonal projection L_n(M) -> P.

- **T1.** Pi_P ∘ E_i = 0 on L_n(M) for i = 1,2, and Pi_P kills every root space other than alpha.
- **T2.** For every W ⊆ L_n(M) stable under all of E_i, F_i, H_i: W^⊥ is stable, P = (P∩W) ⊕ (P∩W^⊥), and Pi_P(W) = P ∩ W.

- **T4.** P ∩ I^(j)_n = Pi_P(I^(j)_n) = span{Pi_P beta(u;q_j)}, over all ordered u ∈ B^(n−d_j) with deg u = alpha − deg q_j.

## 3. Lemma-by-lemma reconstruction

**L1. Set-up (§1).** Every L_n(M)_alpha is finite-dimensional. At fixed level n, the map (a,b) -> (2a−b−n, −a+2b−n) is injective, because the A2 Cartan matrix is invertible. So a "root space" of L_n(M) is exactly an sl3 weight space, and P is well defined. **OK.**

**L2. Metric and adjointness (§2).**
- On M: tr([A,X]^*Y) = tr((X^*A^*−A^*X^*)Y) = tr(X^*[A^*,Y]). Hence (ad A)^dagger = ad A^*, and e_i^* = f_i. The h_i are real diagonal, so ad h_i is self-adjoint.
- On M^(⊗n): E_i is the sum of single-slot actions, and the product form gives E_i^dagger = F_i.
- Restriction to L_n(M): L_n(M) is stable under E_i, F_i and H_i, because derivations of T(M) that extend endomorphisms of M preserve the Lie subalgebra generated by M. The restricted operators therefore stay mutually adjoint.
- For x ∈ P: <x, E_i y> = <F_i x, y> = 0, so Pi_P E_i y = 0 for every y, whatever its weight.
- Exact checks:
  - the Gram matrix of B is as stated;
  - adjointness holds on M and on M^(⊗2) (all 64×64 word pairs);
  - the Cartan operators are self-adjoint.
- **CORRECT.**
- **MINOR-1.** "block(3,4)" and "G77" index B from 0: positions 3 and 4 are E11−E33 and E11−E22, and position 7 is −2E13. Read with 1-based indexing the statement is false: positions 3 and 4 would be E21 and E11−E33, which are orthogonal with norms 1 and 2. The indexing convention is not stated.

**L3. Stable-submodule projection (§3).**
- Let W be stable under E_i, F_i, H_i, and take w' ∈ W^⊥, w ∈ W. Then <w,E_iw'> = <F_iw,w'> = 0 and <w,F_iw'> = <E_iw,w'> = 0, and similarly for H_i. So W^⊥ is stable.
- Split x ∈ P as x = w + w'. Then F_i w and F_i w' lie in W and W^⊥, so both vanish. The H_i-eigenvalues of w and w' equal alpha.
- So P = (P∩W) ⊕ (P∩W^⊥) orthogonally, and Pi_P|_W = Pi_{P∩W}|_W.
- I^(j) is l-stable, because D beta(u;z) = Σ_t beta(…,Du_t,…;z) + beta(u;Dz) and each Q_j is an l-module. The sum I is then l-stable too.
- All three stabilities are used:
  - **E-stability of W** makes W^⊥ F-stable.
  - F- and H-stability split P.
- The ideal J = ⟨Cq⟩ generated by the seed **line** is F-stable but **not** E-stable, and the conclusion fails for it (control NC-line).
- **CORRECT.**

**L4. The whole one-seed ideal from nested letters (§4).**
- For a Lie algebra L and a subspace Q, the span of the elements ad(x_1)⋯ad(x_m)z (x_t ∈ L, z ∈ Q, m ≥ 0) contains Q, is ad(L)-stable, and lies in every ideal containing Q. It is therefore ⟨Q⟩.
- By Jacobi, ad[x,y] = [ad x, ad y]. Since M generates L(M), each ad(x) with x ∈ L_j(M) is a combination of products ad(u_1)⋯ad(u_j) with u_t ∈ M.
- The grading then fixes k = n − d letters.
- Mixed brackets: [z_r,z_s] = −ad(z_s)z_r lies in I^(r), and equals ad(z_r)z_s, which lies in I^(s).
- ⟨Q_r+Q_s⟩ = ⟨Q_r⟩ + ⟨Q_s⟩.
- **CORRECT.**

**L5. Moving every seed raising to the outer letters (§4).**
- For each derivation D in {E_1, E_2, E_12}: D beta(u;z) = Σ_t beta(…,Du_t,…;z) + beta(u;Dz). This is the displayed identity, and its sign checks out.
- After Pi_P the term E beta(u;z) vanishes, by T1.
- n_+^l is generated by E_1 and E_2, so Q = U(n_+^l)q is spanned by E-words applied to q.
- Induction on word length gives Pi_P beta(u;z) ∈ span{Pi_P beta(u';q) : u' ∈ B^k}. Together with L3 this proves T4.
- Basis letters are weight vectors, so only tuples of the right degree contribute.
- Load-bearing assumptions, each confirmed by negative controls:
  - q is the **lowest** vector, so raisings of q alone span Q;
  - **every ordered** tuple is used, not a sample.
- **CORRECT.**
- **MINOR-2.** Here "U(n_+)" must mean the finite-Levi nilradical n_+^l. Read as the Kac–Moody n_+, it would give the whole ideal. The phrase "complete finite-Levi modules" and the commutator remark settle the intended meaning, but the symbol is overloaded.

**L6. Two seeds, sum and overlaps (§4).**
- Pi_P is linear, so Pi_P(I^(r)_n + I^(s)_n) = Pi_P I^(r)_n + Pi_P I^(s)_n.
- The rank of the union of the spanning families equals dim(P ∩ I_n) and accounts for overlaps automatically.
- Adding the two ranks, as if the sum were direct, is wrong whenever P∩I^(r)∩I^(s) ≠ 0 (control NC-overlap).
- **CORRECT.**

**L7. Coordinates and the actual quotient (§5 ¶1).**
- From <x_i, y − Pi_P y> = 0 we get Hc = b, so c = H^-1 b. H is positive definite.
- The column span of the c(y) over the spanning family is the coordinate image of P∩I_n.
- Lift:
  - by L3, L_n(M) = I_n ⊕ I_n^⊥ as l-modules;
  - under A1, I_n^⊥ maps l-isomorphically onto S_n;
  - so every lowest x̄ of weight alpha has a unique lift x ∈ I_n^⊥ ∩ P.
- The kernel of P -> Pbar is P ∩ I_n.
- **CORRECT.**
- **MINOR-3.** "EXACTLY c(y)=H^-1 b(y)" assumes the form is linear in its second argument. Under the other convention the coordinates are complex conjugates. Ranks are unaffected, and all data here are rational.
- **MINOR-4.** Symbols are reused:
  - k means n−d (§4) and also dim P (§5: "x1,…,xk", "k−d");
  - d means the seed level and also rank N.
  
  Distinct symbols would prevent misreading "dimension k−d".

**L8. Contraction and D3 (§5 ¶2).**
- These statements are linear-algebra-standard and correct as stated:
  - if A(N) ⊆ N, the induced map on P/N is well defined;
  - a singular A on P can still induce an invertible map on P/N;
  - discarding ordinary kernel vectors is not enough.
- The injectivity conclusion depends on an imported candidate and is outside scope.
- **NOT REVIEWED / INCONCLUSIVE.**
