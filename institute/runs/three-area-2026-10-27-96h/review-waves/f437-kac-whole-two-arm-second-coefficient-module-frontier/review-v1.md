## 2. Proof of the actual \(b=2\) module identification

Put \(v=e_2\). In the exterior square of the first-arm module, define
\[
q_1=v\wedge E_1v,\qquad
q_3=v\wedge E_3v.
\]

Under the bracket map, these are, up to an irrelevant overall sign, exactly the two Serre relations with repeated node \(2\):
\[
(\operatorname{ad}e_2)^2e_1,\qquad
(\operatorname{ad}e_2)^2e_3.
\]

### 2.1 Why the free degree-two source is \(\Lambda^2M\)

First impose the \(b=0\) positive presentation and all \(b=1\) relations. The resulting positive algebra before imposing the repeated-node-\(2\) Serres is
\[
\mathfrak n_{13}^+\ltimes \mathcal L(M),
\]
where \(\mathcal L(M)\) is the ordinary free Lie algebra on the vector space \(M\).

This follows from the elimination/universal-property description:

- the ideal generated by \(v\) is generated, as a Lie algebra, by its \(\mathfrak n_{13}^+\)-translates;
- the \(b=0\) relations impose precisely the \(\mathfrak n_{13}^+\)-action;
- the complete linearized \(b=1\) relations impose precisely the module \(M\);
- no further relation is imposed in its free Lie algebra until the two repeated-node-\(2\) Serres are added.

The homogeneous free-Lie degree-two space is exactly \(\Lambda^2M\).

At \(b=2\), bracketing a degree-two relation with a positive-\(b\) element would increase \(b\). Thus the remaining degree-two ideal consists of the complete \(\mathfrak n_{13}^+\)-closures of \(q_1,q_3\).

The exclusion of additional positive consequences from negative Serres is the retained **f276 whole-ideal bridge**. It is not inferred from this positive-degree argument alone.

### 2.2 Both relation vectors are genuine finite-type lowest vectors

The lowest vector \(v\) satisfies
\[
F_1v=F_3v=0,\qquad h_1v=-v,\qquad h_3v=-sv.
\]

For \(q_1\),
\[
F_1q_1=v\wedge v=0,\qquad F_3q_1=0,
\]
and its labels are
\[
h_1q_1=0,\qquad h_3q_1=-(2s+r)q_1.
\]
Thus \(q_1\) is a lowest vector of labels \((0,2s+r)\).

For \(q_3\),
\[
F_1q_3=0,\qquad F_3q_3=v\wedge sv=0,
\]
and
\[
h_1q_3=-3q_3,\qquad h_3q_3=-(2s-2)q_3.
\]
Thus \(q_3\) is a lowest vector of labels \((3,2s-2)\).

Both exterior vectors are nonzero: \(E_1v\ne0\) and \(E_3v\ne0\), and each has weight different from \(v\).

Because \(\Lambda^2M\) is a finite-dimensional semisimple \(\mathfrak g\)-module, a nonzero cyclic lowest vector of fixed labels generates one irreducible copy. Consequently,
\[
U(\mathfrak g)q_1\cong L_{\mathrm{low}}(0,2s+r),
\]
\[
U(\mathfrak g)q_3\cong L_{\mathrm{low}}(3,2s-2).
\]

Since both vectors are killed by the simple lowering operators,
\[
U(\mathfrak g)q_i=U(\mathfrak n_{13}^+)q_i.
\]
Therefore these are exactly the required positive closures, with no replacement by a larger guessed relation space.

Their labels differ, so the two embedded irreducible modules intersect trivially. This proves Theorem A.

## 3. Exact character and component coverage

For any nonnegative labels \(p,q\), let
\[
P^{(r)}_{p,q}(x,y)
\]
be the relative-lowest-weight character of \(L_{\mathrm{low}}^{(13)}(p,q)\): the coefficient of \(x^ay^n\) is the multiplicity at displacement \(a\alpha_1+n\alpha_3\) from its lowest weight.

The entire actual second-arm character is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
P_N(x,y)
={}&\frac12\left(
P^{(r)}_{1,s}(x,y)^2
-
P^{(r)}_{1,s}(x^2,y^2)
\right)\\
&-xP^{(r)}_{0,\,2s+r}(x,y)
-yP^{(r)}_{3,\,2s-2}(x,y).
\end{aligned}
}
\]

Thus
\[
\boxed{
\dim S_{(a,2,n)}=[x^ay^n]P_N(x,y).
}
\]

This is exact coverage of every multiplicity, including zero spaces. It does not require a multiplicity-free decomposition of \(\Lambda^2M\).

For explicit evaluation, the finite Weyl formula gives
\[
P^{(r)}_{p,q}
=
\frac{
\displaystyle\sum_{w\in W_r}(-1)^{\ell(w)}
e^{\,\lambda+\rho-w(\lambda+\rho)}
}{
\displaystyle\prod_{\beta\in\Delta_r^+}(1-e^\beta)
},
\qquad
\lambda=p\omega_1+q\omega_3,
\]
with \(e^{a\alpha_1+n\alpha_3}=x^ay^n\).

The positive-root degree lists are
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
r&\Delta_r^+\\ \hline
1&(1,0),(0,1),(1,1),\\
2&(1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(1,2),\\
3&(1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(1,2),(1,3),(2,3).
\end{array}
\]

The dimension consequence is
\[
\boxed{
\dim N=
\binom{D_r(1,s)}2
-D_r(0,2s+r)
-D_r(3,2s-2),
}
\]
where
\[
D_1(p,q)=\frac{(p+1)(q+1)(p+q+2)}2,
\]
\[
D_2(p,q)=
\frac{(p+1)(q+1)(p+q+2)(2p+q+3)}6,
\]
and, with node \(1\) the long node,
\[
D_3(p,q)=
\frac{
(p+1)(q+1)(p+q+2)(2p+q+3)(3p+q+4)(3p+2q+5)
}{120}.
\]

For example, the symbolic \(A_2\) specialization simplifies to
\[
\dim N=
\frac{s^4+8s^3+s^2-6s+12}{2}.
\]
This is a written consequence of the module identification and Weyl dimension formula, not executed output.

## 5. Maximum first-arm grade

The maximum third grade in \(M=L_{\mathrm{low}}(1,s)\) is
\[
L(r,s)=
\begin{cases}
s+1,&r=1,\\
2s+2,&r=2,\\
4s+6,&r=3.
\end{cases}
\]

For \(A_2\), the longest Weyl element exchanges the two fundamental labels, giving total displacement
\[
(a,n)=(s+1,s+1).
\]

For \(B_2,G_2\), the longest element is \(-1\). The displacement \((a,n)\) from lowest to highest therefore solves
\[
2a-n=2,\qquad -ra+2n=2s.
\]
This gives
\[
a=\frac{2(s+2)}{4-r},\qquad
n=\frac{2r+4s}{4-r},
\]
which yields the stated values.

In particular,
\[
M_n=0\quad(n>L),\qquad
N_n=0\quad(n>2L).
\]

The second statement is an upper bound inherited from \(\Lambda^2M\), not a claim that every grade up to \(2L\) survives.

## 6. A tensor-lowest-vector bound

The following finite-type lemma supplies the point missing from a direct repetition of the first-arm positivity argument.

### Lemma

Every nonzero joint simple-lowest vector in \(M\otimes M\), for the diagonal \(\mathfrak g\)-action, has total third grade at most \(L\).

The same is true in \(N\).

### Proof

Choose a compact positive Hermitian form on \(M\), with
\[
E_i^*=F_i\qquad(i=1,3),
\]
and use its tensor-product form.

The diagonal positive algebra generates \(M\otimes M\) from \(v\otimes M\). Indeed, the identity
\[
E_iw\otimes z
=
E_i^{\mathrm{diag}}(w\otimes z)-w\otimes E_iz
\]
proves this by induction on the length of a positive word generating the first factor from \(v\).

Let \(y\) be killed by both diagonal \(F_1,F_3\), and suppose its total third grade is greater than \(L\).

It is orthogonal to \(v\otimes M\), whose third grades are at most \(L\). It is also orthogonal to every nonconstant diagonal positive word applied to \(v\otimes M\), because the adjoint word is a product of simple lowering operators and kills \(y\).

Thus \(y\) is orthogonal to all of \(M\otimes M\), a contradiction.

For \(N\), take the orthogonal complement of the two relation modules inside \(\Lambda^2M\). Because those modules are \(\mathfrak g\)-invariant and \(E_i^*=F_i\), their orthogonal complement is an invariant realization of the quotient \(N\). It is graded. A joint lowest vector in \(N\) therefore gives one in \(M\otimes M\) at the same grade. ∎

This is a bound on the starting grades of irreducible constituents. It does not assert that \(N\) itself is irreducible.

## 7. Proof of uniform high-grade invertibility

Assume
\[
n\ge L+2.
\]

The targets of \(W_2,W_3,W_4,W_5\) lie in \(M_{n-1}\), which is zero because \(n-1>L\).

The targets of \(W_6,W_7\) lie in \(\mathfrak g_{13}\) at third grade \(n-1\). Its largest positive-root third grade is \(r\), and \(n-1>L\ge r\). These targets also vanish.

Only after these consuming-side checks does the full operator reduce to
\[
\boxed{
\overline A_n=A^0
=E_3F_3+[E_1,E_3][F_3,F_1].
}
\]

Write
\[
T=E_3,\qquad Z=[E_1,E_3].
\]
On the finite module \(N\), choose its compact positive form. Then
\[
T^*=F_3,\qquad Z^*=[F_3,F_1],
\]
so
\[
\langle x,A^0x\rangle
=
\|F_3x\|^2+\|[F_3,F_1]x\|^2.
\]

Let
\[
K=\ker F_3\cap\ker[F_3,F_1].
\]
The commutators
\[
[E_1,F_3]=0,\qquad
[E_1,[F_3,F_1]]=-F_3,
\]
\[
[F_1,F_3]=-[F_3,F_1],\qquad
[F_1,[F_3,F_1]]=0
\]
show that \(K\) is a node-\(1\) \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\)-submodule, preserving the third grade.

If \(K\cap N_n\ne0\), it contains a nonzero node-\(1\) lowest vector \(y\). Then
\[
F_1y=F_3y=0.
\]
The tensor-lowest-vector bound forces \(n\le L\), contrary to \(n\ge L+2\).

Therefore \(A^0\) is positive definite on the entire \(N_n\), hence invertible. Since the contraction preserves each actual root component, its restriction to every \(S_{(a,2,n)}\) is invertible. This proves Theorem B.

The compact form is used only on a finite-dimensional \(\mathfrak g_{13}\)-module. No positivity statement about the whole nonsymmetrizable algebra enters the proof.

## 8. Complete top-grade calculation for \(r=1\)

Now suppose \(r=1\). For an \(A_2\) lowest module \(L_{\mathrm{low}}(p,q)\), its top third grade is \(p+q\), and that grade is one node-\(1\) module \(V_q\).

Consequently,
\[
M_{s+1}=V_s,
\]
and
\[
(\Lambda^2M)_{2s+2}=\Lambda^2V_s.
\]

The first relation module has labels \((0,2s+1)\) and starts at third grade zero. Its top third grade is \(2s+1\), so it contributes nothing at \(2s+2\).

The second relation module has labels \((3,2s-2)\) and starts at third grade one. Its top third grade is
\[
1+3+(2s-2)=2s+2,
\]
where its node-\(1\) module is \(V_{2s-2}\).

The standard exterior-square decomposition is
\[
\Lambda^2V_s
=
\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\lfloor(s-1)/2\rfloor}
V_{2s-2-4j}.
\]
The second Serre module removes exactly the \(j=0\) summand. Hence
\[
N_{2s+2}
=
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\lfloor(s-1)/2\rfloor}
V_{2s-2-4j}.
\]

This is nonzero precisely when \(s\ge3\).

For complete horizontal placement, the \(j\)-th surviving summand has one-dimensional weight spaces at
\[
\boxed{3+2j\le a\le2s+1-2j.}
\]
Overlaps between these intervals give the actual multiplicities.

Equivalently,
\[
[y^{2s+2}]P_N(x,y)
=
\sum_{1\le i<j\le s+1}x^{i+j}
-
\sum_{a=3}^{2s+1}x^a.
\]

### Full contraction at the top

At \(n=2s+2\), the reduction to \(A^0\) is valid. Moreover, \(T\) and \(Z\) raise the third grade and therefore kill the whole top grade.

For \(A_2\),
\[
[Z,[F_3,F_1]]=h_1+h_3.
\]
Thus, on the top grade,
\[
A^0
=
[E_3,F_3]+[Z,[F_3,F_1]]
=
h_1+2h_3.
\]

At degree \((a,2,2s+2)\),
\[
h_1=2a-2s-4,\qquad
h_3=-a+2s+4.
\]
Therefore
\[
h_1+2h_3=2s+4,
\]
independently of \(a\). This proves Theorem C.

## 9. Explicit actual level-eight component and actual \(F_2\)

Take
\[
r=1,\qquad s=3.
\]
The complete top-grade formula gives
\[
N_8=V_0.
\]
Its only horizontal degree is \(a=5\). Hence
\[
\dim S_{(5,2,8)}=1,\qquad \overline A_8=10I.
\]

Here is an explicit actual-source construction establishing nonvacuity without relying solely on character subtraction.

Set
\[
T=E_3,\qquad v=e_2,\qquad
y=[Tv,T^2v].
\]

The repeated-node-\(2\) Serre relation gives
\[
[v,Tv]=0.
\]
Applying \(T\) gives
\[
[v,T^2v]=0.
\]

For the node-\(3\) string of lowest weight \(-3\),
\[
F_3Tv=3v,\qquad F_3T^2v=4Tv.
\]
Consequently,
\[
F_3y
=
3[v,T^2v]+4[Tv,Tv]=0.
\]
Also \(F_1y=0\), since \(F_1\) commutes with \(T\) and kills \(v\).

The actual \(F_2\)-action is
\[
F_2v=-h_2,\qquad
F_2Tv=-e_3,\qquad
F_2T^2v=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{F_2y=-T^3v.}
\]

This is the original Chevalley action, not an auxiliary adjoint.

Furthermore,
\[
F_3^3T^3v=36v\ne0.
\]
Thus \(T^3v\ne0\), and \(F_2y\ne0\) proves \(y\ne0\).

The degree and lowest labels of \(y\) are
\[
\deg y=(0,2,3),\qquad
(h_1y,h_3y)=(-5y,0).
\]
Hence
\[
U(\mathfrak g_{13})y\cong L_{\mathrm{low}}(5,0).
\]

One explicit nonzero vector in its top grade is
\[
\boxed{x=E_3^5E_1^5y\in S_{(5,2,8)}.}
\]
Indeed, the first string has lowest label \(5\), so \(E_1^5y\ne0\). This vector is \(F_3\)-lowest of label \(5\), so applying \(E_3^5\) is also nonzero.

Thus this is an actual nonzero whole component, not a formal weight or an empty-space invertibility claim.

### Separate radical consequence

Let
\[
\mathcal R=\tau/J.
\]
The adopted first-arm radical theorem gives
\[
\mathcal R\cap M=0.
\]

The intersection
\[
\mathcal R\cap U(\mathfrak g_{13})y
\]
is a submodule of the irreducible module \(L_{\mathrm{low}}(5,0)\). If it were the whole module, then \(y\in\mathcal R\), and ideal stability would imply
\[
F_2y=-T^3v\in\mathcal R\cap M,
\]
contradicting \(T^3v\ne0\).

Therefore
\[
\mathcal R\cap U(\mathfrak g_{13})y=0.
\]
Since this module contains the entire one-dimensional component at \((5,2,8)\),
\[
\boxed{J_{(5,2,8)}=\tau_{(5,2,8)}.}
\]

This is a separate, scoped radical argument. It does not identify contraction no-cancellation with radical equality, and no novelty claim is made for finite-type/Weyl-transport consequences.

## 11. Exact remaining limits

The completed results are:

- the entire actual \(b=2\) module presentation for all \(r\in\{1,2,3\}\), \(s>r\);
- its exact character and component-multiplicity formula;
- full contraction invertibility on every actual component with \(n\ge L(r,s)+2\);
- the exact nonzero top grade and scalar contraction for the entire \(r=1,\ s\ge3\) parameter class;
- an explicit actual level-eight component, its actual \(F_2\)-action, and a separate scoped radical equality.

The following are **not established**:

1. Full \(b=2\) contraction invertibility in the remaining grades \(2\le n\le L(r,s)+1\).
2. A general radical theorem for the whole \(b=2\) quotient. Reducibility prevents copying the first-arm irreducibility argument.
3. Complete low-grade matrices where \(W_2,\ldots,W_7\) have nonzero targets. Those terms remain present and unevaluated outside the proved classes.
4. Both horizontal coefficients arbitrary, equal-parameter extensions by this proof, or short-arm parameter at least four.
5. Whole-level-eight closure, \(B_8=0\), global \(J=\tau\), or the full book conjecture.
6. A simultaneous-lowering counterexample, a new generalized kernel, or a falsification of the original presentation.
7. Independent reconstruction of the imported f276/f306/f314 manuscripts.
8. A new original-source authentication, current-status audit, novelty determination, human review, independent-model-family review, or formal/kernel acceptance.

No acceptance is self-granted.
