## 3. Images, square lifting, and the actual graded image

**Disposition: SUPPORTED.**  
**References:** S1 LF29–35, LF113–127.

Because \(q(s^2)=0\) and \(q\) is pure, every square in \(S\) is idempotent. Consequently every square in any actual image \(T=\phi(S)\) is idempotent.

\[

\[

### Least-group quotient

For any homomorphism \(\alpha:R\to H\) into a group and any \(r,t\in R\) with \(p(r)=p(t)\), take a regular inverse \(r'\) of \(r\). The inverse identities imply
\[
p(r')=p(r)^{-1},\qquad
\alpha(r')=\alpha(r)^{-1}.
\]
Hence \(p(r't)=0\). Purity makes \(r't\) idempotent, so
\[
1=\alpha(r't)=\alpha(r)^{-1}\alpha(t).
\]
Thus \(\alpha\) is constant on the fibers of \(p\), and every group homomorphism factors through \(p\). Since \(p\) itself is onto a group, it is the least group quotient.

Accordingly, \(R\) satisfies the E-unitary hypothesis in precisely the least-group-quotient-pure sense carried by the packet.

## 7. Completely regular images with congruential \(\mathcal H\)

**Disposition: SUPPORTED from the frozen source and COLL imports, without the 1987 theorem.**  
**References:** S1 LF135–153.

Let \(T=\phi(S)\) be completely regular and suppose \(\mathcal H\) is a semigroup congruence.

Every \(t\in T\) lies in a subgroup, and its square is idempotent. Thus \(t^2\) is that subgroup’s identity, so \(t^3=t\) and \(t\mathrel{\mathcal H}t^2\).

For \(t,u\in T\), congruentiality gives
\[
tu\mathrel{\mathcal H}t^2u^2.
\]
The product \(t^2u^2\) is idempotent because \(E(T)\) is a band. Also \((tu)^2\) is the idempotent in the subgroup containing \(tu\). Uniqueness of the idempotent in an \(\mathcal H\)-class yields
\[
(tu)^2=t^2u^2.
\]

Consequently
\[
\Psi:R\longrightarrow E(T)\times C_2,\qquad
\Psi(t,h)=(t^2,h)
\]
is a homomorphism over \(p\).

Its injectivity is correctly justified separately by grade:

- **Grade zero:** purity makes \(t\) idempotent, so \(t^2=t\).
- **Grade one:** if \(t^2=u^2=e\), complete regularity places \(t,u\) in the same maximal subgroup with identity \(e\). Since \((t,1)(u,1)=(tu,0)\in R\), purity makes \(tu\) idempotent. Within that subgroup, \(tu=e\). Together with \(t^2=e\), this gives \(u=t\).
- Different grades cannot have equal \(\Psi\)-images.

The composite \(\Psi\circ(S\to R)\) lands in a band-by-\(C_2\) direct product over \(q\). COLL identifies its \(U,V\) images; injectivity of \(\Psi\) gives \(\phi(U)=\phi(V)\).

The role of each hypothesis is explicit: complete regularity supplies the local subgroups, orthodoxy supplies the idempotence of \(t^2u^2\), and congruential \(\mathcal H\) supplies the square-map multiplicativity. The argument does **not** establish the exclusion for all completely regular orthodox images.

### Rectangular groups and Clifford semigroups

The named subclasses satisfy the hypotheses:

- In a rectangular group, \(\mathcal H\) is the kernel of projection onto its rectangular-band coordinates.
- In a Clifford semigroup, \(\mathcal H\) is the group-fiber congruence over its semilattice of groups.

If one starts with a homomorphism into such an ambient host, the actual image is finite because \(S\) is finite. Its nonempty intersection with each ambient group fiber is a finite subsemigroup of a group, hence a subgroup. Thus the actual image remains completely regular, and its \(\mathcal H\)-classes are the corresponding group fibers, with the stated congruence.

The exclusions therefore apply to those hosts without silently extending to all completely regular orthodox hosts.
