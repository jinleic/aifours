All calculations below are analytic identities, not finite-path simulation.

1. Opposite signs, zero noise, exact strict gap for ALL inputs.
Take n=p=1, P=0, D=1, b=1, c=-1, sigma=0, T=2, h=1, S=1. For each 0<=s<=1, K_s=1_[s,s+1) in L2[0,2]. Thus C(s)=ell0(s)=1 and Gamma=H=1. Split output into its [0,1] and shifted [1,2] pieces. For every path of every L2 input w these pieces are Vw and V*w, where (Vw)(t)=integral_0^t w(s)ds and (V*w)(t)=integral_t^1 w(s)ds.
The normal operator J=V*V+VV* has integral kernel 1-|s-u|. Therefore
  ||J||_HS^2 = 2 integral_0^1 (1-d)^3 dd = 1/2,
  ||output||_2^2 <= ||J||_op ||w||_2^2 <= (1/sqrt(2)) ||w||_2^2,
  E||output||_2 <= 2^(-1/4) E||w||_2.
The bound is pathwise, so it holds even when w is progressive and only E1-integrable. It proves the true gain is strictly below Gamma=1 in this specialization. The deterministic w=1 is admissible and yields ||w||=1, ||output||^2=integral_0^1[t^2+(1-t)^2]dt=2/3, giving a positive-gain lower bound sqrt(2/3). This is not an assertion of the exact operator norm.

2. Opposite signs with genuinely random post-delay cancellation.
Keep all parameters of 1 except sigma=1. Before s+1, K_s(s+v)=nu(s+v,s). After s+1, write X=nu(s+1,s), Y_v=nu(s+1+v,s+1); the independent increments give K_s(s+1+v)=Y_v(X-1), EX=1, EX^2=e, EY_v^2=e^v. Consequently ell0(s)^2=1 and, directly integrating over s and v,
  H^2 = integral_0^1 e^v dv + (e-1) integral_0^1(1-v)e^v dv
      = (e-1)+(e-1)(e-2) = (e-1)^2.
The delayed MEAN cancels completely, but its second moment is nonzero. In particular 1<=Gamma<=e-1; Gamma is not identified with the physical gain. Setting h=0 instead combines b+c=0 and makes the whole output identically zero even for sigma=1.

3. Vector output and partial destructive interference.
Take n=p=2, P=0, D=I, b=(1,0), c=(-1,1), sigma=1, T=2, h=1, S=1. The independent block X above has E||Xb+c||^2=E[(X-1)^2+1]=e. The early contribution and delayed contribution therefore yield
  ell0(s)^2=1+(1-s)=2-s,
  H^2=(e-1)+e(e-2)=e^2-e-1,
  sqrt(3/2)<=Gamma<=sqrt(e^2-e-1).
This checks the candidate's positive square plus variance term with opposite tap directions; evaluating only the squared mean in the noisy delayed region would miss e-1.

4. Invisible immediate tap with visible delayed tap.
Take n=2, p=1, P=0, D=(1,0), b=(0,1), c=(1,0), sigma=0, T=2, h=1, S=1. Then b is invisible, c visible, K_s=1_[s+1,2], ell0(s)^2=C(s)^2=1-s, and Gamma^2=H^2=integral_0^1(1-s)ds=1/2. The nonzero deterministic input w=1 gives ||z||^2=1/3.
