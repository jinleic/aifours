## 7. Elementary binary obstruction

There is no pure four-qubit AME state. For completeness, trace out one qubit. Its three-qubit marginal has all one- and two-qubit marginals maximally mixed, so in the Pauli expansion rho=(I+M)/8, where M is a real linear combination of weight-three Pauli strings. Purity and a maximally mixed fourth qubit give rho^2=rho/2, hence M^2=3I+2M. A product of two weight-three strings has weight three only when they differ in all three local positions; those strings anticommute, so their anticommutator contributes zero. Thus M^2 has no weight-three part, forcing M=0, which contradicts M^2=3I. This is a written elementary argument, not an imported no-OLS or a finite execution.

A closed binary sector means two symbols per party, all four pair values E in every physical pair, and every extension of those six pair-value sets inside that same sector. Each of its three 4-by-4 flattenings is then an actual unitary block of U. Dividing that common binary tensor by 2 would give the forbidden state. Pair occupancy in one cut alone is NOT enough; each use below establishes all-six-pair closure.
