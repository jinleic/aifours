## 2. Shared lemmas reconstructed for this slice

### 2.1 Actual indices, without subgroup normality

At C LF37–52, set V_i=pi(U_i), K_i=U_i intersect N. Since N is normal, NU_i is a subgroup. The map from the set of left K_i-cosets in N to the set of left U_i-cosets in NU_i, sending xK_i to xU_i, is well-defined, injective, and surjective. Also [G:NU_i]=[Q:V_i]. Index multiplicativity therefore gives

n_i=[G:U_i]=[Q:V_i][N:K_i]=a_i b_i.

Since n_i is finite, a_i and b_i are finite. No finiteness of the cosets or subgroup sets is inferred. If d_A=gcd(a_i,a_j) and d_B=gcd(b_i,b_j), then d_A d_B divides both a_i b_i and a_j b_j. Thus (1.2) is divisibility, including when d_A and d_B share prime factors. **SUPPORTED.**

### 2.2 Common fibers are actual left cosets

At C LF54–61, if q lies in A_i, choose t with pi(t)=q and x_i in C_i intersect Nt. Because x_i belongs to C_i, this intersection equals x_i K_i: an element x_i u has the same quotient image as x_i exactly when u belongs to U_i intersect N. Right translation by the same t inverse gives

(C_i intersect Nt)t^{-1}=(x_i t^{-1})(tK_i t^{-1}).

Here x_i t^{-1} belongs to N, and conjugation by t is an automorphism of N, preserving [N:K_i]=b_i. Applying one common bijection to disjoint original slices preserves disjointness. This works simultaneously for every label over a common q and does not require normal U_i. **SUPPORTED.**

### 2.3 Pairwise-coprime subgroup indices force simultaneous intersection

At C LF63–72, put H=intersection H_i. The natural map of left-coset sets X/H into the product of the X/H_i is injective: equality in every coordinate means the relative element belongs to every H_i. ## 3. Obligation C.1 — exact all-pair cross-gcd identity

The definitions at C LF97–103 are

d_A=gcd(a_i,a_j), d_B=gcd(b_i,b_j),

c_ij=gcd(a_i/d_A,b_j/d_B) gcd(a_j/d_A,b_i/d_B).

**(2.1) is an identity for every four positive integers; no coprimality between quotient and kernel factors is needed.** Fix a prime l and write alpha=v_l(a_i), alpha'=v_l(a_j), beta=v_l(b_i), beta'=v_l(b_j). Set x=alpha-alpha' and y=beta-beta', with z_+=max(z,0). The surplus exponent in gcd(a_i b_i,a_j b_j) after removing d_A d_B is

min(alpha+beta,alpha'+beta') - min(alpha,alpha') - min(beta,beta')

= x_+ + y_+ - (x+y)_+

= min(x_+,(-y)_+) + min((-x)_+,y_+).

For x,y of the same weak sign both sides of the last equality vanish. For x>=0>=y both equal min(x,-y); for y>=0>=x both equal min(-x,y). These cases also cover zero differences. The last expression is exactly the sum of the valuations of the two cross-gcd factors defining c_ij. Equality at every prime proves

gcd(a_i b_i,a_j b_j)=d_A d_B c_ij.

This reconstructs, rather than merely repeats, the valuation step at C LF105–111.
