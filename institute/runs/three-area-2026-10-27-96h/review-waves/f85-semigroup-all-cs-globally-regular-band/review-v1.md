## Independent proof of the finite subsemigroup lemma

This proves `gauges:7–17` more directly, rather than treating the author's verifier as a proof. Let nonempty finite `T ⊆ M[K;I,Λ;P]` be a subsemigroup of a completely simple semigroup. The occupied row set `J` and column set `M` form a full rectangle: multiplying an element in a given row by one in a given column occupies the cross-cell. Each occupied cell is a finite nonempty subsemigroup of its ambient H-class group and hence a subgroup containing that cell's ambient identity.

Choose occupied `i0,λ0` and, for this proof only, normalize the **ambient Rees coordinates** so `p_(λ0,i)=p_(λ,i0)=1` for all occupied indices. Let `H` be T's subgroup in the base cell. Since cell identities belong to T, `E_i=(i,1,λ0)` and `F_λ=(i0,1,λ)` belong to T. Now `F_λ E_i=(i0,p_(λ,i),λ0)`, so every occupied sandwich entry `p_(λ,i)` lies in H. If `(i,g,λ)∈T`, then `F_λ (i,g,λ) E_i=(i0,p_(λ,i) g p_(λ,i),λ0)∈H`; the two inverses of `p_(λ,i)` show `g∈H`. Conversely `E_i (i0,h,λ0) F_λ=(i,h,λ)` belongs to T for every `h∈H`. Therefore T is **exactly** `M[H;J,M;P|_(M×J)]`, a completely simple Rees matrix semigroup. This also identifies its R/L classes with occupied ambient rows/columns. It uses finite nonempty T and does not extend to arbitrary infinite subsemigroups.
