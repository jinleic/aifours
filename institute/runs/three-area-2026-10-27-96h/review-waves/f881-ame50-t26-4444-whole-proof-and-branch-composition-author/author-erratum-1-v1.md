## Mathematical repairs and clarifications

**E1 (v1 §1, proof of (2); MINOR, load-bearing wording).** v1 omits why the two rows must intersect at all. Replace its sentence beginning “For each direction” by: Fix a direction j with `N_j(x)={y_0,y_1}`. Every E word with i-symbol x and j-symbol `y_0` or `y_1` has its `(k,l)` coordinates in the four-element set `N_k(x)×N_l(x)`. The two multiplicities are at least two and sum to five, hence are 2 and 3. The actual unitary rows `(x,y_0),(x,y_1)` of the `ij|kl` flattening therefore have supports of sizes 2 and 3 in the same four columns and must intersect. One common nonzero would give a nonzero inner product, so they share at least two columns. Each shared column is a cube edge in direction j. Hence the five vertices induce at least two edges per direction, at least six in all. The remaining count in v1 stands. S2|23 contains this support-size reason.

**E2 (v1 §2, omitted small cases).** Add: a residual component of order one is impossible because every residual line has degree at least two, and a component of order two is full with excess zero. Order at most three permits excess at most three, so an excess-four component has order at least four; (3) bounds it by six. In order six, a low row's columns are its twins of degree at least three, so the low columns meet only high rows. In the Y-side `2334` case with degree-three twins, the full degree-four row meets both twins, so their neighborhood is the low row, the full row and one degree-three row; the other degree-three row avoids them. With degree-four twins, the two remaining columns have degree two each and both contain the full row; each degree-three row uses both twins and exactly one remaining column, and capacity forces the two degree-three rows into different remaining columns. These are the missing steps behind v1's terse sentences. The order-four excess-three exclusion is correct but not load-bearing for t=26: an excess-three component would require a remaining excess-one component, which is impossible. S2|27 also asserts “excess three is a full 3-by-3” without the order-four argument; this is non-load-bearing for the same reason.

**E3 (v1 §4, outside-a extensions; MINOR, load-bearing justification).** Insert before “Each high-high pair among B,C,D has total multiplicity three”: An E word with A-symbol a and high B-symbol b lies in the full F row `(a,b)` of `AB|CD`, which has exactly four entries, all on `H_C×H_D`. Since AC and AD are also F with full high rows at a, the same holds with C or D in place of B. Thus **every E word at a with at least one high B, C or D coordinate lies in cube (4).** The cube supplies exactly two extensions of each high-high pair among B,C,D, and no other word at a does. Each such pair is a high value of a T system and has total multiplicity three, so it has exactly one extension whose A-symbol differs from a. S2|77 states this conclusion without the italicized reason.

**E4 (v1 §4, mates of an all-high outside word).** Replace “These three mates are distinct, since a high-high pair has only one outside-a extension. The four triples form a binary parity class” by: Let tildes denote the other high symbols. If the mate on line `(p,b)` were `(p,b,c,d̃)` or `(p,b,c̃,d)`, the high pair `(b,c)` or `(b,d)` would have two outside-a extensions. Hence that mate is `(p,b,c̃,d̃)`; similarly the other mates are `(p,b̃,c,d̃)` and `(p,b̃,c̃,d)`. The four BCD triples `(b,c,d),(b,c̃,d̃),(b̃,c,d̃),(b̃,c̃,d)` form a binary parity class, and every two agree in exactly one coordinate. For two triples agreeing at `B=b`, the row `(p,b)` of `AB|CD` has exactly those two entries, while row `(a,b)` is supported on all of `H_C×H_D`. Therefore their actual inner product is exactly `z_t+z_{t'}`. S2|79 likewise says “distinct” where this both-coordinates property is what is used.
