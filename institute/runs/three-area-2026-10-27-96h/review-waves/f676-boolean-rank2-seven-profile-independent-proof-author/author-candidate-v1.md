## F. Further necessary location constraint from the same seven profiles

**Location theorem.** Exactly one of the following two necessary alternatives occurs:

1. If the three `a_d` are distinct, the four `delta_i` are the four distinct elements of H. The map `delta:M_1 -> H` is an affine isomorphism with linear part `A:M_0 -> H`, and for all distinct nonzero `d,d' in H`,

   `A(a_d+a_d')=d+d'`.                                                   (F2)

   Every one of the six profiles whose restriction to H is nonzero has four ±1 entries: two in ell=1 graph cosets and two in the active ell=0 cosets. The actual four signs in each profile must still have product -1.

2. If two cells, with directions `d_1,d_2`, lie in one L-coset a and the third, direction `d_3=d_1+d_2`, lies in a different L-coset b, the four exceptions consist of two copies of one element and two copies of its translate by `d_3`. For the unique nonzero alpha with `alpha(d_1)=alpha(d_2)=1`, one of its two L-extensions has one ell=0 coefficient ±2 and no other entries, and the other has four ell=1 coefficients ±1. For each of the other two nonzero alpha, both extensions instead have four ±1 entries, two on either side of ell.

*Proof.* An ell=0 L-coset contains two H-cells, and there are three occupied cells, so the only location multiplicities are 1+1+1 or 2+1. For an extension eta of nonzero alpha and `s=eta(e)`, (F1) gives the ith graph-coset contribution

`Z_{m_i}(eta)=[epsilon_i (-1)^{eta(G_i)}/2] * [(-1)^{alpha(delta_i)}+(-1)^s]`. (F3)

For arbitrary distinct nonzero `d,d'`, there is a unique nonzero alpha taking value 1 on both; these are precisely its active cells. The four nonzero profile positions form an affine plane, so the XOR of its two graph positions equals `a_d+a_d'`. The two graph exceptions have the same alpha value. In case 2, the unique alpha nonzero on both `d_1,d_2` kills `d_3`. Its two active cells lie in the same L-coset. The support-flat condition pairs the two graph cosets with equal alpha value by their difference `a+b`. Since these alpha distinguish the two exception values, delta is constant on each `<a+b>` pair and takes the two different values on the two pairs. It is the stated affine map onto the line.
