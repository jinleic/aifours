## Matching, forced boxes and bundles

Let `c_ij` encode the two high pair values `x_i xor x_j = c_ij`. The source exhausts all 64 labelled six-edge patterns. For each it constructs, from the three cuts, the actual forced binary words and checks the shared-single-low-neighbor condition for the two high rows. The compatible patterns are required to be exactly those with

`c01 xor c23 = c02 xor c13 = c03 xor c12`.

There are 16 compatible assignments, eight in each switching orbit. The orbit invariant is **triangle parity** `p=c01 xor c02 xor c12`, not the common complementary-pair value. Setting the switch at vertex 0 to 0 and the other three switches to `c0i xor p` makes every edge parity equal to `p`. The proposed check requires the actual forced word set to become all eight even binary words when `p=0`, or exactly the six weight-two words when `p=1`. It also rejects a concrete incompatible sign pattern for the actual multiple-low-neighbor reason. This is a necessary local incidence test, not an enumeration of global tensors.

For the p=0 box, the source checks every one of the 14 possible complementary pair values other than the two high values, for each of the six high pairs. The only shared-low values that do not overflow an already-saturated low binary pair must have both symbols nonhigh; a mixed high/nonhigh candidate is a reason-specific negative control. It then enumerates all `2^12=4096` assignments of the two nonhigh labels to the six shared-low complementary values. For both the even box and the no-bundle frustrated case, it independently compares the symbolic equal-low-neighbor condition to actual degree-three neighbor sets in the constructed word incidence. The expected 16 surviving assignments have one common nonhigh symbol per party. In the even case, each high symbol then occurs seven times and the common nonhigh symbol six times; under the separately stated occurrence/degree assumptions, the four remaining E words have at most one distinct all-other-low word available. In the frustrated no-bundle case each high symbol appears six times, and its single remaining occurrence would have to cover three equal-bit low pairs, hence be an excluded constant word. These are **conditional local consequences**: they are not an unrestricted proof that the entire f732 branch is correct.

For p=1, all 8 matching gauges, 4 nonhigh-party choices, both majority values and both nonhigh labels are covered. Each of the resulting 16 bundles in a gauge has three mixed-three-high words; closure explicitly flips the two bits of each high row while retaining its complementary pair. The source requires all 384 seed closures and all 960 pair-of-distinct-bundles/gauge comparisons. A second bundle is rejected by an explicitly overfull high pair, not by a label convention. It also checks all eight one-/three-weight binary words: any high row would force a mate, giving three occurrences of a low complementary pair whose allowed multiplicity is two. With the six balanced words present, the row-degree-two/no-one-overlap condition forces the other constant if either constant occurs, giving the already analyzed even box.

The one-bundle check covers every missing party, majority bit and nonhigh label. It exhausts all 256 possible candidate words to bound, by actual already-saturated low pairs and exclusion of the all-constant word, how many of the three outstanding equal-bit pairs one further word can cover: at most one for the majority bit and at most two for the other. It also examines all 64 assignments of the nonhigh labels in the three shared-low cells of the six D-high extensions. ## Separately scoped written larger-component reductions

These are written arguments for a hypothetical connected complex unitary-support component with minimum degree two, no singleton pair-overlap and the distinct-low-line consequence above. They are not exhaustive computational checks of larger arbitrary matrices. A degree-two row has a pair of twin columns: every other row meets both or neither. In a connected component of order greater than two both twin columns have degree at least three; distinct low rows have disjoint twin pairs. Hence if order is m and excess e, at least `m-e` rows have degree two, at most `e` columns have degree at least three, and `m <= e+floor(e/2)`.

- **m=5, e=4:** there are one or two low rows. With one, the other four rows have degree three. Its twin columns have equal degree three or four; at least one high row misses both and therefore uses all three other columns, whereas a high row meeting both twins uses exactly one of those three, yielding an impermissible one-overlap. With two low rows, four twin columns have degree three and the fifth column degree two. Their neighborhoods on the three high rows are three 2-subsets; no-one-overlap forces these 2-subsets to coincide, leaving the third high row isolated.
- **m=6, e=4:** exactly two low rows and four high rows occur; their disjoint twin pairs are four degree-three columns, with two degree-two columns remaining. The low columns have disjoint two-high-row neighborhoods, since a one-overlap is forbidden and identical neighborhoods would isolate a 2-by-2 unitary block. They partition the four high rows. Each high twin column must use one complete part of this partition; the two twin pairs cannot both use the same part without stranding the other high rows, so they use opposite parts and the component splits.
- **m=6, e=5:** there are one or two low rows, and likewise for columns. With one low row, all other rows have degree three. Its twin columns must both have degree three: degree at least four would leave too little column excess for the other required high columns. They meet two high rows, each with one additional column; any other high row meeting that column gives a one-overlap, so minimum column degree forces the two high rows to share the same additional column. Those three rows and columns are an isolated component. With two low rows, one low column is excluded by transposing the previous case, so there are two. The four high column degrees are `4,3,3,3`; the low-row twin pairs would partition them into two equal-degree pairs, impossible.

No general m=5/e5 exclusion is asserted here: the source addresses only the explicitly degree-3/degree-3 structured subcase above. No m=7/e5 classification is supplied. Nor do these written reductions decide which surviving small support graphs admit phases, fit the same tensor across all three cuts, or occur in an AME state.
