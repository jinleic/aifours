## 4. A fixed, integer, complete seven-map matrix pencil

\[
 D=\begin{bmatrix}F_1B&R_1&0\\F_2B&0&R_2\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The projection of ker D to the B-coordinates is an isomorphism onto the whole lowest space: injectivity holds because R_1,R_2 have independent columns, and the two equations are exactly the quotient-lowest conditions. Its B-projections give integral representatives z_1,...,z_m of **all** lowest copies. Every selection uses only the fixed r=3 free quotient, so the same z_j work for every s>=4.

In the grade-three ordered tensor coordinates, define the complete rectangular pencil M(t), t=s-4, whose j-th column is F_3 z_j. This is the actual map L_(a,b) -> L_3(V)_(a,b), including every source copy and every target tensor coordinate. It is an **integer affine** matrix M(t)=M_0+tM_1. Indeed, for a letter u let D_u act on a letter of degree (i,j) by
\[
D_x=3i+sj-2,\quad D_a=-3E_1,\quad D_b=-sE_2,\quad
D_c=-s[E_1,E_2],\quad D_d=3[E_1,E_2],\quad D_e=D_f=D_g=0,

and extend by derivations, using F_3[u,v]=D_uv-D_vu. In particular F_3 q_3=4(r-3)[x,[x,a]]=0 at r=3 and F_3 commutes with E_1,E_2; therefore F_3 kills **every** relation column for every s, so the quotient map is well-defined. For each type and each m-element set I of grade-three tensor rows put
\[
\Delta_I(t)=\det M(t)_{I,*}\in\mathbb Z[t],\quad \deg\Delta_I\le m.

\[
\Delta_{I_*}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}c_kt^k,\quad
c_k=\sum_{\substack{K\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\\|K|=k}}
\det\big((M_1)_{I_*,j}\text{ for }j\in K;\ (M_0)_{I_*,j}\text{ for }j\notin K\big),

where columns remain in their original order. These are exact, basis-dependent integer coefficients. Their actual entries, I_*, c_k, signs, and roots have **not** been computed here.

The corresponding exact positivity expression is
\[
P(t)=\det(M(t)^TM(t))=\sum_{|I|=m}\Delta_I(t)^2.

For real t, P(t)>0 iff the whole map is injective. ## 5. Proven conditional finite-exception and explicit-tail bound

Assume H4. Since each chosen Δ_{I_*} is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most m, a failure of injectivity for that type can occur at at most m distinct values of s. Summing the seven m's gives at most 36 possible exceptional integer s>=4. If the independent singlet proof in §3 is accepted, its type has no exceptional s and the bound is 35. This argument uses one exact full-rank point to establish a nonzero polynomial; it does **not** infer uniformity by interpolation from two points.

The integer construction also gives an explicit upper bound beyond which no exception is possible. Set
\[
H=8^{17},\qquad K=8192!\,H^{8192},\qquad C=8!\,(2^{22}K)^8.
\]
The following is an intentionally loose theoretical bound, not the result of executing an algorithm:

* A right-nested length-four bracket has tensor ℓ1 norm at most 8. Each E_1 or E_2 changes its tensor ℓ1 norm by at most a factor 8: four letter positions and letter-action coefficients of absolute value at most 2. Thus every seed translate of length at most 16 has norm at most H. An F_1 or F_2 image of a selected right-nested bracket has norm at most 64<=H. Hence every integer entry of D has absolute value at most H.
* There are at most 8^4=4096 ordered tensor rows in each of the two lowering blocks, so rank(D)<=8192. Select an invertible rank-sized pivot submatrix, and for each free variable solve by cofactors, multiplying by its determinant. Every resulting nullspace coordinate has absolute value at most K by the Leibniz determinant bound. The projection to B is injective as shown in §4. Thus the selected integer z_j are independent and have at most 4096 coefficients of absolute value <=K in the chosen right-nested B.
* In the substitution s=4+t, the ℓ1 operator norm on letters of each coefficient (constant and t) of every D_u is at most 16. For a nested bracket of lengths 2,3,4, the corresponding coefficient norms of F_3 are at most 32,128,448, respectively: use F_3[u,v]=D_uv-D_vu, then the derivation recursion; at length four the two contributions are at most 3·16·4 and 2·128. Thus each entry of M(0) is bounded by 4096·448·K<2^{22}K.
* Since m<=8, the nonzero integer c_0=Δ_{I_*}(0) obeys |c_0|<=m!(2^{22}K)^m<=C. If an integer t>=1 is a root of Δ_{I_*}, then t divides c_0, because its other polynomial terms are all divisible by t. Consequently t<=C.

It follows that for every integer s>=C+5, i.e. t>=C+1, every one of the seven complete F_3 maps is injective. At the imported complete type exclusions and R cap S_3=0, a nonzero grade-four radical would contain a lowest vector of one of these seven types, contradicting injectivity. This establishes the conditional **entire-grade-four tail**, not a global J=tau theorem. Even without accepting §3, the upper bound 36 and this tail remain valid under H4 and the named imports. If H4 is not independently established, the elementary dichotomy remains: each fixed type's minors are either all identically zero or else have only finitely many common parameter zeros; the asserted tail and exception count are then not yet established.
