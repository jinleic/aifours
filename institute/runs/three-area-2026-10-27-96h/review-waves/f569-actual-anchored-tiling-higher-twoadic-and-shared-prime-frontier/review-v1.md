## 2. Actual-factor reduction: retaining the consuming interface

Absorb \(u\) into \(C\), so that the hypothesized factorization is

\[
P=\{0,d\}\oplus C.
\]

Because both sums are direct, the original tiling implies

\[
\{0,d\}\oplus C\oplus Q=\mathbb Z_n.
\]

Consequently

\[
T=C\oplus Q
\]

is an actual set, not an unweighted image of a multiset, and

\[
\{0,d\}\oplus T=\mathbb Z_n.
\]

Let

\[
g=\gcd(d,n).
\]

Write \(t(x)=1_T(x)\). Exact directness gives, for every \(x\in\mathbb Z_n\),

\[
t(x)+t(x-d)=1.
\]

Therefore

\[
t(x)=t(x-2d).
\]

The cycles generated by \(d\) have length \(n/g\). Alternation around a cycle requires this length to be even. Thus

\[
2g\mid n.
\]

Moreover,

\[
\gcd(2d,n)=2g,
\]

so \(T\) is a union of complete residue classes modulo \(2g\). There is a set

\[
V\subseteq\mathbb Z_{2g}
\]

such that

\[
T=\{x\in\mathbb Z_n:x\bmod 2g\in V\},
\]

and

\[
V\sqcup(V+g)=\mathbb Z_{2g},\qquad |V|=g.
\]

In particular, \(V\) cannot contain two residues differing by \(g\).

### Orientation normalization

The actual anchor \(0\in P\) implies that the projection of \(C\) modulo \(2g\) contains either \(0\) or \(g\). If necessary, replace the two-point factor by its reversed translate and translate \(C\) oppositely. This leaves \(P\) unchanged and allows the normalization

\[
0\in \operatorname{supp}(C\bmod 2g).
\]

Both orientations of the two-point factor project to \(\{0,g\}\).

Define

\[
S_g=\{0,1,7,13,14\}\bmod 2g.
\]

Since the normalized projected \(C\) contains \(0\),

\[
S_g\subseteq V.
\]

The original head anchors also imply that, for each \(a\in\{0,3,8,11\}\), the projected support of \(C\) contains at least one of

\[
a,\quad a-g\pmod{2g}.
\]

These are actual consequences of the original factorization, not an assumption that the projected head itself tiles the quotient.

### Exact projected coefficients

Put

\[
c_i=\#\{c\in C:c\equiv i\pmod{2g}\},
\]

\[
q_j=\#\{q\in Q:q\equiv j\pmod{2g}\},
\qquad
L=\frac{n}{2g}.
\]

Because \(C\oplus Q=T\) is direct and \(T\) fills each occupied residue class completely,

\[
\boxed{\sum_i c_iq_{s-i}=L\,1_V(s)}
\qquad(s\in\mathbb Z_{2g}).
\]

Thus the consuming-side equation retains all projection multiplicities. This weighted equation is essential in the cases \(g=8,10,11\).

## 4. The cases \(g=3\) and \(g=5\)

### Case \(g=3\)

Here

\[
S_3=\{0,1,2\}\subseteq\mathbb Z_6.
\]

Since \(|V|=3\), necessarily

\[
V=S_3.
\]

Every projected \(C\)-residue \(c\) satisfies

\[
S_3+c\subseteq V=S_3.
\]

The only translation stabilizing \(\{0,1,2\}\) in \(\mathbb Z_6\) is zero. Hence \(C\) is supported only at residue zero modulo six.

The projected head is therefore supported in \(\{0,3\}\). This contradicts the actual head anchor \(8\equiv2\pmod6\).

### Case \(g=5\)

Here

\[
S_5=\{0,1,3,4,7\}\subseteq\mathbb Z_{10}.
\]

It has five elements and contains no antipodal pair, so

\[
V=S_5.
\]

Again, every projected \(C\)-residue must stabilize \(S_5\).

Its stabilizer is trivial. Indeed, a nontrivial stabilizer would have order five, since its order divides \(|S_5|=5\). Then \(S_5\) would be a parity coset, contrary to its containing both \(0\) and \(1\).

Thus \(C\) is supported only at zero modulo ten. The head is supported in \(\{0,5\}\), contradicting the actual head anchor \(3\).

## 5. The case \(g=8\)

Work in \(\mathbb Z_{16}\), with

\[
S=\{0,1,7,13,14\}.
\]

The projected \(C\)-support contains zero. The head anchor \(3\) requires \(3\) or \(11\) in that support.

Residue \(11\) is impossible: \(11+13=8\pmod{16}\), whereas \(0\in S\subseteq V\); these are antipodal. Hence

\[
c_0>0,\qquad c_3>0.
\]

It follows that \(V\) contains

\[
S\cup(S+3)=\{0,1,3,4,7,10,13,14\}.
\]

This union has eight elements, so it equals \(V\).

The resulting support restrictions are

\[
\{c:S+c\subseteq V\}=\{0,3\},
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{supp}(Q\bmod16)
\subseteq V\cap(V-3)
=\{0,1,4,7,10,13,14\}.
\]

Apply the exact coefficient equation at residues \(3,14,0,1\):

\[
L=c_3q_0,
\]

\[
L=c_0q_{14},
\]

\[
L=c_0q_0+c_3q_{13}>c_0q_0,
\]

\[
L=c_0q_1+c_3q_{14}>c_3q_{14}.
\]

The strict inequalities use the actual tail anchors \(13\) and \(1\), together with \(c_0,c_3>0\).

Comparing the first and third equations gives

\[
c_3>c_0.
\]

Comparing the second and fourth gives

\[
c_0>c_3.
\]

Contradiction.
