## Single-pair reduction and exact case dispatch

Fix an arbitrary admissible pair `(ψ,B)`. For each of the three balanced cuts form the `36×36` flattening of the **same** tensor `U=6ψ`. The AME marginals give `UU†=I_36` in each cut; as the matrices are square, their column Gram matrices are also the identity. A unit-modulus entry of a unitary exhausts its row and its column. Thus a maximal entry of this same U is a singleton in its row and column in **every** cut, and any two distinct maximal entries occupy distinct rows and distinct columns in each cut. Write `P` for those entries, `t=|P|`, and `E` for the other nonzero coefficients. Delete the t singleton rows and columns in any cut. What remains is an `n×n` unitary with `n=36−t`, and it has no singleton line: any such line in a unitary would have a unit-modulus entry, already in P. Hence it has at least `2n` nonzeros and

`N = t + |E| ≥ t + 2(36−t) = 72−t`, with `e := |E|−2n = N+t−72 ≥ 0`.

These quantities refer to the **same state and basis** in every case; one cannot choose a different basis, tensor, saturated set P, pair graph, or completion per cut. Pairwise row/column orthogonality also forbids two lines having exactly one common nonzero position. We use this only as a necessary condition, never as a claim that any graph mask is realizable by complex phases.
