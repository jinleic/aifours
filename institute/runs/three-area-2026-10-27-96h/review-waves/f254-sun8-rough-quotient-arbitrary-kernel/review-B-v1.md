## Written attack: arbitrary subgroups and actual fibres

For an arbitrary group `X`, finite-index subgroups `H_i`, and `H=intersection H_i`, the left-coset map `xH -> (xH_i)` is injective because equality in each coordinate says `y^-1 x` lies in every `H_i`. For each `i`, `[X:H_i]` divides `[X:H]`; if these positive indices are pairwise coprime, their product divides `[X:H]`. Injection gives the opposite cardinality inequality, so equality holds and the map is onto. Thus all the prescribed **left** cosets meet simultaneously. This uses only finite index, not normality, commutativity, or a global Helly property. Repeated subgroups are allowed; under pairwise coprimality repetitions with non-unit index cannot occur, whereas any number of copies of `X` can.

For `pi:G->G/N`, `a_i=[G/N:pi(U_i)]`, `K_i=U_i intersection N`, and `b_i=[N:K_i]`, the subgroup `NU_i` exists because `N` is normal. Index multiplication and the left-set bijection `N/K_i -> NU_i/U_i` yield `[G:U_i]=a_i b_i`, including infinite `G` and nonnormal `U_i`. If the actual original-index gcds are at most `T`, then so are the gcds of the `a_i`. Roughness eliminates every prime at most `T` from each `a_i`; any common prime would exceed `T` and force the gcd above `T`. Hence the `a_i` are pairwise coprime, so all projected cosets share a point `q`.

Choose any `t` over `q` and actual `x_i in g_i U_i intersection Nt`. Then `g_i U_i intersection Nt = x_i K_i`, and right translation gives `(x_i t^-1)(t K_i t^-1)` inside `N`. The conjugation is essential and its index is `b_i`; one common right translation retains disjointness. Since `gcd(b_i,b_j)` divides the corresponding original gcd, all `r+1` kernel cosets would contradict `B_T(N)<=r`. The quotient finite-index/finite-quotient equivalence uses the action on `Q/V`: its finite image has kernel `core_Q(V)`, and `[Q:V]` divides the image order. The reverse implication takes kernels of finite quotients.

### m1 — a literal false finiteness assertion, without a gap in the needed proof

Candidate line 67 says “Each is finite” immediately after defining both sets `A_i`, `K_i` and the three indices. Only `a_i,b_i,n_i` must be finite. For instance let `D=(Q,+)`, `G=D x C2 x D`, `N=D x C2 x {0}`, and `U=D x {0} x D`. The two `U`-cosets have index 2 and are disjoint; the quotient `G/N` is the infinite rough group `D`, each projected coset is all of `D`, and `U intersection N` is infinite `D`, although the indices are finite. This structural example intentionally does not satisfy the proposed `r=1` kernel bound, and is not a counterexample to the theorem. Replace the sentence with “The indices `a_i`, `b_i`, and `n_i` are finite”; nothing else in the displayed proof requires the sets to be finite.

## Examples and independent constructions

### Non-Helly rough quotient

For each prime `p>7`, the three subsets `x=0`, `y=0`, and `x+y=1` are actual affine subgroup cosets of `F_p^2`, each of index `p`. Their pairwise intersections are respectively `(0,0)`, `(0,1)`, and `(1,0)`; the triple intersection is empty. Every quotient order divides `p^2`, so the group is 7-rough. However, `gcd(p,p)=p>7`, so these three index labels cannot simultaneously occur in a bad original family. Thus this is a counterexample to unrestricted 3-Helly, not to the transfer.

### Infinite kernel, exact bound, and the true split status

Let `D=(Q,+)` and `N=D x C7`. If `U<=N` has finite index, then `D intersection U` has finite index in `D`; if that index is `m`, multiplication by `m` is onto `D` and also sends `D` into `D intersection U`, so `D<=U`. Consequently every finite-index subgroup of `N` is the full preimage of either `{0}` or `C7`, and the only cosets are `N` itself or the seven fibres over `C7`. Seven index-7 fibres are disjoint with pairwise gcd exactly 7, so `B_7(N)<=6` is false; eight disjoint nonempty cosets do not exist, so `B_7(N)<=7` is true. This supplies the exact meaning of m2 without assuming an undefined numerical `B_7` convention.

For each `p>7`, the transfer gives `Sun8(G)` for **every** extension `1 -> N -> G -> F_p^2 -> 1`. The displayed direct product is one such example. In fact, m3 is stronger than just a lack of a nonsplit witness: **every extension with this particular kernel and quotient splits**. The automorphism group of `D x C7` is `Q^* x Aut(C7)`: cross-factor homomorphisms vanish because `D` is divisible and torsion-free and `C7` is finite torsion. Neither `Q^*` nor the order-6 group `Aut(C7)` has an element of order `p>7`, so the quotient's conjugation action on the abelian kernel is trivial and `N` is central. Multiplication by `p` is bijective on `N`; adjust lifts of a basis of `F_p^2` by central kernel elements to make their `p`th powers 1. Their commutator is central and has `p`th power 1, hence is 1 because `N` has no `p`-torsion. The lifts therefore generate a complement isomorphic to `F_p^2`. Thus this particular universal application is true, but “not just this direct product” should not be presented as evidence of a genuinely nonsplit group in that family.

The finite `p=11` control independently enumerates all 28 subgroups and 2,032 cosets of `C7 x F_11^2`. Its seven index-7 cosets partition the 847 elements. At most one coset in a pairwise-gcd-`<=7` family can have index divisible by 11; no index-1 coset can coexist with a second disjoint coset. Consequently an eight-coset bad family would contain all seven index-7 cosets and a further nonempty coset, which is impossible. This finite product calculation does not replace the written infinite-kernel proof or establish the theorem universally.

### Explicit primes and an actual nonsplit nonabelian application

For m4, take `p=23`, `q=11`. Both are prime and `11 | 22`. Multiplication by 2 on `C23` has order 11: `2^11=1 mod 23`, and no positive smaller exponent through 10 does. Hence `Q=C23 semidirect C11` is nonabelian, of order 253, coprime to 210; all its finite quotient orders divide 253. In particular the candidate's conditional prime pair family is nonempty, although the candidate itself does not supply a witness.

There is also a **nonsplit** extension in the general scope, so this is not a hidden split-extension proof. Let `G=C23 semidirect C121`, with the generator of `C121` acting on `C23` by multiplication by 2. The action is well defined because its order 11 divides 121. The map `(x,y) -> (x, y mod 11)` has kernel `N={(0,11k): k mod 11}`, isomorphic to `C11`, and quotient the nonabelian `Q` above. Every lift of the quotient generator `(0,1)` has form `(0,1+11k)` and eleventh power `(0,11) != 1`; hence no section can send that order-11 element to an element of order dividing 11. Therefore this extension is nonsplit. `N=C11` satisfies `B_7(N)<=1`, hence Sun8. The bounded control checks all 11 such lifts and the elementary action/order facts. It does not enumerate all extensions.

### Roughness is not implied by Sun8

For `T=7`, `r=1`, put `N=1` and `G=Q=C2`. Both the kernel's `B_7<=1` and the quotient's Sun8 property hold (the latter has no eight disjoint nonempty cosets), but `Q` is not 7-rough.
