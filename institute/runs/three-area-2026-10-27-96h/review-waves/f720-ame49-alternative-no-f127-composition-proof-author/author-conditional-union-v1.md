## Universal unitary support bookkeeping

In any balanced flattening, a modulus-one entry in a unitary exhausts its row and column. The \(t\) entries of \(P\) therefore occupy distinct rows and columns. Delete them: the residual is an \(n\times n\) unitary with \(n=36-t\), \(|E|=N-t\) nonzeros, and no singleton row or column: a singleton nonzero in a unitary has modulus one and would belong to \(P\). Hence

\[
 |E|\ge 2n,\qquad N\ge72-t,\qquad e:=|E|-2n=N+t-72\ge0.
\]

The following support facts use ordinary complex row **and** column orthogonality; nonzero summands cannot cancel when two lines meet in exactly one support position.

* If \(e=0\), every line has degree two. Its support components are full \(2\times2\) blocks: a longer degree-two cycle would have two rows with exactly one common column. In particular \(n\) is even.
* If \(e=1\), there is one degree-three row and all other rows have degree two. Every column of that row needs another row; each such row must use two of its three columns. Covering all three requires distinct two-subsets, which meet in exactly one column, impossible. Thus excess one cannot occur.
* If \(e=2\), the row excess is either one degree-four row or two degree-three rows. In the first case, degree-two rows meeting the four-row support pair its four columns into disjoint pairs; columns in different pairs then meet only in the degree-four row, impossible. In the second case the two triples cannot have one common column by orthogonality; disjoint triples give the same three-column covering contradiction, and two common columns would make a low row needed by an exclusive column meet the other triple once. If the triples coincide, column orthogonality and the two available column-excess incidences force exactly one degree-two row on two of those columns, giving one eight-entry \(3\times3\) component. Remaining components have degree two and are full \(2\times2\) blocks. Thus \(n=3+2k\) is odd. These are the support facts also recorded in S6; the reasoning here does not use a new finite enumeration.
