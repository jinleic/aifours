The program exhausts `0<=e<=5` subject to degree at least two on every row and column, no distinct row pair with exactly one common column, no distinct column pair with exactly one common row, and connectedness. It reports **both** the graph-only domain and the subdomain passing the additional actual-unitary twin-isolation necessities in the next section. This is not a test of the existence of amplitudes or of a global tensor. Completeness here concerns this specified finite labeled graph universe; it is not a search over values of `t`, tensors, P sets, phases, or cross-cut gluings.

Rows are `m`-bit integers. The recursive generator visits nondecreasing tuples of row masks with every degree at least two, carrying an exact excess budget of five. It rejects a candidate as soon as it has a singleton overlap with an earlier row and prunes only when even placing an edge in every remaining row could not raise some column degree to two. Neither pruning can remove a target graph. At each leaf it recomputes all six graph conditions, including the column overlaps and full bipartite connectivity. Every matrix with the stated properties has exactly one row-sorted representative for its **labeled columns**. A row multiset with multiplicities `n_s` stands for `m!/product(n_s!)` distinct row labelings; the code sums this exact weight. All column labels are visited, rather than assumed isomorphic, and transposes are included as ordinary labeled matrices. The enumeration emits counts per order/excess, deterministic SHA-256 digests of the encountered row-sorted graphs, and **every twin-filtered row-sorted mask tuple**. ## Twin-isolation necessity and written small-excess analysis

A connected component of an actual unitary support is square: orthonormal rows give rows<=columns and orthonormal columns give columns<=rows. In a component of order greater than two, take a degree-two row incident on columns `c,d`. Every other row meets those columns in both or neither, since one common nonzero gives an uncancellable nonzero inner product. Thus `c,d` have identical neighborhoods. Their common degree is at least three: at degree two the two columns form a square unitary block, exhausting its two rows and splitting off an order-two component. Distinct degree-two rows have disjoint column pairs: intersection in one is forbidden, and intersection in both makes those two rows a square unitary block and isolates them. Transposition supplies the identical statement for degree-two columns. The source applies this extra condition only after all graph-only checks and only for `m>2`. Its failure is a valid unitary exclusion; **passing it is not phase-realizability**.

There are at least `m−e` degree-two rows and at most `e` columns of degree at least three. Their disjoint twin pairs therefore give `m<=e+floor(e/2)` for `m>2`. A connected order-two minimum-degree-two component is full and has `e=0`. The following are written deductions from those support necessities, not outputs of the unrun source.

* `e=1` is impossible. `e=2` forces `m=3`, with one missing cell. `e=3` allows at most `m=4`; at `m=4` there is exactly one low row and one low column, and all other lines have degree three. The low row's two twin columns meet it and only two of the other three rows; the remaining degree-three row avoids them and has only two available columns. Hence `e=3` is the full order-three block.
* At `e=4,m=5`, one low row has equal-degree twins of degree three or four, so some degree-three row avoids them and uses the other three columns; any degree-three row meeting the twins overlaps that avoiding row once. With two low rows, the four twin columns have degree three. Each twin pair uses two of the three high rows; the two two-subsets must coincide to avoid singleton overlap, leaving the third high row at most one neighbor. At `e=4,m=6` there are exactly two low rows/columns; all other lines have degree three. The two low-column neighborhoods are disjoint two-subsets of the four high rows. Every low-row twin column must choose exactly one of those two subsets; choosing the same subset leaves rows of degree one, and choosing different subsets disconnects the graph. Thus an actual-unitary component at `e=4` has order four.
* At `e=5,m=5`, one low row leaves row degrees `3,3,3,4`: a high row avoiding its twins uses all three other columns, so each of at least two high rows meeting the twins needs at least two other neighbors, but only one high row has degree four. With two low rows, their four twin columns all have degree three; their two high-neighbor pairs in a three-row set must be equal, again leaving a row with at most one neighbor. With no low row, all row and column degrees are three. Their two-element omission sets are pairwise intersecting, since disjoint omission sets would give a singleton row overlap; each column is omitted by exactly two rows. Fix one omission pair: at most two other rows can meet it, although four must, contradiction.
* At `e=5,m=6`, there are at most two low lines on either side. With one low row, its twins have degree three: degree at least four on both would use four excess units, and the required two other high columns would use two more. The two high rows meeting those twins each have one other neighbor, which must be common to avoid a singleton column overlap. Those three rows are supported on three columns; their orthonormality isolates that square block. This excludes one low row, and by transposition one low column. With two low rows and two low columns, the four high columns have degrees `3,3,3,4`, but they are partitioned into two equal-degree twin pairs, impossible.
* At `e=5,m=7`, there are two low rows and two low columns, with all other degrees three. The two low columns have disjoint two-subsets `A,B` of the five high rows; identical subsets would isolate an order-two unitary block, and singleton intersection is forbidden. Each low-row twin column has exactly two high neighbors and must meet each of `A,B` in zero or two positions, so it chooses all of `A` or all of `B`. The high row outside `A union B` misses those four twin columns and both low columns, and therefore has at most one neighbor.
