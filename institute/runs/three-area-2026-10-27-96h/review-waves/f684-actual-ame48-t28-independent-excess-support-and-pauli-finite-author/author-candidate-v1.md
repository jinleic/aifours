## 2. Universal unitary support facts

Write `U=6 psi`, so the three `36 x 36` flattenings of the same four-index complex array U are unitary. Let `P` be the 28 unit-modulus positions of U, and `E` the remaining 20 nonzero positions. An entry of modulus 1 saturates its unitary row and column. Consequently P injects into every pair projection, no E position shares any pair value with a P position, and P uses 28 distinct rows and 28 distinct columns in each cut. Deleting these saturated lines gives an `8 x 8` residual unitary with 20 nonzeros. Its residual lines have degree at least two, since a degree-one unitary line would contain an entry of modulus 1. Two distinct rows (or columns) with exactly one shared nonzero column (row) have a nonzero one-term inner product, impossible for arbitrary complex amplitudes. All overlaps of size 3 or 4 retain all their terms.

A connected bipartite component of a unitary support has the same number m of rows and columns: orthonormal rows imply `m_rows <= m_columns`, and orthonormal columns give the reverse inequality. Its excess `e=number of entries-2m` is nonnegative and equals the sum of `degree-2` on either side. The total excess in a residual cut is four.

We reconstruct the necessary component classification, rather than assuming finite enumeration is a universal proof. A degree-2 row at columns c,d has twin columns: any other row meets both or neither, by the one-overlap prohibition. Two low rows cannot share that pair: they span the two-dimensional column subspace, forcing all other rows to vanish in those columns and isolating a `2 x 2` component. Thus in a larger component low-row column pairs are disjoint. Their column degree is at least three; degree two would isolate the same unitary `2 x 2` block. Every low row uses at least two column-excess units and every other row at least one row-excess unit. Hence, for a component larger than two, `m <= e + floor(e/2)`.

An excess-zero component is a full `2 x 2` block: two degree-2 twin columns have exactly two common row neighbors, and those rows have no other support. The bound excludes excess one. An excess-two component has order three and eight entries, namely the `3 x 3` full support with one zero, degree profile `233/233`. If an excess-four component has order six, equality in the bound forces two low and four degree-3 rows. The two disjoint twin pairs have column degree three and exhaust column excess, so the remaining two columns have degree two. Each twin pair meets its low row and two high rows, and no degree-3 row can meet both pairs. The high rows therefore partition into the two pairs' two-neighbor sets. A high row's one remaining entry must share its remaining column with the other high neighbor of its twin pair, otherwise that column and a twin column have one overlap. These degree-2 columns separate the two groups into two components, contradiction. All other components beside an excess-four component have excess zero, so `8-m` is even; order two has no positive excess, order six is excluded, and the only remaining order is four. Otherwise total excess four consists of two excess-two components and zero-excess blocks. Thus the only residual decompositions are `4+2+2` or `3+3+2`.

For the order-four, 12-entry component the only candidate degree multisets are `3333`, `2334`, and `2244`. Exclude `2334`: its unique low row has twin columns. If their common degree is 3, one non-low row misses both and cannot have degree at least 3. If their degree is 4, the two other columns each have degree 2; the degree-4 row meets both and the two degree-3 rows meet one each. If they choose the same column, the other has degree 1; if different, the two columns overlap only in the degree-4 row. Both are forbidden. In the `2244` case the low rows' supports are disjoint and the high rows are full, so the opposite profile is `3333`; transposition completes the list. - `D = (3,3,3,3,2,2,2,2)`, or

## 3. Exact four-qubit obstruction and isolation

Suppose a normalized pure four-qubit state had maximally mixed two-party marginals. Trace out one qubit. Its three-qubit density matrix has Pauli expansion `rho=(I+M)/8`, where M is a real linear combination of the 27 Hermitian weight-three strings: all weight-one and weight-two coefficients vanish. The traced-out one-qubit marginal is `I/2`; the Schmidt spectrum makes the nonzero eigenvalues of rho equal to `1/2`, so `rho^2=rho/2` and

`M^2 = 3 I + 2 M`.

For two weight-three Pauli strings, their product has weight three only if their letters differ at all three sites. Each such pair anticommutes, so the weight-three component of every anticommutator vanishes. Equal strings give the identity. By collecting diagonal and unordered cross terms, the weight-three component of `M^2` is zero; the displayed equation therefore forces `M=0`, which contradicts its scalar component. This is an exact obstruction, including arbitrary complex amplitudes; the normalization `/8` is essential.

Suppose an actual U contains a binary `2 x 2 x 2 x 2` residual subarray B such that all four binary pair values in every physical pair occur in B, and each of those six sets of pair values has **all** of its E extensions inside B. P cannot use an E-occupied pair value. Thus in each of the three actual unitary flattenings, the four B rows and four B columns are a closed `4 x 4` unitary block of the same U. Its squared norm is 4, and B/2 would be a normalized four-qubit state with all three balanced flattenings equal to one-half of a unitary. This contradicts the obstruction. Mere component closure in one cut, or four occupied values without checking external extensions, is not sufficient.

## 4. Marginals, cubes, and the full-cube exclusion

For a symbol x of party i let `s_i(x)` count E words containing it, and let `d_ij(x)` count residual partner values in party j. Every one of the 36 pair values is occupied either by exactly one P word or by residual words, since P uses 28 values and E uses the other eight. Hence the number of P words at x is `6-d_ij(x)`, independent of j; write the common degree `d_i(x)` and let `h_i(x)=s_i(x)-2d_i(x)`. This is the excess incidence seen at x in any partner pair system. It is nonnegative and

`sum_x d_i(x)=8`, `sum_x h_i(x)=4`.

On the residual alphabet `d_i(x)>=2`: if it were 1, all three other coordinates would be fixed, so only one E word could contain x, whereas an occupied residual pair has at least two extensions. Thus the residual alphabet size `q_i<=4`; q=1 is impossible because one symbol has at most six partner symbols. If q=2, every other q is 4 and each pair with the q=2 party fills its `2 x 4` residual rectangle. Its four P words with that party in its residual alphabet use only the two nonresidual symbols in each other party. They form four binary length-four words agreeing in at most one position. Their six mutual Hamming distances total at least 18, whereas each binary coordinate contributes at most four, so their total is at most 16. Hence `q_i` is 3 or 4.

For a P word w let k(w) be the number of coordinates lying in the corresponding residual alphabets. Counting its incidences and pairs of incidences gives

`sum_P k = sum_i(6 q_i-8)`,
`sum_P binom(k,2) = sum_{i<j}(q_i q_j-8)`.

Since `binom(k,2)>=k-1`, these equations exclude three or four q=3 parties. Up to permutation, the only profiles are `4444`, `3444`, and `3344`; in particular at least two parties have q=4. At a q=4 party all four residual degrees are 2. If two parties have q=3, their eight residual pair values fill a `3 x 3` box except one cell; each endpoint of the missing cell is the unique degree-2 **star**.

For a general degree-2 symbol x, its E slice uses a binary three-cube and has `4+h_i(x)` vertices. A five-vertex subset has at most five induced cube edges: deleting three vertices removes at least seven of the twelve edges. Each direction has at least one edge because its two faces together contain five vertices among four possible projected columns; if no forbidden one-overlap existed, each direction would contribute at least two, contradiction. A seven-vertex subset has face sizes 3 and 4 in every direction, incompatible with D and Q. Hence `h` is 0, 2, or 4. For a six-vertex slice the two missing distinct vertices agree in a coordinate exactly when that face split is 4/2 (Q), and differ exactly when it is 3/3 (D). Thus three Q directions are impossible. If precisely two directions are Q, the slice is the product of the two D-coordinate values and three corners in the Q-coordinate square.

Now exclude `h=4` for a degree-2 symbol a. Its slice is the complete cube. Choose another q=4 party j; call the other parties k,l. The three pair systems from a's party are Q, and in each complementary pair system the four binary pair values have multiplicity 3. Each of the two binary j-symbols has h=2, hence six E words: four at a and two outside. The four outside words use the same binary alphabets and occur once at every binary pair value of j,k,l; they are a parity or opposite-parity four-word code. At each binary j symbol its two outside words have the same a-party value, by degree 2. If these values differed between the two j symbols, fixing either k symbol would give a residual row outside a overlapping the corresponding a-row in exactly one `(j,l)` column. They are therefore the same b. The full a-cube together with the b-parity words exhausts, on all six physical pairs, the 4/2 and 3 values described above. Section 3 gives a contradiction. Consequently a q=4 party has exactly two `h=2` high symbols and two `h=0` low symbols.

## 5. Synchronization and the sector lemma

A D pair between two q=4 parties contains exactly the four high-high cells of multiplicity 3 and the four low-low cells of multiplicity 2. Indeed each high symbol has two D neighbors and excess two, so both incident cells are 3s and both opposite endpoints are high; degree two then determines the low-low cells. Thus a D edge synchronizes the high/low bits of every E word. For a Q edge between q=4 parties, the two 4-cells form a perfect matching between the high symbols. Each high endpoint's other incident Q cell has multiplicity 2.

**Sector lemma.** Suppose two specified high symbols at each party support 12 E words and each specified symbol occurs six times there. All four specified pair cells must occur: six words at a fixed symbol cannot fit in a single cell of multiplicity at most four. If the pair is D, each specified endpoint's two neighbors are 3-cells, so all four cells have multiplicity 3. If it is Q, the two 4-cells match the high symbols and the other two cells have multiplicity 2. In either case their total multiplicity is 12; no extension lies outside the specified box. This holds on all six pairs, so Section 3 excludes the sector.

We also need a three-party synchronization lemma. Suppose three q=4 parties have synchronized bits and the fourth party A has q=3. Their high sector contains 12 words. If A has a D edge to one of them, any A symbol x has `h_A(x)<=2` and contributes exactly `3h_A(x)` high-sector words. An A–Q edge forces all h values even. If all three A edges are D instead, h=1 would give three high-sector words while fixing the unique high neighbor at each of the three q=4 parties, leaving at most one word. Hence the h values are 0 or 2, two A symbols contribute six words each, and the sector lemma applies.

If all three A edges are Q, the even h-values sum to 4. An h=2 symbol contributes either four or six high words: its 4-cell has a high partner and its remaining 2-cell may or may not. Four would fix all three high coordinates while requiring four distinct words; six would be a six-vertex binary slice with Q faces in all three directions. Both are impossible. Thus one A symbol a has h=4. Its two 4-cells at any synchronized q=4 party supply eight high words, filling the binary cube. The other four high words fill each binary pair once, forming the parity code. At a fixed high q=4 symbol the two non-a words have the same A symbol because its A-degree is two; the parity incidence graph connects these four words, so this A symbol is common. The resulting full-cube-plus-parity box exhausts every one of its pair values and violates isolation. Therefore three synchronized q=4 parties cannot coexist with a q=3 fourth party.

## 6. The 4444 and 3444 profiles

For 4444, let the D edges form a graph on the four parties. It is connected: a singleton component would have three Q edges, forbidden by the six-cube rule; a two-vertex component would have four crossing Q edges, including a complementary Q pair. D edges synchronize all four bits. Their 12-word high sector has six words at each high symbol, contrary to the sector lemma.

For 3444 write A for the q=3 party and B,C,D for q=4. If the D graph induced on B,C,D is connected, the preceding synchronization lemma applies. If it has exactly one D edge, the isolated q=4 party has two Q edges to its q=4 peers and therefore a D edge to A. At either high symbol its six-word slice is the product of two A values and a three-corner square on the other two q=4 parties. The existing D edge makes those two parties' high/low bits equal at every corner. The connected three-corner square contains the high-high corner from the Q 4-cells, so all its endpoint symbols are high. The isolated party's 12 high words are therefore contained in each peer's 12-word high sector; the three sectors coincide, contradiction.

It remains to consider a Q triangle on B,C,D. Complementarity makes all three edges to A D. A high-symbol slice has two A values times a three-corner square on its two Q neighbors. Each principal neighbor is the high symbol joined to it by the 4-cell; the single missing corner is secondary-secondary, since a principal value must occur four times. Label the two high symbols at B,C,D so the B–C and B–D principal matchings preserve the labels. The C–D matching either swaps or preserves them.

- **Swapped:** for each high B symbol b, let c,d be its principal C,D neighbors. The existing b,c,d corner makes c,d secondary to each other. At c its principal D neighbor is the other high D symbol, and at d its principal C neighbor is the other high C symbol; the required principal-secondary corners make b adjacent to both high symbols of C and both of D. This holds for both high b. Degree two then makes the low-low supports complete on B–C and B–D, synchronizing all three high/low bits, already excluded.
- **Preserving:** a high-high-high triple with nonidentical labels would make both neighbors secondary at the minority-label party, the omitted corner. Thus for each label h, the secondary neighbor of each high symbol is low, and the allowed triples are the principal high-high-high triple plus the three triples with exactly one low symbol. The shared 4-cells and the product slices force the two A values to be a common two-element set `A_h` on all four triples. These give eight words for each h and exhaust all 16 words with any q=4 high symbol. If `A_0 != A_1`, their intersection has h-value 2 and the other two A symbols have h-value 1. A's unique degree-2 symbol cannot have h=1, but the intersection symbol has both high symbols and a low neighbor in every q=4 party, hence degree at least 3. If `A_0=A_1`, those two A symbols each see both high symbols and a low symbol, so their degrees are at least 3; their sum with the remaining degree is 8, giving degrees 3,3,2. Each q=4 party therefore has the same secondary low neighbor for both high labels. In a Q edge both high endpoints on one side saturate that common low neighbor on the other; the remaining low symbol then has at most the remaining low symbol as a neighbor, contradicting degree 2.

## 7. The 3344 profile

Let A,B be q=3 and C,D be q=4. Denote their q=3 stars by `a*`,`b*`.

**Case CD=D.** C and D bits synchronize; their high sector has 12 words. If X is A or B and has a D edge to C or D, its h-values are at most 2 and a symbol x contributes exactly `3h_X(x)` high words. If both its cross edges are D and h=1, those three words have fixed high C,D coordinates and therefore use all three symbols of the other q=3 party, once each.

If each q=3 party has at least one D cross edge and one has a Q edge, its h-values are even, hence 0,2,2, and its high sector uses only its two h=2 symbols. An h=1 symbol at the other party, whose edges must then both be D, would need all three of the first party's symbols, contradiction. If all four cross edges are D and A has an h=1 symbol, its three high words use all B symbols, including `b*`. The star has degree two and thus h=0 or 2. In the first case it has no high words; in the second all six of its words are high, so its pair with the chosen A symbol has only the one extension at the fixed C,D coordinates. Both contradict the residual conditions. Thus both q=3 parties have h-values 0,2,2 and the sector lemma applies.

The only remaining possibility, after interchanging A and B, has A–C and A–D both Q; complementarity makes B–C and B–D both D. A's h-values are even. If they are 0,2,2, an h=2 A symbol contributes four or six high words. Four would fix C and D while permitting at most three B values; hence each of the two contributes six. The preceding three-symbol argument forces B's h-values to be even, giving a forbidden sector. Otherwise a single A symbol a has h=4 and supplies eight high words. If B has an h=1 symbol, its distribution is 2,1,1. Each h=1 B symbol has three high words at fixed C,D and uses every A symbol once, so only two of the eight at a use the h=1 symbols; the other six share one `(A=a,B=b)` pair, exceeding the maximum multiplicity 4. Thus B has h-values 0,2,2. The eight words at a fill the B-high/C-high/D-high cube. The other four high words occur once at every binary pair of B,C,D, hence form a parity code. For each high C or D symbol its two non-a words have the same A value, and the parity incidence graph connects them; the full cube plus parity box is isolated and impossible.

**Case CD=Q.** Complementarity gives AB=D. The P incidence equations for profile 3344 give `sum_P k=52` and `sum_P binom(k,2)=25`. If `n_r` is the number of P words with r residual-alphabet coordinates, then

`n_0+n_3+3n_4 = 25-52+28 = 1`.

Thus `n_4=0` and at most one word has three residual coordinates. There are four P words at `a*`, contributing respectively `1+2+2=5` incidences with residual alphabets of B,C,D. Since no P word has four residual coordinates, at least one of these four has three. The same argument at `b*` makes this the unique three-coordinate word; it contains both stars. Interchange C,D if necessary so its C coordinate `c*` is residual and its D coordinate is not. The two C-neighbor sets of `a*` and `b*` each have size two and both avoid `c*`; they intersect in a C symbol `c0`.

A high C or D symbol cannot neighbor both stars: its six words then lie in a `2 x 2` A,B box with the star-star cell absent and two values of the other q=4 coordinate. All six allowed words occur. The two star incidences have multiplicity 2, so its other incidence in each system has multiplicity 4; that q=4 symbol would have three Q edges, impossible. Therefore `c0` is low. Its four words include at least two with each star, and these sets are disjoint. Its two A neighbors are `a*` and one nonstar, its two B neighbors `b*` and one nonstar; the four words have both values of the same two-element D set T at the pairs `(a*,nonstar)` and `(nonstar,b*)`. Both D symbols in T neighbor both stars, so are low. Each star has exactly those two D neighbors; each corresponding cell has multiplicity 2, so each star has four E words and h=0.

Let `cA` and `cB` be the stars' other C neighbors. AB is D and each star has h=0, so its two occupied AB cells each have multiplicity 2. The words at c0 saturate one such cell for each star; the words at cA and cB use their respective other AB cells and both D values in T. If `cA != cB`, either symbol of T has the three C neighbors c0,cA,cB, violating degree 2. Thus `cA=cB=c1`, which is low. The four low-low CD cells `{c0,c1} x T` are saturated by these eight star words. Degree 2 then makes all remaining CD cells the high-high `2 x 2` box, so C,D bits synchronize. AB's four 3-cells are exactly the two nonstar A symbols crossed with the two nonstar B symbols. Their 12 words are all in the high-high CD sector, six at each of the four q=3 nonstar symbols and six at each high C,D symbol. The sector lemma gives the contradiction.
