## A. Original physical caller and conditional boundary

For one normalized complex AME(4,6) state in one product basis let U=6ψ, P={w: |U(w)|=1}, t=|P|, and E={w: 0<|U(w)|<1}. Every one of the three balanced 36×36 flattenings of this **same U** is unitary. Every entry of P exhausts its line in each cut. Thus P is injective on all six physical-pair projections and its pair values are disjoint from E. Deleting those t singleton rows and columns leaves, for each cut, an n×n unitary with n=36−t, all entries E, and minimum line degree two. Consequently

    e = |E|−2n = N+t−72 ≥ 0.

All actual complex amplitudes remain common to all cuts. In a full 2×2 component with entries a,b,c,d, the exact row and column conditions include

    |a|²=|d|²=λ,  |b|²=|c|²=1−λ,  0<λ<1,
    a conjugate(c)+b conjugate(d)=0,
    a conjugate(b)+c conjugate(d)=0.

The same a,b,c,d are the corresponding tensor coefficients in the other two cuts; no equal-amplitude or real-phase assumption is made. The prepared program checks only necessary finite consequences of this caller, never assigns different tensors to different cuts, and does not solve this global system.

For completeness, the count reduction used below is as follows. In a unitary support no two rows or columns meet in exactly one entry, and every connected component is square. With excess zero, all degrees are two; meeting rows have identical two neighbors, giving a full 2×2 component, hence even total order. Excess one is impossible: the three neighbors of the unique degree-three row must all meet degree-two rows; any such row selects a pair of the three columns, and different pairs have a forbidden one-point overlap, so one column cannot acquire its required second neighbor.

At excess two, either there is one degree-four row or two degree-three rows. In the first case the pairs selected by adjacent degree-two rows must be equal or disjoint, and covering the four columns forces two disjoint pairs. A column from each pair then meets exactly once, at the degree-four row, impossible. In the second case, the two 3-supports cannot meet in one point; meeting in two prevents the private column from getting a degree-two neighbor; disjoint supports would disconnect the component. Hence they coincide. Every adjacent degree-two row selects the same pair of those three columns. Squareness forces a single 3×3 component with eight entries. Other components have excess zero. Thus total excess two implies odd order.

Therefore, under the explicit local hypothesis t≤28, N=45 gives t=27 or 28, excluded respectively by odd order at excess zero and by excess one. N=46 gives t=26,27,28; the latter two are excluded respectively by excess one and by even order at excess two. Only **N=46,t=26** remains. Supplying t≤28 globally from packet S3 retains its stated f113 provenance, including classical no-OLS(6) and the prior t≤30 boundary; these upstream sources were not independently reconstructed here. These deductions say nothing by themselves about N≤44 or a minimum.

At N=46,t=26, each cut has a residual 10×10 unitary consisting of five full 2×2 components. E has 20 distinct words. Each of the six physical pair projections has exactly ten E values, each with two extensions, while the other 26 values are occupied once by P. For any residual symbol x in party i, let d_i(x) be its number of E-neighbors in any one other party and s_i(x) its number of E words. The same P/E gives

    s_i(x)=2 d_i(x),   p_i(x)=6−d_i(x),   sum_x d_i(x)=10.

Every used residual symbol has d_i(x)≥2: if it had just one neighbor in each of the other three parties, its two supposed extensions would coincide as a word.

## B. Four-binary closure and actual phase propagation

A closed eight-word binary block means that in each of the six physical pair projections the four binary pair values each have exactly two E extensions in the block. Since these pair values are fully exhausted and P is disjoint, the same actual U has an isolated 4×4 unitary in **each** of its three cuts. Dividing the corresponding subarray by two would give a normalized four-qubit AME state, impossible as follows. Its three-qubit marginal has two eigenvalues 1/2 and all its one- and two-qubit marginals maximally mixed. In the Pauli basis write ρ=(I+M)/8 with M a real combination of weight-three strings. The identity ρ²=ρ/2 gives M²=3I+2M. In M², terms from two distinct weight-three strings differing in one or three positions cancel in the anticommutator, while those differing in two positions have weight two; squares are scalar. Therefore M² has no weight-three part, forcing M=0 and a contradiction. This uses all three physical cuts and arbitrary complex amplitudes, not an isolated support assertion.

Now suppose two E words differ only in the fourth coordinate, with values d0,d1. Their common base triple s is part of a maximal set S such that both (s,d0) and (s,d1) occur. In each of the three cuts, no-one-overlap and line degree two force a second such base triple sharing any chosen one of the three base coordinates. Each base symbol occurs in exactly two triples of S: it makes an actual E-pair with d0, and that pair has exactly two extensions. Two different triples of S cannot share two base coordinates, as that physical pair would have four extensions. Thus joining triples sharing a coordinate gives a simple connected cubic graph. For adjacent s,s′ the actual column orthogonality equation is

    z_s+z_s′=0,       z_s=U(s,d0) conjugate(U(s,d1)) ≠ 0.

The very same complex z_s is used on its three incident edges. An odd cycle would yield z_s=−z_s; hence the graph is bipartite. Its two sides have equal size at least three, and 2|S|≤20, so |S| is 6, 8 or 10. Each physical pair used by this block is already saturated by two of its words, leaving respectively 10−|S| pair values, twice each, outside it.

* At |S|=10, the three base parties use five symbols each and the fourth uses d0,d1, with degree five. Each d value has one P word. Every pair of dν with a used base symbol is already E, so these two P words must use the unique outside symbol in every base party. They share a base pair, contradicting P injectivity.
* At |S|=8, four E words remain and every physical pair has exactly two values, each twice. Every nonconstant coordinate splits the four words into two equal classes. Two such coordinates have only two joint values, so their partitions coincide. All variable coordinates therefore give at most two distinct words, contradiction.
* At |S|=6, eight E words remain, with four pair values in each system, twice each. As above, every used symbol has at least two neighbors, so each remaining alphabet has at most two symbols. An alphabet of size one would force four symbols in each other coordinate; each pair of the remaining coordinates is then a matching of four pair values, yielding at most four distinct words. Hence all alphabets are binary, and all four pair values occur twice in every projection: the forbidden closed binary block.

Thus no two E words differ in one coordinate, conditional only on this actual unitary caller. The finite program tests the cubic bipartite graph and edge-equation primitives at the three bounded orders; it does **not** independently implement or establish the physical realization, above |S| case split, or a full tensor search.

## C. Low symbols, three low faces, and 3444

Call a residual symbol low when d=2. Its four E words use exactly two neighbors in each other party, two occurrences of each. With no distance-one E pair they form a four-element independent subset of the binary 3-cube. Such a subset is exactly one parity class: if it contained opposite parities, the two vertices would have to be antipodal; every other cube vertex is adjacent to one of those two. There are 70 four-vertex subsets, arranged in 35 complement pairs, and two qualifying subsets—not 35 qualifying independent sets. The source is designed to enumerate all 70 explicitly.

The low-symbol graph has an edge for a physical low–low E pair. Suppose a,b,c from three parties formed a triangle. The two E extensions at any of the pairs ab, ac, bc differ in both remaining coordinates; they therefore identify the respective two-neighbor sets for both low slices. The three slices sit in one binary four-party box. Pairwise intersections make their three parity choices the same four-bit parity class; each slice is a fixed-coordinate face of size four, their pair intersections have size two, and their triple intersection has size one. Their union is seven of the eight vertices of that class.

The eighth vertex is forced using the **actual** degree-two/no-one-overlap condition. In any of the corresponding cuts, the incomplete pair-row has one displayed column in common with a complete degree-two pair-row; the latter's other column is the only possible second common neighbor. Hence that actual tensor entry must also be nonzero. The eight words now saturate every binary pair twice, forming the forbidden closed block. Consequently there is no low triangle. In a low slice the projection to any two other neighbor pairs is complete, so a low symbol cannot have low neighbors in two distinct other parties; any word contains at most two lows. The code's binary-box fixture verifies this local support implication and its six pair counts and three 2×2 **support** structures, not the existence of compatible AME phases.

The residual alphabet sizes satisfy q_i≤5 and q_iq_j≥10. A size-one alphabet is impossible. If q_i=2, all three others have size five; both i-symbols have degree five and exactly one P word each. Each P word must use the sole outside symbol in every other party, so the two P words collide on a physical pair. Hence q_i∈{3,4,5}, with at most one 3.

If two parties have size five then every one of their symbols is low. At least one of the other parties has a low symbol, whose neighbors in both size-five parties are low, contradiction. If exactly one party D has size five, at least two other parties B,C have size four. A size-four party has degree multiset (2,2,2,4) or (2,2,3,3). Every low B symbol has low neighbors in D and therefore only high neighbors in C, so C has two highs; similarly B. Both have type (2,2,3,3), and there is no B–C low–low edge. Their ten E pairs consist of the full low-B/high-C and high-B/low-C 2×2 blocks plus a high–high matching. These are exactly its two 2×2 complete subgraphs. Each of the five low D slices projects to one such subgraph. The four B-low-to-D incidences force exactly two D slices of the first type; the four C-low-to-D incidences force exactly two of the second. No type remains for the fifth. Thus the only profiles left are 3444 and 4444.

For 3444, take q_A=3 and q_B=q_C=q_D=4. Each latter degree multiset is (2,2,3,3); A has either (2,4,4) or (3,3,4). For w∈P let k(w) count coordinates lying in the E alphabets. Exact P/E pair complementarity gives

    sum_P k = 6(3+4+4+4)−40 = 50,
    sum_P binom(k,2) = (3·12+3·16)−60 = 24.

For k=0,1,2,3,4, the values of binom(k,2)−k+1 are 1,0,0,1,3. Their sum is 24−50+26=0, so every P word has k=1 or 2, and exactly two have k=1. For an A symbol of degree d, its 4−d P neighbors inside each of B,C,D lie in disjoint P words, hence 3(4−d)≤6−d and d≥3. A therefore has degree (3,3,4). Call the degree-four symbol z and the others u0,u1. The two z P words have no other residual coordinate. Pair injectivity lets us label the two outside symbols in each of B,C,D so that these words have outside triples 000 and 111.

For either u, exactly three P words have respectively a B-, C-, or D-residual symbol. A degree-three B/C/D high is adjacent in E to all three A symbols, while each degree-two symbol is adjacent to z and exactly one u. Thus the missing E-neighbor of either u in each of B,C,D is a low symbol, and the two u's missing lows are different. Write its three outside-bit pairs as

    B-low word (C,D)=(1−r,r),
    C-low word (B,D)=(r,1−r),
    D-low word (B,C)=(1−r,r).

These formulas are forced by P pair injectivity with the two z words and among the three u words. The two u symbols have different r because otherwise their B-low words have the same outside (C,D) pair. For a fixed u let its missed lows be b,c,d. If (b,c) were not an E pair, P/E complementarity would put it in P; k≤2 would force A and D outside. Its outside D value is either 0 or 1, but each value collides on a B–D or C–D pair with one of the known u words. The same argument forces (b,d) and (c,d) into E. Thus b,c,d form the forbidden low triangle. The code independently lists all 64 outside assignments for each u, all 4096 paired assignments, every allowed r pair and both possible outside residual values for each low-pair obligation; labels for the two lows in each party are independently chosen so that label i denotes the low missed by ui. This is an abstract partial-P encoding of necessary conditions, not a complete P or physical state.

## D. 4444 incidence and the finite pattern domain

For 4444, every party has type (2,2,2,4) or (2,2,3,3). If there is no low–low E edge, every E word contains at most one low, but the at-least-eight low symbols each occur in four E words, giving at least 32 low incidences among only 20 words, impossible.

If there is a low–low edge between i and j, either endpoint has only high neighbors in each complementary party. Those complementary parties each have two highs and hence type (2,2,3,3); the low slice contains all four high–high pair values between them. For two such parties, if x is the number of low–low pairs, their quadrant counts are LL=x, LH=HL=4−x and HH=2+x. Therefore x=2 for the complementary pair. Applying the same reasoning to one of those edges makes i,j type (2,2,3,3) too, and their pair also has x=2. Thus, whenever any low edge exists, all four parties have exactly two lows, each physical low-pair graph has either zero or two edges, and complementary physical pairs are simultaneously empty or nonempty.

Two different complementary pairings cannot be nonempty. Each party would then have low neighbors in two distinct destination parties; its two low symbols must split between those destinations. Any pair graph of either pairing would then have only one available vertex on each side, so it could contain at most one edge, not the required two. Thus at most one complementary pairing is nonempty and the low graph has at most four edges. There are exactly 32 low-coordinate incidences. If N2 E words contain two lows, no word has three lows, so 32≤20+N2 and N2≥12. Each low edge has exactly two E extensions and each such extension has exactly two lows, so N2=2·edges≤8, contradiction.

The prospective program checks the labelled abstract low-graph domain rather than assuming the last bound. Six party pairs each have either no edge or one of the six 2-edge subsets of the 2×2 low-pair box: 7^6=117649 patterns. For each of the three complementary pairings, there are 1+6²=37 choices, so 37³=50653 are complement-symmetric. It tests all applicable patterns for the no-triangle and one-destination restrictions, records the maximum edges, and includes intended-reason controls with destination omitted, complement omitted, a wrong pair count, and a triangle. These combinatorial domain sizes are specifications and arithmetic predictions, **not observed run output**. The proof that these are necessary physical conditions is the actual-tensor derivation above; the graph is not a substitute tensor.
