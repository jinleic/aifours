## 3. Preliminary algebra and actor transport

### 3.1 Purity and the physical projection

For a group-valued homomorphism \(q\), every idempotent maps to the group identity. Under idempotent purity,

\[
q^{-1}(1)=E(S).
\]

Consequently, if \(e,f\in E(S)\), then \(q(ef)=1\), so \(ef\in E(S)\). Thus \(E(S)\) is closed under multiplication and is a band. This argument does not require commutativity of idempotents.

For the normalized host

\[
(c,g)(d,h)=\bigl(c(g\cdot d),gh\bigr),
\]

an idempotent \((c,g)\) must have \(g^2=g\), hence \(g=1\). Conversely,

\[
(c,1)^2=(c^2,1)=(c,1).
\]

Therefore an injective source homomorphism into this host induces an idempotent-pure group-valued actor map: actor identity makes the image idempotent, and injectivity reflects that equality back to the source. This supports S1 LF52–82 without adding regularity or surjectivity assumptions to that necessary direction.

### 3.2 Least group congruence

Let \(\sigma\) be the congruence generated by identifying all source idempotents, and let \(\varepsilon\) denote their common class. Nonemptiness and regularity ensure that idempotents exist.

For an inverse \(s'\) of \(s\), both \(ss'\) and \(s's\) are idempotent. Hence

\[
[s][s']=[s'][s]=\varepsilon.
\]

Moreover,

\[
\varepsilon[s]=[ss's]=[s],
\qquad
[s]\varepsilon=[ss's]=[s].
\]

Thus \(S/\sigma\) is a group. Every homomorphism to a group identifies the source idempotents, so \(\sigma\) is contained in every group-valued kernel.

The prescribed onto \(q\) factors through an onto group homomorphism

\[
\bar q:S/\sigma\longrightarrow G.
\]

If \(\bar q([s])=1\), purity makes \(s\) idempotent, and therefore \([s]=\varepsilon\). The kernel of \(\bar q\) is trivial, so it is an isomorphism and

\[
\ker q=\sigma.
\]

This independently supports S1 LF88–141.

### 3.3 Source-only actor transport, not whole-host extension

Suppose

\[
j:S\hookrightarrow B\rtimes H,
\qquad j(s)=(b_s,r(s)).
\]

The preceding physical-projection argument makes \(r\) pure. Its image \(K=r(S)\) is a subgroup of \(H\): multiplication is inherited, a regular inverse \(s'\) provides \(r(s')=r(s)^{-1}\), and \(r(ss')=1\).

Both \(q:S\twoheadrightarrow G\) and \(r:S\twoheadrightarrow K\) therefore have kernel \(\sigma\). There is a well-defined group isomorphism

\[
\theta:G\longrightarrow K,
\qquad \theta(q(s))=r(s).
\]

Transport the restricted \(K\)-action on \(B\) by

\[
g\star b=\theta(g)\cdot_H b.
\]

Then

\[
F(s)=(b_s,q(s))
\]

satisfies, at the consuming multiplication,

\[
\begin{aligned}
F(s)F(t)
&=\bigl(b_s(q(s)\star b_t),q(s)q(t)\bigr)\\
&=\bigl(b_s(r(s)\cdot_H b_t),q(st)\bigr)\\
&=(b_{st},q(st))\\
&=F(st).
\end{aligned}
\]

Equality of two \(F\)-images gives equality of their band coordinates and of their \(q\)-values, hence equality of their \(r\)-values and of their \(j\)-images. Injectivity follows.

Crucially, this uses the action of \(K\), then \(G\cong K\). It constructs no homomorphism from all of \(H\) to \(G\).

The assigned S3 LF5–9 boundary is therefore respected. In its explicit \(C_4\) example, any homomorphism \(\phi:C_4\to C_2\) satisfies

\[
\phi(2)=2\phi(1)=0,
\]

whereas the prescribed source isomorphism has \(q(2)=1\). A matching whole-host actor extension fails, but the source still embeds over its own \(q\). The candidate does not conflate these statements.

### 3.4 Green-coordinate separation

Assume \(s\mathcal R t\), \(s\mathcal L t\), and \(q(s)=q(t)\). Choose an inverse \(s'\), and put

\[
e=s's,\qquad a=s't.
\]

The group image of \(a\) is \(1\), so \(a^2=a\).

From the Green relations choose \(u,v,w\in S^1\) with

\[
t=su,\qquad s=tv,\qquad t=ws.
\]

Then

\[
av=s'tv=s's=e.
\]

Since \(se=s\),

\[
te=wse=t,
\qquad ae=s'te=a.
\]

But idempotence of \(a\) and \(e=av\) also give

\[
ae=a(av)=a^2v=av=e.

Thus \(a=e\). Finally,

\[
t=ss't=sa=se=s,

where \(ss't=t\) follows from \(t=su\).

The proof supports S1 LF219–272. It supplies source separation, not an unproved multiplication or action on the Green-class coordinates.

## 4. Universal band presentation and the exact embedding criterion

### 4.1 The full presentation is a band

Take

\[
A=G\times S
\]

and the free semigroup \(A^+\) of **nonempty** words. Let \(\equiv_q\) be the least semigroup congruence containing

\[
ww\equiv_q w
\quad(w\in A^+)
\]

and

\[
(g,st)\equiv_q(g,s)(gq(s),t)
\quad(g\in G,\ s,t\in S).
\]

The all-word quantifier in the first family is essential and is present in S1 LF288–306. For every class \([w]\),

\[
[w]^2=[ww]=[w].
\]

Therefore \(C_q=A^+/{\equiv_q}\) is a band. It is nonempty because \(G\) and \(S\) are nonempty.

No empty-word element, extra identity or commutative-band law has been inserted.

### 4.2 Translation descends to an invertible action

The letter substitution

\[
L_h(g,s)=(hg,s)
\]

extends multiplicatively to \(A^+\). It sends a word-square relation to another word-square relation. It sends a cocycle relation to

\[
(hg,st)\equiv_q(hg,s)(hgq(s),t),
\]

which is again one of the defining relations.

Thus \(L_h\) preserves the generated congruence. The same is true of \(L_{h^{-1}}\), and the two substitutions are inverses. Consequently the induced map on \(C_q\) is a band automorphism, not merely an endomorphism. The identities

\[
L_1=\operatorname{id},
\qquad L_hL_k=L_{hk}
\]

hold on letters, hence on words and on the quotient.

This establishes the action needed at the subsequent semidirect-product multiplication.

### 4.3 The source coordinate is an actual cocycle

Set

\[
c_s=[(1,s)].
\]

The \(g=1\) defining relation gives

\[
c_{st}
=[(1,s)(q(s),t)]
=c_s\bigl(q(s)\cdot c_t\bigr).
\]

Therefore

\[
\eta_q(s)=(c_s,q(s))
\]

satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
\eta_q(s)\eta_q(t)
&=\bigl(c_s(q(s)\cdot c_t),q(s)q(t)\bigr)\\
&=(c_{st},q(st))\\
&=\eta_q(st).
\end{aligned}
\]

Its actor projection is literally \(q\). This construction needs only a nonempty semigroup \(S\) and a homomorphism \(q:S\to G\), as stated; neither regularity nor purity was used.

### 4.4 Universal property for an arbitrary target band action

Let \(B\) be any band with a \(G\)-action, and let

\[
H(s)=(b_s,q(s))
\]

be any homomorphism into \(B\rtimes G\). Injectivity is not assumed.

Evaluate letters by

\[
(g,s)\longmapsto g\cdot b_s
\]

and evaluate words by multiplication in \(B\).

Every word-square relation is respected because every evaluated word is an element of the band \(B\). For the cocycle relation, the homomorphism property of \(H\) gives

\[
b_{st}=b_s(q(s)\cdot b_t),
\]

and therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
g\cdot b_{st}
&=g\cdot\bigl(b_s(q(s)\cdot b_t)\bigr)\\
&=(g\cdot b_s)((gq(s))\cdot b_t).
\end{aligned}
\]

The order \(gq(s)\) is preserved; no commutation is used.

The evaluation consequently descends to a band homomorphism

\[
\Phi:C_q\longrightarrow B.
\]

On letters, translation by \(h\) evaluates to

\[
(hg)\cdot b_s=h\cdot(g\cdot b_s),
\]

so \(\Phi\) is \(G\)-equivariant. It has \(\Phi(c_s)=b_s\).

Every letter class is \(g\cdot c_s\), so these classes generate \(C_q\). Equivariance and the values on the \(c_s\) therefore determine \(\Phi\) uniquely.

The resulting map on the consuming host,

\[
\widehat\Phi:C_q\rtimes G\longrightarrow B\rtimes G,
\qquad
\widehat\Phi(c,g)=(\Phi(c),g),
\]

is multiplicative precisely because \(\Phi\) preserves band multiplication and is equivariant. It satisfies

\[
\widehat\Phi\eta_q=H.
\]

Thus the universal property applies to the actual physical multiplication, not merely to an alphabet-level assignment.

### 4.5 Exact source-embedding criterion

Injectivity of \(\eta_q\) is exactly

\[
q(s)=q(t)
\ \text{and}\
(1,s)\equiv_q(1,t)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad s=t.
\]

If this condition holds, \(\eta_q\) itself is the required embedding.

Conversely, suppose an embedding \(H:S\hookrightarrow B\rtimes G\) over \(q\) exists. Congruence of \((1,s)\) and \((1,t)\) makes their images under \(\Phi\) equal, so \(b_s=b_t\). Equality of \(q(s)\) and \(q(t)\) then makes \(H(s)=H(t)\); injectivity gives \(s=t\).

This proves the claimed equivalence with **existence of some band host over the prescribed \(q\)**. It does not assert that the separation condition always holds.

### 4.6 Finite derivations and all-host countercertificates

The least congruence generated by the defining pairs can be described by finite chains of contextual substitutions, allowing either orientation of each defining pair. Left or right context may be empty, while every word being related remains in \(A^+\).

The finite-chain relation is:

1. an equivalence relation;
2. stable under adjoining left and right word contexts;
3. inclusive of the defining pairs;
4. contained in every congruence containing those pairs.

It is therefore exactly \(\equiv_q\).

Accordingly, if the proposed certificate supplies:

- the required source and its stated regularity;
- a pure onto \(q\);
- distinct \(s,t\) with \(q(s)=q(t)\);
- a valid finite defining-relation derivation from \((1,s)\) to \((1,t)\);

then every homomorphism over \(q\) has equal images on \(s,t\). No band host over \(q\) can embed the source. For the regular-source setting, actor transport then excludes embeddings with any other group actor as well.

This is a valid countercertificate **format**. S1 supplies no such collision, does not claim an algorithm or efficiency bound, and does not identify this presentation with an unspecified canonical construction.

## 5. Unit-regular monoids: equivalences and the full isomorphism

### 5.1 Unit restriction is injective

For a monoid \(M\), the group-valued homomorphism sends \(1_M\) to \(1_G\) and restricts to a group homomorphism on \(U(M)\).

A unit in its kernel is idempotent by purity. An idempotent unit is \(1_M\). Hence

\[
q|_{U(M)}
\]

is injective.

### 5.2 The three equivalent conditions

If \(q(U(M))=G\), the restriction is a group isomorphism. Let \(u_g\) be the unique unit with image \(g\). For \(g=q(s)\),

\[
q(su_g^{-1})=1.
\]

Thus \(e_s=su_g^{-1}\) is idempotent and

\[
s=e_su_g.
\]

This proves \(M=E(M)U(M)\).

Conversely, if every element has the form \(eu\), then \(q(eu)=q(u)\). Surjectivity of \(q\) gives \(q(U(M))=G\).

For \(s=eu\),

\[
su^{-1}s=eu\,u^{-1}eu=e^2u=s,
\]

so \(M=E(M)U(M)\) implies unit regularity.

If \(svs=s\) for a unit \(v\), then

\[
(sv)^2=svsv=sv,
\]

and

\[
s=(sv)v^{-1}.
\]

Thus unit regularity implies \(M=E(M)U(M)\). All three conditions are equivalent, with no finiteness assumption.

### 5.3 Conjugation on the actual band

The unique lifts obey

\[
u_gu_h=u_{gh},\qquad u_1=1_M.
\]

The actual \(E(M)\) is a band by purity. Define

\[
\alpha_g(e)=u_geu_g^{-1}.
\]

Conjugation preserves products and idempotents, and conjugation by \(u_g^{-1}\) is its inverse. Moreover,

\[
\alpha_g\alpha_h(e)
=u_gu_heu_h^{-1}u_g^{-1}
=\alpha_{gh}(e).
\]

Therefore this is a genuine action by band automorphisms. No interchange of two idempotents has been used.

### 5.4 Consuming multiplication and both inverse compositions

Write

\[
F(s)=\bigl(su_{q(s)}^{-1},q(s)\bigr).
\]

For \(q(s)=g\), \(q(t)=h\), put \(s=e_su_g\), \(t=e_tu_h\). Then

\[
st=e_su_ge_tu_h
   =e_s\alpha_g(e_t)u_{gh}.
\]

Consequently,

\[
F(st)=\bigl(e_s\alpha_g(e_t),gh\bigr)=F(s)F(t).
\]

The proposed inverse is

\[
\Psi(e,g)=eu_g.
\]

Its compositions are explicitly

\[
\Psi F(s)
=(su_g^{-1})u_g=s
\]

and

\[
F\Psi(e,g)
=F(eu_g)
=\bigl((eu_g)u_g^{-1},g\bigr)
=(e,g).
\]

Thus \(F\) is a two-sided isomorphism

\[
M\cong E(M)\rtimes_\alpha G
\]

with projection exactly \(q\).

The distinction at S1 LF495 is correct: failure of \(q(U(M))=G\) prevents this full realization but is not a general obstruction to a proper source embedding.
