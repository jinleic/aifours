## Independent proof of the spatial and parameter rule

### Depth and face geometry

The path bits bisect axes `0,1,...,5,0,...` in order. At any finite depth `d>=6`, each spatial interval has been bisected at least once; it is a positive-length dyadic subinterval of either `[0,1/2]` or `[1/2,1]`. Hence each interval has at least one endpoint in `(0,1)`: an interval touching 0 has its other endpoint strictly between 0 and 1, an interval touching 1 likewise, and an interval touching neither has both endpoints there. Choosing one such endpoint per coordinate gives at least one vertex of L in `(0,1)^6`.

Conversely, for this exact cyclic tree, at depth `<6` at least one axis is still `[0,1]`; neither of its endpoints is in `(0,1)`, so L has **no** fully admissible spatial vertex. Thus the rule's explicit nonvacuity clause would fail there. Depth 6 is the first depth uniformly guaranteeing the required geometry; the rule does not say that every `R` leaf must have depth 6, nor that no other mathematical positivity test could ever handle a shallower leaf.

Let F be any spatial face of L meeting `(0,1)^6`. Every coordinate fixed by F is fixed at an endpoint in `(0,1)`; on each free coordinate choose an endpoint in `(0,1)`, which the preceding paragraph guarantees. These choices give a vertex `e` of F that is admissible in all six coordinates. The same argument applies to each admissible subface, including zero-dimensional ones. It applies separately on either side of a shared dyadic face. A face fixing even one coordinate to 0 or 1 has no actual finite-positive point, although a face not so fixed may have some excluded boundary points in its closure.

### Bernstein proof, including all z endpoints

Let `u_i=(s_i-a_i)/(b_i-a_i)` and, when `z_-<z_+`, `v=(z-z_-)/(z_+-z_-)`. For spatial degrees `n_i>=0` and parameter degree `m>=0`, write

`P(s,z) = sum_{k,j} c_{k,j} [prod_i B^{n_i}_{k_i}(u_i)] B^m_j(v)`,

where `B^n_k(t)=binom(n,k)t^k(1-t)^(n-k)`. On `[0,1]` all basis terms are nonnegative and their sum on each axis is 1. Assume every `c_{k,j}>=0`, and at every spatial vertex e with all coordinates in `(0,1)` every vertex coefficient `c_{e,j}>0`, for every j. A degree-zero spatial axis causes no exception: its only basis function is 1 and both geometric endpoint choices correspond to its sole index.

For any `s in L intersect (0,1)^6`, choose a vertex e coordinatewise as follows: if `s_i` is a leaf endpoint, choose that same endpoint; otherwise choose any interior-to-`(0,1)` endpoint of that leaf interval. Thus e is fully admissible. Its spatial vertex-basis product `B_e(s)` is strictly positive: at a fixed leaf endpoint the matching endpoint factor is 1, and at a coordinate in the open leaf interval either endpoint factor is positive. All other tensor terms are nonnegative, so

`P(s,z) >= B_e(s) sum_j c_{e,j} B^m_j(v) >= B_e(s) min_j c_{e,j} > 0`.

The minimum is over a finite nonempty set. With exact rational coefficients, or certified positive rational lower enclosures, it gives a positive rational coefficient bound; the extra spatial factor need not be uniformly bounded away from zero on the entire closed leaf. The same selected e works for every z at a fixed s. At `v=0` only the first parameter basis term survives, and at `v=1` only the last survives; their coefficients are strictly positive by the rule. For `m=0` the single term is 1. Thus neither endpoint of J is silently removed. The argument for a singleton J is the same after direct restriction to that point.

Equivalently, for an admissible face F, choose the admissible vertex e constructed above. Its spatial vertex basis is strictly positive throughout the relative interior of F and its coefficient is strictly positive for every z index. At an arbitrary point on the boundary of F, apply the argument to the smaller face containing that point in its relative interior. This proves strictness on every actual point of every internal face and admissible subface, not on just sampled interiors. The full coefficient condition also proves `P>=0` everywhere on the entire closed leaf x J, including excluded compactification faces.

The rule is **sufficient, not necessary**. It does not follow that each positive polynomial has all-positive admissible vertex/parameter coefficients, or that a failed sufficient label excludes a cell. Nor does the argument supply one positive rational lower bound uniformly over a closed leaf that touches an excluded outer face. On a compact subset contained in the actual spatial domain and J, continuity does give a positive minimum, but that is a different assertion.

## Exact paper counterexamples and intended reasons

These are independently evaluated algebraic examples, not executed scientific controls or claims about the actual Q/Hpol. They use `J=[0,1]`; the nondegenerate interval examples apply after affine rescaling of any J.

1. **Excluded boundary zero is allowed.** Take the depth-six lower leaf `L=[0,1/2]^6`, `P=s_1`, spatial degree 1 in `s_1` and degree 0 in the other coordinates, and parameter degree 0. The spatial coefficients are `0,1/2`, hence nonnegative. The unique fully admissible spatial vertex is `(1/2,...,1/2)`, and its coefficient is `1/2>0`. Nevertheless `P=0` on the excluded face `s_1=0` and its infimum on actual points of L is 0. This is exactly why the old whole-closed-leaf uniform positive lower bound cannot be imposed on this `pos` rule.
2. **A single positive coefficient does not protect an admissible internal face.** On the depth-six leaf `L=[1/2,1] x [0,1/2]^5`, set `P=2s_1-1`, with degree 1 in `s_1` and degree 0 otherwise. Its two spatial coefficients are `0,1`, so all are nonnegative and one is strictly positive. But at the actual point `(1/2,1/4,...,1/4)` on the internal lower face, `P=0`. Its admissible vertex `(1/2,1/2,...,1/2)` has coefficient 0, so the stated vertex rule rejects it for the intended reason.
3. **The parameter endpoint cannot be discarded.** On `L=[0,1/2]^6`, set `P=z` on `J=[0,1]`, spatial degree 0 and parameter degree 1. Its parameter coefficients are `0,1`: it is positive for `z>0` but vanishes at `z=0` even at every actual spatial point. The all-indices vertex condition correctly rejects it. The same construction with an independently varying dual coordinate shows why an omitted endpoint of a dual box cannot be assumed strict.
4. **The rule is conservative.** Let `P=(1-z)^2+z^2`, independent of spatial variables. In degree 2 its parameter coefficients are `1,0,1`, all nonnegative, but the middle coefficient fails the all-indices vertex strictness rule. Nevertheless `P=2(z-1/2)^2+1/2>=1/2` on the full closed J. Thus rejection is not a counterexample to positivity.
5. **Endpoint signs alone require an additional structural argument.** The polynomial `h(a)=(a-1/2)^2-1/8` has `h(0)=h(1)=1/8>0` but `h(1/2)=-1/8`. For an independently varying dual interval, positive endpoint values alone do not prove positivity in its interior for an arbitrary polynomial. If a checked expression is separately affine in two normalized independent dual variables, its four corner values instead combine with the four nonnegative bilinear weights, so positive corner values do prove positivity throughout that rectangle. The actual MC expressions and any required affine/interval argument are not in the supplied sources and are not verified here.
6. **R3's strong strict test is not necessary at infinity.** For a generic compactified coordinate `s in [0,1)`, `p=-(1-s)` is strictly negative at every finite s, but its degree-one coefficients on `[0,1]` are `-1,0` and it vanishes at the artificial face `s=1`. Hence an all-coefficient-strict-negative rule can reject a valid finite-domain inequality without proving the inequality false. This is an illustration, not an identity for the actual R3 polynomial.
