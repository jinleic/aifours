## 3. Independent reconstruction

### 3.1 Product target and the common-projector variational principle

For a pure one-qubit input, the output eigenvalues are

\[
a=\frac{1+t}{2},\qquad b=\frac{1-t}{2},\qquad a\ge b\ge0.
\]

Thus a pure four-qubit product input has largest eigenvalue \(a^4\), followed by the fourfold value \(a^3b\). Its top-three sum is

\[
R_3=a^4+2a^3b
=\frac{(1+t)^3(3-t)}{16}
=\frac{3+8t+6t^2-t^4}{16}.
\]

Degeneracies at \(t=0\) and \(t=1\) do not alter that sum.

For any Hermitian \(H\), diagonalize it with eigenvalues \(h_1\ge\cdots\ge h_{16}\). In this basis, a complex Hermitian rank-three projector has diagonal entries in \([0,1]\) summing to three. Consequently,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(QH)\le h_1+h_2+h_3,
\]

and the spectral projector onto three leading eigenvectors attains equality. Therefore

\[
K_3(H)=\max_{\operatorname{rank}Q=3}\operatorname{Tr}(QH).
\]

Applying this formula to a sum at the **same** arbitrary \(Q\), and then maximizing, gives subadditivity. Positive homogeneity is used only for nonnegative scalar coefficients.

### 3.2 Pauli identities and \(A_2=s+2+4\tau\)

For Pauli strings \(P\), write their coefficients as \(x_P=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho P)\). These are real because both \(\rho\) and \(P\) are Hermitian. Pauli orthogonality gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(PQ)=16\delta_{P,Q}.
\]

With \(A_w=\sum_{\mathrm{wt}(P)=w}x_P^2\), purity yields

\[
1+s+A_2+A_3+A_4=16.
\]

The sum of one-qubit marginal purities is

\[
\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_i^2)=2+\frac{s}{2}.
\]

For the four complementary three-qubit marginals, a weight-one coefficient is counted three times, a weight-two coefficient twice, and a weight-three coefficient once. Hence their summed purities are

\[
\frac{4+3s+2A_2+A_3}{8}.
\]

A pure state’s two marginals across a bipartition have the same nonzero eigenvalues, including for complex amplitudes. Equating these sums gives

\[
A_3=12+s-2A_2,
\qquad
A_4=3-2s+A_2.
\]

For each single-qubit nonidentity Pauli matrix,

\[
Y\sigma^TY=-\sigma.
\]

Thus \(S(A)=WA^TW\), \(W=Y^{\otimes4}\), multiplies a weight-\(w\) Pauli string by \((-1)^w\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
16\tau
&=1-s+A_2-A_3+A_4\\
&=4A_2-4s-8,
\end{aligned}
\]

and consequently

\[
\boxed{A_2=s+2+4\tau}.
\]

Moreover, \(S(\rho)\) is the rank-one projector onto \(W\overline\psi\), so

\[
\tau=|\langle\psi|W\overline\psi\rangle|^2\in[0,1].
\]

This proof uses complementary-marginal **purity**, not an erasure-channel Ky Fan premise.

### 3.3 Exact ordering of the first correlation layer

The four local terms in \(C_1\) commute. Their eigenvalues are the signed sums of the radii. Put \(R=r_1+r_2+r_3+r_4\), with radii ordered descending.

The three largest signed sums are

\[
R,\qquad R-2r_4,\qquad R-2r_3.
\]

Indeed, a nonempty sign-flip subset has cost at least \(r_4\). Apart from that first minimum, its cost is at least \(r_3\): the only subset using no radius at least \(r_3\) is the single smallest-radius subset, with zero-radius ties causing equality rather than a different sum.

It follows that

\[
K_3(C_1)=3R-2r_3-2r_4
=3r_1+3r_2+r_3+r_4=L.
\]

This remains valid with repeated or zero radii. Cauchy–Schwarz then gives

\[
L^2\le(9+9+1+1)s=20s.
\]

### 3.4 The coefficient \(39\)

Since \(C_2\) is traceless,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(QC_2)
=\operatorname{Tr}\left[\left(Q-\frac3{16}I\right)C_2\right].
\]

For a rank-three orthogonal projector,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}\left(Q-\frac3{16}I\right)^2
=3-\frac{18}{16}+\frac9{16}
=\frac{39}{16}.
\]

Also \(\operatorname{Tr}(C_2^2)=16A_2\). Hermitian Hilbert–Schmidt Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives, for every complex \(Q\),

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(QC_2)\le\sqrt{39A_2}
=\sqrt{39(s+2+4\tau)}=c.
\]

Maximization proves \(K_3(C_2)\le c\). No entrywise-real assumption is present.

### 3.5 Exact decomposition and signed \(B\) spectrum

For \(0\le t\le1\), let

\[
\alpha=\frac{t^3(1+t)}2,\qquad
\beta=\frac{t^3(1-t)}2.
\]

Since \(\alpha-\beta=t^4\) and \(\alpha+\beta=t^3\),

\[
B=\alpha\rho-\beta\widetilde\rho
=\frac1{16}\left[t^4(I+C_2+C_4)+t^3(C_1+C_3)\right].
\]

Adding the three displayed residual layers gives exactly

\[
\Lambda_{1-t}(\rho)
=
B+\frac{1-t^4}{16}I
+\frac{t-t^3}{16}C_1
+\frac{t^2-t^4}{16}C_2.
\]

There is no omitted higher-order or perturbative remainder.

For distinct \(\psi\) and \(W\overline\psi\), the restriction of \(B\) to their span has trace \(t^4\) and determinant

\[
-\alpha\beta(1-\tau).
\]

Its discriminant is

\[
t^8+4\alpha\beta(1-\tau)
=t^6\left[1-(1-t^2)\tau\right].
\]

Hence, with \(d=\sqrt{1-(1-t^2)\tau}\),

\[
\lambda_\pm=\frac{t^4\pm t^3d}{2}.
\]

Because

\[
d^2-t^2=(1-t^2)(1-\tau)\ge0,
\]

one has \(\lambda_-\le0\le\lambda_+\). There are at least fourteen zero eigenvalues, so the top-three sum is precisely

\[
K_3(B)=\lambda_+.
\]

The degeneracies are direct, not assumptions:

- \(t=0\): \(B=0\).
- \(t=1\): \(\beta=0\), \(B=\rho\).
- \(\tau=1\): the two projectors coincide, \(B=t^4\rho\), and the displayed \(\lambda_-\) is zero.

Thus all rank drops are covered.

### 3.6 Ky Fan bound and universal structural certificate

All residual coefficients in the exact decomposition are nonnegative on \([0,1]\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
16K_3(\Lambda_{1-t}(\rho))
&\le16\lambda_+
+3(1-t^4)+L(t-t^3)+c(t^2-t^4)\\
&=3+Lt+ct^2+(8d-L)t^3+(5-c)t^4.
\end{aligned}
\]

Subtracting from the exact product target gives

\[
16(R_3-K_3)
\ge
t(1-t^2)\bigl[8-L-(c-6)t\bigr]+8t^3(1-d).
\]

For \(x\in[0,1]\),

\[
\sqrt{1-x}\le1-\frac{x}{2}.
\]

This follows by squaring the nonnegative right-hand side; the difference of squares is \(x^2/4\). Apply it to \(x=(1-t^2)\tau\). The preceding bound becomes

\[
16(R_3-K_3)\ge t(1-t^2)f(t),
\]

where

\[
f(t)=8-L-(c-6)t+4\tau t^2.
\]

Now

\[
\begin{aligned}
c^2-(6+8\tau)^2
&=39s+42+60\tau-64\tau^2\\
&\ge38.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last quadratic is concave, and its endpoint values on \([0,1]\) are \(42\) and \(38\). Thus \(c>6+8\tau\), and

\[
f'(t)=-(c-6)+8\tau t<0.
\]

Consequently,

\[
f(t)\ge f(1)=14-L+4\tau-c=\kappa.
\]

Since \(t(1-t^2)\ge0\),

\[
\boxed{
K_3(\Lambda_{1-t}(\rho))
\le R_3(t)-\frac{\kappa}{16}t(1-t^2).
}
\]

No positivity assumption on \(\kappa\) was used. In particular, the inequality remains valid when \(\kappa<0\); only its interpretation as a sufficient certificate for \(K_3\le R_3\) is then unavailable.

### 3.7 Strict uniform certificate for \(s\le1/4\)

Under this hypothesis,

\[
L\le\sqrt5<\frac94,
\qquad
c^2\le\frac{351}{4}+156\tau.
\]

The exact comparison is

\[
\left(\frac{93}{8}+4\tau\right)^2
-\left(\frac{351}{4}+156\tau\right)
=\frac{3033}{64}-63\tau+16\tau^2.
\]

Its derivative is negative throughout \([0,1]\), and its endpoint value at \(1\) is \(25/64\). Both quantities being compared are nonnegative, so

\[
c<\frac{93}{8}+4\tau.
\]

It follows that

\[
\kappa
=14-L+4\tau-c
>14-\frac94-\frac{93}{8}
=\frac18.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
K_3(\Lambda_{1-t}(\rho))
\le R_3(t)-\frac{t(1-t^2)}{128}.
}
\]

For \(0<t<1\), this is a strictly positive certified gap below the product target. At \(t=0\) and \(t=1\), the displayed gap vanishes, as it must.

For \(s=0\), the additional source claim is also valid. The derivative of

\[
14+4\tau-\sqrt{78+156\tau}
\]

is \(4-78/\sqrt{78+156\tau}<0\) throughout \([0,1]\). Its minimum is therefore \(18-\sqrt{234}>0\).

### 3.8 Physical range and the consuming complex projector

Set \(q=1-p\). The channel has the Pauli-mixture expression with coefficients

\[
r_I=\frac{1+3q}{4},\qquad
r_X=r_Y=r_Z=\frac{1-q}{4}.
\]

This expression follows from

\[
XAX+YAY+ZAZ=2\operatorname{Tr}(A)I-A.
\]

The normalized Choi matrix is diagonal in the four orthogonal Pauli-generated Bell states, with these four coefficients as eigenvalues. Complete positivity therefore holds precisely for

\[
-\frac13\le q\le1,
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
0\le p\le\frac43.
\]

The spin flip \(S(A)=WA^TW\) changes each Pauli weight by \((-1)^w\), so

\[
\Lambda_{1+t}(\rho)=S(\Lambda_{1-t}(\rho)).
\]

This is a **full transpose** followed by unitary conjugation, not a partial transpose. It preserves eigenvalues. The physical negative-\(q\) branch requires only \(0\le t\le1/3\).

The observable’s consuming-side transport is also correct. Four factors of \(Y^T=-Y\) give \(W^T=W\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Tr}(QS(A))
&=\operatorname{Tr}(WQW A^T)\\
&=\operatorname{Tr}((WQW)^T A)\\
&=\operatorname{Tr}(WQ^TW A).
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus

\[
Q'=WQ^TW
\]

is the required transported projector. Transposition preserves its rank and projector identity, and unitary conjugation preserves orthogonality. This verifies the actual complex-\(Q\) dispatch, not merely the output spectrum.

Combining this with the positive-\(q\) proof establishes the stated bounds throughout the physical range.

### 3.9 Endpoints and convex mixtures

At \(t=0\), \(\Lambda(\rho)=I/16\), and every rank-three projector gives \(3/16=R_3(0)\).

At \(t=1\), the physical channel is the identity channel. A pure input has \(K_3=1=R_3(1)\). The \(B\)-spectrum and comparison above remain valid at this endpoint without division by a vanishing factor.

For a mixture

\[
\rho=\sum_jw_j\rho_j
\]

of pure states individually satisfying \(s_j\le1/4\), fix one arbitrary rank-three \(Q\). Linearity gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Tr}(Q\Lambda_p(\rho))
&=\sum_jw_j\operatorname{Tr}(Q\Lambda_p(\rho_j))\\
&\le R_3(t)-\frac{t(1-t^2)}{128}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Maximize only after this sum. This proves the stated convex-hull extension with the actual common projector.

This argument does **not** replace the decomposition condition by small one-qubit Bloch radii of the mixed state itself. The pure-state identity and certificate are not applied directly to an arbitrary mixed state.
