## 2. Three identities already true in \(S_0\)

All band manipulations here use only associativity and \(X^2=X\) for a **nonempty** band word \(X\). They establish equality of coordinates of source words; none applies R1–R4 as a band equation.

### 2.1. Twisted three-letter identities

The semidirect multiplication gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
aa'b&=(A\tau(\tau A)B,1)=(A^2B,1)=(AB,1),\\
ab'b&=(A\tau(\tau B)B,1)=(AB^2,1)=(AB,1).
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence \(aa'b=ab'b\) as actual elements of \(S_0\), both of grade 1. Similarly,

\[
\begin{aligned}
bb'a&=(B\tau(\tau B)A,1)=(B^2A,1)=(BA,1),\\
ba'a&=(B\tau(\tau A)A,1)=(BA^2,1)=(BA,1),
\end{aligned}
\]

so \(bb'a=ba'a\) in \(S_0\), again at grade 1. The contractions \(A^2\to A\) and \(B^2\to B\) are nonempty one-letter square contractions.

### 2.2. A fully spelled out composite-word square calculation

For arbitrary band elements \(x,y,z\), the following is an elementary identity. Arrows display the exact square expanded or contracted; parenthesized words are nonempty:

\[
\begin{aligned}
xyzyxyz
 &= (xy)zy(xy)z\\
 &\longrightarrow (xy)(xy)zy(xy)z
       &&\text{expand the initial }xy\text{ to }(xy)^2\\
 &= x(yxyz)(yxyz)\\
 &\longrightarrow x(yxyz)=xyxyz
       &&\text{contract }(yxyz)^2\\
 &= (xy)(xy)z\\
 &\longrightarrow xyz
       &&\text{contract }(xy)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is an expansion/contraction of literal contiguous words, not the invalid general law \(xyx=xy\) or a regular-band assumption. In our application \(x=E,y=A,z=Z\); the exact seven- and nine-letter strings are

\[
 EAZAEAZ\ \longrightarrow\ EAEAZAEAZ
 =E\,(AEAZ)(AEAZ)\ \longrightarrow\ EAEAZ
 =(EA)(EA)Z\ \longrightarrow\ EAZ.
\]

The first step expands the initial \(EA\), the second contracts \(AEAZ\), and the third contracts \(EA\); all three are nonempty words. Since

\[
eaf=(E A\tau(F_0),1)=(EAZ,1),
\]

and \(a'=(\tau A,1)\), we have

\[
(eaf)a'(eaf)=((EAZ)\,\tau(\tau A)\,(EAZ),1)
             =(EAZAEAZ,1)=(EAZ,1)=eaf.
\]

Both sides have grade 1 and are elements represented by literal words in \(S_0\).

## 3. The least-congruence chain proving \(V=U\)

Let \(\rho\) be the least semigroup congruence on \(S_0\) generated just by the R1 and R4 pairs. The exact chain below takes place in \(S_0\), where \(=_{S_0}\) means genuine prequotient equality and \(\mathrel\rho\) is a contextual use of a defining pair:

\[
\begin{aligned}
V=ebW
 &\mathrel\rho eaa'bW
      &&\text{R1 reversed, right context }bW\in S_0,\\
 &=_{S_0} eab'bW
      &&aa'b=ab'b,\text{ left context }e\text{ and right context }W,\\
 &\mathrel\rho eaW
      &&\text{R4, left context }ea\in S_0,\\

      &&\text{Section 2.2, right context }g\in S_0.
\end{aligned}

Every context just named is a nonempty product of source generators; \(bW\) has grade 1, \(e\) and \(W\) grade 0, \(ea\) grade 1, and \(g\) grade 0. Thus R4 is applied to the original source relation \(b'bW=W\) after multiplying it on the **left by the actual source element \(ea\)**; no translated relation is imposed on the ambient free band. The intermediate products, including \(eaa'bW\) and \(eab'bW\), all have grade 1.

Since R1 and R4 occur among the generators of \(\theta\), minimality gives \(\rho\subseteq\theta\). Consequently the quotient maps form

## 6. What remains of the all-host question

For a precise residual, one can use the following source-first criterion. Its elementary reduction is included so that no unspecified host or printed-source identification is hidden.

1. The original \(S_0\) is regular. Indeed its grade-0 fibre is a band: \((u,0)^2=(u,0)\). Each of the seven generators has an inverse in \(S_0\): \(a'\) is an inverse of \(a\), \(b'\) an inverse of \(b\), and \(e,f,g\) are their own inverses. For example \(aa'a=a\) and \(a'aa'=a'\), since the relevant band coordinate is a cube of \(A\) or \(\tau A\). If \(x',y'\) are inverses of regular elements \(x,y\), put \(p=x'x\) and \(r=yy'\). These are idempotents of grade 0, as are their products \(pr,rp\). Then
   \[
   xy(y'x')xy=xrpy=xprpry=x(pr)^2y=xpry=xy,
   \]
   using \(xp=x\) and \(ry=y\). Similarly,
   \[
   (y'x')xy(y'x')=y'prx'=y'rprpx'=y'(rp)^2x'=y'rpx'=y'x'.
   \]
   Hence \(y'x'\) is an inverse of \(xy\). Induction over source words proves regularity, which passes to \(S\) under the quotient map. Also \(q\) is pure: every grade-0 representative is idempotent, while an idempotent must map to 0 in \(C_2\).

2. If any embedding \(j:S\hookrightarrow\mathcal B\rtimes H\) exists, let \(r:S\to H\) be its actor projection. It is pure: grade-identity elements of \(\mathcal B\rtimes H\) are idempotent, and injectivity reflects idempotence. Because \(S\) is regular, \(r(S)\) is a subgroup. For \(s,t\in S\) choose an inverse \(s'\) of \(s\). If \(q(s)=q(t)\), then \(q(s't)=0\), so \(s't\) is idempotent and \(r(s')r(t)=1\), implying \(r(s)=r(t)\). Conversely, equality of \(r(s),r(t)\) gives \(r(s't)=1\), so purity of \(r\) makes \(s't\) idempotent and therefore \(q(s)=q(t)\). Thus \(q\) and \(r\) have the same kernel, and \(q(s)\mapsto r(s)\) is an isomorphism from \(C_2\) to \(r(S)\). Restricting the action to \(r(S)\) and transporting it along that isomorphism gives an embedding over the prescribed \(q\). This does not claim a map from the whole of \(H\) onto \(C_2\).

3. Let \(C_q\) be the semigroup presented by generators \((h,s)\), \(h\in C_2,s\in S\), all nonempty word-square relations \(XX=X\), and all relations
   \[
   (h,st)=(h,s)(h+q(s),t).
   \]
   It is a band; index translation is a \(C_2\)-action. The map
   \[
   \eta_q(s)=([(0,s)],q(s))\colon S\longrightarrow C_q\rtimes C_2
   \]
   is a homomorphism by the displayed relation. If \(H_0(s)=(u_s,q(s))\) is any homomorphism to a band-by-\(C_2\) host, the evaluation \((h,s)\mapsto h\cdot u_s\) respects the word-square and cocycle relations, so every pair collapsed by \(\eta_q\) is also collapsed by \(H_0\). Consequently \(S\) has an embedding into **some** band-by-group semidirect product if and only if
   \[
   q(s)=q(t)\ \text{and}\ [(0,s)]=[(0,t)]\text{ in }C_q\quad\Longrightarrow\quad s=t
   \quad\text{for all }s,t\in S.
   \]
   The reverse implication uses the actual embedding \(\eta_q\); the forward implication uses item 2 and the universal evaluation. This is a criterion, not a proof of injectivity or of a distinct collapsed pair.
