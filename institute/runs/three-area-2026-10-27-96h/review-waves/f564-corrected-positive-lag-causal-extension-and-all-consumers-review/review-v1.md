## 4. E1 extension, causality, strict upper bound and zero cases

The delayed coefficient at physical noise time \(t\) is \(w_{t-\tau}\), hence uses information no later than \(t-\tau\). It is progressive for the original filtration after zero extension. The shifted representation uses
\[
\beta_s=W_{\tau+s}-W_\tau,
\]
but does not permit choosing \(w_s\) using the larger information set at time \(\tau+s\). This distinction is maintained in every lower construction.

For the pointwise cutoffs \(w^{(k)}=w\,1_{\{|w|\le k\}}\), progressivity and boundedness give E2 admissibility. Their pathwise L2 differences tend to zero and are dominated by the original path norm. Thus
\[
\mathbb E\|w-w^{(k)}\|_T\longrightarrow0
\]
without a second-moment assumption. Applying the imported zero-lag E2 inequality to the delayed difference gives the stated \(r c_H\) Lipschitz estimate on the actual cutoff outputs. They converge in \(L^1(\Omega;L^2_{\mathrm{time}})\).

This Banach limit is not accepted as a surrogate solution. The variation-of-constants martingale has pathwise finite trace bracket, because an E1 input has finite time-energy almost surely. The difference bracket is bounded by a deterministic finite kernel constant times \(\|w-w^{(k)}\|_T^2\), tending to zero almost surely. Stopping at bracket level \(\delta\), Doob L2 bounds the probability of a large supremum by
\[
\mathbb P(A_T^{(k)}>\delta)+4\delta/\eta^2.
\]
Taking \(k\to\infty\), then \(\delta\downarrow0\), proves uniform convergence in probability of the actual local cutoff solutions. Consequently their Hilbert outputs converge in probability to the actual local output, uniquely identifying the L1 limit. The weighted clock is \(c_H\)-Lipschitz, so its expectation passes to the limit. Both expectations in every nonzero cutoff quotient converge, with positive limiting denominator; this proves E1/E2 supremum equality.

For \(c_H>0\), equality in
\[
a^\top G(H-s)a\le a^\top G(H)a\le c_H^2|a|^2
\]
at \(s>0\) would force both equality at the top value and vanishing of \(Ce^{Pv}Ba\) on a nonempty interval. Analytic continuation would force its entire impulse response to vanish, contradicting that top value. The pointwise matrix gap is therefore strict.

Any path with positive input energy has either positive active energy at positive input times or wasted tail energy. In either case,
\[
Q_H(w)<c_H\|w\|_T.
\]
The positive integrable gap on a positive-probability set gives the strict expectation inequality. This rules out attaining \(r c_H\), **not** attaining a possibly smaller \(L_\tau\).

If \(\tau\ge T\), zero history leaves no nonzero-duration applied input. If \(0<\tau<T\), zero \(c_H\) is equivalent to a zero analytic impulse kernel, hence to

## 7. Lag regularity, margins and attainment

For \(\tau_1<\tau_2\), remove a \(\tau_2\)-input’s unused tail and delay its input profile by \(\tau_2-\tau_1\). This remains progressive, preserves the active input norm and produces exactly the same physical applied-noise coefficient under lag \(\tau_1\). The gain is therefore nonincreasing. Zero-output lags are consistent with this statement.

For any fixed E1 input, zero-extended time translations converge pathwise in L2 and are bounded by twice its path norm, giving \(L^1(\Omega;L^2)\) convergence. The zero-lag E1 bound applied to the difference of the actual delayed coefficients makes the outputs continuous in delay. Each fixed-input ratio is continuous. Their supremum is lower semicontinuous; combined with nonincreasing dependence, this gives right continuity. No uniform-in-input continuity or unjustified interchange of supremum and limit is assumed.

The upper estimate \(r c_{T-\tau}\) gives the limit zero as \(\tau\uparrow T\). At zero lag, the **separate exact-value import** gives \(L_0=r c_T\). Continuity for each fixed nearly sharp zero-lag input supplies the matching lower limit as \(\tau\downarrow0\).

The strict-feasibility cases follow from the supremum definition:

- At the actual critical value, feasibility holds exactly when no actual input attains the supremum.
- A uniform normalized margin requires strictly larger \(\gamma\).
- Zero-output gain is attained by every nonzero input, so \(\gamma=0\) fails.
- In the nonzero general case, the strict deterministic lower bound makes \(\gamma\le\kappa c_H\) fail.
- The strict clock inequality makes \(\gamma=r c_H\) pointwise feasible without establishing a uniform margin there.
- In the long-lag case, \(D_H<c_H\) supplies a uniform margin at \(c_H\) and \(r c_H\).

For long-lag attainment, equality of a random input’s ratio with \(D_H\) forces both the conditional deterministic gap and unused-tail gap to vanish almost surely. An actual attaining deterministic profile is therefore necessary, and is sufficient by taking that profile as input.

Compactness of the larger measure space alone does not settle this representation question. The candidate correctly leaves actual nonattainment and critical feasibility unresolved rather than mistaking strictness below \(r c_H\) for strictness below \(L_\tau\).
