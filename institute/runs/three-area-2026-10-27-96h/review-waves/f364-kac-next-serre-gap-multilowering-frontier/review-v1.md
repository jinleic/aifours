## 4. Proof of Theorem A

Assume \(r<s\), \(r\le3\), and its stated hypotheses. Since \(d\le s-2\),
\[
n=t-d\ge r+2\ge3.
\]

Every nonzero-charge letter except \(u_1\) satisfies \(rc+se\ge s\). For a target with nonnegative horizontal coordinates,
\[
(n-1)-\bigl[r(a-c)+s(b-e)\bigr]
=rc+se-d-1\ge1.
\]
The target level is at least two, so rootwise saturation applies. Mixed-sign targets vanish separately. The remaining singleton Gram entries are \(G_{00}=G_{11}=1\); hence
\[
\overline A_n=E_3F_3+E_\gamma F_\gamma,
\qquad
E_\gamma=[E_1,E_3],\quad
F_\gamma=[F_3,F_1].
\]

### A finite-type auxiliary positive form

Nodes \(1,3\) satisfy the finite-type rank-two presentation with matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-r&2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad r=1,2,3.
\]
Their adjoint operators on \(S\) therefore define a representation of the corresponding finite-dimensional semisimple algebra \(\mathfrak g_{13}\).

Every vector belongs to a finite-dimensional \(\mathfrak g_{13}\)-submodule:

- Simple raising and lowering adjoints are locally nilpotent by the actual all-Serre input.
- Every rank-two root vector is a finite-Weyl conjugate of a simple root vector, so its action is locally nilpotent.
- A PBW ordering of the **finite-dimensional** root basis has finitely many root factors. Acting from right to left on a fixed weight vector produces finitely many vectors at each stage; Cartan powers only multiply weight vectors by scalars.

This proves local finiteness. It uses ordinary finite-type PBW, not preliminary-\(G_{\mathcal C}\) PBW.

Choose a finite-dimensional submodule \(M\) containing \(x\in S_\alpha\), and average a Hermitian form over the compact real form. The resulting positive form satisfies
\[
E_1^*=F_1,\qquad E_3^*=F_3,
\qquad
E_\gamma^*=[F_3,F_1]=F_\gamma.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\langle x,\overline A_nx\rangle
=\|F_3x\|^2+\|F_\gamma x\|^2.
\]

If \(\overline A_nx=0\), then \(F_3x=0\). But \(x\) has weight
\[
m=t-2d>0.
\]
The adopted elementary simple-string argument says that \(F_3\) is injective at positive weight when \(E_3\) is locally nilpotent. Hence \(x=0\).

Fixed-root finite dimensionality makes \(\overline A_n\) invertible.

This is a positive form on a finite-dimensional **auxiliary rank-two module**, not on the entire nonsymmetrizable algebra. Both complete seed modules and all mixed ideal contributions remain imposed throughout. Node exchange proves the mirrored theorem. ∎

## 5. The actual full-two-seed quotient in horizontal degree \((1,1)\)

This establishes the genuine quotient used in Theorem B for every \(n\), not just the prior band.

Let \(F\) be the ordinary free Lie algebra on \(e_1,e_2,t_0\). Free-Lie elimination gives
\[

\cong
\mathbb Ct_0\ltimes
\operatorname{Lie}\langle X_i,Y_j:i,j\ge0\rangle,
\]
with
\[
[t_0,X_i]=X_{i+1},\qquad [t_0,Y_j]=Y_{j+1}.
\]
The maps sending \(X_i,Y_j\) to the corresponding iterated \(t_0\)-adjoints and fixing \(t_0\) are inverse on generators.

Thus the \((1,1,n)\) component has the independent basis
\[
Z_{i,j}=[X_i,Y_j],\qquad i+j=n,
\]
and dimension \(n+1\).

### Matching the actual source

\[

is an isomorphism on this component. Indeed, eliminating \(u_0\) in the imported actual \(L(V)\) gives exactly

(\operatorname{ad}u_0)^{n-1}u_4,

\qquad p+q=n-2.

For \(n\ge1\), these are \(n+1\) independent vectors and exhaust the charge. At \(n=1\), only the first two occur. At \(n=0\), both presentations have the one-dimensional Levi root \([e_1,e_2]\).

### Both complete seed modules and their whole ideal

Let \(H\) be the ideal of \(F\) generated by \(X_{r+1},Y_{s+1}\). Because positive Levi raising words generate the complete modules from their lowest raw seeds,
\[
\pi(H)=I,\qquad \pi^{-1}(I)=H+\ker\pi.

The other fixed positive Serres are already accounted for by \(\ker\pi\) and the component isomorphism.

This proves the basis, dimension, and \(n>r+s\) zero-space assertions.
