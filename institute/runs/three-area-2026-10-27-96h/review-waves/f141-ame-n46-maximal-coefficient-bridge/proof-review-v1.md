## 1. Reconstruction of the residual setup (candidate §2)

For any 2|2 cut, the 36×36 flattening M of a normalized AME(4,6) state has MM† = I/36, so U = 6M is unitary. An entry of ψ with modulus 1/6 is an entry of U with modulus 1; since each row and column of U has norm 1, it is the unique nonzero of its row and of its column, and two maximal entries cannot share a row or column. After permutation U = (t×t diagonal of phases) ⊕ V with V an n×n unitary, n = 36−t, whose support is exactly the E positions; so nnz(V) = |E| = N − t = 46 − t. A row (column) of V with a single nonzero would have modulus 1, i.e. be a maximal coefficient, so every row and column of V has degree ≥2. Reading the rows of V as the (i,j)-pair values not used by P: the P words are pair-injective in every pair (singleton rows), the n unused pair values each carry a non-empty residual row, and E never uses a P pair value; hence each of the six pair projections of E is exactly the n-cell complement of P's projection, every cell having ≥2 extensions. Over the three cuts the row side and column side together cover all six pairs. Table (t,n,|E|,excess) = (27,9,19,1), (28,8,18,2), (29,7,17,3) verified. **Correct.**

## 2. Reconstruction of the support lemmas (candidate §3, sources 2 and 3)

* **L-A (no unique overlap).** Two distinct rows of a unitary sharing exactly one nonzero column have a one-term, hence nonzero, inner product. Same for columns. Correct.
* **L-B (square components).** For a connected component with row set R and column set C, the rows of U[R,C] are the full rows restricted to their whole support, hence orthonormal in C^{|C|} ⇒ |R| ≤ |C|; symmetrically |C| ≤ |R|. So U[R,C] is square and unitary. Correct.
* **L-C (excess 0 ⇒ 2×2).** All degrees exactly 2 ⇒ 2-regular connected bipartite graph = one even cycle; a cycle of length ≥6 contains two adjacent rows {c_k,c_1},{c_1,c_2} sharing only c_1, violating L-A. So a 4-cycle, i.e. a full 2×2 block. Correct.
* **L-D (degree-two pairs disjoint in a connected component, m≥3).** Sharing one column violates L-A; identical pairs {a,b} give two orthonormal vectors spanning C² on {a,b}, forcing every other row to vanish on a,b, so {ρ1,ρ2}×{a,b} is a component, contradicting m≥3. Hence 2s ≤ m = s+b, s ≤ b; with excess r each higher row contributes ≥1 so b ≤ r and m ≤ 2r. Correct.
* **L-E (each pair met by ≥2 higher rows).** Column a has degree ≥2, so ρ has a neighbor; any neighbor uses both a,b (else unique overlap); neighbors are higher-degree rows (L-D). If σ were the only one, columns a,b would be orthonormal and supported on {ρ,σ} only, so [ρ,σ]×[a,b] has orthonormal columns ⇒ unitary ⇒ σ's restriction has norm 1 ⇒ deg σ = 2, contradiction (and the pair would split off). Correct; this is the step that is genuinely algebraic rather than a support-graph filter.

**Excess one.** Positive excess forces m≥3 (m=1 violates degree ≥2, m=2 with degrees ≥2 is full with excess 0). r=1 ⇒ m ≤ 2r = 2. Contradiction. Globally, component excesses are nonnegative integers summing to 1, so one component has excess exactly 1: impossible. **Verdict: correct for all n, self-contained in the candidate** (no dependence on the lower44 file).

**Excess two.** Component excesses sum to 2 with no part equal to 1 ⇒ exactly one component of excess 2, rest full 2×2. m ≤ 4. m=4: s ≤ b ≤ 2 and s+b=4 ⇒ s=b=2; nnz = 10 so the two higher rows have degrees summing to 6, each ≥3 ⇒ both 3; the two disjoint pairs cover all four columns and a higher row meets each pair in 0 or 2 columns ⇒ even degree. Contradiction. So m=3, nnz = 8, and n = 3 + 2k is odd. I also reconstructed the support source's alternative degree-pattern proof (one degree-4 row: the two disjoint pairs, the C²-span uniqueness of the degree-two row per pair, and the 2×2 norm exhaustion contradicting R's entries in c,d; two degree-3 rows: share none ⇒ a column of degree 1; share two ⇒ the third column of R has degree 1 via the unique overlap with S; share three ⇒ exactly one degree-two row and a 3×3 block with eight entries). Both proofs are correct. The sharpness matrix (1/2)[[√2,√2,0],[1,−1,√2],[1,−1,−√2]] was verified orthonormal with exactly 8 nonzeros in **exact** Q(√2) arithmetic (control C-EXP). **Verdict: correct for all n.** For the bridge only the parity consequence (even n impossible) is used.

**Excess three.** Component excesses sum to 3 with no part 1 ⇒ one component of excess 3 (2+1 is excluded because 1 is impossible; two positive parts would be ≥2+2). m ≤ 2r = 6.
* m=4: (s,b) ∈ {(2,2),(1,3)} since s ≤ b ≤ 3 (b=4 would give excess ≥4). (2,2): higher degrees sum to 11−4 = 7, i.e. {3,4}; the degree-3 row meets each covering pair in 0 or 2 ⇒ even. ✗. ## 3. The three t cases

* **t=27:** n=9, |E| = 19 = 2·9+1 ⇒ excess one ⇒ impossible. Needs only one cut. **Correct, unconditional.**
* **t=28:** n=8, |E| = 18 = 2·8+2 ⇒ n must be odd, but 8 is even ⇒ impossible. Needs only one cut. **Correct, unconditional.**
* **t=29:** n=7, |E| = 17 = 2·7+3 ⇒ in every cut V = 3×3 ⊕ 2×2 ⊕ 2×2, so every one of the six E pair projections has 7 cells with 2 or 3 extensions each.
  * *Fiber variation.* With A_i the residual alphabet at coordinate i (r = |A_i|) and F_a the fiber of a ∈ A_i: if F_a varied in at most one other coordinate j, any cell (a,b) of the (i,j) projection would be extended only by words agreeing in i, j and in the two constant coordinates, i.e. by one word — contradicting ≥2 (if F_a is constant everywhere, |F_a| = 1, same contradiction). So each fiber varies in ≥2 other coordinates. For fixed j, Σ_a m_a(j) = 7 with m_a(j) ≥ 1 gives #{a : m_a(j) ≥ 2} ≤ 7−r. Summing over the three other coordinates: 2r ≤ 3(7−r), r ≤ 4. (Exact table in control C1: r = 1..4 pass, 5, 6 fail.)
  * *r=4 excluded.* Each fiber varies in some j, so two distinct cells (a,b1),(a,b2) each with ≥2 disjoint extensions give |F_a| ≥ 4; a coordinate constant on F_a would produce a cell with ≥4 extensions, contradicting ≤3; so all three other coordinates vary in all four fibers and the (i,j) projection has ≥ 4·2 = 8 > 7 cells. ✗. Hence r_i ≤ 3 for every i, and r_i r_j ≥ 7 with r_j ≤ 3 forces r_i ≥ 3. **All four alphabets have size exactly 3.** The degree-≤3 bound is load-bearing here: control C-DEG shows that with the cell cap relaxed to 5 (no |E| constraint) the profile (3,3,3,4) becomes satisfiable at the E-only level (witness of 19 words, independently rechecked), whereas with cap 3 every profile with some r_i ≥ 4 (all 44 sorted profiles with r_i r_j ≥ 7, both with and without |E| = 17) is UNSAT (control C2, z3, supplementary only — no independently checked proof certificate).
  * *Active count.* P is pair-injective; for party i and any other party j the 18 pairs with first symbol in A_i contain all 7 E cells and therefore exactly 11 distinct P pairs, i.e. exactly 11 P words active at i; for parties i,j the 9 pairs of A_i × A_j contain the 7 E cells and exactly 2 P pairs, i.e. exactly 2 P words active at both. So Σ_P k = 4·11 = 44 and Σ_P C(k,2) = 6·2 = 12. Since k − C(k,2) ∈ {0,1,1,0,−2} for k = 0..4 has maximum 1, Σ_P [k − C(k,2)] = 32 ≤ |P| = 29 is false. ✗. Control C1 confirms by exact arithmetic that among all alphabet profiles with r_i r_j ≥ 7 the active-count inequality alone kills **only** (3,3,3,3) (e.g. (3,3,3,4) gives 50−21 = 29 ≤ 29), so the preceding alphabet-size reduction is essential and not decorative; the E-only constraints for (3,3,3,3) are satisfiable (17-word witness, rechecked), so the P-side count is likewise essential. Control C-XC reproduces, with the same formula, the prior t=30 disposition's published right-hand sides 30 and 33 for n=6 — a positive cross-check of my reading of the identity.
  * **Verdict: t=29 is excluded; correct, unconditional.** MINOR-1: the one-line justification "some occurring pair would have only one extension" is correct but compressed; the explicit reason is that the only varying coordinate j together with i determines the word when the other two coordinates are constant.
