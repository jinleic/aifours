## 5. Actual kernel: exact dimension and its limits

For \(r=3,s\ge4\), the free degree-four coefficient at \(X^2Y^2\) is
\[
 \tfrac14\left(\sum_{k=0}^2{4\choose k}{4\choose 2-k}^2-6\right)
 =\tfrac14(36+64+6-6)=25.
\]
Subtracting the **two-dimensional whole relation weight** from §4 gives \(\dim S_{2,2,4}=23\). At grade three no seed is yet present, and
\[
 \operatorname{ch}L_3(V)=\tfrac13\{H^3-H(X^3,Y^3)\};\qquad
 [X^2Y^2]H^3=9+27+3=39.
\]
Thus \(\dim S_{2,2,3}=13\). These are component dimensions, not \(F_3\)-ranks.

A separate actual-map argument yields surjectivity. In the whole Serre quotient, \(E_3,F_3,H_3=\operatorname{ad}h_3\) form an \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\)-triple and both \(E_3,F_3\) are locally nilpotent: this is immediate on every Chevalley generator and the full Cartan from the **positive and negative** Serre relations and the Chevalley brackets, and extends to each finite Lie word by the derivation rule. For a root-homogeneous vector, the ordinary PBW ordering for this classical \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\), together with local nilpotence of \(E_3,F_3\), gives a finite-dimensional generated \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\)-module. Finite-dimensional \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) theory implies that \(F_3\) maps weight \(m+2\) **onto** weight \(m\) whenever \(m<0\): an irreducible vector at a negative weight cannot be highest.

More precisely, on \(S_{a,b,n}\), the \(h_3\)-weight is \(m=2n-ra-sb\), and the \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) action preserves the horizontal pair \((a,b)\). Hence whenever \(2n-ra-sb<0\), the actual map
\[
 F_3:S_{a,b,n+1}\longrightarrow S_{a,b,n}
\]
is surjective. For \(r=3,(a,b,n)=(2,2,3)\), \(m=-2s<0\), giving actual rank 13 and
\[
 \dim\ker(F_3:S_{2,2,4}\to S_{2,2,3})=23-13=10.
\]
This does **not** use a character to determine rank. It uses actual full-sign Serres, ordinary finite-dimensional \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) theory, and the two independently established dimensions.

The full lowest space at \((2,2,4)\) has dimension 7 by the exact quotient multiplicity in §4. Therefore the kernel cannot be contained in it: at least \(10-7=3\) kernel directions survive modulo the lowest space. Nor does the one equation \(F_3v=0\) establish \(v\in\mathcal R\): an ideal must also satisfy all other lowering/raising and Cartan constraints, and its grade-three images under all relevant negative grade-one operators must vanish. We do not claim that any particular kernel vector is radical or nonradical, nor that the seven-dimensional lowest space lies in the kernel. The actual frontier is to compute the **complete** lowest multiplicity spaces and the actual \(F_3\) map on each, including all parameter-dependent seed overlaps; even a nonzero lowest-vector kernel requires a separate ideal-closure/radical analysis.
