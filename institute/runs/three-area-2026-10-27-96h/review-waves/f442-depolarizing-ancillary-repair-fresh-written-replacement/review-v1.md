## 3. Complete reconstruction for finite ancillas

### 3.1 Output identity and complete positivity

For every \(\rho_{AB}\), linearity and partial trace give

\[
R:=(\operatorname{id}_A\otimes\mathcal D_{p,d})(\rho_{AB})
=t\rho_{AB}+b\,\rho_A\otimes I_d,
\qquad
\rho_A=\operatorname{Tr}_B\rho_{AB}.
\]

This agrees with S1 LF186–190.

For the normalized maximally entangled vector

\[
|\Omega_d\rangle
=\frac1{\sqrt d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|j\rangle\otimes|j\rangle,
\]

the normalized Choi operator is

\[
J=t|\Omega_d\rangle\langle\Omega_d|
+\frac{p}{d^2}I_{d^2}.
\]

Its eigenvalue on the span of \(\Omega_d\) is

\[
t+\frac p{d^2}
=1-p\left(1-\frac1{d^2}\right),
\]

and its eigenvalue on the orthogonal complement is \(p/d^2\). Both are nonnegative precisely on the stated interval. Thus \(\mathcal D_{p,d}\) is completely positive there.

Moreover,

\[
t+db=(1-p)+p=1,
\]

so the map is trace-preserving. Consequently,

\[
R\ge0,\qquad \operatorname{Tr}R=1.
\]

This positivity check is essential in the \(t<0\) regime: negativity of the coefficient of \(\rho_{AB}\) does not undermine positivity of the channel output.

### 3.2 The reference is a probability vector

Throughout the interval,

\[
b\ge0,
\]

and

\[
\alpha
=1-\frac{d-1}{d}p
\ge
1-\frac{d-1}{d}\frac{d^2}{d^2-1}
=\frac1{d+1}>0.
\]

Also,

\[
\alpha+(d-1)b=t+db=1.
\]

Thus the unsorted reference has nonnegative entries and total mass one.

Its ordering is determined exactly by

\[
\alpha-b=t:
\]

- If \(t\ge0\),

  \[
  q_{p,d}=(t+b,b,\ldots,b).
  \]

- If \(t<0\),

  \[
  q_{p,d}=(\underbrace{b,\ldots,b}_{d-1},t+b).
  \]

Therefore, for \(1\le k<d\),

\[
K_k(q_{p,d})
=
\begin{cases}
t+kb,&t\ge0,\\
kb,&t<0,
\end{cases}
\]

while every prefix of the zero-padded reference of length at least \(d\) is one.

The reversed ordering for \(t<0\) is correctly handled in S1; no assumption that \(\alpha\) remains the largest entry is made.

### 3.3 Bounds valid for every complex orthogonal projector

Let \(P\) be any complex orthogonal rank-\(k\) projector. Since \(\rho_{AB}\) is a density operator,

\[
0\le \operatorname{Tr}(P\rho_{AB})\le1.
\]

Since \(\rho_A\) is also a density operator,

\[
0\le\rho_A\le I_A,
\qquad
0\le S:=\rho_A\otimes I_d\le I_{AB}.
\]

Hence

\[
0\le\operatorname{Tr}(PS)\le\operatorname{Tr}P=k.
\]

These conclusions do not require \(P\) to commute with \(\rho_{AB}\), \(\rho_A\otimes I_d\), or \(R\). For example,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(P\rho_{AB})
=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(\rho_{AB}^{1/2}P\rho_{AB}^{1/2}\right)
\]

makes positivity and the trace bound explicit.

#### Case \(t\ge0\)

Because both coefficients are nonnegative,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Tr}(PR)
&=t\operatorname{Tr}(P\rho_{AB})
+b\operatorname{Tr}(PS)\\
&\le t+bk.
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(k<d\), this is exactly \(K_k(q_{p,d})\).

#### Case \(t<0\)

Here \(t\rho_{AB}\le0\), so

\[
R\le bS\le bI_{AB}.
\]

Taking the trace against \(P\ge0\) gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(PR)\le bk.
\]

For \(k<d\), this is again exactly \(K_k(q_{p,d})\), now using the correctly sorted negative-\(t\) reference.

#### Ranks \(k\ge d\)

For either sign of \(t\), positivity and unit trace give

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(PR)\le \operatorname{Tr}R=1,
\]

which is the corresponding padded-reference prefix. The rank-zero inequality is trivial.

### 3.4 Majorization follows without changing the consuming projector

The finite-dimensional Ky Fan principle gives

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_j^\downarrow(R)
=\max_{\substack{P=P^\dagger=P^2\\\operatorname{rank}P=k}}
\operatorname{Tr}(PR).
\]

The preceding estimates hold for **every** such \(P\), so maximizing preserves them. Together with total trace one, they prove

\[
\lambda(R)\prec q_{p,d}\oplus0.
\]

There is no independently chosen projector for different summands and no replacement of joint operator data by freely assigned weights. Both terms of \(R\) are bounded against the same arbitrary \(P\).

### 3.5 Mixed inputs require no additional reduction

Every step above used only

\[
\rho_{AB}\ge0,\qquad \operatorname{Tr}\rho_{AB}=1,
\]

and the corresponding properties of its marginal. No Schmidt decomposition, pure-state assumption, or convexity reduction was used. The theorem therefore covers mixed inputs directly.

## 4. Symbolic boundary checks and sharpness

### 4.2 Sign-change boundary

\[

Thus the reference is uniform on \(d\) entries. The two prefix expressions coincide:

\[

### 4.3 Upper completely positive boundary

\[

gives the stated upper endpoint. There,

\[
b=\frac{d}{d^2-1},
\qquad
t=-\frac1{d^2-1},
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{d-1}{d^2-1}=\frac1{d+1}.

Accordingly, the decreasing reference is

\[
\left(
\underbrace{\frac{d}{d^2-1},\ldots,
\frac{d}{d^2-1}}_{d-1},
\frac1{d+1}
\right).

The Choi operator is positive semidefinite at this boundary, although one Choi eigenvalue vanishes. None of the projector inequalities requires strict positivity.

### 4.4 Universal sharpness family

For **arbitrary** normalized vectors \(a\in A\) and \(\psi\in\mathbb C^d\), let

\[
\rho_{AB}
=|a\rangle\langle a|\otimes|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|.
\]

Then

\[
R
=|a\rangle\langle a|
\otimes\left(t|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|+bI_d\right).
\]

The second factor has eigenvalue \(t+b\) on \(\psi\) and eigenvalue \(b\) on its orthogonal complement. The nonzero spectral support of the first factor has rank one. Therefore the output spectrum is exactly \(q_{p,d}\) with the required zeros.

Thus the reference is attained throughout the parameter interval, for either sign of \(t\), and all its prefix constants are sharp. This establishes S1 LF276 through a quantified product-state family, not a newly selected example.

## 5. Separate infinite-ancilla functional-analytic check

S1 LF277 is **SUPPORTED** for trace-class density operators, with the usual spectral-sequence interpretation. The details are as follows.

Let \(A\) now be an arbitrary ancillary Hilbert space, while \(B=\mathbb C^d\) remains finite-dimensional, and let \(\rho_{AB}\) be positive trace-class with trace one.

### 5.1 The output is a positive trace-class density operator

The partial trace

\[
\rho_A=\operatorname{Tr}_B\rho_{AB}
\]

is positive trace-class with trace one. Since the second tensor factor has finite dimension,

\[
S=\rho_A\otimes I_d
\]

is trace-class and

\[
\operatorname{Tr}S=d.
\]

Consequently,

\[
R=t\rho_{AB}+bS
\]

is trace-class and has trace \(t+db=1\).

To establish positivity without silently extending a merely finite-ancilla assertion, use the finite Kraus representation of the finite-dimensional channel:

\[
\mathcal D_{p,d}(X)=\sum_\nu K_\nu X K_\nu^\dagger,
\qquad
\sum_\nu K_\nu^\dagger K_\nu=I_d.
\]

The finite sum

\[
R=\sum_\nu
(I_A\otimes K_\nu)\rho_{AB}(I_A\otimes K_\nu^\dagger)
\]

is positive and trace-class on the infinite-dimensional tensor product as well. It agrees with the displayed channel identity.

### 5.2 Every finite-rank prefix estimate survives

A positive trace-class density operator satisfies \(\rho_A\le I_A\), so

\[
S\le I_{AB}.
\]

For every finite-rank orthogonal projector \(P\) of rank \(k\),

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(P\rho_{AB})\le1,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(PS)\le k.
\]

The finite-dimensional proof therefore applies verbatim to each such \(P\):

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(PR)\le
\begin{cases}
t+kb,&t\ge0,\ 1\le k<d,\\
kb,&t<0,\ 1\le k<d,\\
1,&k\ge d.
\end{cases}
\]

The ambient identity need not be trace-class: only its finite-rank compression is traced, giving \(\operatorname{Tr}P=k\).

### 5.3 Compact spectral majorization is well-defined

A positive trace-class operator is compact. Its positive eigenvalues form a finite or countable decreasing sequence, counted with multiplicity, with total sum one. Zeros are appended when needed.

The compact-operator Ky Fan principle gives, for every finite \(k\),

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_j^\downarrow(R)
=
\sup_{\operatorname{rank}P=k}\operatorname{Tr}(PR).
\]

Thus every finite prefix is bounded by that of

\[
(q_{p,d},0,0,\ldots),
\]

and both sequences have total mass one. This is the claimed infinite-sequence majorization.

Nonseparability of the ambient ancilla does not create an additional spectral obstruction: a trace-class state has separable support, and adjoining a finite-dimensional tensor factor preserves that reduction. No trace of an infinite-dimensional identity or unproved finite-dimensional truncation limit is required.
