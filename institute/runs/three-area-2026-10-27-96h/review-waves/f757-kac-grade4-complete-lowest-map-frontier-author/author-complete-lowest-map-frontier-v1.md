## 5. Exact ideal criterion for an actual lowest kernel

The implication \(v\in\mathcal R\Rightarrow F_3v=0\) in (1) is the imported whole-grade-three theorem applied to the root-graded ideal \(\mathcal R\).

Conversely suppose \(0\ne v\in S_{a,b,4}\) is homogeneous and \(F_1v=F_2v=F_3v=0\). Let \(I_+\) be the linear span of \(v\) and all repeated positive-simple adjoint brackets \(\operatorname{ad}e_{i_1}\cdots\operatorname{ad}e_{i_t}v\). It is stable under all \(e_i\) and the whole Cartan. It is also stable under each \(f_i\): induct on \(t\), using
\[
 [f_i,[e_j,w]]=-\delta_{ij}[h_i,w]+[e_j,[f_i,w]],
\]
and the base \([f_i,v]=0\). Thus \(I_+\) is an ideal of the whole Chevalley quotient, not just of the Levi subalgebra. Every root occurring in it is the original positive root plus a nonnegative combination of positive simple roots; full Cartan/root grading and the positive/negative bridge make its intersection with the whole Cartan zero. Its inverse image in \(G\) is an ideal disjoint from the Cartan, so maximality of \(\tau\) gives \(I_+\subseteq\mathcal R\), proving (1). The argument uses the **actual** full Cartan, both Serre signs and actual quotient; it is not a numerical rank-to-radical inference.

This pays the specific ideal-closure analysis which S2 leaves open for a genuinely nonzero lowest kernel. It does not establish that such a vector exists. The rank-ten \((2,2,4)\) *root-space* kernel alone does not meet the hypothesis \(F_1v=F_2v=0\).
