## 2. Definitions as reconstructed (the review was carried out against these, not against paraphrases)

Q,R,S,T are 8×8 arrays of unit vectors, each in its own C^8, rows and columns ONBs; for every distinct pair M,N the 64 vectors M_x⊗N_x form an ONB of C^8⊗C^8, hence for distinct cells x≠y: ⟨M_x,M_y⟩⟨N_x,N_y⟩=0. Fix any board row (call it row 0). Independent local unitaries send the j-th entry of row 0 of each member to its own e_j; U_M⊗U_N preserves pair-product ONBs; column labels are shared, Hilbert spaces are not identified. Light = coordinate ray; mixed = otherwise; support = nonzero coordinates (size ≥2 iff mixed). Nonclassical ⇔ at least one mixed cell (row 0 is already a full ONB). Two consequences used everywhere and re-derived here: (i) *first-row comparison*: if ⟨Q_x,e_a⟩≠0 for a cell x off row 0 then ⟨M_x,e_a⟩=0 for every mate M (compare with cell (0,a)); (ii) *line lemma*: on a line with k mixed rays the 8−k light rays are distinct coordinate rays, so the mixed rays are an ONB of the complementary k-axis coordinate subspace; hence degree ≥2, degree 2 ⇒ one common 2-support, degree 3 ⇒ at most one support-2 ray, and two support-2 rays on one line have equal or disjoint supports (a single shared nonzero coordinate cannot cancel). Lines with no mixed cell are permutations of e_0..e_7 (rows **or** columns), so they contain e_a exactly once; a line whose mixed rays avoid axis a also contains a light e_a.

### 3.1 Core algebra reconstructed without a support-only surrogate

The following calculations explain precisely the continuous-vector steps behind the chapter summaries; none relies on a numerical fit or a finite support catalogue.

- **The line-subspace and degree-4 facts.** Let the light axes on a line be L. Orthogonality to these axes puts all k mixed rays in the coordinate space L^⊥ of dimension k; k orthonormal rays fill it. For orthogonal support-2 rays a singleton intersection is impossible, so their supports are equal or disjoint. A line all of whose rays have support 2 splits into disjoint 2-planes, each filled by exactly two rays, hence has even degree. In a four-axis line, two support-2 rays and one support-3 ray are impossible: equal supports fill their plane; disjoint supports cover four axes and every 3-set meets one of them exactly once. For P21's stronger fact, let p have support U and x,y have support 3. Each support must contain all of U; its nonzero U-projection is along the unique ray p^⊥ in U. If the extra axes of x,y differ, their inner product is the nonzero product of their parallel U-projections; if the extra axes agree, p,x,y fill the corresponding three-axis space and the fourth ray is a coordinate singleton. This is why the alternatives in P21 are exhaustive even at arbitrary complex phases.
- **Three-space observation used in P11/P12/P14/P18/P21/P22.** In the actual ONB (u_1,u_2,e_z), with u_1,u_2 an orthogonal pair of full support U, full support on T=U∪{z} and x_i⊥u_i imply x_1=αu_2+βe_z, x_2=γu_1+δe_z with βδ≠0. Therefore ⟨x_1,x_2⟩=β̄δ≠0. No assertion that α or γ is nonzero is necessary. This calculation is phase-insensitive for the stated reason, not because the entries are assumed real.
- **Exclusive cycles and disjoint transition supports.** On a support-constant simple 2k incidence cycle, Q alternates p,p^⊥. An exclusive axis a supplies n−k distinct light-e_a cells on exterior rows. For any mate M, their rays are an orthonormal (n−k)-set orthogonal to all cycle rays. The p and p^⊥ sets each contain k orthogonal M rays, so both are ONBs of the same k-space — this is an exact dimension equality, not a guessed common span. For fixed cell indexing, the transition row corresponding to a p-cell has two distinct zeros at its actual row/column p^⊥ neighbors. Every mate's unitary transition row must be nonzero, and two mates cannot use the same nonzero entry because their actual M⊗N products at the corresponding two distinct cells are orthogonal. This proves t−1≤k−2. In P02, e_b is orthogonal to the cycle span and lies in the span of those n−k light-e_a M rays, one of which is e_a. Since b≠a, some other such cell must have a nonzero b-coordinate in each mate; M⊗N against (0,b) forbids a shared cell, giving t−1≤n−k−1. All coordinate references are in the respective member's own first-row basis with the same column labels.
- **Five-dimensional six-ray saturation in P04/P07.** The three p-rays and three q-rays of a mate are orthonormal within each class and orthogonal across classes except at three matched pairs. Full Q support on a three-axis set puts all six mate rays in its five-dimensional coordinate complement. Thus each mate has a nonempty exceptional-overlap index set. Actual products between each pair of the three mates force these sets to be disjoint, hence each is a singleton. The four rays outside the exceptional pair plus one member of that pair are five orthonormal vectors, filling the complement; the omitted member is collinear with the included one. This proves saturation for every mate separately. At an off-row light Q=e_a cell y, orthogonality to the saturated six-ray set puts each mate into its local three-axis space; comparison with (0,a) removes axis a. Actual mate-pair comparisons with every (0,b) force pairwise disjoint coordinate-label supports at y. Three nonzero vectors cannot have disjoint supports in two labels. No equality between vectors in different Hilbert spaces is used.
- **Actual projector identities.** On each line the sum of the actual mixed rank-one projectors is its coordinate-subspace projector, so summing by rows and columns gives the same operator. P03 subtracts two copies of I on each side and obtains equal rank-2 projections, hence equal one-ray complements in the actual mate's three-space. P10 uses Σ_i P_(K_i)=Σ_j P_(T_j): every T_j is in at least two K_i because its column has at least two full-support rays. An axis in exactly one K_i could occur in no T_j, contradiction. With three distinct K_i, let g count triple axes and d_ij pair-only axes; equal cardinality gives all d_ij=d and g+2d=4. A pair-only axis must appear in two T_j contained in its pairwise intersection of size 4−d, so d=1,g=2; the three pairs then require six columns, impossible. Thus K_i=K and the multiplicities force four distinct omitted axes. P17 uses 3P_K=P_K+P_(T_1)+P_(T_2)+P_U to identify U with the two distinct omitted axes. P22 uses P_(C_1)+P_(C_2)=P_K+P_U to identify C_1,C_2 as the two different 3-subsets containing U. These operator identities are stronger than mere support-incidence constraints (controls C2/C3 are diagnostics of their exact coordinate arithmetic).
- **Cross-Gram operator norm in P19/P20.** If X,Y:C²→C⁸ are the two ONB isometries and f is a unit vector in the common one-dimensional intersection of their planes, write Xα=f=Yβ with ‖α‖=‖β‖=1. For G=X*Y, Gβ=α, so ‖G‖=1 (also ‖G‖≤1). The actual high-row orthogonalities make G=[[0,b],[c,0]]. Then G*G=diag(|c|²,|b|²), hence one off-diagonal entry has modulus 1. Equality in Cauchy–Schwarz puts the paired unit rays in the plane intersection. Here that intersection is the coordinate ray e_c or e_d, contradicting their full support 3. The coinciding-plane boundary is not forced into this contradiction: P19 handles collinear low rays separately. This also covers genuinely complex phase choices (C4 samples them exactly but is not the universal justification).
- **P23 cube classification, including degeneracies.** On a face, independent opposite rays leave a one-dimensional common orthogonal complement for the other opposite pair, so choose a collinear opposite pair p and denote the face p,q,p,r. If q∥r, all leaving rays equal p_0=(p,q)^⊥; they form a perfect matching, and deletion leaves the two opposite faces, each alternating an ONB of p_0^⊥. Otherwise q,r are independent in p^⊥. The leaving rays are r^⊥,q^⊥,q^⊥,r^⊥ in cyclic order. On the opposite face, edges between different leaving rays are forced to p; the remaining two are q and r. The four p-edges form a perfect matching; deletion leaves two squares alternating {q,q^⊥} and {r,r^⊥}. The case q⊥r is allowed here and is the coinciding-square-bases boundary, not discarded as generic. A support-2 matching ray forces every square ray to have support 3; a support-3 matching ray allows at most one support-2 class per square. Hence at most two coordinates are omitted across the square classes, and one of the three axes is used by all twelve rays. Four light-e_a rays then restrict every mate to four dimensions. Each square's four mate rays form an ONB there; distinct square Q bases would make those two ONBs mutually orthogonal, impossible. Once the square bases coincide, the three Q classes are perfect matchings, and the p-to-q transition row has two distinct adjacency zeros; three mates cannot fit into its two available positions.
