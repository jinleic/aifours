## 2. Exact source and the property of its images

Let `F` be the free band on `a0,a1,b0,b1,e0,e1,f0,f1,g0,g1`, with `tau` swapping subscripts. In `F semidirect C2` form `S0` from

```
a=(a0,1),  a'=(a1,1),  b=(b0,1),  b'=(b1,1),
e=(e0,0),  f=(f0,0),  g=(g0,0).
```

`theta` is the least **semigroup** congruence on `S0` generated by exactly

```
eaa'=e,       ebb'=e,       a'afg=fg,
b'bfa'eafg=fa'eafg.                                  (R1–R4)
```

Set `S=S0/theta`, with the frozen pure onto `q:S -> C2`, and

```
U=eafg,       V=ebfa'eafg,       q(U)=q(V)=1.
```

The frozen source properties are that `S` is finite and regular, its grade-zero elements are exactly its idempotents, and its idempotents form a band. In particular **every square in S is idempotent**, since `q(s^2)=0`. For any homomorphism `phi:S -> T`, replace `T` below by its actual image `phi(S)`. This image is regular, and every square is idempotent. Its idempotents are exactly `phi(E(S))`: if `t=phi(s)` is idempotent, then `s^2` is an idempotent in `S` and `t=phi(s^2)`. Thus `E(T)` is a band, even if the chosen ambient codomain was not orthodox.

## 3. Explicit 12-element quotient: `E(S)` fails the regular-band identity

The following construction is written, with no executable computation. Let the state set be `{p0,pE,pF,acc,rej}`. For a state transformation, multiplication `xy` means **first apply x, then apply y**. Define six distinct transformations:

* `I`: identity.
* `E` and `F`: send each of `p0,pE,pF` respectively to `pE` and `pF`, fixing `acc,rej`.
* `G`: sends `pE` to `acc`, `p0,pF` to `rej`, and fixes `acc,rej`.
* `A` and `Z`: send each of `p0,pE,pF` respectively to `acc` and `rej`, fixing `acc,rej`.

They have the following complete multiplication table, with left factors indexing rows:

| · | I | E | F | G | A | Z |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | I | E | F | G | A | Z |
| E | E | E | F | A | A | Z |
| F | F | E | F | Z | A | Z |
| G | G | G | G | G | G | G |
| A | A | A | A | A | A | A |
| Z | Z | Z | Z | Z | Z | Z |

Associativity is composition of the displayed maps; closure and all six idempotences are visible in the table. The six maps are distinct, so `B={I,E,F,G,A,Z}` is a six-element band with identity. In particular `A != Z`. Its regular-band identity fails explicitly:

```
E F E G E = A,             E F G E = Z.              (3.1)
```

The equalities follow from `EF=F`, `FE=E`, `EG=A`, `FG=Z` and the last rows. Consider the 12-element semigroup `B x C2` with componentwise multiplication. It is associative, its grade projection is onto and idempotent-pure, and every element `(x,h)` is its own regular inverse, since its cube equals itself. Map the ten free-band letters equivariantly, for the **trivial** involution on `B`, by

```
a0,a1,b0,b1 -> I,       e0,e1 -> E,
f0,f1 -> F,             g0,g1 -> G.
```

The induced map from `S0` sends `a,a',b,b'` to `(I,1)`, and `e,f,g` to `(E,0),(F,0),(G,0)`. R1 and R2 reduce to `E=E`, R3 to `FG=FG`, and R4 to `FEFG=FEFG`; all have grade zero. It therefore factors through the **actual** source quotient `S0/theta`, without imposing any extra ambient-band congruence. It is onto `B x C2`: `(I,0)=a^2`, `(I,1)=a`, `E,F,G` generate the remaining grade-zero band elements via `EG=A` and `FG=Z`, and multiplication by `(I,1)` toggles grades.

By (3.1), the idempotents `e,f,g` of the original source satisfy

```
e f e g e != e f g e   in S.                          (3.2)
```

Hence **`E(S)` is not a regular band**. This is an unconditional source result at the frozen source-construction import boundary; it does not assume SEP or the Szendrei theorem. The quotient itself does **not** establish SEP:

```
phi(U)=(Z,1),    phi(V)=(Z,1).
```

For example, the first components are `EFG=Z` and `EFEFG=Z`. This example is not a new all-host counterexample and is not an executable replay.

## 4. Explicit finite image: `S` is not completely regular

Let `D` be the free band on two letters `x,y` with an absorbing zero `0` adjoined. It is finite by the frozen free-band finiteness argument: the two-letter representative bound is `L2=6`. Swap `x,y` and fix `0`; this is a band involution. Map the original ten letters into `D` equivariantly by

```
a0,b0 -> x,       a1,b1 -> y,
e0,e1,f0,f1,g0,g1 -> 0.
```

This induces a map `S0 -> D semidirect C2`. Each side of every R1–R4 relation has first coordinate `0` and grade zero, so the map factors through `S`. The corresponding image of `a` is `(x,1)`, while

```
image(a^3) = (xyx,1) != (x,1) = image(a).
```

Indeed, `xyx` contains both free letters and `x` contains only one; contextual insertion/removal of a nonempty word square preserves letter content, so those two elements of the free band cannot be equal. Thus `a^3 != a` in the actual `S`. Every square in `S` is idempotent. If `S` were a union of groups, `a^2` would be the identity of its group and would give `a^3=a`, a contradiction. Therefore **`S` is not completely regular**. The model maps both `U` and `V` to `(0,1)` and does not pay SEP.

## 5. Uniform necessary condition: every separator has a non-regular idempotent band

A *regular band* here means an idempotent semigroup satisfying

```
xyxzx = xyzx       for all x,y,z.                     (RB)
```

**Theorem, conditional on the Szendrei 1987 primary-abstract import.** For every semigroup homomorphism `phi:S -> T`, if `phi(U) != phi(V)` then the idempotent band of the image `phi(S)` fails (RB). This is a uniform obstruction to searches in source images, not merely a ban on imposing R1–R4 directly inside an ambient band.

**Proof.** Replace `T` by `phi(S)` and form the actual graded image

```
R = { (phi(s), q(s)) : s in S } <= T x C2.
```

It is regular as an image of regular `S`; the projection `p:R -> C2` is onto and pure: grade zero is idempotent because a grade-zero source representative is idempotent, whereas an idempotent cannot have grade one. Moreover

```
E(R) = { (t,0) : t in E(T) },
```

with the indicated map an isomorphism of bands. The reverse inclusion uses the square-lifting argument in Section 2, so it does not assume that arbitrary idempotents lift automatically.

`p` is the least group quotient. For any group homomorphism `alpha:R -> H` and `p(r)=p(t)`, choose a regular inverse `r'` of `r`. Then `p(r't)=0`, hence `r't` is idempotent and `alpha(r)^(-1) alpha(t)=1`. Thus every group homomorphism factors through `p`, while `p` itself is a group homomorphism. Purity therefore makes `R` E-unitary in the sense used by the imported theorem.

Suppose `E(T)` satisfies (RB). Then `E(R)` is a regular band. The cited Szendrei theorem, used **as an explicit external theorem import**, embeds `R` into `K semidirect H` for some band `K` and group `H`. The group projection of this embedding factors through `p`. It cannot be trivial: then the embedded `R` would consist only of idempotents, contradicting the existence of grade-one elements. Consequently the image of `C2` in `H` is a two-element subgroup, and the embedding actually lands in a band-by-`C2` host over `p`. Composing with `S -> R` gives a homomorphism over `q`. The frozen adopted COLL result then forces the two images of `U,V` to agree, so the embedding forces `(phi(U),1)=(phi(V),1)`, a contradiction. Thus (RB) must fail. □

As a numerical structural corollary, any separating image has at least three idempotents: in a band, (RB) automatically holds if any two of `x,y,z` coincide, so a failure requires three distinct elements. Its grade-enriched `R` then has at least five elements, because the three distinct grade-zero idempotents and the two distinct grade-one images of `U,V` are disjoint. These are necessary bounds, not an existence claim. Section 3 shows the original source has the required non-regularity, so the condition does not by itself settle SEP.

## 6. A second exclusion without the Szendrei theorem

**Theorem, using only frozen COLL and standard Green-class facts.** No image `T=phi(S)` can separate `U,V` if `T` is both completely regular (a union of groups) and has Green `H` as a semigroup congruence. In particular this excludes arbitrary rectangular-group and Clifford-semigroup images, even if the original chosen codomain was not presented as a band-by-group host.

**Proof.** Construct `R` and its pure onto grade `p` exactly as in Section 5. Every `t` in `T` has idempotent square. Since `T` is a union of groups, `t^2` is the identity of the subgroup containing `t`, and `t^3=t`; the same is true for elements of `R`, including the grade coordinate.

For `t,u` in `T`, `t H t^2` and `u H u^2`. Because `H` is a congruence, `tu H t^2u^2`. The latter is idempotent since `E(T)` is a band, while `(tu)^2` is the idempotent in the `H`-class of `tu`. Uniqueness of the idempotent in an `H`-class gives

```
(tu)^2 = t^2 u^2.                                    (6.1)
```

Thus

```
Psi:R -> E(T) x C2,        Psi(t,h)=(t^2,h)
```

is a homomorphism into a band-by-`C2` direct product over `p`. It is injective. For grade zero, purity makes `t` idempotent, so its square is itself. For grade one, suppose `(t,1),(u,1)` have the same square `e=t^2=u^2`. Then `t,u` belong to the same subgroup with identity `e`. Their product is idempotent because `(tu,0)` lies in `R`; as an idempotent in that subgroup it is `e`. Also `t^2=e`, so `u=t`. The frozen COLL therefore forces `Psi(phi(U),1)=Psi(phi(V),1)` and injectivity yields `phi(U)=phi(V)`. □

The theorem is **not** asserted for all completely regular orthodox images without the `H`-congruence hypothesis. Section 4 shows the original source is not completely regular; it does not establish SEP.
