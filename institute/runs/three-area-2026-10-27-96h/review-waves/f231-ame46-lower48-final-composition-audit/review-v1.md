## Independent reconstruction of the union

Let `S` be the set of normalized complex AME(4,6) states. A local product basis `B` is a choice of orthonormal basis in each of the four six-dimensional factors. For a *single* pair `(ψ,B)`, let `N(ψ,B)` be the number of nonzero tensor coefficients and let `t(ψ,B)` count coefficients of modulus exactly `1/6`. The same coefficients are reshaped into each of the three 36-by-36 2|2 matrices. AME two-uniformity gives `U=6ψ` unitary in every cut. A modulus-one entry of `U` exhausts its row and column; removing all `t` singleton rows and columns leaves a `(36−t)`-square unitary whose remaining lines cannot be singleton lines. Therefore, on this same state and basis,

\[
 N-t\ge 2(36-t),\qquad N\ge72-t.
\]

The empty residual case is vacuous, and a one-dimensional residual cannot occur without another maximal entry. This common notation is a compatibility check, not a substitute for any imported global theorem.

The applicable premises are the following **statements expressly adopted by pinned parent dispositions**, not conclusions taken merely from the two status reviews:

1. The f226 parent disposition adopts `N≥46` for **every** normalized complex AME(4,6) state in **every** local product basis, by combining its `N=45` exclusion with the separately adopted lower-45 statement and its named prior bounds. This is conditional on the inherited provenance below.
2. The f222 parent disposition adopts that **no** such state in **any** local product basis has `N=46`, and separately that **no** such state/basis has `N=47`, at their recorded import boundaries.
3. The f89 lower-44 parent disposition adopts the recorded actual 60-distinct-nonzero tensor and the exact Gram-check assertion explained below. This is an existential claim in a particular product basis, not a universal upper bound on the support of every state in every basis.

For an arbitrary fixed `(ψ,B)`, premise 1 gives integer `N≥46`. If `N=46`, premise 2 contradicts that very same pair; if `N=47`, its other part contradicts that very same pair. Hence `N≥48` for **every** pair. No change of basis, change of state, or application of an `N=46`-only subcase at `N=47` is needed.

For the upper bound, let `A` denote the four-index tensor whose existence and Gram data are stated in the f89 disposition, and let `B₀` be its table's product basis. That disposition reports exactly 60 distinct nonzero entries and, in **each** of the three 36-by-36 cuts, both row and column Gram matrix `2I₃₆` over `Z[√2]`. Setting `ψ₀=A/√72` gives, for every cut,

\[
 (A/\sqrt{72})(A/\sqrt{72})^\dagger=I_{36}/36,
 \qquad
 (A/\sqrt{72})^\dagger(A/\sqrt{72})=I_{36}/36.
\]

Each two-party marginal is therefore maximally mixed, and its trace is one, so `ψ₀` is normalized and AME at the **adopted Gram assertion**. Multiplication by a nonzero scalar preserves all 60 nonzero positions. This derives the consequence of the parent-stated check; it is not a fresh inspection of the actual entries or a replay of the checker.

The universal lower argument applies in particular to **the same** `(ψ₀,B₀)` supplied by the witness. The existential witness need not be identified with an arbitrary pair before this instantiation: universality is precisely what permits the instantiation without changing basis or state. The set of attained support counts across all pairs is nonempty and consists of positive integers (indeed at most `6⁴`), so it has a least element `m`. The universal result gives `m≥48`; `(ψ₀,B₀)` gives `m≤60`. Equivalently `m∈{48,49,…,60}` is an outer set of possible **minimum values**, not a claim that every displayed value is realizable.
