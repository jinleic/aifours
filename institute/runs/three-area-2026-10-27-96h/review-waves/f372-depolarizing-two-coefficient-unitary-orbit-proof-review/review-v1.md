## 2. Class A: actual output blocks

**Status: SUPPORTED.** Sources: S1 LF 143–194.

Write the positive-Bloch channel as
\[
\mathcal D_t=\Phi_{1-t},\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\]
Its matrix-unit action is
\[
\mathcal D_t(|0\rangle\langle0|)=
\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\0&b\end{pmatrix},
\quad
\mathcal D_t(|1\rangle\langle1|)=
\begin{pmatrix}b&0\\0&a\end{pmatrix},
\quad
\mathcal D_t(|0\rangle\langle1|)=t|0\rangle\langle1|.
\]

Take two distinct basis strings in the same local tensor-product basis. At sites where they coincide, their common local basis vector is a spectator. At sites where they differ, their two local vectors are orthogonal. Local basis changes, bit flips, and a physical-qubit permutation therefore reduce their superposition to
\[
|\psi_m\rangle=
\sqrt u\,|0^m\rangle+
e^{i\phi}\sqrt{1-u}\,|1^m\rangle,
\qquad 1\le m\le4,
\]
tensored with \(4-m\) pure \(|0\rangle\) spectators. A one-coefficient state is a product state.

Let
\[
D_m=\operatorname{diag}(a,b)^{\otimes m},
\qquad d_x=a^{m-|x|}b^{|x|}.
\]
Pair each active string \(x\) with its complement \(\bar x\).

For every pair other than \(\{0^m,1^m\}\), the actual output block, in the ordered basis \((x,\bar x)\), is
\[
H_x=
\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
ud_x+(1-u)d_{\bar x},
(1-u)d_x+ud_{\bar x}
\right).
\]
Thus, with \(X\) denoting the two-dimensional swap,
\[
H_x=uD_x+(1-u)XD_xX,\qquad
D_x=\operatorname{diag}(d_x,d_{\bar x}).
\]

There are no other active coherences: the only input cross term is
\[
|0^m\rangle\langle1^m|
=\bigotimes_{i=1}^m |0\rangle\langle1|,
\]
which is multiplied by \(t^m\).

The endpoint block is exactly
\[
H_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
ua^m+(1-u)b^m&
t^m\sqrt{u(1-u)}e^{-i\phi}\\
t^m\sqrt{u(1-u)}e^{i\phi}&
(1-u)a^m+ub^m
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

These are blocks of the **actual channel output**, not bounds on replacement or erasure operators.

For \(m<4\), the full canonical output is
\[
H_m\otimes D_{4-m}.
\]
In particular, spectators do not remain pure after depolarization. Their mixed output supplies the correct spectator weights for every active block.

## 3. Endpoint alignment and an explicit complex diagonalizer

**Status: SUPPORTED.** Sources: S1 LF 199–244.

Set
\[
\delta=a^m-b^m,\qquad z=2u-1,\qquad
c=t^m\sqrt{u(1-u)},\qquad
\mu=\frac{a^m+b^m}{2}.
\]
Then
\[
H_0=
\mu I+
\begin{pmatrix}
z\delta/2&ce^{-i\phi}\\
ce^{i\phi}&-z\delta/2
\end{pmatrix},
\]
and its eigenvalues are \(\mu\pm h\), where
\[
4h^2=z^2\delta^2+4c^2
=z^2\delta^2+(1-z^2)t^{2m}.
\]

Since \(a=b+t\) and \(b\ge0\),
\[
\delta=(b+t)^m-b^m
=\sum_{j=1}^m\binom mj b^{m-j}t^j
\ge t^m.
\]
This holds for every required \(m=1,2,3,4\). Therefore
\[
(2h)^2
\le z^2\delta^2+(1-z^2)\delta^2
=\delta^2,
\qquad 2h\le\delta.
\]

For \(t>0\), \(\delta>0\). Also \(h>0\): if \(0<u<1\), then \(c>0\); if \(u=0\) or \(1\), then \(|z|\delta>0\).

An explicit diagonalizer, including complex phase, is obtained by defining
\[
r_*=2h,\qquad d_*=z\delta,
\]
\[
A_0=\sqrt{\frac{r_*+d_*}{2r_*}},\qquad
B_0=\sqrt{\frac{r_*-d_*}{2r_*}},
\]
and
\[
V_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
A_0&-B_0\\
e^{i\phi}B_0&e^{i\phi}A_0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its columns are orthonormal. Because
\[
A_0B_0=\frac{c}{r_*},
\]
direct multiplication gives
\[
H_0=V_0
\operatorname{diag}(\mu+h,\mu-h)V_0^\dagger.
\]

Now put
\[
q_0=\frac12+\frac h\delta.
\]
The bound \(2h\le\delta\) gives \(q_0\in[1/2,1]\), and
\[
H_0=
V_0\left[
q_0\operatorname{diag}(a^m,b^m)
+(1-q_0)X\operatorname{diag}(a^m,b^m)X
\right]V_0^\dagger.
\]

Thus the endpoint block is obtained from its reference block by a convex swap followed by an explicitly specified complex unitary.

## 4. Explicit finite whole-space ensemble

**Status: SUPPORTED.** Sources: S1 LF 123–137 and 224–248.

Let the \(n=2^{m-1}\) complement pairs be indexed by \(j=0,\ldots,n-1\), with \(j=0\) the endpoint pair. Put
\[
q_j=u\quad(j\ne0),
\]
and use the \(q_0\) just constructed.

For \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^n\), define
\[
w_\varepsilon
=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}
\begin{cases}
q_j,&\varepsilon_j=0,\\
1-q_j,&\varepsilon_j=1.
\end{cases}
\]
These weights are nonnegative and sum to one.

Let
\[
T_\varepsilon=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n-1}X_j^{\varepsilon_j},
\qquad
V=V_0\oplus I_{\text{nonendpoint pairs}},
\qquad
U_\varepsilon=VT_\varepsilon.
\]
Each \(U_\varepsilon\) is unitary on the entire active space. Taking the expectation block by block yields
\[
H_m=\sum_{\varepsilon}w_\varepsilon
U_\varepsilon D_mU_\varepsilon^\dagger.
\]

This is also a direct proof of the relevant block-assembly fact: independent choices give product probabilities, and a direct sum of block unitaries is a whole-space unitary. Different block traces are harmless because every target block has the same trace as its corresponding reference block.

There are at most \(2^n\) choices: at \(m=4\), at most \(2^8=256\). Neither the probabilities nor the unitaries refer to \(Q\).

Tensoring with spectator identities gives
\[
H_m\otimes D_{4-m}
=
\sum_\varepsilon w_\varepsilon
(U_\varepsilon\otimes I)D_t
(U_\varepsilon\otimes I)^\dagger.
\]

If \(M\) is the local-unitary/permutation map from canonical coordinates back to the physical state, the physical ensemble unitaries can be taken as
\[
\widehat U_\varepsilon=M(U_\varepsilon\otimes I).
\]
This is the precise transport of the orbit elements while retaining the fixed reference \(D_t\); no invariance of \(D_t\) under arbitrary local basis rotations is assumed.

### Degeneracies

All required degeneracies are covered:

- **\(t=0\):** Actual and reference outputs are \(I/2^m\), and the four-qubit output is \(I/16\). Use the identity ensemble; do not divide by \(\delta\) or \(h\).
- **\(t=1\):** \(\delta=1\), \(2h=1\), and \(q_0=1\). Reference zero eigenvalues cause no division problem.
- **\(u=0\) or \(1\):** \(h=\delta/2\) and \(q_0=1\). The endpoint eigenvector is the appropriate endpoint basis vector, and other pair choices become deterministic.
- **\(m=1\):** There is only the endpoint pair; \(\delta=t\), \(2h=t\), and \(q_0=1\).
- **Equal reference eigenvalues within other pairs:** A swap has no effect on a scalar block, so the same probabilities remain valid.

No omitted limiting argument is needed.

## 5. All sixteen Ky Fan inequalities and basis-varying mixtures

**Status: SUPPORTED on \(\mathcal C_A\).** Sources: S1 LF 43–59, 123–137, 236–248.

For one fixed arbitrary complex orthogonal rank-\(k\) projector \(Q\),
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(QH)
=\sum_jw_j\operatorname{Tr}(U_j^\dagger QU_jD_t).
\]
Every \(U_j^\dagger QU_j\) is again an orthogonal rank-\(k\) projector. In an eigenbasis of \(D_t\), its diagonal entries lie in \([0,1]\) and sum to \(k\). Their weighted sum against the ordered reference eigenvalues is at most \(K_k(D_t)\). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(QH)\le K_k(D_t).
\]

This is a bound for the **same fixed \(Q\)** in every summand. It does not assert that different summands have the same maximizing projector.

For completeness, write
\[
d_r=a^{4-r}b^r,\qquad r=0,\ldots,4.
\]
The eigenvalue multiplicities are \(1,4,6,4,1\), in nonincreasing order. Consequently the proved bounds cover all sixteen indices:
\[
K_k(D_t)=
\begin{cases}
d_0,&k=1,\\
d_0+(k-1)d_1,&2\le k\le5,\\
d_0+4d_1+(k-5)d_2,&6\le k\le11,\\
d_0+4d_1+6d_2+(k-11)d_3,&12\le k\le15,\\
1,&k=16.
\end{cases}
\]
This formula remains valid at ties and endpoints.

In particular,
\[
K_3(D_t)=a^4+2a^3b=R_3(t),
\]
\[
16R_3(t)=3+8t+6t^2-t^4.
\]

For a finite convex decomposition
\[
\rho=\sum_\ell\lambda_\ell|\psi_\ell\rangle\langle\psi_\ell|,
\]
each component may have a different product basis, active subset, phase, and ensemble. Concatenating the component ensembles with weights \(\lambda_\ell w_{\ell j}\) proves the same representation and fixed-\(Q\) inequalities for \(\rho\). There is no common-basis or common-maximizer requirement.

For product pure inputs the Ky Fan sums equal those of the reference. Equality at an arbitrary fixed \(Q\) is not implied.

\[
\Phi_p(LXL^\dagger)=L\Phi_p(X)L^\dagger,

\[
W=Y^{\otimes4},\qquad S(A)=WA^TW.
\]
Here \(W^\dagger=W\), \(W^2=I\), and \(W^T=W\). The map \(S\) changes Pauli weight \(w\) by \((-1)^w\), giving
\[
\Lambda_{1+t}(\rho)=S(\Lambda_{1-t}(\rho)).

Q'=WQ^TW.
