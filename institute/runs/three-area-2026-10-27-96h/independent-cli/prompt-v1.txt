S5|36|- b_s(x) = 0 if (x⁻¹, s) ∉ N;
S5|37|- b_s(x) = 1 if (x⁻¹, s) ∈ Ψ₁;
S5|38|- b_s(x) = [(x⁻¹, s)] otherwise.
S5|39|
S5|40|**Proof of the host identity.** Put w = w(s,t) and compare b_st(x) with b_s(x)·b_t(w⁻¹x).
S5|41|
S5|42|1. *(x⁻¹, st) ∉ N.* Since N is closed under composition, one of the factors (x⁻¹, s) or (x⁻¹w, t) lies outside N. So both sides are 0.
S5|43|2. *(x⁻¹, st) ∈ N.* Since N is closed under factorization, both factors lie in N. Split on where (x⁻¹w, t) lies.
S5|44|   - *(x⁻¹w, t) ∈ Ψ₁.* The right side is b_s(x). If (x⁻¹, s) ∈ Ψ₁, composition puts (x⁻¹, st) in Ψ₁, so both sides are 1. Otherwise (x⁻¹, s) and (x⁻¹, st) both lie in N ∖ Ψ₁; the latter does, because a placement in Ψ₁ would put its factor (x⁻¹, s) in Ψ₁. The second generating relation of ≈ makes them equivalent, so both sides are equal classes.
S5|45|   - *(x⁻¹w, t) ∈ N ∖ Ψ₁.* The right side is the class of (x⁻¹w, t). By factorization closure of Ψ₁, (x⁻¹, st) lies in N ∖ Ψ₁, and the first generating relation makes it ≈ (x⁻¹w, t).
S5|46|
S5|47|In every case the two sides agree, so B is a host. ∎
S5|48|
S5|49|**Check on the counterexample.** In {e, f, m}, take N = {e, f}, Ψ₁ = ∅ and m ↦ 0. N is closed, the relation ≈ is trivial on {e, f}, and the host separates e from f.
S5|50|
S5|51|**Remark (general hosts).** Iterating this idea gives right-regular hosts with several semilattice levels: a chain of closed sets with right-zero labels on each layer. A general V-host may also use local structure that is not right regular. So neither E nor E′ is claimed to be necessary for (R).

S6|15|
S6|16|Let j_1 and j_2 be hosts over q, with bands B_1 and B_2.

S6|22|## 2. Corollary F (separation by ξ)
S6|23|
S6|24|**Statement.** Let ρ ⊆ τ be any congruence with S/ρ completely simple and q_ρ idempotent pure. If q_ρ(e) ≠ q_ρ(f) for idempotents e, f with q(e) = q(f), then β(e) ≠ β(f).
S6|25|
S6|26|In particular, the least completely simple congruence ξ is pure (SQ-E §7) and lies inside τ. Hence every fibre pair that ξ separates is separated by β.
S6|27|
S6|28|**Corollary.** Suppose ξ separates every R-pair and every L-pair of every syntactic fibre. Then SEP holds, and S embeds into an allowed host.
S6|29|
S6|30|*Proof.*
S6|31|
S6|32|1. **A host over q_ρ.** Put D = S/ρ. Take the one-point band {b0}, which lies in V, and the trivial action. Then j_ρ(s) = (b0, q_ρ(s)) is a host over q_ρ, and it separates e from f.
S6|33|2. **The factor map is pure.** By U205 Lemma U1, ker q_ρ ⊆ τ. So the induced map φ : D → C with φ∘q_ρ = q is a well-defined onto homomorphism. It is idempotent pure, by the FACTOR argument of U1.
S6|34|3. **Push forward.** U205 Lemma U3 gives an injective homomorphism Ψ : P({b0}, D) → P(D_band, C) over φ, with D_band ∈ V.
S6|35|4. **Conclusion.** Ψ∘j_ρ is a host over q, and it separates e from f because Ψ is injective. The universal property then gives β(e) ≠ β(f). ∎
S6|36|
S6|37|**Example.** Take S = (I × Λ) × T, where T is an E-unitary orthodox monoid whose idempotent band lies in V. Here τ merges the columns, while ξ keeps them. So ξ separates the R-pairs (i, x, λ1) and (i, x, λ2).
S6|38|
S6|39|**Limits of Corollary F.** In the band {g, e, f} (g an identity, {e, f} right zero), both ξ and τ are universal. So Corollary F does not apply there, although S embeds. Corollary F is sufficient only.

S6|41|## 3. Row bands and the position-1 vertex class
S6|42|
S6|43|For a row j of C, put
S6|44|
S6|45|`E_j = { s ∈ E(S) : the row of q(s) is j }`.
S6|46|
S6|47|**Lemma 3.1.** E_j is a subband of S, and it lies in V.
S6|48|
S6|49|*Proof.* For s, t ∈ E_j, the product q(s)q(t) is the idempotent (j, p_(λ_t, j)⁻¹, λ_t). Purity then gives st ∈ E_j. The local corners of E_j lie inside E(kSk), which are regular bands, so E_j ∈ V. ∎
S6|50|
S6|51|**Lemma 3.2.** Let e be an idempotent in row i, and let Vert(1, i) be the vertex class of R_e at position 1 for row i. Then:
S6|52|
S6|53|1. Vert(1, i) = E(R_e).
S6|54|2. E(R_e) is a right-zero subband, and it is an R-class of E_i.
S6|55|3. If s ∈ E_i keeps one element of E(R_e) inside R_e, then s maps all of E(R_e) into E(R_e). The action a ↦ a·s is idempotent.
S6|56|
S6|57|*Proof.*
S6|58|
S6|59|- *Part 1.* a ∈ Vert(1, i) means g_a·p_(λ_a, i) = 1, which says that q(a) is idempotent. By purity, a is idempotent.
S6|60|- *Part 3, all of E(R_e) stays.* This follows from VERT.
S6|61|- *Part 3, images are idempotent.* The q-images of the images are idempotents, so purity makes the images idempotent.
S6|62|- *Part 2.* R-related idempotents a and a' satisfy a·a' = a', so E(R_e) is right zero, and it is the R-class of e inside E_i. ∎
S6|63|
S6|64|**Meaning.** At the base position, an R-pair (e, f) of a fibre is an R-pair of the band E_i ∈ V, acted on by right multiplication. Separating it by a host over q is therefore a "positioned" version of separating an R-pair inside a single band of V. Any construction for (R) must extend the right regular structure of these row bands across positions consistently.
