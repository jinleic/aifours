42: **Lemma 3 (weight ≤ 2 bases).** In an ONB of C^n all of whose vectors have weight ≤ 2 (w.r.t. a fixed ONB), every
43: vector of weight 2 with support {a,b} has exactly one partner in the basis with the same support {a,b}, and no other
44: basis vector meets {a,b}.
45: *Proof.* Consider the bipartite support graph (vectors vs coordinates). A connected component with k vectors spans the
46: coordinate space of its k′ coordinates, so k = k′ (unitary). If k ≥ 2, no vector of weight 1 lies in it (a weight-1
47: vector e_a and a vector meeting a would share exactly one coordinate, Lemma 2), so all its vectors have weight 2 and
48: every coordinate has degree ≥ 2 (a degree-1 coordinate gives a column of the unitary with one nonzero entry, forcing
49: that vector to have weight 1); counting edges, the component is a single cycle of length 2k. For k ≥ 3 consecutive
50: vectors share exactly one coordinate — impossible by Lemma 2. So k = 2. ∎
