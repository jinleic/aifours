15: ## Lemma K7-BLOCK [NEW]
16: 
17: Let B ⊆ U be a twin block with s := |B| ≥ 3.
18: 
19: **Part (a): one subgroup for the whole block.**
20: - For distinct j, k ∈ B, the set U_jU_k is a subgroup W_B of index 6, and it does not depend on the pair.
21: - U_j ⊆ W_B for every j ∈ B.
22: - The cosets g_jW_B (j ∈ B) are pairwise distinct.
23: 
24: **Part (b): exclusive cosets.**
25: - For every v ∉ B, g_vU_v meets none of the s cosets g_jW_B (j ∈ B).
26: - g_vU_v meets exactly α_vB of the remaining 6 − s cosets of W_B.
27: - Hence α_vB ≤ 6 − s.
28: 
29: **Part (c): F-packing in the remaining cosets.** In each remaining coset C of W_B, let S_C be the set of vertices v ∉ B whose cosets meet C. Put
30: 
31:   λ_v := n_v·α_vB/6.
32: 
33: Then every T ⊆ S_C with 2 ≤ |T| ≤ 6 contains v ≠ v′ with gcd(λ_v, λ_v′) ≥ |T|. Also Σ_{v∉B} α_vB = Σ_C |S_C|.

15: ## Lemma K7-PAIR [NEW]
16: 
17: Let B = {j, k} ⊆ U be a twin block of size 2. Put W := ⟨U_j, U_k⟩ and m := [G:W].
18: 
19: - **(i)** m ∈ {3, 6}.
20: - **(ii)** If m = 3, then α_vB ∈ {2, 4} for every v ∉ B.
21: - **(iii)** If m = 6:
22:   - U_jU_k = W, a subgroup of index 6;
23:   - g_jW ≠ g_kW;
24:   - no g_vU_v with v ∉ B meets g_jW or g_kW;
25:   - each v ∉ B meets exactly α_vB of the other 4 cosets of W, with the F-packing of K7-BLOCK(c) and λ_v = n_v α_vB/6.
26: 
27: ### Proof
28: 
29: **Step 1: what L6 gives.** For any v ∉ B, K7-TWIN(d) gives U_vU_j = U_vU_k. L6 applied to (v; j, k) gives:
30: - W ≠ G;
31: - m divides 6;
32: - t_v := [U_v : U_v ∩ W] = m·α_vB/6 ∈ ℤ.
33: 
34: Also t_j = t_k = 1, because U_j, U_k ⊆ W. By K7-TWIN(c), α_vB ≤ 4. In L5 (F) with H = W, each g_lU_l meets exactly t_l cosets of W. Inside a coset, the pieces are pairwise disjoint and are translates of cosets in W of subgroups of index λ_l = n_l t_l/m.
35: 
36: **Step 2: m = 2 is impossible.** If m = 2:
37: - α_vB/3 ∈ ℤ forces α_vB = 3, so t_v = 1 for all five v ∉ B. All seven vertices then have t = 1.
38: - W has only 2 cosets, so one coset contains the pieces of at least 4 vertices.
39: - λ_l = n_l/2 for every l, and gcd(n_x/2, n_y/2) = gcd(n_x, n_y)/2 = 3 for every pair, since all n are even.
40: - Sun(4) in W needs a pair with gcd ≥ 4. Contradiction.
41: 
42: So m ∈ {3, 6}, because m = 1 would mean W = G. This proves (i).
43: 
44: **Step 3: (ii).** If m = 3, then α_vB/2 ∈ ℤ for every v ∉ B.
45: 
46: **Step 4: (iii).** If m = 6:
47: - U_jU_k ⊆ W, and μ(U_jU_k) = 1/6 = μ(W), so U_jU_k = W.
48: - Disjointness gives g_j⁻¹g_k ∉ U_jU_k = W.
49: - For v ∉ B, λ_v = n_v α_vB/6, while λ_j = n_j/6 = w_j. Since gcd(w_j, λ_v) = 1, Sun(2) in W forbids v from meeting g_jW or g_kW.
50: - The rest is K7-BLOCK's argument with s = 2. ∎

71: ## Corollary P3 (Q24 with M = ∅) [NEW]
72: 
73: If M = ∅, then in a K7 configuration of type Q24, every twin pair B has **at least three** outside vertices v with α_vB = 4.
74: 
75: *Proof.* All five outside vertices are unmarked, with α_vB ∈ {2, 4}. Let k_4 be the number with α_vB = 4. By (i), m ∈ {3, 6}.
76: 
77: **Case m = 3.** W has 3 cosets.
78: - For v ∉ B: α_vB = 2 gives t_v = 1 and λ_v = 2w_v ("light"); α_vB = 4 gives t_v = 2 and λ_v = 4w_v ("heavy").
79: - j and k are light, with t = 1 and λ = 2w.
80: - The gcds are 2 for light–light, 2 for light–heavy and 4 for heavy–heavy.
81: - So an allowed set in a coset has at most 4 vertices. If it has at least 3 vertices, it has at most one light vertex, because every triple needs a pair with gcd ≥ 3.
82: - Hence a coset holding 2 light vertices holds nothing else, and at most 6 light incidences fit in total.
83: - The light incidences number 2 + (5 − k_4) = 7 − k_4.
84: 
85: | k_4 | light incidences | contradiction |
86: |---|---|---|
87: | 0 | 7 | more than 6 |
88: | 1 | 6 | all three cosets hold exactly 2 light vertices, leaving no coset for the heavy vertex |
89: | 2 | 5 | two cosets hold 2 light vertices and nothing else, so both heavy vertices must use 2 distinct cosets among the single remaining one |
90: 
91: So k_4 ≥ 3.
92: 
93: **Case m = 6.** By (iii), the five outside vertices lie in the 4 remaining cosets.
94: - Heavy vertices (α_vB = 4) meet all 4 cosets; light vertices (α_vB = 2) meet 2. λ_v = α_vB·w_v, with the same gcd pattern as above.
95: - An allowed coset set has at most 4 vertices, and at most one light vertex once it has at least 3.
96: 
97: | k_4 | contradiction |
98: |---|---|
99: | 0 | at most 2 light vertices per coset: 10 incidences against a capacity of 8 |
100: | 1, 2 | at most one light vertex per coset: 2(5 − k_4) > 4 light incidences needed |
101: | 4 | every coset already holds 4 vertices, so the light vertex cannot be placed |
102: | 5 | 5 vertices per coset |
103: 
104: So k_4 = 3.
105: 
106: In both cases k_4 ≥ 3. ∎

25: **Excess vector.** ε(v) = (e_v − 1, f_v − 1, g_v). Then ℓ_v = n_v/6 = 2^{ε_2(v)} 3^{ε_3(v)} 5^{ε_5(v)} w_v in every triangle, because γ = 6 throughout.
26: 
27: **The function d.** For 1 ≤ r ≤ 5 put
28: 
29:   d_v(r) := 2^{min(ε_2(v), v_2(r))} · 3^{min(ε_3(v), v_3(r))} · 5^{min(ε_5(v), v_5(r))}.
30: 
31: So d_v ≡ 1 if v ∈ U. If v ∈ M, then d_v(r) > 1 only when r shares one of v's marked primes: r ∈ {2, 4} for marker 2, r = 3 for marker 3, r = 5 for marker 5.

35: ## 1. Lemma K7-TRI (the triangle conditions in K7) [NEW, specialisation of L1 and L4]
36: 
37: For a triangle {x, y, z} in K7, let a ≤ b ≤ c be its three α's in sorted order.
38: 
39: **(T)**
40: - τ_min = lcm(r_x/d_x(r_x), r_y/d_y(r_y), r_z/d_z(r_z)).
41: - τ_max = a·b.
42: - T requires τ_min ≤ a·b.
43: 
44: **(P at apex v)**, with the other two vertices j, k, so that α_vj = r_k, α_vk = r_j and α_jk = r_v:
45: - u_v = 1/(6·d_v(r_v));
46: - μX_j = r_k/6 and μX_k = r_j/6;
47: - Lb = max(τ_min/(6 r_v), (r_j + r_k)/6 − 1, 0);
48: - lo = u_v⌈Lb/u_v⌉;
49: - hi = min(r_j, r_k)/6, minus u_v when the containment that equality needs is barred by K2. That containment is X_j ⊆ X_k when r_k < r_j, X_k ⊆ X_j when r_j < r_k, and both when the two are equal. K2 bars X_j ⊆ X_k unless r_k + r_v < 6.
50: - Ppair kills the pattern if lo > hi at some apex.
51: 
52: *Proof.*
53: 1. **Triangle quantities.** In K7, γ_y = lcm(6,6) = 6 and g = 6 on every edge.
54: 2. **τ_max.** L1 gives τ_max = min over apexes y of ⌊6·α_xz·min(α_xy, α_yz)/6⌋. Each apex contributes (opposite α)·(the smaller adjacent α). These are c·a, b·a and a·b, so the minimum is ab.
55: 3. **τ_min.** The τ_min term of the edge opposite v is r_v/gcd(r_v, ℓ_v). Since r_v ≤ 5 is coprime to w_v, gcd(r_v, ℓ_v) = d_v(r_v).
56: 4. **u.** L4 gives u_vjk = 1/gcd(n_v, I_jk) with I_jk = lcm(n_j, n_k)·α_jk. For p = 2, v_p(gcd) = min(1 + ε_2(v), max(v_2 n_j, v_2 n_k) + v_2(r_v)).
57:    - If v carries marker 2, then j and k have e = 1, and this equals 1 + min(ε_2(v), v_2 r_v).
58:    - Otherwise both expressions equal 1.
59:    - The same holds for p = 3. For p = 5 it equals min(ε_5(v), v_5 r_v).
60:    - The w-parts contribute nothing.
61:    - Hence gcd(n_v, I_jk) = 6·d_v(r_v).
62: 5. **Lb, lo, hi and K2.** These follow verbatim from L4 and rules-k7-addendum-v1 (F6), with g = 6 and γ = 6. Since d_v(r_v) is an integer, min(r_j, r_k)/6 is a multiple of u_v, so the floor in hi is exact. ∎

64: ## 2. Lemma K7-U (the table for all-unmarked triangles) [NEW]
65: 
66: If x, y, z ∈ U, then d ≡ 1 and u = 1/6. After T and Ppair, the sorted type (a, b, c) of {x, y, z} is one of these **11 types**:

68:   (1,1,1), (1,2,2), (1,3,3), (1,4,4), (2,2,2), (2,2,4), (2,3,3), (2,4,4), (3,3,3), (4,4,4), (5,5,5).

70: *Proof.*
71:

96: ## 3. Lemma K7-TWIN (twin blocks) [NEW]
97: 
98: **Definition.** x ~ y for x, y ∈ U means x = y or α_xy = 1.
99: 
100: **(a)** ~ is an equivalence relation on U. Its classes are the **twin blocks**.
101: 
102: **(b)** If x ~ y with x ≠ y, then α_xz = α_yz for **every** z ∈ V ∖ {x, y}, marked or unmarked.
103: 
104: **(c)** If x ~ y with x ≠ y, then α_xz ≠ 5 for every z.
105: 
106: **(d)** If x ~ y with x ≠ y, then U_zU_x = U_zU_y for every z ∈ V ∖ {x, y}.
107: 
108: *Proof.*
109: 
110: **(b) and (a).** Take a triangle {x, y, z} with α_xy = 1.
111: - The edge xy contributes 1/d_z(1) = 1 to τ_min.
112: - The edges xz and yz are opposite the unmarked vertices y and x, so they contribute α_xz and α_yz.
113: - So τ_min = lcm(α_xz, α_yz). The sorted type is (1, b, c) with b = min(α_xz, α_yz), so τ_max = b.
114: - T gives lcm(α_xz, α_yz) ≤ min(α_xz, α_yz), hence α_xz = α_yz. This is (b).
115: - For (a): with α_xy = α_yz = 1 and z ∈ U, (b) gives α_xz = α_yz = 1. So ~ is transitive. Reflexivity and symmetry are clear.
116: 
117: **(c).** Suppose α_xz = α_yz = 5. Take the apex x (u_x = 1/6), with j = y and k = z:
118: - μX_y = 1/6 < μX_z = 5/6, and τ_min = lcm(1, 5, 5) = 5;
119: - Lb ≥ 5/(6·5) = 1/6, so lo = 1/6;
120: - equality needs X_y ⊆ X_z, which K2 bars because α_xy + α_zy = 6. So hi = 0 < lo.
121: - Ppair kills this for every marking of z.
122: 
123: **(d).** Take the apex z, with j = x and k = y. By (b), μX_x = μX_y = b/6 with b = α_zx.
124: - τ_min = lcm(b, b, 1/d_z(1)) = b, so Lb ≥ τ_min/(6·1) = b/6.
125: - L4 gives μ(X_x ∩ X_y) ≥ b/6 = μX_x = μX_y. These are finite sets, so X_x = X_y. ∎
126: 
127: **Consequences.**
128: - α between two distinct blocks B and B′ is well defined (β(B, B′) := α_xy for x ∈ B, y ∈ B′), and β ∈ {2,3,4,5}.
129: - For z ∈ M, a_{zB} := α_zx (x ∈ B) is well defined, and a_{zB} ∈ {1,2,3,4} whenever |B| ≥ 2.

131: ## 4. Lemma K7-Q (the quotient trichotomy) [NEW]
132: 
133: Let B_1, …, B_b be the twin blocks of U. Exactly one of the following holds.
134: 
135: - **(Q5)** Every block is a singleton (b = |U|), and every β equals 5.
136: - **(Q23)** Every β lies in {2,3}, and "β = 2" is an equivalence relation on the blocks. So U carries a two-level nested partition: twin blocks inside **2-classes**, with α = 1 within a block, 2 between blocks of the same 2-class, and 3 between 2-classes.
137: - **(Q24)** Every β lies in {2,4}. The triangle tables put no further condition on the 2/4 colouring of the block pairs.
138: 
139: The degenerate cases b = 1 (all of U one block) and "all β = 2" fall under both Q23 and Q24. A coverage enumeration may assign them to either family, provided it treats them exactly once.
140: 
141: *Proof.* Triangles on three distinct blocks have every α ≥ 2. By K7-U their types lie in
142: 
143:   {(2,2,2), (2,2,4), (2,3,3), (2,4,4), (3,3,3), (4,4,4), (5,5,5)}.
144: 
145: **A 5 forces Q5.** Suppose β(s, t) = 5.
146: - By K7-TWIN(c), B_s and B_t are singletons.
147: - For every other block u, the triangle (s, t, u) must be (5,5,5).
148: - For blocks u, u′ ∉ {s}, the triangle (s, u, u′) then has two 5's, so β(u, u′) = 5.
149: - So every β = 5, and by TWIN(c) every block is a singleton.
150: 
151: **3 and 4 never coexist.** Suppose β(s, t) = 3 and β(x, y) = 4.
152: - A triangle containing a 3 is (2,3,3) or (3,3,3), so s and t have no 4-edges. In particular x, y ∉ {s, t}.
153: - The triangle (s, x, y) contains a 4 and its other two values lie in {2,3}. A type containing a 4 is (2,2,4), (2,4,4) or (4,4,4), so β(s,x) = β(s,y) = 2. Likewise β(t,x) = 2.
154: - Then (s, t, x) has type (2,2,3), which T excludes. Contradiction.
155: 
156: **Q23.** With only 2's and 3's, the type (2,2,3) is excluded, so β = 2 is transitive.
157: 
158: **Q24.** With only 2's and 4's, all four types (2,2,2), (2,2,4), (2,4,4) and (4,4,4) are allowed. ∎

160: ## 5. Lemma K7-Q5 (a written elimination of a sub-family) [NEW]
161: 
162: **Claim.** A K7 configuration of type Q5 has |U| = 4, that is, exactly three marked vertices. Every Q5 configuration with |U| ≥ 5 is impossible.
163: 
164: *Proof.* Let v ∈ U and apply Corollary VFC-count at v.
165: 
166: 1. **Unmarked neighbours.** For each of the |U| − 1 unmarked neighbours j, α_vj = 5, so q_j ≤ 6 − 5 = 1. Hence q_j = 1 and d_j = q_j·n_j/6 = w_j.
167: 2. **Marked neighbours.** For z ∈ M, d_z = q_z·n_z/6 = q_z·m_z·w_z with m_z := 2^{ε_2}3^{ε_3}5^{ε_5} of z. The m_z of distinct marked vertices are pairwise coprime, because each marker sits on one vertex.
168: 3. **Unmarked neighbours stand alone.** w_j is coprime to 30, to every q ≤ 5 and to every other w. So gcd(d_j, d_x) = 1 for every other neighbour x, and no allowed set contains j together with another vertex.
169: 4. **The weights.** Put y_j = 1 on each unmarked neighbour. This contributes (|U| − 1)/6 to Σ y q/6.
170: 
171: The cases:
172: 
173: | \|U\| | \|M\| | argument | total |
174: |---|---|---|---|
175: | 7 | 0 | unmarked neighbours alone | 6/6 = 1 |
176: | 6 | 1 | add y_z = 1 for the marked z | 5/6 + q_z/6 ≥ 1 |
177: | 5 | 2 | see below | ≥ 4/6 + 2/6 = 1 |
178: 
179: For |U| = 5, with marked vertices z and z′:
180: - If {z, z′} is not allowed, put y_z = y_z′ = 1.
181: - If it is allowed, then gcd(d_z, d_z′) ≥ 2 forces max(q_z, q_z′) ≥ 2, since q_z = q_z′ = 1 gives gcd(m_z w_z, m_z′ w_z′) = 1. Put weight 1 on the larger q.
182: 
183: In every case some feasible y has Σ y_j q_j/6 ≥ 1, contradicting VFC-count. This holds for every admissible q-vector at v. ∎
184: 
185: **Not covered.** For |U| = 4 the same bound can fall below 1, for example when the marked q-vector is (2,2,1) with the q = 1 vertex carrying marker 2. Q5 with |U| = 4 therefore stays in the coverage family below.

187: ## 6. Theorem K7-COV (the coverage statement for a reduced representation) [NEW]
188: 
189: **(i) Reduced data.** Every K7 pattern (profiles and α) that passes T and Ppair on all triangles of U, and on all triangles with two vertices in one twin block, is determined by:
190: 1. **Marking data:** M (|M| ≤ 3), with the capped profiles of its vertices. Each marker 2/3/5 sits on at most one vertex, the capped excesses are ε_2 ∈ {0..3}, ε_3 ∈ {0,1} and ε_5 ∈ {0,1,2}, and every pairwise gcd is 6.
191: 2. **The twin partition** of U (|U| = 7 − |M| ≥ 4).
192: 3. **Quotient data**, one of:
193:    - Q5 (all blocks singletons, every β = 5). K7-Q5, which uses VFC at an unmarked vertex, allows this only when |U| = 4;
194:    - Q23 (a partition of the blocks into 2-classes);
195:    - Q24 (a 2/4 colouring of the block pairs).
196: 4. **Marked rows:** a_{zB} ∈ {1,…,5} for z ∈ M and each block B, with a_{zB} ≤ 4 when |B| ≥ 2.
197: 5. **α among the marked vertices.**
198: 
199: **(ii) Lifting.** The data of (i) determine the full α-matrix by α_xy = 1 within blocks, β between blocks, and a_{zB} towards marked z. Each data set lifts to exactly one pattern up to Sym(U).
200: 
201: **(iii) Symmetry.**
202: - The profile stabiliser is Sym(U) × {id_M}. All unmarked vertices have the profile (1,1,0), and distinct marked vertices have distinct marker sets, hence distinct profiles.
203: - A permutation of U maps twin blocks to twin blocks of the same size, and transforms the quotient data and the marked rows accordingly. Two data sets give the same pattern orbit iff some size-preserving bijection of the blocks carries one data set onto the other: quotient data, marked rows, and the same marked–marked α's.
204: 
205: **(iv) Soundness obligation.** The reduction removes nothing that T and Ppair keep. Every rule (T, K, VFC, P, RPLP) must still be applied to the **lifted 7-vertex pattern**, including every triangle and star that contains marked vertices, using the d-relaxed conditions of K7-TRI. No rule may be evaluated on the quotient alone.
206: 
207: **(v) Completeness up to symmetry.** Enumerate the data of (i), one representative per orbit of (iii), and evaluate every rule on the lift. This covers every K7 configuration in every group: the pattern of any configuration passes T, Ppair and VFC, so it is one of these lifts.
208: 
209: *Proof.*
210: - (i) and (ii) follow from K7-TWIN (a)–(c), K7-Q and K7-Q5.
211: - (iii): the stabiliser is the stabiliser of the profile tuple; the CAP hypotheses hold by README P0–P1.
212: - (iv) and (v): the pattern of any configuration passes T and Ppair, so (i) and (ii) apply to it. It also passes VFC, so K7-Q5 applies to it, and it is one of the lifts. Rules are evaluated on actual patterns. ∎
213: 
214: **Marked-vertex relaxation (exact form).** In a triangle containing z ∈ M:
215: - only the edge opposite z can have its τ_min term reduced, namely to r_z/d_z(r_z);
216: - at the apex z the P-grid refines to 1/(6·d_z(r_z));
217: - triangles with two marked vertices z, z′ reduce at most the two edges opposite them.
218: 
219: **Example of the relaxation.** Let z carry marker 2 with ε_2(z) = 1, and let x, y be unmarked vertices in different blocks with α_xy = 4, α_zx = 1 and α_zy = 2.
220: - T: τ_min = lcm(4/d_z(4), 1, 2) = lcm(2, 1, 2) = 2 ≤ τ_max = 1·2.
221: - Ppair passes at all three apexes. I checked this by hand: at the apex z the grid is 1/12, with lo = 1/12 and hi = 1/6.
222: 
223: For an all-unmarked triangle the type (1,2,4) fails T. So α = 1 between a marked and an unmarked vertex does not force equal rows; TWIN(b) holds only for unmarked twins.

225: ## 7. Structural leads (proved facts, not used for any kill; FH remains UNUSED)
226: 
227: **Twins generate a subgroup.** By K7-TWIN(d) and L6, twins j ~ k satisfy:
228: - W := ⟨U_j, U_k⟩ ≠ G, with m = [G:W] dividing 6;
229: - [U_v : U_v ∩ W] = m·α_vj/6 ∈ ℤ for all v ∉ {j, k}.

231: So:
232: - m = 2 forces α_vj = 3 for every v outside the pair;
233: - m = 3 forces α_vj ∈ {2, 4};
234: - m = 6 gives U_jU_k = W, a subgroup of index 6, since μ(U_jU_k) = 1/6.
