35: ## 1. Lemma ZS
36: 
37: **Lemma ZS.**  Let p >= 5 be prime and let k, kappa be integers with
38:   (G) k >= 1,  3k <= 2p + 1,  0 <= kappa <= min(k - 1, p - k).
39: Let Y be a sequence of R = 2p + 2kappa - k odd elements of Z_(2p) such that
40:   (ZP) Y has no zero-sum subsequence of length in [2kappa + 2, 2p].
41: Then there is an odd generator g of Z_(2p) such that, with b = b(y) relative to g,
42:   cost(Y) := sum over y with b > 0 of (b - 1)  +  sum over y with b < 0 of |b|  <=  k - 1.
43: In particular every |b| <= k (so no term is (1,0) = p g), and with E := sum_y (|b| - 1) and N := the number of terms
44: with b < 0, E <= k - 1 - N.
45: (If k <= (p+1)/2, then min(k - 1, p - k) = k - 1, so (G) reads 0 <= kappa <= k - 1; this is the v1 lemma.)
46: 
47: ## 2. Proof of Lemma ZS
48: 
49: Consequences of (G) used below:
50: - (G1) kappa <= (p-1)/2, so 4kappa <= 2p - 2, because min(k - 1, p - k) <= ((k - 1) + (p - k))/2;
51: - (G2) 3(k - 1) + 2 <= 2p;
52: - (G3) k <= p - 2 (from 3k <= 2p + 1 if p >= 7; for p = 5, k <= 3);
53: - (G4) 3k <= 2p + 2;
54: - (G5) 2kappa <= 2p - 2k.
55: 
56: Step 1 (every zero-sum is short).  Every zero-sum subsequence T of Y has |T| <= 2kappa.
57: A minimal zero-sum subsequence has length <= 2p (deleting one term leaves a zero-sum-free sequence, of length <= 2p - 1
58: by (D)), and its length is even, so by (ZP) it is <= 2kappa.  A zero-sum T is a product T_1 ... T_r of minimal zero-sum
59: subsequences (take one minimal zero-sum subsequence, then induct on the rest).  If |T| >= 2kappa + 2, let j be least
60: with |T_1| + ... + |T_j| >= 2kappa + 2.  Then T_1 ... T_j is a zero-sum of length at least 2kappa + 2 and at most
61: 4kappa <= 2p - 2 (G1), contrary to (ZP).  []
62: 
63: Step 2 (splitting).  Let Z be a zero-sum subsequence of Y of maximal length (Z may be empty), and Y' the complementary
64: subsequence.  Y' is zero-sum-free: a nonempty zero-sum V <= Y' would make Z V a longer zero-sum.  By Step 1
65: |Z| <= 2kappa, so L := |Y'| = R - |Z| >= 2p - k, and e := 2p - 1 - L = k - 1 - 2kappa + |Z| satisfies 0 <= e <= k - 1
66: (L <= 2p - 1 by (D)).
67: 
68: Step 3 (structure of Y').  Since 3e + 2 <= 2p (G2), Lemma MULT (iii), (ii) applies to Y'.  Let m be the largest
69: multiplicity in Y', attained by g.  Then m >= max(2, e + 1), g is a generator, and Y' = g^m (d_1 g) ... (d_q g) with
70: odd d_j >= 3 and e' := sum_j (d_j - 1) <= e.  MULT (i) gives m >= L - e >= 2p - 2k + 1.  Put W' := m + sum_j d_j =
71: L + e'.
72: **Lemma COMP.**  (a) Every integer t in [0, W'] is the weight w(Q) := #(g in Q) + sum of the d_j of the terms d_j g in Q
73: of some subsequence Q <= Y'.  (b) W' <= 2p - 1.  (c) Every Q <= Y' has sum w(Q) g and |Q| = w(Q) - (sum of d_j - 1 over
74: its terms d_j g) >= w(Q) - e'.
75: Proof.  (a) Every d_j <= e' + 1 <= e + 1 <= m.  The weights of subsequences of g^m are all integers in [0, m].  If all
76: integers in [0, M] are weights and d <= M + 1, then after adding d g all integers in [0, M + d] are weights (t <= M,
77: or t - d in [0, M]).  Add d_1, ..., d_q in turn.  (b) If W' >= 2p, some Q has weight 2p by (a), so sum 2p g = 0: a
78: nonempty zero-sum in Y'.  (c) Clear.  []
79: Consequences:
80: - (C1) Every d_j <= e' + 1 <= k <= p - 2 (G3), so each term of Y' is b g with b in {1} u [3, k] and b = b(y).
81: - (C2) W' >= L >= 2p - k.
82: - (C3) e' <= e.
83: - (C4) m >= 2p - 2k + 1 >= k - 1 (G4).
84: 
85: Step 4 (zero-sums through Z).  For U <= Z put s(U) := sum over y in U of b(y), an integer, and let tau(U) in
86: {0, 1, ..., 2p - 1} satisfy tau(U) = -s(U) (mod 2p).  If 1 <= tau(U) <= W', then every Q <= Y' with w(Q) = tau(U) has
87: |U| + |Q| <= 2kappa.  Hence |U| + tau(U) - e' <= 2kappa (COMP (a), (c)), and if tau(U) <= m also |U| + tau(U) <= 2kappa
88: (take Q = g^tau(U)).
89: Proof.  U and Q are disjoint parts of Y, and U Q has sum (s(U) + tau(U)) g = 0.  Apply Step 1.  []
90: 
91: Step 5 (every sub-sum of Z is small).  For every U <= Z, tau(U) lies in {0} u [1, k - 1] u [2p - k + 1, 2p - 1].
92: Proof.  Suppose k <= tau(U) <= 2p - k, and let U^c be the complement of U in Z.  Z is a zero-sum, so s(Z) = 0 (mod 2p).
93: Hence tau(U^c) = 2p - tau(U), which also lies in [k, 2p - k].  Both values are <= 2p - k <= W' (C2), so Step 4
94: applies twice: |U| + tau(U) - e' <= 2kappa and |U^c| + 2p - tau(U) - e' <= 2kappa.  Adding gives
95: |Z| + 2p - 2e' <= 4kappa.  With e' <= e = k - 1 - 2kappa + |Z| this becomes |Z| >= 2p - 2k + 2.  But
96: |Z| <= 2kappa <= 2p - 2k (G5): a contradiction.  []
97: 
98: Step 6 (Z is balanced).  Every term of Z has |b| <= k - 1, by Step 5 with U a single term (k - 1 < p).  Let Z+ and Z- be
99: the terms of Z with b > 0 and b < 0, and let sigma+ := sum over Z+ of b and sigma- := sum over Z- of |b| (integers).
100: Then sigma+ <= k - 1, sigma- <= k - 1 and sigma+ = sigma- =: sigma.
101: Proof.  List Z+ as y_1, ..., y_r.  The integer partial sums S_j = b(y_1) + ... + b(y_j) increase by at most k - 1 per
102: step.  If sigma+ = S_r >= k, the least j with S_j >= k has k <= S_j <= 2k - 2.  The representative of S_j mod 2p in
103: (-p, p] is S_j if S_j <= p, and S_j - 2p otherwise.  Its absolute value is at least min(k, 2p - 2k + 2) >= k (G4),
104: contrary to Step 5 for U = {y_1, ..., y_j}.  Likewise sigma- <= k - 1.  Then s(Z) = sigma+ - sigma- lies in
105: [-(k-1), k - 1] and is 0 mod 2p, so sigma+ = sigma-.  []
106: 
107: Step 7 (room left in Y').  W' <= 2p - 1 - sigma.
108: Proof.  For sigma = 0 this is COMP (b).  Let sigma >= 1; then tau(Z+) = 2p - sigma lies in [2p - k + 1, 2p - 1].
109: Suppose 2p - sigma <= W'.  Step 4 gives |Z+| + 2p - sigma - e' <= 2kappa.  With e' <= k - 1 - 2kappa + |Z| this
110: becomes 2p + 1 <= sigma + k + |Z-|.  Since every |b| >= 1, |Z-| <= sigma- = sigma, so 2p + 1 <= 2sigma + k <= 3k - 2,
111: contrary to (G4).  So W' < 2p - sigma.  []
112: 
113: Step 8 (negatives of Z).  |Z-| + sigma <= 2kappa.
114: Proof.  If sigma = 0, Z- is empty.  Otherwise tau(Z-) = sigma lies in [1, k - 1], and sigma <= k - 1 <= m (C4), so
115: Step 4 with Q = g^sigma gives |Z-| + sigma <= 2kappa.  []
116: 
117: Step 9 (count).  By (C1) the terms of Y' contribute e' to cost(Y).  By Step 6 the terms of Z contribute
118: (sigma - |Z+|) + sigma.  Hence
119:   cost(Y) = (W' - L) + 2sigma - |Z+|
120:           <= (2p - 1 - sigma - L) + 2sigma - |Z+|        (Step 7)
121:           = e + sigma - |Z+|  =  k - 1 - 2kappa + |Z-| + sigma
122:           <= k - 1                                       (Step 8).
123: Every |b| is <= k: for Y' by (C1), for Z by Step 6.  Finally cost(Y) = sum_y (|b| - 1) + #{b < 0} = E + N.  []

125: Remarks.
126: (1) The bound is attained.  For kappa = k - 1 and Y = g^(2p-1) (-g)^kappa (which needs kappa <= p - k, i.e.
127: k <= (p+1)/2): Z = (g (-g))^kappa, Y' = g^(2p-1-kappa), sigma = kappa, cost(Y) = k - 1, with E = 0 and N = k - 1.  For
128: kappa = 0 and Y = g^(2p-k) the cost is 0.
129: (2) The new step is Step 5.  It uses the full length R through e, and the complementary pair U, U^c of the maximal
130: zero-sum Z.  Steps 6-9 then bound positive and negative weights together, so neither G >= 2kappa + w_+ (WT (b), (c) of
131: ROT-few) nor (F) 5k <= 2p + 4 is needed.
132: (3) Where (G) is used:
133: - (G1) in Step 1;
134: - (G2) for MULT;
135: - (G3) in (C1);
136: - (G4) in (C4) and Steps 6-7;
137: - (G5) only in Step 5.
138: With kappa > p - k, Step 5's contradiction disappears.  The lemma's conclusion itself fails at k = p: take kappa = p - 1
139: and Y = g^(2p-1) (3g)^(p-1) (R = 3p - 2).
140: - (ZP) holds: the only length in its window is 2p, and g^a (3g)^b with a + b = 2p has sum 2b g, which is 0 only for b in
141:   {0, p}.  Neither is available.
142: - Every generator costs too much.  For any odd generator h, write g = beta h.  The 2p - 1 copies of g already cost at
143:   least 2p - 1 > p - 1 unless beta = 1, and for h = g the copies of 3g cost 2(p - 1) > p - 1.
144: So near k = p a proof must use GRX patterns with sign changes (Phi >= 2), not only (ZP).  For p - k < kappa <= k - 1
145: with 3k <= 2p + 1, the lemma is neither proved nor refuted here.

147: ## 3. Theorem ROT-ZS and Corollary ROT-HALF
148: 
149: **Theorem ROT-ZS.**  Let p >= 5 be prime and w an avoider of length 4p - 2 over D_(2p) with o := o_F(w) such that
150: 3o <= 2p + 1 and F(w) >= 3o - 2 (equivalently kappa(w) <= p - o).  Then all rotation letters of w lie in one set
151: {rho_c, rho_-c}, c != 0.
152: Proof.  Take the normal form w^ of proof-ROT-few-E-v4.md Sec. 0 (L32-43).  It is an avoider of the same length,
153: rotation multiset and F, given by a cyclic representation with 1 <= k <= o odd blocks and every empty run between blocks
154: of different values; the ending with one even block is impossible there.  If k = 1, Proposition K1 makes all rotations
155: equal.  Let k >= 2.  Then H = (F - k)/2 and kappa := p - 1 - H = kappa(w) - (o - k)/2.  Also kappa >= 0
156: (proof-SHgen-v3.md L37), and Lemma DAV gives kappa <= k - 1.  Moreover
157: kappa <= (p - o) - (o - k)/2 <= p - k, as k <= o.  And 3k <= 3o <= 2p + 1, so (G) holds.  GRX gives (ZP) for the
158: sequence Y of the values Y(x) over the rotation letters, of length R = 2p + 2kappa - k (L42-43).  So Lemma ZS applies.
159: Write its generator as g = Y(c); then c != 0, since g != (1,0).  Y(x) = b g means x = b c (mod p), so the SHgen weight
160: of rho_x relative to c (proof-SHgen-v3.md L39-43) is b_x = b(Y(x)).  Every |b| <= k <= p - 2, so no rotation is rho_0,
161: the lights are the terms with b = +-1, and E and N are those of Lemma ZS: E <= k - 1 - N.  If E = 0, every rotation is
162: rho_c or rho_-c.  Otherwise eta >= 1, and the two hypotheses of Theorem SHgen hold.
163: - (H): 2H = 2p - 2 - 2kappa >= 2p - 2 - 2(p - k) = 2k - 2 >= k - 1 >= E.
164: - (K): every heavy has |b| >= 3, so eta <= E/2 <= (k-1)/2, and a_max <= E + 1 <= k.  Hence
165:   2p - eta - a_max - 1 >= 2p - (k-1)/2 - k - 1 >= k, because 5k <= 4p - 1 (from 3k <= 2p + 1 and p >= 4).
166: If all runs of w^ are nonempty, w^ is in the setting of proof-SHgen-v3.md Sec. 0 and Theorem SHgen gives w^ an identity
167: 2p-subword.  Otherwise Lemma SHgen-E (proof-ROT-few-E-v4.md Sec. 1) gives one; its REFL instances have
168: o_F <= k <= p, supplied by Corollary REFL-few-odd.  Either way this contradicts that w^ avoids, so E = 0.  The rotation
169: multiset of w is that of w^.  []

171: **Corollary ROT-HALF.**  Every avoider w of length 4p - 2 (p >= 5 prime) with o := o_F(w) <= (p+1)/2 has all rotation
172: letters in one {rho_c, rho_-c}, c != 0.
173: Proof.  With k and kappa as above, kappa(w) = kappa + (o - k)/2 <= (k - 1) + (o - k)/2 <= o - 1.  And o - 1 <= p - o as
174: o <= (p+1)/2, so F(w) >= 3o - 2.  Also 3o <= (3p+3)/2 <= 2p + 1.  Apply Theorem ROT-ZS.  []

27: ## 1. Lemma ZS
28: 
29: **Lemma ZS.**  Let p >= 5 be prime, let k, kappa be integers with 1 <= k <= (p+1)/2 and 0 <= kappa <= k - 1, and let Y be
30: a sequence of R = 2p + 2kappa - k odd elements of Z_(2p) such that
31:   (ZP) Y has no zero-sum subsequence of length in [2kappa + 2, 2p].
32: Then there is an odd generator g of Z_(2p) such that, with b = b(y) relative to g,
33:   cost(Y) := sum over y with b > 0 of (b - 1)  +  sum over y with b < 0 of |b|  <=  k - 1.
34: In particular every term has |b| <= k (so no term is (1,0) = p g), and with E := sum_y (|b| - 1) and N := the number of
35: terms with b < 0, E <= k - 1 - N.

37: ## 2. Proof of Lemma ZS
38: 
39: Step 1 (every zero-sum is short).  Every zero-sum subsequence T of Y has |T| <= 2kappa.
40: A minimal zero-sum subsequence has length <= 2p (deleting one term leaves a zero-sum-free sequence, of length <= 2p - 1
41: by (D)), and its length is even, so by (ZP) it is <= 2kappa.  A zero-sum T is a product T_1 ... T_r of minimal zero-sum
42: subsequences (take one minimal zero-sum subsequence, then induct on the rest).  If |T| >= 2kappa + 2, let j be least
43: with |T_1| + ... + |T_j| >= 2kappa + 2.  Then T_1 ... T_j is a zero-sum of length at least 2kappa + 2 and at most
44: 2kappa + 2kappa = 4kappa <= 4k - 4 <= 2p - 2, contrary to (ZP).  []
45: 
46: Step 2 (splitting).  Let Z be a zero-sum subsequence of Y of maximal length (Z may be empty), and Y' the complementary
47: subsequence.  Y' is zero-sum-free: a nonempty zero-sum V <= Y' would make Z V a longer zero-sum.  By Step 1
48: |Z| <= 2kappa, so L := |Y'| = R - |Z| >= 2p - k, and e := 2p - 1 - L = k - 1 - 2kappa + |Z| satisfies 0 <= e <= k - 1
49: (L <= 2p - 1 by (D)).
50: 
51: Step 3 (structure of Y').  Since 3e + 2 <= 3k - 1 <= 2p, Lemma MULT (iii), (ii) applies to Y'.  Let m be the largest
52: multiplicity in Y', attained by g.  Then g is a generator, and Y' = g^m (d_1 g) ... (d_q g) with odd d_j >= 3 and
53: e' := sum_j (d_j - 1) <= e.  MULT (i) gives m >= L - e >= 2p - 2k + 1.  Put W' := m + sum_j d_j = L + e'.
54: **Lemma COMP.**  (a) Every integer t in [0, W'] is the weight w(Q) := #(g in Q) + sum of the d_j of the terms d_j g in Q
55: of some subsequence Q <= Y'.  (b) W' <= 2p - 1.  (c) Every Q <= Y' has sum w(Q) g and |Q| = w(Q) - (sum of d_j - 1 over
56: its terms d_j g) >= w(Q) - e'.
57: Proof.  (a) Every d_j <= e' + 1 <= e + 1 <= m.  The weights of subsequences of g^m are all integers in [0, m].  If all
58: integers in [0, M] are weights and d <= M + 1, then after adding d g all integers in [0, M + d] are weights (t <= M,
59: or t - d in [0, M]).  Add d_1, ..., d_q in turn.  (b) If W' >= 2p, some Q has weight 2p by (a), so sum 2p g = 0: a
60: nonempty zero-sum in Y'.  (c) Clear.  []
61: Consequences: (C1) every d_j <= e' + 1 <= k <= p - 2 (as k <= (p+1)/2 and p >= 5), so each term of Y' is b g with
62: b in {1} u [3, k] and b = b(y); (C2) W' >= L >= 2p - k; (C3) e' <= e; (C4) m >= 2p - 2k + 1 >= k - 1, as 3k <= 2p + 2.
63: 
64: Step 4 (zero-sums through Z).  For U <= Z put s(U) := sum over y in U of b(y), an integer, and let tau(U) in
65: {0, 1, ..., 2p - 1} satisfy tau(U) = -s(U) (mod 2p).  If 1 <= tau(U) <= W', then every Q <= Y' with w(Q) = tau(U) has
66: |U| + |Q| <= 2kappa.  Hence |U| + tau(U) - e' <= 2kappa (COMP (a), (c)), and if tau(U) <= m also |U| + tau(U) <= 2kappa
67: (take Q = g^tau(U)).
68: Proof.  U and Q are disjoint parts of Y, and U Q has sum (s(U) + tau(U)) g = 0.  Apply Step 1.  []
69: 
70: Step 5 (every sub-sum of Z is small).  For every U <= Z, tau(U) lies in {0} u [1, k - 1] u [2p - k + 1, 2p - 1].
71: Equivalently, the representative of s(U) mod 2p in (-p, p] lies in [-(k-1), k - 1].
72: Proof.  Suppose k <= tau(U) <= 2p - k, and let U^c be the complement of U in Z.  Z is a zero-sum, so s(Z) g = 0, i.e.
73: s(Z) = 0 (mod 2p).  Hence tau(U^c) = 2p - tau(U), which also lies in [k, 2p - k].  Both values are <= 2p - k <= W' (C2),
74: so Step 4 applies twice: |U| + tau(U) - e' <= 2kappa and |U^c| + 2p - tau(U) - e' <= 2kappa.  Adding gives
75: |Z| + 2p - 2e' <= 4kappa.  With e' <= e = k - 1 - 2kappa + |Z| this becomes 2p - 2k + 2 <= |Z| <= 2kappa <= 2k - 2,
76: i.e. 4k >= 2p + 4, contrary to k <= (p+1)/2.  []
77: 
78: Step 6 (Z is balanced).  Every term of Z has |b| <= k - 1 (Step 5 with U a single term).  Let Z+ and Z- be the terms of Z
79: with b > 0 and b < 0, and sigma+ := sum over Z+ of b, sigma- := sum over Z- of |b| (integers).  Then
80: sigma+ <= k - 1, sigma- <= k - 1 and sigma+ = sigma- =: sigma.
81: Proof.  List Z+ as y_1, ..., y_r.  The integer partial sums S_j = b(y_1) + ... + b(y_j) increase by at most k - 1 per
82: step.  If sigma+ = S_r >= k, the least j with S_j >= k has k <= S_j <= 2k - 2 <= p - 1.  Then the representative of
83: s({y_1, ..., y_j}) is S_j >= k, contrary to Step 5.  The same argument gives sigma- <= k - 1.  Then
84: s(Z) = sigma+ - sigma- lies in [-(k-1), k - 1] and is 0 mod 2p, so sigma+ = sigma-.  []

86: Step 7 (room left in Y').  W' <= 2p - 1 - sigma.
87: Proof.  For sigma = 0 this is COMP (b).  Let sigma >= 1; then tau(Z+) = 2p - sigma lies in [2p - k + 1, 2p - 1].

89: becomes 2p + 1 <= sigma + k + |Z-|.  Since every |b| >= 1, |Z-| <= sigma- = sigma, so 2p + 1 <= 2sigma + k <= 3k - 2,
90: contrary to k <= (p+1)/2.  So W' < 2p - sigma.  []

93: Proof.  If sigma = 0, Z- is empty.  Otherwise tau(Z-) = sigma lies in [1, k - 1], and sigma <= k - 1 <= m (C4).  So
94: Step 4 with Q = g^sigma gives |Z-| + sigma <= 2kappa.  []

97: (sigma - |Z+|) + sigma.  Hence
98:   cost(Y) = (W' - L) + 2sigma - |Z+|

100:           = e + sigma - |Z+|  =  k - 1 - 2kappa + |Z-| + sigma
101:           <= k - 1                                       (Step 8).
102: Every |b| is <= k: for Y' by (C1), for Z by Step 6.  Finally cost(Y) = sum_y (|b| - 1) + #{b < 0} = E + N.  []

104: Remarks.  (1) The bound is attained: kappa = k - 1 and Y = g^(2p-1) (-g)^kappa give Z = (g (-g))^kappa, Y' =
105: g^(2p-1-kappa), sigma = kappa and cost(Y) = k - 1 (E = 0, N = k - 1); kappa = 0 and Y = g^(2p-k) give cost 0.
106: (2) The new step is Step 5.  It uses the full length R through e, and the complementary pair U, U^c of the maximal
107: zero-sum Z.  Steps 6-9 then bound positive and negative weights together, so neither G >= 2kappa + w_+ (WT (b), (c))
108: nor (F) is needed.

110: three hold exactly when k <= (p+1)/2.  At k = p the conclusion is false.  Take kappa = p - 1 and
111: Y = g^(2p-1) (3g)^(p-1) (R = 3p - 2).  The only length in the (ZP) window is 2p, and g^a (3g)^b with a + b = 2p has
112: sum 2b g, which is 0 only for b in {0, p}; neither is available.  For any odd generator h, writing g = beta h, the
113: 2p - 1 copies of g already cost at least 2p - 1 > p - 1 unless beta = 1, and for h = g the copies of 3g cost
114: 2(p - 1) > p - 1.  So for k near p a proof must use GRX patterns with sign changes (Phi >= 2), not only (ZP).  For
115: (p+3)/2 <= k <= p - 1 neither a proof nor a counterexample of Lemma ZS is known here.

117: ## 3. Theorem ROT-HALF
118: 
119: **Theorem ROT-HALF.**  Let p >= 5 be prime and w an avoider of length 4p - 2 over D_(2p) with o_F(w) <= (p+1)/2.  Then
120: all rotation letters of w lie in one set {rho_c, rho_-c}, c != 0.
121: Proof.  Take the normal form w^ of proof-ROT-few-E-v4.md Sec. 0 (L32-43).  It is an avoider of the same length and
122: rotation multiset, given by a cyclic representation with 1 <= k <= o_F(w) odd blocks and every empty run between blocks
123: of different values; the ending with one even block is impossible there.  If k = 1, Proposition K1 makes all rotations
124: equal.  Let k >= 2.  Then H = (F - k)/2, kappa = p - 1 - H >= 0, R = 2p + 2kappa - k, Lemma DAV gives kappa <= k - 1,
125: and GRX gives (ZP) for the sequence Y of the values Y(x) over the rotation letters (L42-43).  As k <= (p+1)/2, Lemma ZS
126: applies.  Write its generator as g = Y(c).  Then c != 0, since g != (1,0).  Y(x) = b g means x = b c (mod p), so the
127: SHgen weight of rho_x relative to c (proof-SHgen-v3.md L39-43) is b_x = b(Y(x)).  Every |b| <= k <= p - 2, so no rotation
128: is rho_0, the lights are the terms with b = +-1, and E and N are those of Lemma ZS: E <= k - 1 - N.  If E = 0 every
129: rotation is rho_c or rho_-c.  Otherwise eta >= 1 and we check the two hypotheses of Theorem SHgen.
130: - (H): 2H = 2p - 2 - 2kappa >= 2p - 2k >= k - 1 >= E, since 3k <= 2p + 1.
131: - (K): every heavy has |b| >= 3, so eta <= E/2 <= (k-1)/2, and a_max <= E + 1 <= k.  Hence
132:   2p - eta - a_max - 1 >= 2p - (k-1)/2 - k - 1 >= k, because 5k <= 4p - 1 (from k <= (p+1)/2 and p >= 3).
133: If all runs of w^ are nonempty, w^ is in the setting of proof-SHgen-v3.md Sec. 0 and Theorem SHgen gives w^ an identity
134: 2p-subword.  Otherwise Lemma SHgen-E (proof-ROT-few-E-v4.md Sec. 1) gives one.  Its REFL instances have o_F <= k <= p,
135: and Corollary REFL-few-odd supplies them.  Either way this contradicts that w^ avoids.  So E = 0.  The rotation multiset
136: of w is that of w^.  []

33: a = b = 0.  Then |A + B| >= |A| + |B| - 1.
34: Proof.  Induction on |B|; |B| = 1 is clear.  Let |B| >= 2.  Some e in A has e + B not inside A: otherwise A + b = A

36: B' := B n (A - e).  Then 0 in A' n B' (e in A), |B'| < |B| (some b in B has e + b outside A), |A'| + |B'| = |A| + |B|

39: outside A, b + (e + y) = 0 with b in B, e + y in A forces b = 0, so x = e in A, a contradiction.  By induction

43: 
44: Let D be a zero-sum-free SET of d >= 3 distinct odd elements with |A(D)| <= 2p-2.  Then some x in D has
45: |A(D)| >= |A(D - x)| + 2.

48: B on the cycle 0, x, 2x, ..., (2p-1)x.  As A(D) = B u (B + x) with B := A(D - x), B is ONE x-arc {u, u+x, ..., u+jx}

94: ## 5. Lemma WT (weight bounds)
95: 
96: Let Y satisfy (ZP) for some kappa >= 0 with 4kappa <= 2p, and let g be an odd generator of multiplicity G in Y.
97: Every term h != g of Y is h = r g with r odd in [3, 2p-1].
98:  (a) If 2p - r <= G then b := 2p - r <= 2kappa - 1.  Call such h NEGATIVE of weight b (h = -b g); otherwise h is a
99:      POSITIVE heavy of weight r, and r <= 2p - G - 1.  g is positive of weight 1.  P, N: the positive / negative terms;
100:      w_+ := the largest weight in P.
101:  (b) G <= 2p - 1; and if G >= 2kappa + w_+ then sum_(h in P) r_h <= 2p - 1.
102:  (c) If G >= 2kappa + w_+ and |P| >= 4kappa - 2, then sum_(h in N) (b_h + 1) <= 2kappa.
103: Proof.  (a) h g^(2p-r) is a zero-sum of length 2p - r + 1 in [2, 2p-2], so 2p - r + 1 <= 2kappa.
104: (b) g^(2p) would be a zero-sum of length 2p.  Suppose sum_P r >= 2p; then P has heavies.  Add positive heavies one at a
105: time until their weight sum s first reaches 2p - G; I := those added (|I| >= 1), s <= 2p - G - 1 + w_+ <= 2p.  Then
106: I g^(2p-s) (0 <= 2p - s <= G) is a zero-sum of length |I| + 2p - s, which is <= 2p (|I| <= s) and
107: >= 1 + 2p - (2p - G - 1 + w_+) = G + 2 - w_+ >= 2kappa + 2: contrary to (ZP).
108: (c) Suppose sum_N (b+1) >= 2kappa + 2; take the shortest prefix S (any order) with sum_S (b+1) >= 2kappa + 2.  Since
109: b + 1 <= 2kappa by (a) and the shorter prefix has sum <= 2kappa, |S| >= 2, sum_S (b+1) <= 4kappa <= 2p, and
110: t := sum_S b <= 4kappa - 2 <= |P| <= W_P := sum_P r.  Choose Q in P of weight exactly t: if t <= G, Q := g^t;

22: **Corollary OLSON-FEW.**  Let p >= 5 be prime and w a word of length 4p-1 over D_(2p).
23:  (a) If o_F(w) <= 3, or p >= 7 and o_F(w) <= 4, then w has an identity 2p-subword (conditional on REFL-few-odd only).
24:  (b) If 5 o_F(w) <= 2p + 4, then w has an identity 2p-subword (conditional on REFL-few-odd and SHgen).

26: ## Proof
27: 
28: Suppose w has no identity 2p-subword; every subword then avoids.  Let B denote the bound on o_F in (a) or (b); by
29: Corollary INV*-FEW every avoider of length 4p-2 with o_F <= B is RGR (rotations in one {rho_c, rho_-c}, c != 0, every
30: maximal cyclic reflection run constant).
31: Case 1: R <= 2.  Then F >= 4p - 3.  If R = 0 and all reflections are equal, any 2p of them multiply to
32: sigma_y^(2p) = 1.  Otherwise read w in the cyclic frame that starts at a rotation letter (R >= 1) or at a change of
33: reflection value (R = 0); no block wraps around this frame, so the blocks contain (F - o_F(w))/2 >= p disjoint equal
34: pairs sigma_y sigma_y adjacent in the framed word (o_F(w) <= B <= 2p - 3), and p of them form an identity 2p-subword
35: of the framed word.  A cyclic shift changes the product of a selection only by conjugation, so this selection is an
36: identity 2p-subword of w as well (conjugating back as in R/olson-runs-parent-verdict-v1.md L49): contradiction.
37: Case 2: some odd block has length >= 3.  Deleting one of its letters gives a word w' of length 4p-2 with
38: o_F(w') = o_F(w) - 1 (that block becomes even; nothing else changes).  w' is RGR.  w has the same rotations as w', and
39: its reflection runs differ from those of w' only by one more copy of a letter inside a constant block, so w is RGR.
40: Case 3: R >= 3 (the argument does not use that Case 2 fails; Case 3 alone covers every R >= 3, and Case 2 is the
41: shorter argument when it applies).  Delete two different rotation letters x1, x2: w_i := w - x_i has
42: o_F(w_i) <= o_F(w) (deleting a rotation never raises o_F: if its run becomes empty the two neighbouring blocks keep
43: their identity or fuse, and fusing never increases the number of odd blocks).  Both w_i are RGR.  A third rotation
44: lies in both, so their pairs {c_1, -c_1}, {c_2, -c_2} coincide and every rotation of w lies in it; deleting a rotation
45: only creates new adjacencies between reflections, so two adjacent reflections of different values in w would stay
46: adjacent in w_1.  Hence w is RGR.
47: In Cases 2 and 3, RGR-LONG(p) gives w an identity 2p-subword: contradiction.  []
