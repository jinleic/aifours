240: ## 3. What M(7) would then equal, and exactly why
241: 
242: **If** the composition (Link 14) is accepted:
243: 1. No MOQLS(7) triple has exactly 1, 2 or 3 non-classical squares (Links 1, 2, 14). By (C0), no non-classical
244:    triple of MOQLS(7) exists.
245: 2. For t ≥ 3, a non-classical set of t MOQLS(7) would contain an individually non-classical square (C0). That square
246:    together with any two other members is a set of 3 MOQLS(7), because orthogonality is pairwise, and it is
247:    non-classical by (C0). This contradicts step 1. Hence M(7) ≤ 2.
248: 3. The published non-classical pair (Ball–Simoens Corollary 7, or Huang–Li Theorem 5.2), cited by the accepted
249:    baseline verdict a39911fd…3ff2, gives M(7) ≥ 2.
250: 4. **Therefore M(7) = 2.** The baseline bound M(7) ≤ 3 is consistent with this but is not used in step 2.
251:

133: ## 3. Transport lemma (all 14 own lines, all 42 off-line points, every role assignment)
134: Let ℓ be any of the 14 own row/column lines and p any point off ℓ. For each square x, let w_x be the number of
135: r ∈ ℓ with g_x(p,r) = 1.
136: - w_x ≥ 1: ψ_{x,p} is a unit vector expanded in the ONB on ℓ (Gram-G3).
137: - The point of ℓ on p's other line (its column if ℓ is a row, its row if ℓ is a column) has g_x(p,·) = 0 by Gram-G1.
138:   It carries no variable. On the remaining six points, the neighbour sets of the three squares are pairwise disjoint
139:   by Gram-G5. Hence w_1 + w_2 + w_3 ≤ 6.
140: 
141: **Case (4,1,1).** If some w_a ≥ 4, the counts are exactly (4,1,1) and all six positions are used. Apply, in order:
142: - if ℓ is a column, the transpose of the cell array, acting simultaneously on all three squares (m14);
143: - a row relabelling ρ with ρ(ℓ) = 0 and ρ(row of p) = 1;
144: - a column relabelling κ with κ(column of p) = 0, the four a-neighbours sent to columns {1,2,3,4}, the b-neighbour to
145:   {5} and the c-neighbour to {6};
146: - the square relabelling a → 1, b → 2, c → 3.
147: 
148: These maps preserve the QLS axioms and mutual orthogonality, so the image is a K3 triple. Its Gram pattern meets the
149: 18 signed C411 cube literals, the `--sym` units, Gram-G1 … Gram-G5, and G4 on all lines (H2); G6 follows by
150: `G6-G2-DERIVATION.r1.md`. With the auxiliaries reconstructed as in §8, it therefore satisfies every clause of the
151: CNF A_C411 2b92f5b15cd641e4a07ecec241b7957deac972aa3c7333056ff3e739e46addf2 and every clause of the CNF B_C411
152: 01acf46491feef2fc26060266af8c2dead0cec9832c9e9ecbed83f886ecb8bf8. This contradicts G2.
153: 
154: **Case (3,2,1).** If w_a ≥ 3 and w_b ≥ 2 for distinct a, b, the counts are exactly (3,2,1). Use the same maps with
155: the a-neighbours sent to {1,2,3}, the b-neighbours to {4,5} and the c-neighbour to {6}. The image satisfies every
156: clause of A_C321 d598507d84a8d7595b30a100d528dca08bc0ce1aa71978583f09aac188298388 and of B_C321
157: 8b7b8124dbaf7088626ae853f4e10fe9590459dde1676377326ceecfb6d812c6, which contradicts G1.

160: claimed to be one of the source's listed set-isotopy generators. No transport through arbitrary ONBs or through
161: classical symbol lines is used.

195: Execution safety (parent source reviews of drafts 1–3; §11):
196: - The driver, generator r6 and controls r5 refuse to run unless they are the session and process-group leader
197:   started by the supervisor file that the frozen delta pins (`code/supervise_r3.py`). The supervisor is one
198:   single-threaded process. Its scope
199:   is the command's process group and nothing else. Members keep the group across re-parenting. A process that
200:   leaves the group is detected while it is still a child of a live member; it is never signalled and capacity is
201:   quarantined. The production commands (lane python drivers, gram_encB.py, cadical, kissat, drat-trim) do not leave
202:   the group. The /usr/bin/python3 shim execs in place, so the driver is the leader.
203: - Every 0.25 s it probes itself, the leader and every member through proc_pidinfo, against a fresh group listing
204:   from the same sample. Each probe is one of four states:
205:   - alive: full reads and RSS > 0;
206:   - zombie: BSD status SZOMB or, as on this Darwin host, ESRCH while the pid is still listed in our process group;
207:   - gone: ESRCH while not listed;
208:   - unknown: any other short, failed or zero-RSS read.
209:   A failed read never counts as death. The run trips on aggregate RSS above 3.5 GiB, free space below the 12e9 floor,
210:   the wall cap, an external SIGTERM, or a member alive after the leader exited. It also trips on any uncertainty: an
211:   unknown probe, a failed listing, a leader gone (ESRCH and not listed) while unreaped, or an escape.
212: - Stop: killpg(group, SIGTERM), sent only while the group has live or unknown members, and only while the leader is
213:   unreaped. The zombie leader keeps the group ID reserved, so no unrelated reused PID can be signalled. A group whose
214:   only member is a zombie is not signalled, because killpg raises EPERM on Darwin. If killpg fails while live members
215:   exist, those members stay unresolved and are quarantined as survivors. The supervisor waits at most 30 s.
216: - Survivors, or any uncertain trip, give an exclusive-create capacity quarantine at
217:   `evidence/k3cubes/C311cuts.QUARANTINE.json` listing process identities (pid, ppid, pgid, start time). Code never
218:   removes it, and the supervisor, driver, generator and controls refuse to start while it exists.
219: - When the leader becomes a zombie, the supervisor runs a final synchronous check of members, RSS, floor and wall
220:   before reaping it. No SIGKILL is sent and no subprocess timeout is used.
221: - Solver and checker status lines are parsed from bytes: remove only `\r`, split on `\n`, keep the lines starting
222:   with `s `, with no stripping. A certificate needs exactly [`s VERIFIED`] from drat-trim and exactly
223:   [`s UNSATISFIABLE`] from the solver.
224: - Each route uses one deliberately created directory, `evidence/k3cubes/C311cuts_<route>_r5` (made by mkdir; if it
225:   already exists, nothing runs). Every output in it is exclusive-create. The driver writes a receipt on every outcome
226:   it survives; if the supervisor stops the group, the supervisor record is the receipt of that stop.
227: - Fail-closed exception path (unchanged from supervisor r2). Any exception after the command starts records an
228:   uncertain UNKNOWN.
229:   The supervisor then SIGTERMs the still reserved group (the leader is unreaped), waits at most 30 s and quarantines
230:   capacity. If measurement fails during the stop, it falls back to one killpg, a bounded unmeasured wait and
231:   survivors reported as unknown, which quarantines. It always attempts the record, falling back to
232:   RECORD.failure.json, and restores its signal handlers in a finally block.
233: - Acceptance of a route needs both the driver receipt (VERIFIED_UNSAT_CASE) and the supervisor record of that run
234:   (completed_cleanly, no trip, no quarantine).

29: ## 1. Objects
30: Let S be a set of mutually orthogonal QLS(7). For all cells p,q define
31: G_a(p,q) = ⟨ψ_a,p, ψ_a,q⟩, conjugate-linear in the first argument. Unit norms give G_a(p,p)=1, and
32: G_a(q,p)=conj(G_a(p,q)). For distinct cells the Boolean support g_a(p,q)=[G_a(p,q) != 0] is therefore symmetric and
33: does not depend on phases or a global unitary. Diagonal Gram entries are one, not false Boolean predicates; the
34: encoders never represent them as off-diagonal variables. The ONB lines of each quantum square are:
35: - its 7 rows and 7 columns (QLS axioms);
36: - in K2, the 7 symbol lines of the classical square L. By (C1), Ψ_a ⊥ L iff Ψ_a is an ONB on each symbol line of L.

38: ## 2. Necessary conditions (each holds for every quantum square a)
39: - **G1.** If p ≠ q lie on a common ONB line, then g_a(p,q) = 0.
40: - **G2 (frame consistency).** Let ℓ be an ONB line and p, q ∉ ℓ. Then ψ_p = Σ_{r∈ℓ} G(r,p) ψ_r, so
41:   G(p,q) = Σ_{r∈ℓ} conj(G(r,p)) G(r,q). Hence #{r ∈ ℓ : g(p,r) ∧ g(q,r)} = 0 forces g(p,q) = 0, and = 1 forces
42:   g(p,q) = 1. When p and q are collinear, G1 then forbids the count 1.
43: - **G3 (unitary transitions).** For ONB lines ℓ1 ≠ ℓ2, the matrix U[r,q] = G(r,q) (r ∈ ℓ1, q ∈ ℓ2) is the product
44:   of two unitaries. If the lines meet in s, then U[s,s] = 1 and the 6 × 6 block on (ℓ1∖s) × (ℓ2∖s) is unitary.
45:   Hence:
46:   - every r ∈ ℓ1∖ℓ2 has a neighbour on ℓ2∖ℓ1;
47:   - if r has exactly one neighbour q there, then |U[r,q]| = 1, so q has no other neighbour on ℓ1∖ℓ2.
48: - **G5 (orthogonality, exact).**
49:   - For quantum squares a ≠ b: ⟨ψ_a,p ⊗ ψ_b,p, ψ_a,q ⊗ ψ_b,q⟩ = G_a(p,q)·G_b(p,q). So Ψ_a ⊥ Ψ_b iff
50:     g_a(p,q)·g_b(p,q) = 0 for all p ≠ q.

2: 
3: This file is a late, explicit documentation correction following the static reviews. It did not exist in the
4: original review snapshot; the old references to it are not evidence that it had been written before those runs.
5: No frozen encoder or old evidence is changed. Definitions and Lemma 10 are from the pinned Ball–Simoens
6: arXiv:2607.12933v2, layout LF90–148 and LF178–192. The theorem packages retain their own explicit trust boundaries.
7: 
8: ## C0. Classicality, straightening and rays
9: A QLS is classical iff, after entry phases, all entries lie in one ONB. Row/column permutations do not alter its
10: ray set. Each ONB row supplies seven distinct rays; if there are exactly seven in total every entry is parallel to
11: one row-basis vector, and conversely a classical QLS has exactly seven. Independent global unitaries are allowed
12: on the individual squares in a set, so the set is classical iff all its members are classical. These unitaries
13: straighten each classical member in place to a Latin square of standard-basis labels; no independent reindexing of
14: cells is required.
15: 
16: ## C1. Orthogonality to a classical member
17: For a classical L, the product-vector inner product is
18: 
19:     <psi_p tensor |L(p)>, psi_q tensor |L(q)>> = <psi_p,psi_q> [L(p)=L(q)].
20: 
21: Hence a quantum square is orthogonal to L iff its seven entries on each L-symbol line form an ONB. With two
22: classical members, K1 is exactly the non-classical ONB-assignment problem on their 4-net. The ONB-assignment
23: problem is symmetric in the four classes; net isomorphism is sufficient even if not every class permutation is a
24: source set-isotopy. This is an existence transport, not a claim about the narrower isotopy group.
25: 
26: ## W. Fresh three-square support bound
27: Independently straighten the first row of each square to e_0,...,e_6. At an off-first-row cell (i,j), column
28: orthogonality excludes coordinate j from every support. For any two squares, compare their product at (i,j) to
29: its first-row product e_k tensor e_k. Orthogonality gives
30: 
31:     <e_k,psi_a(i,j)> <e_k,psi_b(i,j)> = 0.
32: 
33: Their supports are therefore disjoint, for every pair a,b. Three nonempty, pairwise disjoint supports lie inside
34: six coordinates, so their weights sum to at most six. Each individual weight is at most four, and weight four
35: forces the other two to be singletons. Weight two is allowed. This is derived for three squares, not imported
36: from the old four-square theorem.
37: 
38: ## K1 frame and F1–F4
39: Choose a net line ell as the basis. Each point outside ell lies on three other net lines meeting ell at three
40: distinct points: two coincident meetings would make two different net lines share two points. Orthogonality to
41: those three basis vectors excludes their coordinates, leaving four allowed coordinates.
42: 
43: For each ONB line, supports must satisfy:
44: - F1 nonempty, by unit norm;
45: - F2 no two orthogonal entries share exactly one support coordinate, since then their inner product is one
46:   nonzero product;
47: - F3 every coordinate is covered, since the line vectors form a unitary matrix;
48: - F4 unique coverage of coordinate k forces weight one at its covering vector: column k has one coefficient of
49:   modulus one, exhausting that row's norm.
50: 
51: These are only necessary conditions for general unitary realisation. A Boolean SAT pattern is not a vector
52: witness. The full K1 exclusions and seven-phase relation are in `K1-THEOREM.r3.md`.
53: 
54: ## Lemma 10 in an arbitrary basis, with every partner preserved
55: Suppose every entry of a square has weight at most two in one fixed ONB. Pick a weight-two vector with support
56: {a,b}, and a unitary U on that two-dimensional coordinate plane sending it to a coordinate ray. Apply U to all
57: entries with exactly this support, leaving all other entries untouched. If another weight-at-most-two entry was
58: orthogonal to one of these, it had either the same support or disjoint support (a one-coordinate overlap cannot
59: cancel). Thus every previously zero inner product stays zero. Unit norms and the row/column ONBs remain intact,
60: and no support grows; at least one weight-two entry disappears. Repeating finitely many times yields a classical
61: square with support contained pointwise in the old support.
62: 
63: For tensor orthogonality with any other square, every required zero product still has its former zero factor.
64: Thus the same replacement preserves orthogonality to all partners simultaneously, not just one preselected
65: partner. Before this argument one may apply a global unitary on the chosen square alone to send any ONB B to the
66: standard basis; this preserves all its Gram inner products. Undoing that unitary preserves classicality and the
67: partner orthogonalities. Therefore the printed lemma is basis-free in precisely the sense used here.
68: 
69: ## G4. Heavy minimal counterexamples; separate from K1 section 8
70: Minimise the number m of individually non-classical squares over all non-classical MOQLS(7) triples, if any exist.
71: The minimum exists because m is one of 1,2,3. Theorem K1 excludes m=1. A non-classical square light in any ONB
72: could be replaced by the preceding lemma, producing a triple with m-1 >= 1 non-classical squares, a contradiction.
73: Every non-classical square of a minimal triple is consequently heavy in every ONB.
74: 
75: Given K1, every K2 triple is minimal; after K2 is excluded, the same applies to K3. In each own-line frame the
76: weight of a point is its number of nonzero Gram overlaps with that line. Points on the line have weight one, so a
77: heavy witness lies off it. This justifies G4 in `GRAM-ENCODING.r1.md` independently of the more limited
78: standard-frame reduction in K1 section 8. The chronological prerequisites may not be reversed.
