27: ## Part B: Lemma T (connected tetrad families)
28: Let F be a set of distinct 4-sets, any two meeting in 0 or 2 points; call two members adjacent if they meet in 2
29: points. Suppose the adjacency graph on F is connected, and let S be the union of F. Then at least one of the
30: following holds (a family may satisfy both (d) and (e8)):
31: * (d) S has a partition into pairs such that every member of F is the union of two of the pairs;
32: * (e7) |S| = 7 and F is contained in the 7 tetrads of an e7 structure on S (complements of the lines of a Fano plane);
33: * (e8) |S| = 8 and F is contained in the 14 tetrads of an e8 structure on S (planes of AG(3,2)).
34: (For (d) the case |F| = 1 is included: pair the four points arbitrarily.)
35: 
36: Proof.
37: 1. Over GF(2), the span C of F consists of words that meet every member of F (indeed every word of C) in an even
38:    number of points, because members pairwise meet evenly.
39: 2. For an adjacent pair T, T' in F, T is split into the two pairs T cap T' and T \ T'. Suppose first that for every
40:    T in F all its neighbours induce the same split of T (call the two parts the parts of T). If parts P of T and P' of
41:    T' share exactly one point z, then T != T' (the two parts of one member are disjoint) and z is in T cap T', so T,
42:    T' are adjacent (they meet, hence in 2 points); then the
43:    part of T containing z is T cap T' and the part of T' containing z is T' cap T, so P = P' -- a contradiction.
44:    Hence all parts are pairwise equal or disjoint, every member of F with a neighbour is the union of its two parts,
45:    and the parts partition S (by connectivity every member has a neighbour unless |F| = 1). This is case (d).
46: 3. Otherwise some T in F has neighbours T1, T2 inducing different splits of T. Any part of one split of a 4-set meets
47:    any part of a different split in exactly one point, so after renaming T = {1,2,3,4}, T cap T1 = {1,2},
48:    T cap T2 = {1,3}. T1 and T2 share 1, so they meet in exactly 2 points; 2 is not in T2 and 3 is not in T1 (else T
49:    would meet T2 or T1 in 3 points), so T1 = {1,2,a,b}, T2 = {1,3,a,d} with 1,2,3,4,a,b,d distinct. Put
50:    X = {1,2,3,4,a,b,d}. The span of {T, T1, T2} has the seven nonzero words T, T1, T2, T+T1 = {3,4,a,b},
51:    T+T2 = {2,4,a,d}, T1+T2 = {2,3,b,d}, T+T1+T2 = {1,4,b,d}, all of weight 4: it is a [7,3,4] code on X, i.e. e7, and
52:    its seven tetrads are the complements of the lines of a Fano plane on X.
53: 4. Order F as T, T1, T2, T3, ... so that each later member is adjacent to an earlier one (connectivity). Let G be the
54:    set of members before T'. Inductively span(G) is e7 on X or e8 on X + {y} (one extra point y), and G is inside its
55:    tetrads. By step 1, T' meets every word of span(G) evenly.
56:    * If span(G) = e7 on X: T' cap X lies in the dual of e7 on X, the [7,4,3] Hamming code (weights 0, 3, 4, 7). T'
57:      meets a member of G in 2 points, so |T' cap X| is 3 or 4. If 4, T' is a weight-4 Hamming word, i.e. one of the
58:      seven e7 tetrads. If 3, T' = L + {y} with L a Hamming weight-3 word (a line) and y outside X; then the span
59:      becomes the extended Hamming code e8 on X + {y}, whose 14 tetrads are the seven e7 tetrads and the seven
60:      L + {y}.
61:    * If span(G) = e8 on X + {y}: e8 is self-dual, so T' cap (X + {y}) is an e8 word of weight 0, 4 or 8; it is at
62:      least 2, so T' is one of the 14 e8 tetrads.
63:    Hence F lies in the tetrads of e7 on X (|S| = 7) or of e8 on X + {y} (|S| = 8), and S is all of X or X + {y}.
64:    This is case (e7) or (e8). Every F falls under step 2 or step 3.
