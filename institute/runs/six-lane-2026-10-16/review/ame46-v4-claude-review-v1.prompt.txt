42: **Statement.** Let ψ be an AME(4,6) state with e = 0, with non-classical support N. Fix a coordinate i and two values
43: a ≠ a′. Write points as (x_i; z), with z ∈ [6]³ the other three coordinates. Let z1, z2, z3 satisfy:

45: - z2 and z3 agree in c2 and differ in c1 and c3;

50: *Proof.* Suppose they do.
51: - **Facts.** At e = 0, every line meeting N contains exactly two points of S, both in N (L42-9, L42-2(a)). Every
52:   component of a reshaping that meets N is a 2×2 block (L42-7(a), (c)).
53: - **A closed block for the pair (z1, z2).** Take the reshaping whose row pair is (i, c1).
54:   - The row (a, z1_{c1}) contains (a; z1) and (a; z2), since z1 and z2 agree in c1. So it is a hole line with
55:     exactly these two points.
56:   - Likewise the row (a′, z1_{c1}) contains exactly (a′; z1) and (a′; z2).
57:   - The column of (a; z1), i.e. its values in coordinates c2, c3, contains (a; z1) and (a′; z1), so it holds exactly
58:     these two. Likewise the column of (a; z2) holds exactly (a; z2) and (a′; z2).
59:   - Hence the four points form a closed 2×2 component.
60: - **Its congruence.** By L42-10, φ(a; z1) − φ(a; z2) − φ(a′; z1) + φ(a′; z2) ≡ π (mod 2π), up to a global sign,
61:   which does not matter since −π ≡ π. With δ(z) := φ(a; z) − φ(a′; z), this reads δ(z1) − δ(z2) ≡ π.
62: - **The other pairs.** In the same way, the pair (z2, z3), using the reshaping with row pair (i, c2), gives
63:   δ(z2) − δ(z3) ≡ π. The pair (z1, z3), with row pair (i, c3), gives δ(z1) − δ(z3) ≡ π.
64: - **Contradiction.** (δ1 − δ2) + (δ2 − δ3) − (δ1 − δ3) = 0, but the three congruences give π + π − π ≡ π
65:   (mod 2π). ∎
