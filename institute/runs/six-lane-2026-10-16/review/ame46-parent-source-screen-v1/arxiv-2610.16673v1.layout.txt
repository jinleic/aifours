                                                             Absolutely maximally entangled states of four six-level systems, AME(4, 6), are the quantum solutions of
                                                          Euler’s problem of the thirty-six officers. Until recently all reported solutions were complex, and it was con-
                                                          jectured that no real orthogonal√  2-unitary matrix of order 36 exists. We give real AME(4, 6) states with 60
                                                                                                √
                                                          non-zero coefficients in {±1, 2}/ 72. Of their thirty-six officers, 12 are classical, as some must be when-
                                                          ever fewer than 72 coefficients are non-zero. They lie in the −1 eigenspace of an order-4 local symmetry, and
                                                          on their support the AME conditions become linear. We derive two closed-form families of AME(4, 6) states
                                                          that meet at one of these states: a 10-parameter family that is supported on 72 basis states, and an 11-parameter




arXiv:2610.16673v1 [quant-ph] 12 Oct 2026
                                                          family on 60 basis states. At each of its two ends the first family loses 12 coefficients, and the AME states on the
                                                          remaining 60 basis states form an 11-parameter family: the second family at one end, and an analogous family
                                                          at the other. These two 60-term families contain 4096 real AME states up to diagonal local unitaries, 1024 of
                                                          which are limits of the first family. They fall into exactly 20 classes under local unitaries and permutations of
                                                          the parties, and we certify their separation with interval arithmetic.
