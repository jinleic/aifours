1|# Lemmas used by the moqls7-maximum lane
2|
3|This file is a late, explicit documentation correction following the static reviews. It did not exist in the
4|original review snapshot; the old references to it are not evidence that it had been written before those runs.
5|No frozen encoder or old evidence is changed. Definitions and Lemma 10 are from the pinned Ball–Simoens
6|arXiv:2607.12933v2, layout LF90–148 and LF178–192. The theorem packages retain their own explicit trust boundaries.
7|
8|## C0. Classicality, straightening and rays
9|A QLS is classical iff, after entry phases, all entries lie in one ONB. Row/column permutations do not alter its
10|ray set. Each ONB row supplies seven distinct rays; if there are exactly seven in total every entry is parallel to
11|one row-basis vector, and conversely a classical QLS has exactly seven. Independent global unitaries are allowed
12|on the individual squares in a set, so the set is classical iff all its members are classical. These unitaries
13|straighten each classical member in place to a Latin square of standard-basis labels; no independent reindexing of
14|cells is required.
15|
16|## C1. Orthogonality to a classical member
17|For a classical L, the product-vector inner product is
18|
19|    <psi_p tensor |L(p)>, psi_q tensor |L(q)>> = <psi_p,psi_q> [L(p)=L(q)].
20|
21|Hence a quantum square is orthogonal to L iff its seven entries on each L-symbol line form an ONB. With two
22|classical members, K1 is exactly the non-classical ONB-assignment problem on their 4-net. The ONB-assignment
23|problem is symmetric in the four classes; net isomorphism is sufficient even if not every class permutation is a
24|source set-isotopy. This is an existence transport, not a claim about the narrower isotopy group.
25|
26|## W. Fresh three-square support bound
27|Independently straighten the first row of each square to e_0,...,e_6. At an off-first-row cell (i,j), column
28|orthogonality excludes coordinate j from every support. For any two squares, compare their product at (i,j) to
29|its first-row product e_k tensor e_k. Orthogonality gives
30|
31|    <e_k,psi_a(i,j)> <e_k,psi_b(i,j)> = 0.
32|
33|Their supports are therefore disjoint, for every pair a,b. Three nonempty, pairwise disjoint supports lie inside
34|six coordinates, so their weights sum to at most six. Each individual weight is at most four, and weight four
35|forces the other two to be singletons. Weight two is allowed. This is derived for three squares, not imported
36|from the old four-square theorem.
37|
38|## K1 frame and F1–F4
39|Choose a net line ell as the basis. Each point outside ell lies on three other net lines meeting ell at three
40|distinct points: two coincident meetings would make two different net lines share two points. Orthogonality to
41|those three basis vectors excludes their coordinates, leaving four allowed coordinates.
42|
43|For each ONB line, supports must satisfy:
44|- F1 nonempty, by unit norm;
45|- F2 no two orthogonal entries share exactly one support coordinate, since then their inner product is one
46|  nonzero product;
47|- F3 every coordinate is covered, since the line vectors form a unitary matrix;
48|- F4 unique coverage of coordinate k forces weight one at its covering vector: column k has one coefficient of
49|  modulus one, exhausting that row's norm.
50|
51|These are only necessary conditions for general unitary realisation. A Boolean SAT pattern is not a vector
52|witness. The full K1 exclusions and seven-phase relation are in `K1-THEOREM.r3.md`.
53|
54|## Lemma 10 in an arbitrary basis, with every partner preserved
55|Suppose every entry of a square has weight at most two in one fixed ONB. Pick a weight-two vector with support
56|{a,b}, and a unitary U on that two-dimensional coordinate plane sending it to a coordinate ray. Apply U to all
57|entries with exactly this support, leaving all other entries untouched. If another weight-at-most-two entry was
58|orthogonal to one of these, it had either the same support or disjoint support (a one-coordinate overlap cannot
59|cancel). Thus every previously zero inner product stays zero. Unit norms and the row/column ONBs remain intact,
60|and no support grows; at least one weight-two entry disappears. Repeating finitely many times yields a classical
61|square with support contained pointwise in the old support.
62|
63|For tensor orthogonality with any other square, every required zero product still has its former zero factor.
64|Thus the same replacement preserves orthogonality to all partners simultaneously, not just one preselected
65|partner. Before this argument one may apply a global unitary on the chosen square alone to send any ONB B to the
66|standard basis; this preserves all its Gram inner products. Undoing that unitary preserves classicality and the
67|partner orthogonalities. Therefore the printed lemma is basis-free in precisely the sense used here.
68|
69|## G4. Heavy minimal counterexamples; separate from K1 section 8
70|Minimise the number m of individually non-classical squares over all non-classical MOQLS(7) triples, if any exist.
71|The minimum exists because m is one of 1,2,3. Theorem K1 excludes m=1. A non-classical square light in any ONB
72|could be replaced by the preceding lemma, producing a triple with m-1 >= 1 non-classical squares, a contradiction.
73|Every non-classical square of a minimal triple is consequently heavy in every ONB.
74|
75|Given K1, every K2 triple is minimal; after K2 is excluded, the same applies to K3. In each own-line frame the
76|weight of a point is its number of nonzero Gram overlaps with that line. Points on the line have weight one, so a
77|heavy witness lies off it. This justifies G4 in `GRAM-ENCODING.r1.md` independently of the more limited
78|standard-frame reduction in K1 section 8. The chronological prerequisites may not be reversed.

25|## 2. Finite necessary representation
26|For every pair of distinct cells p,q, let g_a(p,q) be true exactly when <psi_a,p, psi_a,q> is nonzero. These are
27|symmetric predicates. The five conditions and corrected proofs are frozen in `GRAM-ENCODING.r2.md`; diagonal
28|Gram entries are one, the inner product is conjugate-linear in its first argument, and off-diagonal support is
29|symmetric. G4 here is the independent minimality argument, not the standard-frame reduction of K1 section 8.
30|
31|- G1: cells on a common ONB line are orthogonal (their predicates are false).
32|- G2: expand both vectors in any ONB line. Zero common support coordinates force g(p,q)=0; exactly one forces
33|  g(p,q)=1. This condition is imposed in every line frame, not just row 0.
34|- G3: transition matrices between ONB lines are unitary; each point off the other line has a neighbour there, and
35|  a unique neighbour forces the reverse transition to be unique as well.
36|- G4: on every own ONB line, some off-line point has at least three neighbours (the preceding heavy lemma).
37|- G5: tensor orthogonality is exactly edge-disjointness: for p != q the product of the two Gram entries vanishes,
38|  so g_1(p,q) and g_2(p,q) cannot both be true.
39|
40|Any K2 configuration induces a Boolean model of these conditions for its 3-net. Therefore a certified UNSAT of the
41|necessary conditions excludes that net, with no real/complex-field restriction or numerical tolerance.
