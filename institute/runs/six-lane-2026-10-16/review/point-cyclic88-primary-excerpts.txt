Parent functions.read scope: definitions and conventions (pp.1–4), short-block/class orbit discussion (pp.5–6), explicit 64-point example (pp.6–7), Section7 open statement (p.11). Primary full text was accessible. Priority relative to older papers is under a separate audit.

Definition2 (PDFp.3): “A parallel class is a partition of the point set by blocks. A resolution of a 2-(v,k,1) design is a partition of the collection of blocks into parallel classes.”

The paragraph before Definition4 (PDFp.4): “Two resolutions are isomorphic if there is an automorphism of the underlying design that maps each parallel class of the first resolution to a parallel class of the second one.”

Definition5 (PDFp.4): “A resolution is point-cyclic if it has an automorphism permuting the points in one cycle.”

Convention (PDFp.2): “In the present paper, when we talk about cyclically resolvable designs, we mean designs with point-cyclic resolutions.” The paper explicitly excludes the distinct convention that only asks an automorphism group to map the parallel classes transitively.

Section7 (PDFp.11): “v = 88 is the smallest v for which the existence of cyclically resolvable 2-(v,4,1) designs has not yet been determined.”

The same paragraph reports 149,494,720 cyclic 2-(88,4,1) designs and says their possible point-cyclic resolutions are type T1. That classification count is an imported source statement, not used in the proposed proof. The proof must derive T1, not assume it.

Related application, not a verbatim book problem: Section1.1 discusses LDPC parity-check matrices, equidistant codes and group testing. General resolvable 2-(88,4,1) designs and cyclic designs without invariant resolution are not excluded by this target.
