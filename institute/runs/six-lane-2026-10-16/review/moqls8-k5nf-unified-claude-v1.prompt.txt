9: ## 0. Setting
10: Definitions as in Ball–Simoens arXiv:2607.12933v2 §2.2–2.3 (sources/2607.12933v2.raw.txt): a QLS(8) is an 8×8 array
11: of unit vectors of C⁸ whose rows and columns are orthonormal bases (ONBs); QLS Ψ, Φ are orthogonal if the 64 vectors
12: Ψ(c)⊗Φ(c) form an ONB of C⁸⊗C⁸; a set is classical if every square has exactly 8 rays (up to phase), equivalently is
13: equivalent to a set of MOLS. Indices 0..7; row 0 is the reference row; standard form w.r.t. row 0: Ψ_s(0,j) = |j⟩
14: (one unitary per square; preserves orthogonality and classicality). supp Ψ(c) = {k : ⟨k|Ψ(c)⟩ ≠ 0}, weight = |supp|.
15: In standard form a square is classical iff all weights are 1.

17: ## 1. Tools
18: - Prop 1. Ψ ⊥ Φ ⇔ there is no pair of distinct cells c, c′ with ⟨Ψ(c),Ψ(c′)⟩ ≠ 0 and ⟨Φ(c),Φ(c′)⟩ ≠ 0.
19:   (⟨Ψ(c)⊗Φ(c), Ψ(c′)⊗Φ(c′)⟩ = ⟨Ψ(c),Ψ(c′)⟩⟨Φ(c),Φ(c′)⟩.) In particular if Φ is classical, Ψ is an ONB on every
20:   symbol line of Φ.
21: - Lemma 2. If supp u ∩ supp v = {k} then ⟨u,v⟩ = ū_k v_k ≠ 0.
22: - Lemma 3. In an ONB of C⁸ all of whose vectors have weight ≤ 2, a vector with support {a,b} has exactly one partner
23:   with support {a,b}, and no other vector meets {a,b} (component argument; proof in the n = 7 lane,
24:   moqls7-problem16/review/PROOF_PARTIAL.md §1, independent of n).
25: - Lemma 10 (source, raw.txt:342–356). Statement: a QLS Ψ without entries of weight ≥ 3 admits a classical Ψ′ such that
26:   every QLS orthogonal to Ψ is orthogonal to Ψ′. Construction (from the printed proof): repeatedly pick a support
27:   {a,b} and apply one unitary U on span{|a⟩,|b⟩} (fixing the other basis vectors) to every entry whose support is
28:   exactly {a,b}. Same-or-disjoint step: if supp Ψ(c) = {a,b} and Ψ(c′) ⊥ Ψ(c) with weight ≤ 2, Lemma 2 forbids
29:   |supp Ψ(c′) ∩ {a,b}| = 1, so supp Ψ(c′) is {a,b} or disjoint from it. Properties used: (i) supp Ψ′(c) ⊆ supp Ψ(c)
30:   for every c; (ii) weight-1 entries (in particular row 0 of a standard form) are literally unchanged; (iii) the
31:   non-orthogonality graph only shrinks, so orthogonality to every other square of a set is preserved, in any order of
32:   application; (iv) Ψ′ is again a QLS.
33: - W8. In row-0 standard form of 5 MOQLS(8), at a cell (i,j), i ≠ 0, the five supports are pairwise disjoint (rule #3:
34:   ⟨k|Ψ_s(c)⟩⟨k|Ψ_u(c)⟩ = 0 from Ψ_s(c)⊗Ψ_u(c) ⊥ |k⟩⊗|k⟩) and avoid j (rule #2), so Σ_s w_s ≤ 7: every weight is ≤ 3,
35:   and a weight-3 entry forces weight 1 in the other four squares.

37: ## 2. Theorem A8 (all weights ≤ 2 in some row standard form ⇒ classical)
38: Fix s. Apply Lemma 10 to the four squares Ψ_r (r ≠ s) one after another: they become classical Ψ′_r with symbol
39: arrays L′_r (Latin, first row identity), pairwise orthogonal and orthogonal to Ψ_s; hence (Prop 1) Ψ_s is an ONB on
40: every line of the six classes 𝒞 = {rows, columns, L′_r-lines}. 𝒞 is a net: lines of different classes meet in exactly
41: one cell (rows/columns trivially; rows or columns with L′_r-lines since L′_r is Latin; L′_r- with L′_r′-lines since
42: L′_r ⊥ L′_r′). Apply Lemma 10 to Ψ_s: Ψ′_s classical, symbol array L′_s orthogonal to every L′_r, supports shrink.
43: If Ψ_s had an entry with support {a,b}, let E = {c : supp Ψ_s(c) = {a,b}} ⊆ V = {c : L′_s(c) ∈ {a,b}} (16 cells).
44: Every line M ∈ 𝒞 meets V in one a-cell and one b-cell, and (Lemma 3, Ψ_s|M an ONB of weights ≤ 2) meets E in 0 or 2
45: cells, hence in 0 cells or in exactly V ∩ M. So E is a union of components of the bipartite graph H on V joining the
46: a-cell and b-cell of each line of 𝒞. H is 6-regular on 8 + 8 vertices (the six lines through a cell meet only there);
47: two a-cells share ≥ 6 + 6 − 8 = 4 neighbours, so H is connected and E ∈ {∅, V}. The row-0 cell (0,a) ∈ V has weight
48: 1, so E = ∅: contradiction. Thus every square has only weight-1 entries: the set is classical. ∎

50: ## 3. Lemma R (regridding), general form
51: Let 𝒩 be a net of order 8 with k classes (k = 5 or 6) on a 64-point set P, and Ψ_1, …, Ψ_q : P → unit vectors of C⁸
52: (q = 7 − k ∈ {1, 2}) such that each Ψ_s is an ONB on every line of 𝒩, and for s ≠ u the 64 vectors
53: Ψ_s(p)⊗Ψ_u(p) form an ONB. Choose two classes R, C as rows and columns, number their lines, and number the lines of
54: every other class D. With A_s[i][j] = Ψ_s(R_i ∩ C_j) and L_D[i][j] = number of the D-line through R_i ∩ C_j:
55: (i) each A_s is a QLS(8); (ii) each L_D is a Latin square, distinct L_D are orthogonal (two lines of different classes
56: meet exactly once), so the k − 2 classical squares |L_D⟩ are mutually orthogonal; (iii) A_s ⊥ |L_D⟩ (Prop 1:
57: A_s is an ONB on the D-lines); (iv) A_s ⊥ A_u for s ≠ u (the set of 64 tensor products is the same set of vectors,
58: re-indexed); (v) the multiset of vectors of A_s equals that of Ψ_s, so its number of rays (classical or not) is
59: unchanged, and for any line ℓ of 𝒩 the ℓ-frame supports {p ∈ ℓ : ⟨Ψ_s(p),Ψ_s(q)⟩ ≠ 0} do not depend on the gridding.
60: Conversely a set of 5 MOQLS(8) with q non-classical and 5 − q classical squares yields such data with 𝒩 = {rows,
61: columns, classical symbol classes} (k = 7 − q). Re-gridding is a re-indexing of points, not a claimed set isotopy.

19: ## 1. Tools
20: - Prop 1. Ψ ⊥ Φ ⇔ no pair of distinct cells c, c′ has ⟨Ψ(c),Ψ(c′)⟩ ≠ 0 and ⟨Φ(c),Φ(c′)⟩ ≠ 0 (the tensor-product inner
21:   product factorizes; 64 orthonormal vectors in C⁶⁴ form a basis). If Φ is classical, Ψ is an ONB on Φ's symbol lines.
22: - Lemma 2. If supp u ∩ supp v = {k} then ⟨u,v⟩ = ū_k v_k ≠ 0.
23: - Lemma 3 (any n). Let an ONB of Cⁿ consist of vectors of weight ≤ 2. A vector with support {a,b} has exactly one partner
24:   with support {a,b}, and no other basis vector meets {a,b}. Proof: in the bipartite support graph (vectors vs
25:   coordinates) every component spans the space of its coordinates, so it has as many vectors as coordinates. A
26:   component with ≥ 2 vectors contains no weight-1 vector (Lemma 2 against any vector meeting its coordinate) and no
27:   coordinate of degree 1 (a unit column with one nonzero entry would make that vector a basis vector, weight 1); with
28:   all vectors of weight 2 and all coordinates of degree ≥ 2, edge counting gives degree exactly 2: the component is a
29:   single cycle of length 2k. For k ≥ 3 consecutive vectors share exactly one coordinate, contradicting Lemma 2. So k = 2.
30: - Lemma 10 (source, printed proof raw.txt:345–356). While Ψ (no entry of weight ≥ 3) has an entry ψ of weight 2, say
31:   supp ψ = {a,b}, take the unitary U on span{|a⟩,|b⟩} (fixing the other basis vectors) with Uψ = |a⟩ up to phase, and
32:   apply U to every entry whose support is exactly {a,b}; the number of weight-2 entries strictly decreases, so the

342: Lemma 10 ([2, Lemma 13]). If Ψ is a quantum Latin square without entries of weight three or more,
343: then there exists a classical quantum Latin square Ψ′ such that whenever Ψ and Φ are orthogonal, Ψ′ and
344: Φ are orthogonal as well.

346: that, as long as Ψ has entries of weight two, we can replace Ψ by a quantum Latin square with strictly
347: fewer entries of weight two and no entries of weight three or more, while still being orthogonal to Φ. We
348: can repeat this operation until all entries have weight one.
349: Choose an entry ψij of weight two, and assume without loss of generality that it has pattern 110. . . 0. Let U
350: be the unique (up to phase factors) unitary transformation that converts ψij into |1⟩ and fixes |3⟩ , . . . , |n⟩.
351: Let Ψ′ be the quantum Latin square obtained from Ψ by applying U to every entry with pattern 110. . . 0,
