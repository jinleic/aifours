    For a positive integer m, if m(A) ⩾ m (respectively, |[1, k]∗ (x)| = m for
all x ∈ G) then we call (1.1) an m-cover (resp. exact m-cover) of G. If
(1.1) forms an m-cover of G but none of its proper subsystems does, then
it is said to be a minimal (or an irredundant) m-cover of G. A cover of
G refers to a 1-cover of G, and a disjoint cover (or partition) of G means
an exact 1-cover of G. Obviously an exact m-cover is a minimal m-cover
and any minimal m-cover has covering multiplicity m. Disjoint covers of
general groups were investigated by I. Korec [K74], M. M. Parmenter [P84]
and R. Brandl [Br90], while exact m-covers of groups were studied by the
author in [S01] and [S04b].

   Any infinite cyclic group is isomorphic to the additive group Z of inte-
gers. The subgroups of Z different from {0} are in the form nZ = {nx :
x ∈ Z} where n ∈ Z+ = {1, 2, 3, . . . }. For any positive integer n, the index
of nZ in Z is n and a coset of nZ in Z is just a residue class

               a + nZ = {x ∈ Z : x ≡ a (mod n)} (a ∈ Z).

Definition 1.1. Let (1.1) be a finite system of left cosets in a group G. If
[1, k]∗ (G) = {[1, k]∗ (x): x ∈ G} contains I ∗ (G) for no ∅ 6= I ⊂ [1, k] (i.e.,

[1, k]∗ (G) 6⊇ ∅∗ (G) = {∅} (i.e., (1.1) is a cover of G), then we call (1.1) a
regular cover of G.
    When (1.1) is a minimal m-cover of a group G, it forms a regular cover
of G because for any I ⊂ [1, k] there is a g ∈ G such that |I ∗ (g)| < m while
|[1, k]∗ (x)| ⩾ m for all x ∈ G. A regular system may not be a cover, e.g.,
we can easily check that {2Z, 4Z, 2 + 4Z} is a regular system of residue
classes but it is not a cover of Z. As we will see later, if (1.1) forms a
regular cover of a group G then all the indices [G : Gi ] must be finite.

Lemma 2.1. Let H and K be subgroups of a group G. If [G : H] and
[G : K] are finite and relatively prime, then HK coincides with G.
Proof. Use [m, n] to denote the least common multiple of m, n ∈ Z+ . Then

     [[G : H], [G : K]] | [G : H ∩ K] and [G : H ∩ K] ⩽ [G : H][G : K].

As ([G : H], [G : K]) = 1, we have [G : H ∩ K] = [G : H][G : K], i.e.,
[K : H ∩ K] = [G : H]. Since HK contains exactly [K : H ∩ K] right
cosets of H, by the above we must have HK = G. 
