23: ## 0. Definitions and the reduction to the computational basis

25: - [6] = {0, 1, …, 5}. A state is ψ = Σ_{x∈[6]^4} ψ_x |x⟩ with x = (x1, x2, x3, x4).
26: - The **support** is S = {x : ψ_x ≠ 0}, and s = |S|.
27: - **Reshapings.** For the three pairings π = (ab|cd) ∈ {12|34, 13|24, 14|23}, U_π is the 36×36 matrix with rows
28:   indexed by (x_a, x_b), columns by (x_c, x_d), and entries U_π[(x_a,x_b),(x_c,x_d)] = 6ψ_x.

50: ## 1. Lemmas (written proofs)
51: 
52: **Lemma 1 (norms).** For every P-line L, Σ_{x∈L} 36|ψ_x|² = 1.
53: *Proof.* Every row and column of a unitary matrix is a unit vector, and L is a row or column of some U_π. ∎
54: 
55: **Lemma 2 (classical officers).**
56: - (a) If x ∈ T, then no y ∈ S ∖ {x} agrees with x in two or more coordinates.
57: - (b) If a P-line meets S in a single element x, then x ∈ T.
58: *Proof.* (a) If y agrees with x on a pair P, then x and y lie in one P-line. By Lemma 1, 36|ψ_x|² + 36|ψ_y|² ≤ 1,
59: and 36|ψ_x|² = 1, so ψ_y = 0. (b) Lemma 1 for that line gives 36|ψ_x|² = 1. ∎
60: 
61: **Lemma 3 (code).** Two distinct classical officers agree in at most one coordinate. In particular they lie in
62: distinct lines, and t ≤ 36.
63: *Proof.* Lemma 2(a). ∎

65: **Lemma 4 (count).** s ≥ 72 − t, i.e. e ≥ 0. Every line containing no classical officer contains at least two
66: elements of S, all of them in N.
67: *Proof.* Fix U_12|34; each x ∈ S lies in exactly one of its rows.
68: - Each classical officer's row contains nothing else (Lemma 2(a)), and distinct classical officers lie in distinct
69:   rows (Lemma 3). This accounts for t rows.
70: - Any other row is non-empty (Lemma 1), has no single element (Lemma 2(b)), and contains no classical officer. So it
71:   contains at least two elements, all in N.
72: - Hence s ≥ t + 2(36 − t). The same argument applies to any line. ∎
73: 
74: **Lemma 5 (overlap).** If V is unitary and r ≠ r′ are rows, then the column supports of r and r′ do not meet in
75: exactly one column. The same holds for columns.
76: *Proof.* Otherwise the inner product of r and r′ would be a single non-zero product. ∎
77: 
78: **Lemma 6 (components are unitary blocks).** Let K be a component of a unitary V. Then |rows(K)| = |cols(K)|,
79: V restricted to rows(K) × cols(K) is unitary, and every other entry in these rows and columns is 0.
80: *Proof.*
81: - The rows of K are orthonormal and supported in cols(K), so |rows(K)| ≤ |cols(K)|.
82: - Symmetrically, |cols(K)| ≤ |rows(K)|.
83: - So the restriction is square with orthonormal rows, hence unitary. ∎
84: 
85: **Lemma 7 (component sizes).** In each U_π:
86: - the components with one row are exactly the entries of classical officers;
87: - every other component K has k_K ≥ 2, every one of its rows has at least two entries, and e_K ≥ 0.
88: Moreover:
89: - (a) e_K = 0 iff k_K = 2; such a component is a 2×2 block with four non-zero entries;
90: - (b) e_K ≠ 1;
91: - (c) e = Σ_K e_K over the components with k_K ≥ 2.
92: *Proof.*
93: - A one-row component is an entry alone in its row, hence classical (Lemma 2(b)).
94: - A classical officer is alone in its row and column (Lemma 2(a)), so it is a one-row component.
95: - In any other component, a row with a single entry would be a classical officer and form its own component. So rows
96:   have at least two entries, and |K| ≥ 2k_K.
97: - (a) Suppose all rows of K have exactly two entries, and let r have support {c, c′}. A row r′ meeting {c, c′} meets
98:   it in at least two columns (Lemma 5), so supp(r′) = {c, c′}. No row of K reaches a third column, so k_K = 2, and
99:   the two rows have the same two entries. Conversely, k_K = 2 with at least two entries per row gives |K| = 4.
100: - (b) Suppose e_K = 1. Then one row r0 has three entries, with support C0, and all other rows have two.
101:   - A two-entry row meeting C0 lies in C0 (Lemma 5).
102:   - Let r be a two-entry row disjoint from C0. Every row meeting supp(r) has support exactly supp(r): two-entry rows by
103:     the argument of (a), and r0 does not meet supp(r). So such rows are not in K.
104:   - Hence cols(K) = C0, and k_K = 3 by Lemma 6. The other two rows have 2-subsets of C0 as supports; these meet, so
105:     by Lemma 5 they are equal.
106:   - The remaining column of C0 then has a single entry, in r0. By Lemma 2(b) that entry is classical, so by
107:     Lemma 2(a) r0 has no other entry. Contradiction.
108: - (c) S is the disjoint union of the components. The one-row components contribute t entries; the others contribute
109:   Σ (2k_K + e_K) = 2(36 − t) + Σ e_K. ∎
110: 
111: **Lemma 8 (slices).** Let x ∈ N with x_i = a. Then x_j ∈ D_j^{(i,a)} for every j ≠ i.
112: For b ∈ D_j^{(i,a)}, the {i,j}-line through (x_i, x_j) = (a, b) contains no classical officer and at least two
113: elements of N. Also |D_j^{(i,a)}| = d_{i,a}. Consequently:
114: - (a) d_{i,a} ≠ 1;
115: - (b) Σ_a d_{i,a} = h;
116: - (c) every x ∈ N has x_i ∈ V_i for every i.
117: *Proof.*
118: - If x_j = y_j for some y ∈ T with y_i = a, then x and y agree on {i, j}, contradicting Lemma 2(a). So x_j ∈ D.
119: - The classical officers with y_i = a have pairwise distinct j-coordinates (Lemma 3). So |D_j^{(i,a)}| = 6 − t_{i,a},
120:   and the line (a, b), b ∈ D, contains no classical officer. By Lemma 4 it contains at least two elements of N.
121: - (a) If d_{i,a} = 1, every x ∈ N with x_i = a has each x_j (j ≠ i) equal to the single element of D_j^{(i,a)}. So
122:   there is at most one such x, while the line (a, b) for that b contains at least two.
123: - (b) Σ_a t_{i,a} = t.
124: - (c) If d_{i,b} = 0, then D_j^{(i,b)} = ∅, so no x ∈ N has x_i = b. ∎
125: 
126: **Lemma 9 (e = 0).** If e = 0, every line containing no classical officer contains exactly two elements of S.
127: *Proof.* By Lemma 7(a) and (c), every component with k_K ≥ 2 is a 2×2 block with four entries. A line without a
128: classical officer is a row or column of such a block. ∎
129: 
130: **Lemma 10 (block congruence).** Let K be a 2×2 component of U_π, with rows r ≠ r′, columns c ≠ c′, four non-zero
131: entries, and φ_{rc} := arg U_π[r, c]. Then
132:   φ_{rc} − φ_{rc′} − φ_{r′c} + φ_{r′c′} ≡ π (mod 2π).
133: *Proof.* By Lemma 6, rows r and r′ are orthogonal and supported on {c, c′}:
134: U[r,c] conj(U[r′,c]) + U[r,c′] conj(U[r′,c′]) = 0. Both terms are non-zero, so their arguments differ by π:
135: (φ_{rc} − φ_{r′c}) − (φ_{rc′} − φ_{r′c′}) ≡ π. ∎
136: Swapping r with r′, or c with c′, negates the left side, and the congruence is unchanged because −π ≡ π.

138: ## 2. Proof of the Theorem
139: 
140: Suppose ψ is AME(4,6) with s ≤ 41. By Lemma 4, t ≥ 72 − s ≥ 31, and t ≤ 36 by Lemma 3.
141: 
142: - **t = 36.** The 36 classical officers lie in 36 distinct rows of U_12|34 (Lemma 3), so every row contains one. A
143:   row containing a classical officer contains nothing else (Lemma 2(a)), so S = T and N = ∅.
144:   - Every P-line is non-empty (Lemma 1) and contains at most one point of T (Lemma 3). So T is a set of 36 points of
145:     [6]^4 meeting every P-line exactly once.
146:   - Setting L1(x1, x2) = x3 and L2(x1, x2) = x4 for (x1, x2, x3, x4) ∈ T defines two Latin squares of order six:
147:     each line through (x1, x3), (x2, x3), (x1, x4) or (x2, x4) is met once. They are orthogonal, because each line
148:     through (x3, x4) is met once.
149:   - No such pair exists (Tarry's theorem; cited through Pandya LF42–46, refs [7–9]). **[cited]**
150: - **t = 35.** h = 1. By Lemma 8(b), Σ_a d_{1,a} = 1, so some d_{1,a} = 1, contradicting Lemma 8(a).
151: - **t ∈ {33, 34}.** h ∈ {2, 3}.
152:   - For each i the positive deficits d_{i,a} are at least 2 (Lemma 8(a)) and sum to h ≤ 3 (Lemma 8(b)), so V_i is a
153:     single value.
154:   - By Lemma 8(c), all elements of N agree in every coordinate, so |N| ≤ 1.
155:   - But h > 0 lines lack a classical officer, and each contains at least two elements of N (Lemma 4).
156:     Contradiction.
157: - **t = 31.** Then e = s − 41 ≤ 0, so e = 0. By Lemma 7(a) and (c), the 36 − 31 = 5 non-classical rows of U_12|34
158:   are partitioned into 2×2 components, and 5 is odd. Contradiction.

160:   relabelling, one of ten explicit 8-point sets in a 2^4 subcube. §4 shows that for each of them the congruences of

168:   - The positive deficits d_{i,a} are at least 2 (Lemma 8(a)) and sum to 4, so each coordinate has the profile {4} or
169:     {2, 2}.

173:   - So V_i = {a_i, b_i} for every i, and N ⊆ V_1 × V_2 × V_3 × V_4 (Lemma 8(c)).

175:   - Fix a pair {i, j}. The lines of that pair which lack a classical officer number 36 − t = 4 (Lemma 3: the t
176:     classical officers lie in t distinct lines).

178:     officer meets no element of N by Lemma 2(a).

180:     four lines contains exactly two elements of N.
181: - **Conclusion.** Identify V_i with {0, 1} by a_i ↦ 0 and b_i ↦ 1. Then N becomes an 8-point subset of {0,1}^4 whose
182:   six 2-dimensional marginals are uniform, i.e. an orthogonal array OA(8, 4, 2, 2).

184: **Classification (written proof).** An 8-point set N ⊆ {0,1}^4 with all six 2-marginals uniform equals
185: {x : Σ_{i∈U} x_i ≡ c (mod 2)} for some U with |U| ∈ {3, 4} and some c ∈ {0, 1}. There are ten such sets.
186: *Proof.*
187: - Let g = 2·1_N − 1, a ±1-valued function, and ĝ(u) = 2^{−4} Σ_x g(x)(−1)^{u·x}.
188: - |N| = 8 gives ĝ(0) = 0. Uniform 2-marginals imply uniform 1-marginals (four points per value). For |u| ∈ {1, 2}
189:   they give Σ_x 1_N(x)(−1)^{u·x} = 0, and Σ_x (−1)^{u·x} = 0, so ĝ(u) = 0.
190: - Hence g = Σ_{u∈F} ĝ(u)χ_u, with χ_u(x) = (−1)^{u·x} and F = {1110, 1101, 1011, 0111, 1111}.
191: - Since g² ≡ 1, the coefficient of χ_w in g² vanishes for every w ≠ 0. For distinct u, v ∈ F the sums u ⊕ v are
192:   pairwise distinct and non-zero: two weight-3 vectors give the six weight-2 vectors, and a weight-3 vector with 1111
193:   gives the four weight-1 vectors.
194: - So the coefficient of χ_{u⊕v} in g² is 2ĝ(u)ĝ(v) = 0 for all distinct u, v ∈ F. At most one ĝ(u) is non-zero, and
195:   Parseval (Σ ĝ(u)² = 1) gives g = ±χ_u. That is the stated form. ∎
