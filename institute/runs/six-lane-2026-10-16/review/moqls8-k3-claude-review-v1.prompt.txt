37: ## 2. Theorem A8 (all weights ≤ 2 in some row standard form ⇒ classical)
38: Fix s. Apply Lemma 10 to the four squares Ψ_r (r ≠ s) one after another: they become classical Ψ′_r with symbol
39: arrays L′_r (Latin, first row identity), pairwise orthogonal and orthogonal to Ψ_s; hence (Prop 1) Ψ_s is an ONB on

41: one cell (rows/columns trivially; rows or columns with L′_r-lines since L′_r is Latin; L′_r- with L′_r′-lines since
42: L′_r ⊥ L′_r′). Apply Lemma 10 to Ψ_s: Ψ′_s classical, symbol array L′_s orthogonal to every L′_r, supports shrink.
43: If Ψ_s had an entry with support {a,b}, let E = {c : supp Ψ_s(c) = {a,b}} ⊆ V = {c : L′_s(c) ∈ {a,b}} (16 cells).
44: Every line M ∈ 𝒞 meets V in one a-cell and one b-cell, and (Lemma 3, Ψ_s|M an ONB of weights ≤ 2) meets E in 0 or 2
45: cells, hence in 0 cells or in exactly V ∩ M. So E is a union of components of the bipartite graph H on V joining the
46: a-cell and b-cell of each line of 𝒞. H is 6-regular on 8 + 8 vertices (the six lines through a cell meet only there);
47: two a-cells share ≥ 6 + 6 − 8 = 4 neighbours, so H is connected and E ∈ {∅, V}. The row-0 cell (0,a) ∈ V has weight
48: 1, so E = ∅: contradiction. Thus every square has only weight-1 entries: the set is classical. ∎

50: ## 3. Lemma R (regridding), general form
51: Let 𝒩 be a net of order 8 with k classes (k = 5 or 6) on a 64-point set P, and Ψ_1, …, Ψ_q : P → unit vectors of C⁸
52: (q = 7 − k ∈ {1, 2}) such that each Ψ_s is an ONB on every line of 𝒩, and for s ≠ u the 64 vectors
53: Ψ_s(p)⊗Ψ_u(p) form an ONB. Choose two classes R, C as rows and columns, number their lines, and number the lines of
54: every other class D. With A_s[i][j] = Ψ_s(R_i ∩ C_j) and L_D[i][j] = number of the D-line through R_i ∩ C_j:
55: (i) each A_s is a QLS(8); (ii) each L_D is a Latin square, distinct L_D are orthogonal (two lines of different classes
56: meet exactly once), so the k − 2 classical squares |L_D⟩ are mutually orthogonal; (iii) A_s ⊥ |L_D⟩ (Prop 1:
57: A_s is an ONB on the D-lines); (iv) A_s ⊥ A_u for s ≠ u (the set of 64 tensor products is the same set of vectors,
58: re-indexed); (v) the multiset of vectors of A_s equals that of Ψ_s, so its number of rays (classical or not) is
59: unchanged, and for any line ℓ of 𝒩 the ℓ-frame supports {p ∈ ℓ : ⟨Ψ_s(p),Ψ_s(q)⟩ ≠ 0} do not depend on the gridding.
60: Conversely a set of 5 MOQLS(8) with q non-classical and 5 − q classical squares yields such data with 𝒩 = {rows,
61: columns, classical symbol classes} (k = 7 − q). Re-gridding is a re-indexing of points, not a claimed set isotopy.

286: “quantum sudoku rules” that were introduced in [2]. We refer to that paper for a more detailed explanation.
287: #1 (Standard form.) We can apply unitary transformations and assume that, up to isotopy, the
288: squares are in standard form where the first row in the pattern of each quantum Latin square is
289: 10. . . 0

294:

297: #2 (Unitary patterns.) Each row and each column of a quantum Latin square form a unitary
298: pattern, by definition of quantum Latin squares. In particular, if there is an entry of weight one
299: with support {k}, then all other entries in the same row and column have a zero in position k.
300: #3 (Zero overlap.) From the second row onward, at a fixed cell (i, j) and coordinate k, two orthogonal squares cannot both have a pattern in that cell with a 1 in the kth coordinate. Indeed,
301: since the quantum Latin squares are mutually orthogonal, ψij ⊗ ϕij and ψ1k ⊗ ϕ1k = |k⟩ ⊗ |k⟩
302: are orthogonal, so ⟨k|ψij ⟩ ⟨k|ϕij ⟩ = 0.

340: In particular, a set of n − 2 MOLS(n) can be extended to a set of n − 1 MOLS(n). This also holds for

342: Lemma 10 ([2, Lemma 13]). If Ψ is a quantum Latin square without entries of weight three or more,
343: then there exists a classical quantum Latin square Ψ′ such that whenever Ψ and Φ are orthogonal, Ψ′ and
344: Φ are orthogonal as well.
345: Proof. Let n be the order of Ψ. We may assume that n ≥ 4, otherwise the statement is trivial. We show
346: that, as long as Ψ has entries of weight two, we can replace Ψ by a quantum Latin square with strictly
347: fewer entries of weight two and no entries of weight three or more, while still being orthogonal to Φ. We
348: can repeat this operation until all entries have weight one.
349: Choose an entry ψij of weight two, and assume without loss of generality that it has pattern 110. . . 0. Let U
350: be the unique (up to phase factors) unitary transformation that converts ψij into |1⟩ and fixes |3⟩ , . . . , |n⟩.
351: Let Ψ′ be the quantum Latin square obtained from Ψ by applying U to every entry with pattern 110. . . 0,
352: while keeping the other entries the same. First, the operation never increases the weight of any entry, and
353: decreases the weight of ψij . Second, the operation preserves the orthogonality with other quantum Latin
354: squares because whenever two entries in Ψ are orthogonal, the corresponding entries in Ψ′ are orthogonal
355: as well. The only case where this could go wrong is when one of the two orthogonal entries has pattern
356: 110. . . 0, but then the other entry has either the same support {1, 2} or a support disjoint from {1, 2}.
