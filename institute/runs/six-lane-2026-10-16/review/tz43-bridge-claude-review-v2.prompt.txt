205:      A combinatorial structure named candelabra quadruple system CQS plays a central role
206: in the recursive constructions of SQSs. In order to produce RDSQSs, candelabra quadruple
207: systems with resolvable derived designs RDCQSs were introduced and intensively studied in
208: the last decade. In this section, we introduce several coloring types of the derived designs of a
209: CQS as a generalization of an RDCQS. Then we display a number of recursive constructions
210: to produce mcDSQSs. For later use, we introduce or recall some definitions.
211:      An incomplete Steiner triple system on a v-set X with an h-subset H as a hole, denoted by
212: STS(v, h), is a triple (X, H, B), where B is a block set of a GDD(2, 3, v) of type 1v−h h1 with H
213: as the long group. Further let v ≡ 1, 3 (mod 6). If B can be partitioned into r := χ′ (v) PPCs
214: of X, in which there are s := χ′ (v) − v−h2 PPCs of X \ H, then we say that (X, H, B) is good
215: colorable, denoted by gcSTS(v, h). It then follows from (1.1) that

227:     An incomplete Steiner quadruple system on a v-set X with an h-subset H as a hole, denoted
228: by SQS(v, h), is a triple (X, H, B), where B is a set of quadruples of X (blocks), such that every
229: 
230:                                                      4
231: ⟨U+000C⟩triple T ⊆ X with T 6⊆ H is contained in a unique block and no triple of H is contained in any
232: block. We can also define the derived designs of an SQS(v, h). For x ∈ X, put Bx = {B \ {x} :
233: x ∈ B, B ∈ B}. It is immediate that each (X \ {x}, Bx ) with x ∈ X \ H forms an STS(v − 1)
234: and each (X \ {x}, H \ {x}, Bx ) with x ∈ H forms an STS(v − 1, h − 1). An SQS(v, h) with good
235: colorable derived designs, denoted by gcDSQS(v, h), if all derived STS(v − 1)s are minimum
236: colorable and all derived STS(v − 1, h − 1)s are good colorable.
237:     A Kirkman frame F(2, 3, v{m}) (as in [18]), briefly by KF(mv ), is a GDD(2, 3, mv) of type
238: mv (X, G, B) such that the block set B can be partitioned into subsets Pr , r ∈ R, each Pr being
239: a PC of X \ G for some group G ∈ G. A KF(mv ) exists if and only if m ≡ 0 (mod 2), v ≥ 4
240: and m(v − 1) ≡ 0 (mod 3) (see [17]).
241:      Let (X, G, A) be a GDD(3, 4, mv) of type mv . For any x ∈ X, let Ax = {A\{x} : x ∈
242: A, A ∈ A}. Then the derived design (X\G, G\{G}, Ax ) is a GDD(2, 3, m(v − 1)) of type mv−1 .
243: Here we examine two types of coloring of the derived designs. We use RDGDD(3, 4, v{m})
244: to denote a GDD(3, 4, mv) of type mv with resolvable derived designs, meaning that its de-
245: rived design at every point is a resolvable GDD(2, 3, m(v − 1)). An RDTD(3, 4, m) denotes an
246: RDGDD(3, 4, 4{m}). Parallelly, we use FDGDD(3, 4, v{m}) to denote a GDD(3, 4, mv) of type
247: mv whose derived design at every point is a KF(mv−1 ).
248:      Let X be a v-element set. Suppose that G, Bi (1 ≤ i ≤ s) and T are collections of
249: some subsets of X and G is a partition of X. Let Ki (1 ≤ i ≤ s) and KT be the block size
250: sets of Bi and T respectively. An s-fan design, denoted by s-FG(3, (K1 , . . . , Ks , KT ), v), is an
251: (s + 3)-tuple (X, G,    S BS1 , . . . , BsS, T ) such that each (X, G ∪ Bi ) with 1 ≤ i ≤ s is a Steiner 2-
252: system and (X, G ( si=1 Bi ) T ) is a Steiner 3-system. The type of an s-FG              S is the Smultiset
253: {|G| : G ∈ G}. Note that an s-FG(3, (K1 , . . . , Ks , KT ), v) of type 1v is an S(3, ( si=1 Ki ) KT , v)
254: having s subdesigns S(2, Ki , v). For such a case we simply say that (X, B1 , . . . , Bs , T ) is an s-fan
255: S(3, (K1 , . . . , Ks , KT ), v).
256:      For a v-set X, take an s-subset S ⊆ X and call it a stem. Let G be a collection of subsets,
257: called groups, of X \ S which partition X \ S. A candelabra t-system (as in [13]) of order v with
258: block sizes from a set K, briefly by CS(t, K, v), is a quadruple (X, S, G, A) satisfying that
259: 
260:  (1) A is a family of subsets of X, called blocks, each of cardinality from K;
261:  (2) each t-subset of X except those contained in S ∪ G for some G ∈ G is contained in exactly
262:      one block (while no t-subset in S ∪ G is contained in any block).
263: 
264: By the type of a CS(t, K, v) we mean the list ({|G| : G ∈ G} : s). Exponential notation is also
265: applied to denote the type of G. A candelabra system with t = 3 and k = 4 is a candelabra
266: quadruple system and denoted by CQS.
267:      It is a standard construction to build an SQS(gn + s) from a CQS(g n : s) by filling in holes.
268: To produce mcDSQSs, we need to define desirable colorings of the derived designs of CQSs.
269: The rest of this section is divided into two subsections. In the first subsection, we generalize
270: known definitions and constructions related with RDSQSs to develop recursive constructions
271: applicable for generic mcDSQSs. In the second subsection, we turn our attention to mcDSQSs
272: only of orders 6n+2. We introduce two new types of colorings of derived designs of a CQS(gn : 2)
273: and display effective recursive constructions.

1092: 4    Summary and open problems
1093: 
1094:       In this paper, we considered the constructions and existence of mcDSQS(v)s. There has
1095: been an amount of work on RDSQS(6n+4)s, while the study on mcDSQS(6n+2)s has just begun
1096: in this paper. We generalized a number of existing recursive constructions about RDSQSs to
1097: produce general mcDSQSs. In particular, a construction for gcDCQSs is developed, which is also
1098: new even for RDCQSs, see Construction 2.4. Moreover, we demonstrated several constructions
1099: aiming only at mcDSQS(6n + 2)s (in Subsection 2.2). As the main results, we obtained a new
1100: infinite family of RDSQS(6n+4)s and displayed a new infinite family of LKTSs. (More families of
1101: RDSQSs and thus more LKTSs could be produced by substituting known orders to the product
1102: construction [3, 24], but we did not study this in depth to avoid distraction.) We obtained the
1103: first infinite family of mcDSQS(6n + 2)s and presented their applications in diameter perfect
1104: constant-weight codes. Because the chromatic index of an STS(v) equals (v + 1)/2 only when
1105: v ≡ 1 (mod 6) and v ≥ 19, direct constructions for auxiliary designs to build mcDSQS(6n + 2)s
1106: usually require significant computation. For future work, we pose the following problems.
1107: 
1108:  (1) Find more examples of mcDSQS(v)s, for example, an mcDSQS(38) and an RDSQS(52)
1109:      (the smallest unknown mcDSQS(6n + 2)s and the smallest unknown RDSQS(6n + 4)s,
1110:      respectively).
1111:  (2) Find a gcDCQS(v 4 : 0) for v = 20, 32. Apply Corollary 2.6. If a gcDCQS(204 : 0) exists,
1112:      then there exists an mcDSQS(5 · 4n ) for any integer n ≥ 3. In the same way, the existence
1113:      of a gcDCQS(324 : 0) will yield an mcDSQS(22n+1 ) for any integer n ≥ 4.
1114:  (3) Construct a gcDCQS(184 : 2) and an FDGDD(3, 4, 5{18}) or RDGDD(3, 4, 5{18}). By
1115:      Construction 2.2 or 2.3 and Lemma 3.8, the existence of an mcDSQS(18 · 4n + 2) would
1116:      be obtained by filling with an mcDSQS(20).
1117:  (4) Develop more effective recursive constructions for mcDSQS(v)s.
