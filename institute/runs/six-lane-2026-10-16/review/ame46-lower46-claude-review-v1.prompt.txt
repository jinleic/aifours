36: ## 0. Definitions and the reduction to the computational basis
37: 
38: - [6] = {0, 1, …, 5}. A state is ψ = Σ_{x∈[6]^4} ψ_x |x⟩ with x = (x1, x2, x3, x4).
39: - The **support** is S = {x : ψ_x ≠ 0}, and s = |S|.
40: - **Reshapings.** For the three pairings π = (ab|cd) ∈ {12|34, 13|24, 14|23}, U_π is the 36×36 matrix with rows
41:   indexed by (x_a, x_b), columns by (x_c, x_d), and entries U_π[(x_a,x_b),(x_c,x_d)] = 6ψ_x.
42: - **AME ⇔ unitary reshapings.** For a pure state, ρ_ab = Tr_cd |ψ⟩⟨ψ| has entries
43:   (ρ_ab)_{(x_a x_b),(y_a y_b)} = Σ_{x_c,x_d} ψ_{x_a x_b x_c x_d} conj(ψ_{y_a y_b x_c x_d}), so ρ_ab = U_π U_π†/36.
44:   Similarly ρ_cd = (U_π† U_π)^T/36.
45:   - Hence all six two-party marginals equal I/36 iff U_π U_π† = U_π† U_π = I for the three pairings, i.e. iff all
46:     three U_π are unitary (also Pandya, proof of Lemma 1, LF248–253).

48:   Then ⟨e_x|ψ⟩ is the x-coefficient of (V_1 ⊗ … ⊗ V_4)|ψ⟩,

50:   every AME state in the computational basis.

100: - the components with one row are exactly the entries of classical officers;
101: - every other component K has k_K ≥ 2, every one of its rows has at least two entries, and e_K ≥ 0.
102: Moreover:
103: - (a) e_K = 0 iff k_K = 2; such a component is a 2×2 block with four non-zero entries;
104: - (b) e_K ≠ 1;
105: - (c) e = Σ_K e_K over the components with k_K ≥ 2.
106: *Proof.*
107: - A one-row component is an entry alone in its row (by Lemma 6 it also has a single column), hence classical
108:   (Lemma 2(b)).
109: - A classical officer is alone in its row and column (Lemma 2(a)), so it is a one-row component.
110: - In any other component, a row with a single entry would be a classical officer and form its own component. So rows
111:   have at least two entries, and |K| ≥ 2k_K.
112: - (a) Suppose all rows of K have exactly two entries, and let r have support {c, c′}. A row r′ meeting {c, c′} meets
113:   it in at least two columns (Lemma 5), so supp(r′) = {c, c′}. No row of K reaches a third column. So |cols(K)| = 2,
114:   hence k_K = 2 by Lemma 6, and the two rows have the same two columns. Conversely, k_K = 2 with at least two
115:   entries per row gives |K| = 4.
116: - (b) Suppose e_K = 1. Then one row r0 has three entries, with support C0, and all other rows have two.
117:   - A two-entry row meeting C0 lies in C0 (Lemma 5).
118:   - Let r be a two-entry row disjoint from C0. Every row meeting supp(r) has support exactly supp(r): two-entry rows by
119:     the argument of (a), and r0 does not meet supp(r). So such rows are not in K.
120:   - Hence cols(K) = C0, and k_K = 3 by Lemma 6. The other two rows have 2-subsets of C0 as supports; these meet, so
121:     by Lemma 5 they are equal.
122:   - The remaining column of C0 then has a single entry, in r0. By Lemma 2(b) that entry is classical, so by
123:     Lemma 2(a) r0 has no other entry. Contradiction.

125:   Σ (2k_K + e_K) = 2(36 − t) + Σ e_K. ∎

127: **Lemma 8 (slices).** Let x ∈ N with x_i = a. Then x_j ∈ D_j^{(i,a)} for every j ≠ i.

129: elements of N. Also |D_j^{(i,a)}| = d_{i,a}. Consequently:
130: - (a) d_{i,a} ≠ 1;
131: - (b) Σ_a d_{i,a} = h;
132: - (c) every x ∈ N has x_i ∈ V_i for every i.
133: *Proof.*

135: - The classical officers with y_i = a have pairwise distinct j-coordinates (Lemma 3). So |D_j^{(i,a)}| = 6 − t_{i,a},
136:   and the line (a, b), b ∈ D, contains no classical officer. By Lemma 4 it contains at least two elements of N.
137: - (a) If d_{i,a} = 1, every x ∈ N with x_i = a has each x_j (j ≠ i) equal to the single element of D_j^{(i,a)}. So
138:   there is at most one such x, while the line (a, b) for that b contains at least two.
139: - (b) Σ_a t_{i,a} = t.
140: - (c) If d_{i,b} = 0, then D_j^{(i,b)} = ∅, so no x ∈ N has x_i = b. ∎

146: **Lemma 10 (block congruence).** Let K be a 2×2 component of U_π, with rows r ≠ r′, columns c ≠ c′, four non-zero
147: entries, and φ_{rc} := arg U_π[r, c]. Then
148:   φ_{rc} − φ_{rc′} − φ_{r′c} + φ_{r′c′} ≡ π (mod 2π).
149: *Proof.* By Lemma 6, rows r and r′ are orthogonal and supported on {c, c′}:
150: U[r,c] conj(U[r′,c]) + U[r,c′] conj(U[r′,c′]) = 0. Both terms are non-zero, so their arguments differ by π:
151: (φ_{rc} − φ_{r′c}) − (φ_{rc′} − φ_{r′c′}) ≡ π. ∎
152: Swapping r with r′, or c with c′, negates the left side, and the congruence is unchanged because −π ≡ π.
153: Since U_π[r, c] = 6ψ_x for the point x in row r and column c, φ_rc = arg ψ_x =: φ_x does not depend on π. So the
154: congruences of different reshapings are linear forms in the same unknowns φ_x, x ∈ N.

156: **Lemma 11 (symmetries).** Let g act on points by (g·x)_{σ(i)} = β_i(x_i), where σ is a permutation of the four
157: parties and β_1, …, β_4 are bijections of [6]. Let N be a set of points.
158: - (a) g maps each P-line to a σ(P)-line. The pairing {P, P^c} goes to the pairing {σ(P), σ(P^c)}, possibly with
159:   rows and columns interchanged.
160: - (b) g maps the connected components of the support graph of N in reshaping π onto those of g(N) in the image
161:   reshaping.
162: - (c) Let a 2×2 component have rows r, r′ and columns c, c′. Transporting its Lemma 10 form by x ↦ g·x gives plus or
163:   minus the Lemma 10 form of the image component.
164: Consequently, three components (one per reshaping) whose signed forms sum to the zero form are mapped to three
165: components of g(N) with the same property, and the signed right sides still sum to an odd multiple of π.
166: *Proof.*
167: - (a) The P-line through x is mapped to the σ(P)-line through g·x. The two pairs of a pairing are mapped to the two
168:   pairs of one pairing.
169: - (b) Two points share a row or column iff their images do, by (a).
170: - (c) The four points rc, rc′, r′c, r′c′ go to the four points of the image component, and the checkerboard pattern
171:   (same row, same column, diagonal) is preserved. The Lemma 10 form gives sign +1 to the diagonal pair {rc, r′c′}
172:   and −1 to the other pair. Interchanging rows and columns keeps the two diagonals. Swapping the order of the rows or
173:   of the columns interchanges them, which negates the form. ∎

175: ## 2. Proof of the Theorem
176: 
177: Suppose ψ is AME(4,6) with s ≤ 41. By Lemma 4, t ≥ 72 − s ≥ 31, and t ≤ 36 by Lemma 3.
178: 
179: - **t = 36.** The 36 classical officers lie in 36 distinct rows of U_12|34 (Lemma 3), so every row contains one. A
180:   row containing a classical officer contains nothing else (Lemma 2(a)), so S = T and N = ∅.
181:   - Every P-line is non-empty (Lemma 1) and contains at most one point of T (Lemma 3). So T is a set of 36 points of
182:     [6]^4 meeting every P-line exactly once.
183:   - Setting L1(x1, x2) = x3 and L2(x1, x2) = x4 for (x1, x2, x3, x4) ∈ T defines two Latin squares of order six:
184:     each line through (x1, x3), (x2, x3), (x1, x4) or (x2, x4) is met once. They are orthogonal, because each line
185:     through (x3, x4) is met once.
186:   - No such pair exists (Tarry's theorem; cited through Pandya LF42–46, refs [7–9]). **[cited]**
187: - **t = 35.** h = 1. By Lemma 8(b), Σ_a d_{1,a} = 1, so some d_{1,a} = 1, contradicting Lemma 8(a).
188: - **t ∈ {33, 34}.** h ∈ {2, 3}.

191:   - By Lemma 8(c), all elements of N agree in every coordinate, so |N| ≤ 1.

193:     Contradiction.

45: **Lemma 14 (closure).** Let K be a connected component of a unitary matrix with k ≥ 2 rows. For 0 < k′ < k, no k′
46: rows of K are supported within k′ columns.
47: *Proof.*
48: - Such rows would be k′ orthonormal vectors in the k′-dimensional coordinate space of those columns, so they would
49:   span it.
50: - Every other row is orthogonal to them, so it vanishes on those columns.
51: - Then those k′ rows and k′ columns form a union of components, and K would be disconnected. ∎

53: **Lemma 15 (no AME(4,2); standard theorem, elementary proof).** No four-qubit pure state has all six two-qubit
54: marginals equal to I/4.
55: *Proof.*
56: - Let ρ = |ω⟩⟨ω| be such a state. Expand ρ = (1/16) Σ_P r_P P over the 256 Pauli strings P ∈ {I, X, Y, Z}^{⊗4},
57:   with r_P = tr(ρP) ∈ R and r_{I⊗4} = 1. Let wt(P) be the number of non-identity factors, and
58:   A_w := Σ_{wt(P)=w} r_P².
59: - **A1 = A2 = 0.** A Pauli string of weight 1 or 2 acts on at most two qubits. So r_P = tr(ρ_ab P_ab) = tr(P_ab)/4 = 0.
60: - **A3 = 12.** Each one-qubit marginal is I/2, the partial trace of a two-qubit marginal. For the pure state ω, the
61:   marginal on three qubits {a, b, c} has the same non-zero spectrum as the marginal on the fourth qubit, namely
62:   {1/2, 1/2}. So tr ρ_abc² = 1/2.
63:   - Since ρ_abc = (1/8) Σ_Q r_{Q⊗I} Q over three-qubit Pauli strings Q, this gives (1/8) Σ_Q r_{Q⊗I}² = 1/2, i.e.
64:     Σ_Q r_{Q⊗I}² = 4.
65:   - The identity contributes 1, and weights 1 and 2 contribute 0. So the weight-3 strings supported on {a, b, c}
66:     contribute 3.
67:   - Each weight-3 string is supported on exactly one triple, so summing over the four triples gives A3 = 12.
68: - **A4 = 3.** tr ρ² = 1 gives Σ_P r_P² = 16, so A4 = 16 − 1 − 0 − 0 − 12 = 3.
69: - **Spin flip.** Let ρ̃ := Y^{⊗4} ρ̄ Y^{⊗4}, where ρ̄ is the entrywise complex conjugate in the computational basis.
70:   ρ̄ and ρ̃ are positive semidefinite.
71:   - For single-qubit Paulis, Ī = I, X̄ = X, Ȳ = −Y, Z̄ = Z, and Y I Y = I, Y X Y = −X, Y(−Y)Y = −Y, Y Z Y = −Z. So
72:     Y^{⊗4} P̄ Y^{⊗4} = (−1)^{wt(P)} P.
73:   - Since r_P is real, ρ̃ = (1/16) Σ_P (−1)^{wt(P)} r_P P.
74: - **Contradiction.** Using tr(PQ) = 16 δ_{PQ},
75:   tr(ρ ρ̃) = (1/16) Σ_P (−1)^{wt(P)} r_P² = (A0 − A1 + A2 − A3 + A4)/16 = (1 − 0 + 0 − 12 + 3)/16 = −1/2.
76:   This is negative, but the trace of a product of two positive semidefinite matrices is non-negative. ∎
