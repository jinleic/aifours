# A supplementary necessary-condition lemma

This is an elementary corollary of the imported divisor-multiplier premise.
No novelty is claimed. It was considered during the main scan and is NOT
used to sieve any production candidate or to extend the reported endpoint.
Classical multiplier literature, including M. Hall, *Cyclic projective planes*
(1947), and successors, is likely prior art for these exclusions. That literature
is not staged here for a fresh priority check; the proof below is self-contained
under the explicitly imported divisor-multiplier premise.

Suppose p=n²+n+1 is prime, D=mu_(n+1) is planar, and every prime divisor
of n belongs to D (the valid Feit condition). Let H be the multiplicative
subgroup of F_p^* generated by those prime divisors. Then H is contained
in D. Moreover, 2 belongs to D by the elementary inversion argument in the
premise ledger. The unique ordered pair in D with difference 1 is (2,1).

Consequently, no two distinct positive integers u,v<p whose prime factors
all divide n can satisfy u−v=1, except (u,v)=(2,1). More generally the same
holds for any elements known to lie in the subgroup generated by H and 2.
Indeed, both residues would lie in D and give a second difference-1 pair.

For each a≥1 with 2^a+1<p, the integer 2^a+1 cannot have all its prime
factors dividing n: otherwise both 2^a+1 and 2^a lie in D, contradicting
uniqueness. For a≥2 with 2^a<p the same argument excludes all prime factors
of 2^a−1 simultaneously dividing n. Multiplicities do not matter, since D
is a subgroup. In particular n cannot be divisible by a prime of the form
2^a+1 or 2^a−1 (in the indicated size range), apart from the vacuous value 1.

For n>8, p≥91, so the pairs (4,3), (5,4), (8,7), (17,16), and (32,31)
all lie in the ordinary integer interval [1,p). They exclude the divisors
3,5,7,17,31 of n. Thus gcd(n,3·5·7·17·31)=1 is a necessary condition
under the stated premises. The exclusion of 3 already follows from n=2
(mod 3). This lemma still allows every power of two, as well as orders
with other prime factors, and provides no all-orders nonexistence proof.

The small examples explain why the basic base-two condition alone is
insufficient: n=512 passes that condition and the congruence n=8 (mod 24),
yet its D has the explicit difference collision in controls.json. A broader
classification of such subgroup difference sets remains the hard gap.
